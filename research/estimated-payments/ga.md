## 1. Who must make estimated payments

The threshold is based on income, not on tax. Estimates are required if expected gross income exceeds the sum of:

1. the dependent exemptions,
2. estimated deductions, and
3. $1,000 of income not subject to withholding.

There is no dollar-of-tax threshold. Filing status affects the test only through the standard deduction.

For tax year 2026 the standard deduction is $15,000 for single, head of household or married filing separately, and $30,000 for married filing jointly. The dependent exemption is $5,000. HB 463 (2026) set these amounts. The $12,000, $24,000 and $4,000 figures on [500ES-26] are the tax year 2025 amounts. (corrected in verification)

- [HB463] § 2-3: "a married couple filing a joint return, $24,000.00 $30,000.00" (struck old amount, then new amount).
- [HB463] § 2-2: "a personal exemption in the amount of $4,000.00 $5,000.00 for each dependent".

There is one exception. No estimate is required if the employer, by agreement, withholds extra tax to cover the income.

- [500ES-26]: "reasonably expects to have gross income during the year which exceeds (1) applicable exemptions, plus (2) estimated deductions, plus (3) $1,000 of income not subject to withholding."
- [IT511-25], tax year 2025, uses the same test: "(1) exemption for dependents, plus (2) estimated deductions, plus (3) $1,000 of income not subject to withholding."
- [500ES-26], the exception: "Estimated tax is not required if, under an agreement between the employer and the employee, additional tax is withheld".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 70% of this year's tax. [UET-25] uses 70% of line 3, which is tax less credits.
- Prior year: 100% of the immediately preceding year's tax. The prior-year return must cover a 12-month period.
- There is no 110% tier and no AGI threshold. High-income taxpayers keep the 100% prior-year option. NOT FOUND: any higher percentage or loss of the option in [UET-25], [500ES-26] or [IT511-25].
- Whether the prior-year return must have been filed: not stated. [UET-25] says only "return must be for a 12-month period".

Quotes:

- [UET-25] line 4: "Enter 100% of the Immediately Preceding Year's Tax (return must be for a 12-month period)".
- [UET-25] line 5: "Enter 70% of the Amount Shown on Line 3".
- [UET-25] line 8: "Enter the lesser of Line 6 or Line 7 for each period".

The form also offers three per-installment exceptions (item 5). One of them uses 90% of actual year-to-date tax.

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

There are four equal installments due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. For a calendar year 2026 taxpayer the dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. The dates are given generically; no source lists 2026-specific dates.

- [500ES-26]: "in equal installments during this year on or before April 15, June 15, September 15, and the following January 15."
- [UET-25] line 6 says to divide the required amount "by the number of installments required for the year".

A taxpayer who first meets the requirement later in the year pays fewer, larger installments. [UET-25] instruction B gives the chart:

| Requirement first met | First due | Number of installments |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 1 – Mar 31 | April 15 | 4 |
| Apr 1 – May 31 | June 15 | 3 |
| Jun 1 – Aug 31 | Sept 15 | 2 |
| On or after Sept 1 | Jan 15 | 1 |

Weekend and holiday shifting applies.

- [500ES-26]: "If the due date falls on a weekend or holiday, the tax shall be due on the next day that is not a weekend or holiday."
- [UET-25]: "If any date shown falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, substitute the next regular workday."

January rule: Georgia does not waive the fourth installment when the return is filed by January 31. Instead, a return filed and paid by January 31 counts as paid on January 15. (corrected in verification: the earlier wording was self-contradictory) [UET-25] instruction F: "If you filed your return and paid the balance of tax by January 31st of the following year, consider the balance paid as of January 15th." This treats the payment as timely for the fourth installment. It does not waive earlier installments.

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in equal amounts each quarter. No election to use actual withholding dates was found.

- [UET-25] line 9: "Amounts paid on estimate for each period and tax withheld (withheld treated equally paid for each quarter)".
- [UET-25] line 13 uses the same rule: "(withheld treated equally paid for each quarter)".
- NOT FOUND: an election to use actual dates, in [UET-25], [500ES-26] or [IT511-25].

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

[UET-25] instruction D lets the taxpayer avoid the penalty for an installment if cumulative payments through that date (line 13) meet any one exception. A different exception may be used for each installment, with a separate computation page attached.

- **Exception 1, prior-year income at current rates.** Payments equal what would have been due on the prior year's income at this year's rates. [UET-25]: "what would have been due on your prior year's income if you had computed it at the current year's rates."
  - The form heads this line "using current year rates and exemptions". The instructions say to use "the other facts and the law applicable to your prior year's return". (added in verification)
- **Exception 2, annualized income at 70%.** [UET-25]: "equal or exceed 70 percent of the tax on your annualized taxable income for periods from the first of the year to the end of the month preceding".
- **Exception 3, actual income at 90%.** This uses 90% of tax on actual (not annualized) year-to-date income over 3, 5 and 8 months. It is not available for the fourth installment, and the form marks that column "Not Applicable". [UET-25]: "the law does not permit the use of exception 3 for the fourth installment period".
- **Exception 4, decedent's estate or testamentary trust.** This applies to fiduciaries only, for two years after death.

Other exceptions:

- Tax below an amount: NOT FOUND. There is no "tax under $X" exception on [UET-25]. The only threshold is the income-based filing test in item 1.
- No liability in the prior year: NOT FOUND as an explicit exception in [UET-25], [500ES-26] or [IT511-25]. If the prior-year tax was $0 on a 12-month return, line 4 is $0. Line 8, the lesser of lines 6 and 7, would then be $0. This is my reading of the form arithmetic, not a stated rule.
- First-year residents and part-year residents: NOT FOUND in [UET-25], [500ES-26] or [IT511-25].
- Farmers and fishermen: at least two-thirds of gross income from farming or fishing.
  - They are exempt if they file Form 500/501 and pay the tax by March 1. [UET-25] instruction G: "you filed Form 500 / 501 and paid the tax on or before March 1, of the following year you may be exempt".
  - [500ES-26]: they may "file their return by March 1 and pay the full amount of tax due by that date."
  - If they miss March 1, the penalty is computed on the last quarter only. The base is 66 2/3% of tax less amounts withheld or paid by January 15. It runs at 9% from January 15 to the earlier of payment or April 15. [UET-25] farmer schedule: "Enter 66 2/3% of the amount shown on Line 1".
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in [UET-25], [500ES-26] or [IT511-25].
- Casualty or disaster: no standing waiver on the form. DOR issues disaster-specific postponements that cover estimated payments. [DOR-IAN], 2022: "The relief also includes the quarterly estimated income tax payments due on January 16, 2023". A general casualty or reasonable-cause waiver for estimated tax: NOT FOUND.

## 6. Annualized income installment method

There is no separate annualized installment schedule. Annualization is available only as Exception 2 on Form 500 UET (line 15), at 70% of tax on annualized income.

The [UET-25] instructions give the steps:

1. Figure AGI less deductions through the month before the installment is due.
2. Multiply by 12.
3. Divide by the months in the period.
4. Subtract the dependent exemptions.

Exception 3 (line 16) is a related, non-annualized test. It uses 90% of tax on actual year-to-date income.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is a simple-interest-style charge of 9% per year. It applies to each installment's underpayment for the number of days from the due date to the earlier of the payment date or April 15. It is computed on Form 500 UET lines 17–21 and reported on Form 500 line 42.

The rate is a fixed 9%. Separately, general interest on unpaid tax is prime plus 3% (IT-511), and that does not apply to this penalty.

- [UET-25] line 20: "Penalty (9 percent a year on amount shown on Line 17 for the number of days shown on Line 19)".
- [UET-25] line 18: "Date of payment or April 15, 20_____whichever is earlier".
- [IT511-25]: "Failure to File Estimated Tax Penalty - 9 percent per year for the period of underpayment. Use Form 500 UET to calculate the penalty."
- [IT511-25] on general interest: "Interest that accrues beginning July 1, 2016, accrues at an annual rate equal to the Federal Reserve prime rate plus 3 percent."
- Multiple payments for one installment: [UET-25] instruction F: "If you made more than one payment for an installment attach a separate computation for each payment."

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The current-year safe harbor is 70%, not 90%.
- The prior-year safe harbor is always 100%. There is no 110% rule for high incomes.
- The filing threshold is an income test: gross income above exemptions plus deductions plus $1,000 of unwithheld income. It is not $1,000 of tax.
- The penalty is a fixed 9% a year.
- A return filed and paid by January 31 is treated as paying the fourth installment on January 15. The federal rule instead waives the fourth-installment penalty when the return is filed by January 31.
- Exception 1, prior-year income at current-year rates, is an exception federal law no longer has.
- Exception 3, 90% of actual year-to-date tax, cannot be used for the fourth installment.
- There are no first-year, part-year, retirement or disability exceptions in the DOR materials.
- A taxpayer who first meets the requirement mid-year owes fewer, larger installments.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. The [IT511-25] booklet mentions local income taxes only in the context of other states' taxes, such as the credit for taxes paid to other states and itemized-deduction add-backs. It describes no Georgia city, county or school-district income tax. This is an absence in the DOR materials, not an affirmative statement.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements between sources

- **Year of the voucher.** DOR's download labelled "2026-500-es" is Rev. 05/12/25. It shows "EXEMPTION AMOUNT FOR TAX YEAR 2025". No voucher or instructions specific to tax year 2026 were found.
- **No-prior-year-liability.** It is not stated as an exception. It follows only from the form arithmetic (item 5).
- **Prior-year return filed.** [UET-25] requires a 12-month prior-year return but does not say it must have been filed, or what applies when no return was filed.
- **Statute not verified.** The rules were not checked against O.C.G.A. text, because no official host could be fetched (see Sources).
- **Farmer computation.** The [UET-25] farmer schedule charges penalty "for the last quarter only". It is not clear how it applies if a farmer elected to "file as other taxpayers" under [500ES-26].
- **Fiscal years.** [UET-25] instruction C tells fiscal-year filers to "contact the Department" rather than giving rules.

### 2026 changes

- **Rate.** [HB463] § 2-1 sets 4.99% for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026: "shall be 5.19 percent 4.99 percent for taxable years beginning". Under the prior text of § 48-7-20(a.1), shown struck in [HB463], the 2026 rate would have been 5.09%. From 2027 the rate falls 0.125% a year to 3.99%. Each cut can be delayed by revenue triggers.
- **Deductions.** The standard deduction and dependent exemption changed for 2026 (item 1). From 2027 they rise each year, subject to the same delay triggers.
- **Estimated tax rules.** [HB463] does not amend the estimated tax sections (§§ 48-7-114 to 48-7-120). The 70% and 100% tests, the dates and the 9% rate are unchanged by it.
- **Exception 1 for 2026.** "Current year's rates" means 4.99% for 2026.
