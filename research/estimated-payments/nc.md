# North Carolina

North Carolina calls the underpayment charge "interest", not a penalty.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Anyone whose tax due, after North Carolina withholding and credits, will be $1,000 or more. By statute, the threshold is whatever amount IRC § 6654(e) sets. No filing-status differences were found.

- [NC-WEB]: estimated tax is required "if the tax shown due on your return, reduced by your North Carolina tax withheld and allowable tax credits, is $1,000 or more".
- [GS-15](f): no interest if tax reduced by withholding "is less than the amount set in section 6654(e) of the Code".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90%. Prior year: 100%.
- There is no 110% tier. No higher prior-year percentage appears at any income level, and no taxpayer loses the prior-year option because of high income.
- The prior-year option requires a filed return for a 12-month prior year.

Quotes:

- [GS-15](d)(1)–(2): "Ninety percent (90%) of the tax shown on the return for the taxable year" or "One hundred percent (100%) of the tax shown on the return of the individual for the preceding taxable year".
- [GS-15](d)(2): the prior-year option applies only "if the preceding taxable year was a taxable year of 12 months and the individual filed a return for that year."
- [NC-D422], 2025: "100% of your 2024 tax. (Your 2024 tax return must cover a 12-month period)".

## 3. Due dates and shares

- Due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. Each installment is 25%.
- If a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday, it moves to the next business day.
- If the obligation arises later in the year, the first payment catches up: ½ of the balance in June, ¾ in September, or all of it in January.
  - (corrected in verification) This catch-up schedule is payment guidance in [NC-40] only. [GS-15] has no late-arising rule. Form D-422 still sets each required installment at one quarter of the required annual payment. A later-arising obligation avoids interest on the earlier installments only through the annualised income installment method (item 6).
- The January payment is waived if the return is filed and the balance paid by January 31.

Quotes:

- [GS-15](d): "the amount of any required installment shall be twenty‑five percent (25%) of the required annual payment."
- [NC-40], 2026: "falls on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday, the payment is due on or before the next business day."
- [NC-40], 2026, for an installment first due in June: "Pay 1/2 of the balance of the net estimated income tax at that time"
- [NC-WEB]: "If you file your income tax return by January 31 … and pay your entire balance", the January 15 payment is not required.

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each due date, unless the taxpayer establishes the actual withholding dates.

- [GS-15](g): withholding is "considered to have been paid on each due date … unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld".
- [NC-D422], 2025: "you are considered to have paid one-fourth of these amounts on each payment due date unless you can show otherwise."

## 5. Exceptions with no interest

- **Tax below $1,000.** [NC-D422], 2025: no interest if the total tax shown due on the 2025 return "minus the amount of tax credits and North Carolina tax withheld is less than $1,000."
- **No liability in the prior year.** [NC-D422], 2025: "You did not have any individual income tax liability for 2024." The statute words this as "if the individual did not have any liability for tax … for the preceding taxable year" ([GS-15](f)). Neither source requires a 12-month year or US residence. See item 10.
- **First-year residents and part-year residents.** No separate exception exists. A new resident with no North Carolina liability in the prior year falls under the no-liability exception above.
  - (corrected in verification) [NC-D422A], 2025, has one part-year and nonresident rule for annualising. They "should complete only the column(s) for the period(s) in which they had income from North Carolina sources." They also multiply the annualised taxable income by the Form D-400, line 13 decimal (the North Carolina share of income).
- **Farmers and fishermen.** They qualify if at least two-thirds of gross income comes from farming, including oyster farming, or commercial fishing, in the current or prior year. They have one installment, due January 15, of the lesser of 66⅔% of the current year's tax or 100% of the prior year's tax. Filing and paying in full by March 1 removes interest. They must enter "E" in the exception box next to Form D-400, line 26e.
  - [GS-15](i)(1): "The individual is required to make only one installment payment of tax for that taxable year."
  - [GS-15](i)(2): no interest if "on or before March 1 of the following taxable year, the taxpayer files a return … and pays in full".
- **Retired or disabled; casualty or disaster.** None exists. (corrected in verification) The charge is interest, not a penalty, under [GS-15](a). [GS-237](a)(2) lets the Secretary waive interest only for a bankruptcy period: "taxes imposed prior to or during a period for which a taxpayer has declared bankruptcy". [NC-D401], 2025, lists only two exception codes for line 26e: farmers and fishermen, and annualised income.
- **Estates and trusts.** These are outside the section entirely. [GS-15](k): "This section shall not apply to any estate or trust."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, using Form D-422A, the Annualized Income Installment Worksheet. The applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%. Any reduction from annualising is recaptured in the next installment. Annualising for one period requires annualising for all of them.

- [GS-15](e): "First installment – twenty‑two and one‑half percent (22.5%)" … "Fourth installment – ninety percent (90%)."
- [NC-D422], 2025: "If you annualize your income for any payment period, you must annualize it for all periods."
- (corrected in verification) [NC-D422A], 2025, read in verification. The periods end 3-31, 5-31, 8-31 and 12-31. The annualisation factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1. Each required installment is the smaller of the annualised installment and the regular quarter plus any carried-forward shortfall. An annualising taxpayer must enter "E" in the exception box next to Form D-400, line 26e: "If you annualized income, you must enter the letter E in the exception box".

## 7. How the interest is computed

- **Method.** Interest is charged on each installment's underpayment for the days from its due date to the payment date, or April 15 of the following year if that is earlier. Payments apply to the earliest underpayment first. The computation is on Form D-422 and the result goes on Form D-400, line 26e. There is a short method and a regular method.
  - [NC-D422], 2025, line 26: "Underpayment on Line 23 X Number of days on Line 25 X .07 / 365".
  - [GS-15](b): "A payment of estimated tax shall be credited against unpaid required installments in the order in which such installments are required to be paid."
  - The 2025 short method multiplies the total underpayment by .04655, then reduces it for early payment.
- **Rate rule.** The Secretary of Revenue sets the rate twice a year. Each rate covers six months, from January 1 or July 1. It must be between 5% and 16%, and the Secretary must consider market conditions and the federal rate.
  - [GS-15](a): interest is applied at "the applicable annual rate established under G.S. 105‑241.21".
  - [GS-21](a): "may not be less than five percent (5%) per year and may not exceed sixteen percent (16%) per year."
- **Current rate.** 7% for July 1, 2026 – December 31, 2026, per [NC-INT]: "7% Interest Rate for July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026". [NC-D422] used 7% for April 15, 2025 – April 15, 2026.
  - (corrected in verification) The rate for January 1 – June 30, 2026 is also 7%. [NC-INT]'s history row reads "January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2026" at 7%. Every period touching tax year 2026 so far is 7%.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- There is no 110% rule for high-income taxpayers.
- The rate is set by the Secretary every six months, with a 5% floor and a 16% ceiling.
- The zero-prior-year-liability exception has no stated 12-month or residence condition.
- No relief for retirement, disability or casualty was found.
- Estates and trusts are not subject to estimated-tax interest at all.
- The threshold is pegged by statute to the IRC § 6654(e) amount, so it moves with federal law.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- **Zero prior-year liability.** [NC-D422] and [NC-WEB] state the exception without any 12-month or residence condition. Nebraska, New York and New Mexico do impose such conditions. Line 7 of [NC-D422] says: "If you were required to file a return for 2024 but did not … don't complete Line 7." This leaves open how a 12-month requirement interacts with the zero-liability exception.
- **Weekend shifting.** [NC-D422] says due dates on a weekend or holiday move to the next business day. Its column heading still shows "6/15/25", a Sunday, and its day counts run from that date.
- **Rate history on [NC-INT].** The history table lists "7% January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2026" in the same table that heads "Interest rates for periods before December 31, 2026". The period labels are inconsistent, but every figure shown is 7%.
- **Who "should" pay.** [NC-40] says taxpayers "should make" estimated payments at $1,000 or more. [NC-WEB] says "You are required to pay". The obligation is enforced through interest under [GS-15].
- **Farmer test.** [NC-40] and [NC-WEB] describe it using "estimated gross income" for the current year. [GS-15](i)(3) also allows the prior year's gross income shown on the return. (corrected in verification) [NC-D422], 2025, agrees with the statute: "at least two-thirds of your gross income from all sources for 2024 or 2025".

### Resolved conflicts

- **Zero prior-year liability.** [GS-15](f) governs. It grants the exception whenever the individual "did not have any liability for tax under Part 2 of Article 4 for the preceding taxable year". It sets no 12-month or residence condition. The [NC-D422] line 7 instruction concerns computing the prior-year amount for the required annual payment. It does not narrow the exception. [NC-D422] defines liability as "the amount of tax shown due on the return less any tax credits".
- **Weekend shifting.** [GS-15](c) states fixed dates and has no shift. [NC-40] and [NC-D422] both apply the next-business-day rule. [NC-D422]'s day counts (365, 304, 212, 90 days to April 15, 2026) run from June 15, 2025, a Sunday. No 2026 due date falls on a weekend (April 15, 2026 is a Wednesday; June 15 a Monday; September 15 a Tuesday; January 15, 2027 a Friday). Unresolved but immaterial for tax year 2026: whether a shifted payment is counted late from the nominal date.
- **Rate history labels.** [NC-INT] heads the table "Interest rates for periods before December 31, 2026 are as follows:" and lists "January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2026" at 7%. The labels overlap with the July–December 2026 headline, but every figure is 7%. No number changes.
- **"Should" versus "required".** [GS-15] does not impose a duty to pay. It only charges interest on an underpayment. [NC-WEB]'s "required" and [NC-40]'s "should" describe the same rule. For the data model, the obligation is the interest charge.
- **Farmer test.** [GS-15](i)(3) governs: current-year or prior-year gross income. [NC-D422] matches it. [NC-40] and [NC-WEB] simplify it to the current year.
