South Carolina's statute adopts federal IRC §6654 for individuals, with a $100 small-amount threshold in place of the federal $1,000 (sources 4 and 5). Most federal mechanics therefore carry over.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must make estimated payments if you expect to owe $100 or more with your SC1040, after withholding and nonrefundable credits. The sources show no filing-status difference in the $100 threshold.

- SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "You must file a Declaration of Estimated Tax if you expect to owe an Income Tax liability of $100 or more".
- SC1040ES worksheet line 10, TY2026, source 1: "If $100 or more, you must make Estimated Tax payments."
- S.C. Code §12-6-3910(A)(1), source 5: "the small amount provisions in Internal Revenue Code Sections 6654(e)(1) and 6655(f) are one hundred dollars".

The SC1040ES also exempts farmers and commercial fishermen, people whose prior-year tax was $0 for a full 12 months, and some nonresident contractors with 2% withholding (see question 5).

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% if prior-year AGI was more than $150,000. The SC2210 adds a married-filing-separately threshold of more than $75,000. The SC1040ES and SC1040 instructions do not mention the $75,000 figure.
- The prior-year option requires a filed prior-year SC1040 covering a 12-month year.
- A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option. The percentage rises to 110%.

Quotes:
- SC2210 Part I line 4, TY2025, source 2: "Multiply line 3 by 90%".
- SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "You must have filed a SC1040 for the prior tax year, and it must have covered a 12-month year."
- SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "If the adjusted gross income on your prior year SC1040 was more than $150,000, the 100% rule is adjusted to 110%".
- SC2210 Part I line 7, TY2025, source 2: "110% of the tax if the adjusted gross income was more than $150,000, or if married filing separately for 2025, more than $75,000".
- SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: AGI for the 110% test is computed "by using federal guidelines and making South Carolina adjustments."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% of the required annual payment in each column.
- A taxpayer who first meets the requirement later in the year pays 1/2, 3/4 or all of the estimate at the first required date.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day. South Carolina's legal-holiday list includes state holidays such as Confederate Memorial Day (May 10), which federal law does not.
- January payment waived: yes, if the return is filed and the full balance paid by early February. The documents give different dates (see question 10).

Quotes:
- SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "your Estimated Tax payments are due in four equal installments".
- SC2210 line 9, TY2025, source 2: "multiply SC2210, line 8 by 25% and enter in each column."
- S.C. Code §12-6-3910(A)(3)(a), source 5: due dates are "April 15", "June 15", "September 15" and "January 15 of the following taxable year."
- SC Revenue Procedure #23-1 quoting §12-60-50, source 6: "when the last day of a specified time period is a Saturday, Sunday, or a legal holiday, the end of the period is extended"
- SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "you can choose to file your SC1040 by February 1 and pay the full balance of Income Tax due."
- SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "pay the tax due by January 31, 2026, you will have no penalty for the installment due January 15".

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in equal amounts on the four due dates. The taxpayer can instead use the dates actually withheld, by checking box D on the SC2210.

- SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: withholding is "considered payments of Estimated Tax in equal installments on the four installment dates, unless you prove otherwise."
- SC2210 Part II box D, TY2025, source 2: "treating the South Carolina Income Tax withheld from your wages as paid on the dates it was actually withheld".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if tax minus withholding is less than $100. SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "the total tax on your 2025 return minus the amount of tax you paid through withholding is less than $100."
- No liability in the prior year: no penalty if prior-year tax was $0 for a full 12 months. SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "your prior year tax liability was $0 for a full 12 months". The SC forms state no US citizen or resident condition, but the statute adopts §6654 "in the same manner", so the federal condition applies: the taxpayer must have been "a citizen or resident of the United States throughout the preceding taxable year" (26 U.S.C. §6654(e)(2)(C), source 12). (corrected in verification)
- First-year residents: no South Carolina rule of their own. A new resident with no South Carolina liability for a 12-month prior year falls under the exception above, if a US citizen or resident all that year (§12-54-55(1) adopting §6654(e)(2), sources 4 and 12). (corrected in verification)
- Part-year residents: no exception of their own, confirmed in verification. The SC1040ES tells nonresidents to use Schedule NR to estimate tax but gives no penalty exception.
- Farmers and fishermen: 2/3 of gross income from farming or fishing in the current or prior year. No penalty if the return is filed and all tax paid by March 1 (next business day if a weekend). The SC1040ES also allows one payment by January 15. If the return is not filed by March 1, the federal Form 2210-F is completed "for South Carolina purposes". No 66⅔% required-payment percentage is stated in South Carolina's own documents; it comes in through the federal 2210-F.
  - SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "paid the entire tax due by March 1 following the end of the tax year." Also: "If March 1 falls on a weekend, the due date is the next business day."
  - SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "pay all of their Estimated Tax by January 15, 2027 or file their 2026 SC1040 and pay the total tax due by March 1"
  - SC1040 instructions line 33, TY2025, source 3: farmers who "pay your tax due by March 2, 2026" (March 1, 2026 was a Sunday).
- Retired or disabled: waiver available if the taxpayer retired after age 62 or became disabled in the current or prior year and the underpayment was due to reasonable cause. SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "In 2024 or 2025, you retired after age 62, or became disabled and your underpayment was due to reasonable cause."
- Casualty or disaster: waiver available. SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "An underpayment was due to a casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstance and it would be inequitable to impose the penalty."
- South Carolina-only exception: no penalty on underpayment caused by personal service income earned in another state that withheld its own tax. S.C. Code §12-54-55(1), source 4: "No interest or penalty is due under this item for underpayments attributable to personal service income earned in another state".
- South Carolina-only filing exemption: nonresidents working under contracts over $10,000 with 2% withholding need not file a declaration. SC1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "where the contract is more than $10,000 and tax is withheld at 2% from each contract payment".

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed. South Carolina has no schedule of its own. The taxpayer completes federal Form 2210 Schedule AI "for South Carolina purposes", writes "Annualized" next to SC2210 line 9, attaches Schedule AI, and enters exception code A on SC1040 line 33.

- SC2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "Complete Schedule AI of the federal 2210 for South Carolina purposes."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

An interest-like charge on each installment's shortfall for the days it is late. The period ends on the date paid or April 15 of the next year, whichever is earlier. The charge is simple interest.

- Rate rule: the SCDOR sets the rate in the same manner and at the same time as the federal underpayment rate under IRC §6621(a)(2). S.C. Code §12-54-25(D), source 4: "established by the department in the same manner and at the same time as the underpayment rate provided in Internal Revenue Code Sections 6621(a)(2)"
- Simple, not compound: SC Information Letter #26-15, source 7: "simple interest applies to the underpayment of declaration of estimated tax."
- Rates in effect: 7% for 01/01/25 – 03/31/26; 6% for 04/01/26 – 06/30/26; 7% for 07/01/26 – 12/31/26. The last period comes from SC Information Letter #26-19 (August 24, 2026, source 10), which supersedes #26-15's "07/01/26 – 09/30/26". (corrected in verification) The 2025 SC2210 uses .07 in all four rate periods (source 2), including April 1–15, 2026, when the published rate was 6%.
- Day count: days / 365 (SC2210 lines 19–25, source 2).
- Form: SC2210, Part III. If only a waiver box applies, the SCDOR computes the penalty and bills.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The threshold is $100, not $1,000.
- No penalty on underpayment caused by income earned in another state that withheld its own tax.
- Nonresident contractors with 2% contract withholding are exempt from filing a declaration.
- The state legal-holiday list used for weekend and holiday shifting includes state holidays (for example Confederate Memorial Day, May 10).
- Act 110 of 2026 changed 2026 rates and replaced the federal-taxable-income starting point with federal AGI. The SC1040ES (Rev. 10/13/25) worksheet predates the Act and still starts from federal taxable income with the old rate schedule (see question 10).

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. Sources 1–3 mention no city, county or school-district income tax. No primary source was found that states this explicitly.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- 110% AGI threshold wording. The SC1040ES and SC2210 say "more than $150,000". The SC1040 instructions (source 3, line 33) say "If your adjusted gross income is $150,000 or more". The SC1040 instructions also do not say whether the AGI is the prior-year or current-year AGI. The SC1040ES says prior-year.
- Married filing separately. Only the SC2210 states the $75,000 MFS threshold. The SC1040ES and SC1040 instructions omit it.
- January waiver date. The SC1040ES (TY2026) says "file your SC1040 by February 1". The SC2210 (TY2025) says "by January 31, 2026". These may be the same rule with weekend shifting applied differently, but neither document says so.
- The SC2210 Part I line 7 text gives only the 110% rule and does not restate the 100% rule for other taxpayers. The SC1040ES and SC1040 instructions state the 100% rule.
- 2026 worksheet out of date. SC Information Letter #26-20 (source 8) says Act 110 of 2026 sets 2026 rates at 1.99% and 5.21% and starts from federal AGI. The SC1040ES 2026 worksheet (Rev. 10/13/25) uses federal taxable income and a 0%/3%/6% schedule. The SCDOR news item (source 9) tells taxpayers to read IL 26-20 before the September payment, but no revised SC1040ES was found.
- Farmers' required percentage. South Carolina documents do not state a 66⅔% figure. It applies only through the federal 2210-F reference.

### Conflicts resolved

- **110% threshold wording and year.** The statute governs. §12-54-55(1) applies §6654 "in the same manner". §6654(d)(1)(C)(i) applies 110% when prior-year AGI "exceeds $150,000" (source 12). So the SC1040ES and SC2210 ("more than") are right, and the SC1040 instructions ("$150,000 or more") are wrong at exactly $150,000. The AGI is the prior year's. Neither section has been amended since 2005 (§12-54-55) and 2010 (§12-6-3910), and Act 110 does not touch them. Still open: whether "adjusted gross income" means federal AGI or the SC-adjusted figure the SC1040ES describes.
- **Married filing separately.** Resolved by the same incorporation. §6654(d)(1)(C)(ii) substitutes "$75,000" for a separate return.
- **January waiver date.** Same rule. §6654(h) sets "on or before January 31", and §12-60-50 (quoted in RP #23-1) moves a weekend date to the next business day. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the SC1040ES's February 1, 2027 is the shifted date. January 31, 2026 was a Saturday. The SC2210's "January 31, 2026" is one business day earlier than the law requires.
- **SC2210 line 7 omits the 100% rule.** The statute governs: 100% of prior-year tax, 110% only above the threshold (§6654(d)(1)(B)(ii) and (C)).
- **Farmers' percentage.** Resolved: 66⅔% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax, through §6654(i)(1)(C). The 110% rule does not apply to farmers.
- **2026 worksheet out of date.** Confirmed. Act 110 changes the tax computation, not the estimated-tax rules. No revised SC1040ES was found. This affects the size of the estimate, not the thresholds or dates.
- **Rate for April 1–15, 2026 (new).** The 2025 SC2210 charges .07 through April 15, 2026. IL #26-19 lists 6% from 04/01/26. §12-54-25(D) sets the rate "at the same time" as the federal rate, so those 15 days should carry 6%. The difference is at most 15 days at 1 point.

### 2026 changes

- Act 110 of 2026 (H.4216; ratified March 25, approved March 30, 2026) "first applies to tax years beginning after 2025" (source 11). It sets two brackets (1.99%, and 5.21% minus $966 from $30,000) and starts from federal AGI. Its eight sections do not amend §12-6-3910 or §12-54-55. Thresholds, percentages and dates are unchanged.
- Rate: 6% for April 1 – June 30, 2026, otherwise 7% through December 31, 2026 (source 10).
- The statehouse code pages do not yet show any 2026 act; §12-6-510 still shows the old brackets. I checked Act 110's text directly but did not search every 2026 act.
