## 1. Does Florida tax individual income for tax year 2026?

**No.** Florida has no tax on wages, interest, dividends, capital gains or other income of individuals.

- Source 2: "Florida does not impose a personal income tax, so there are no filing requirements."
- Source 3: "Florida does not have an income tax for individuals, and therefore, no capital gains tax for individuals."
- Source 4: "Florida does not impose personal income tax, inheritance tax, gift taxes, or tax on intangible personal property."
- Source 1, Art. VII, s. 5(a): "No tax upon … the income of natural persons who are residents or citizens of the state shall be levied by the state". The sentence continues "or under its authority". It caps any such tax at amounts creditable against a similar federal or other-state tax.

## 2. Estimated payment rules

Not applicable. No individual income-type tax exists. Florida's corporate income tax applies to corporations (Source 3), not to individuals.

## 3. Local income taxes

None. The constitutional limit covers any tax levied "by the state, or under its authority", which includes counties and municipalities. Source 4 lists local taxes on residents, such as transient rental taxes, and includes no income tax.

- Art. VII, s. 9(a) confirms the link (Source 1). Local governments "may be authorized by general law to levy other taxes", except "taxes prohibited by this constitution." (corrected in verification)

## 4. Ambiguities

- **The constitutional cap is not literally zero.** Art. VII, s. 5(a) caps a tax on residents' income at the amount creditable against a similar federal or other-state tax. No such credit is currently allowed, so the cap is effectively zero. Florida levies no individual income tax (Sources 2–4).
- **Nonresidents.** The constitutional text protects "residents or citizens of the state". The Department of Revenue statements (Sources 2 and 3) say Florida has no individual income tax at all, so nonresidents owe none either.
