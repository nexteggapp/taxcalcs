## 1. Who must make estimated payments

For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, you must pay if your expected Virginia tax after withholding and credits is more than $1,000. You are also exempt if your expected Virginia AGI is under the filing threshold: $11,950 single, $23,900 married filing jointly, $11,950 married filing separately.

- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "is expected to be more than $1,000, then you must make estimated tax payments or have additional income tax withheld".
- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: exempt if "your expected estimated tax liability exceeds your withholding and tax credits by $1,000 or less."
- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: exempt if "you are single and your expected Virginia adjusted gross income ... is less than $11,950" (married filing jointly: "less than $23,900").

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90% (66⅔% for farmers, fishermen and merchant seamen).
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage for high income: none. No 110% rule was found in sources 1–9.
- The prior-year exception requires a prior-year return covering 12 months that showed a tax liability.
- High-income taxpayers keep the prior-year option.

Virginia also keeps two older exceptions that federal law dropped: prior-year income at current-year rates, and 90% of tax on actual income to date (question 6).

Quotes:
- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "based on 90% of the current year's income tax liability or 100% of your liability for the preceding year, whichever is less."
- 760C Instructions, TY2025, source 3, Exception 1: "Your 2024 Virginia income tax return must have covered a full (twelve month) taxable year, and must have reflected an income tax liability."
- 760C Instructions, TY2025, source 3, Exception 2: "You do not need to have filed a Virginia return or incurred a Virginia tax liability for the preceding taxable year"

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: May 1, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027. The first payment is due May 1, not April 15.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% each. A taxpayer who first meets the requirement later pays 1/3, 1/2 or all at the first required date.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day.
- January payment waived: yes, if the return is filed and the balance paid in full by March 1, 2027.

Quotes:
- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "in equal installments on or before May 1, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027."
- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, you may file and make payment without penalty or interest on the next succeeding business day."
- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "pay the balance of tax due in full by March 1, 2027, you are not required to make the estimated tax payment"
- Code §58.1-491(A)(1), source 6: "the estimated tax shall be paid in four equal installments."

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each installment date, unless the taxpayer shows the actual withholding dates. On Form 760C the taxpayer chooses by dividing by four or entering actual amounts with a schedule.

- Code §58.1-492(D)(2), source 7: "an equal part of such amount shall be deemed paid on each installment date ... unless the taxpayer establishes the dates"
- 760C Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "you may divide the total amount withheld by four, or enter the exact amount withheld for each installment and enclose a schedule".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: for 2026, no addition to tax if total underpayments for the year are $1,000 or less. For 2025 the figure was $150.
  - 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "the sum of all installment underpayments for the taxable year is $1,000 or less."
  - Code §58.1-492(D)(3), source 7: "There shall be no addition to tax imposed for underpayment of estimated tax of $1,000 or less for the taxable year."
  - 760C line 14, TY2025, source 2: "If the sum of all underpayments ... reported is $150 or less, stop here".
- Below the filing threshold: no Form 760C or 760F is needed if VAGI is below the filing threshold (760F, source 4).
- No liability in the prior year: Exception 1 does not apply if the prior year showed no liability. But the underpayment itself is measured against the lesser of 90% of current tax or "100 percent of the tax shown on the return of the taxpayer for the preceding taxable year" (Code §58.1-492(A)(1), source 7), with no liability or 12-month condition. Form 760C line 4 does the same: "Enter the Amount From Line 2 or Line 3, Whichever Is Less" (source 2). So a filed prior-year return showing $0 tax makes the measured underpayment zero. The Department does not say this in words; it is how the statute and form compute. Exception 2 (prior-year income at current rates) needs no prior-year return or liability. (corrected in verification)
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND. Exception 2 is the nearest rule.
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND as an exception. The 760ES says part-year residents must prorate deductions and exemptions when estimating.
- Farmers, fishermen and merchant seamen (two-thirds of gross income from self-employed farming, fishing or being a merchant seaman): one payment, due January 15, of 66⅔% of the current-year tax (or 100% of prior year). No addition to tax if the return is filed and the tax paid in full by March 1. Otherwise use Form 760F.
  - 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "you only need to file Payment Voucher 4 by January 15, 2027."
  - 760F line 4, TY2025, source 4: "Enter 66 2/3% (.667) of the Amount on Line 3".
  - 760C Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "paid the entire tax due by March 1, 2026".
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in sources 1–8.
- Casualty or disaster: NOT FOUND in sources 1–8.

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed, as Exception 3 on Form 760C. Virginia also allows Exception 4, 90% of tax on actual income for the first 4, 5 and 8 months. Neither exception applies to the fourth installment. Both are worksheets on Form 760C itself, not a separate schedule.

- 760C line 18, TY2025, source 2: "Exception 3 Worksheet: Tax on Annualized 2025 Income". Factors are 3, 2.4 and 1.5 for periods ending April 30, May 31 and August 31. Applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45% and 67.5%.
- 760C, TY2025, source 2: "Exceptions 3 and 4 do not apply to the fourth installment period."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

An interest-like "addition to tax" on each installment's shortfall for the days late. The period runs from the installment date to the date paid or May 1 of the next year, whichever is earlier. Exceptions are tested installment by installment and void the addition for that period only.

- Rate rule: the federal IRC §6621(a)(2) underpayment rate plus 2%. Code §58.1-15(A), source 8: "the 'Underpayment Rate' established pursuant to § 6621(a)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code plus two percent." Code §58.1-15(B), source 8, extends the rate for the third month after year-end "through May 1".
- Rate on the 2025 form: 9% a year, applied as a daily rate of .00025. 760C line 23, TY2025, source 2: "Multiply the Number of Days ... by the Daily Rate .00025 (9% Per Annum)". The same figure is on 760F line 11.
- 2026 rates: 9% for the first quarter, 8% for the second, 9% for the third and 9% for the fourth (Tax Bulletins 25-7, 26-2, 26-3, 26-4, source 11). Bulletin 26-2: "the Virginia rates for the second quarter of 2026 will be 8% for tax underpayments". Under §58.1-15(B) the rate for the third month after year-end applies through May 1. (corrected in verification) The Penalties and Interest page (source 9) states only the rule.
- Period end: Code §58.1-492(B)(1), source 7: "May 1, if a calendar year".
- Forms: Form 760C (individuals, estates, trusts); Form 760F (farmers, fishermen, merchant seamen).

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The first installment is due May 1, not April 15. The penalty period also ends May 1.
- No 110% rule for high incomes.
- Exception 1 fails if the prior year showed no tax, but a prior-year return showing $0 still makes the measured underpayment zero (question 5). (corrected in verification)
- Two extra exceptions: prior-year income at current rates, and 90% of tax on actual income to date.
- The rate is the federal underpayment rate plus 2 points.
- Merchant seamen get the farmer and fisherman rules.
- Estimated payments may be filed with the taxpayer's city or county instead of the state.
- If fewer than four installments were required, the taxpayer must file Form 760C to show it. Otherwise the Department computes the addition as if four were required.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

No local income taxes with their own estimated payments were found. Virginia localities do, however, accept state estimated payments: a taxpayer may mail the 760ES voucher to the local Commissioner of the Revenue or Treasurer. The claim that "the statute directs the commissioner of the revenue to record declarations" had no citation and is not in §§58.1-490 to 58.1-492; it is removed. (corrected in verification)

- 760ES, TY2026, source 1: "send your voucher and payment directly to the Department of Taxation ... or to the Virginia city or county where you live."
- 760ES voucher, source 1: "If you file locally, make your check payable to your local Treasurer."

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Thresholds changed for 2026. The 2025 760C, 760F and 760C instructions use $150 for both the filing test and the small-underpayment exception. The 2026 760ES and current §58.1-492(D)(3) use $1,000. The statute's history cites 2025, c. 725 and 2026, c. 7. The 2026 penalty form, which will show how the $1,000 is applied, was not yet published.
- Two kinds of small-amount test. The 760ES filing test is tax after withholding and credits "more than $1,000". The penalty test is "the sum of all installment underpayments ... is $1,000 or less". These measure different things.
- Statute versus form on the prior-year option. §58.1-492(A)(1) computes the underpayment against the lesser of 90% current or 100% prior-year tax with no liability condition. §58.1-492(C)(1)(a) and the 760C instructions require that the prior-year return showed a liability for the exception. The 760C Part I line 3 uses prior-year tax without stating that condition.
- The 9% rate on the 2025 forms is applied as .00025 a day, which is 9.125% over 365 days. The forms do not explain the rounding.

### Conflicts resolved

- **$150 versus $1,000.** Resolved. 2026 Acts c. 7 (H 29, approved February 20, 2026), fifth enactment, changed §58.1-492(D)(3) from "$150" to "$1,000". The sixth enactment says it applies "to taxable years beginning on and after January 1, 2026" (source 10). The 2025 forms correctly show $150; the 2026 760ES correctly shows $1,000.
- **Filing threshold (new conflict).** Unresolved. §58.1-490(A) leaves the filing threshold to regulation. 23VAC10-112-20 still requires a declaration if estimated tax "is greater than $150", with $5,000 and $8,000 VAGI floors (source 12). The 2026 760ES uses $1,000, $11,950 and $23,900. No amended regulation was found. Under the statute, no addition is charged unless underpayments exceed $1,000, so the stale regulation changes no penalty number.
- **Two small-amount tests.** Resolved. The filing test (760ES Section I) and the penalty test (§58.1-492(D)(3)) are different rules. The statute says "underpayment of estimated tax of $1,000 or less for the taxable year", which the 760ES reads as the sum of installment underpayments.
- **Prior-year option.** Resolved by statute. §58.1-492(A)(1) measures the underpayment against the lesser of 90% of current tax or 100% of prior-year tax shown on a return, with no liability or 12-month condition. Exception 1's conditions (§58.1-492(C)(1)(a)) matter only when (A)(1) already shows an underpayment. A filed prior-year return showing $0 gives a zero base. Unresolved: what applies when no prior-year return was filed at all. (A)(1) needs a "return" to read the prior-year tax from.
- **.00025 a day.** Unresolved, small. 9% / 365 is .000247. The forms round to .00025, an effective 9.125%. 23VAC10-112-42's examples use the annual rate × days / 365. The rounding overstates the addition by about 1.4%.
