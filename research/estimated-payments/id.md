# Idaho: individual estimated income tax and underpayment penalty

Idaho does not require individuals to make estimated payments and has no individual underpayment penalty. The statutes on estimated tax and on interest for underpaying it apply only to corporations. Individuals may prepay voluntarily with Form 51.

The closest equivalent is the extension prepayment test. To get the automatic six-month extension, a taxpayer must pay by April 15 the smaller of 80% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's. Missing that test brings a late-payment penalty of 2% a month.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

No individual is required to.

- [F40-I25], tax year 2025: "Idaho doesn't require estimated tax payments. You can prepay at any time."
- [IC-3036A] § 63-3036A(a) applies only to corporations: "Any corporation subject to this chapter which is required to make a payment of estimated taxes to the internal revenue service and will have an Idaho income tax liability of five hundred dollars ($500) or more shall pay".
- Voluntary payments: [F51] can be used to "Make estimated payments for tax year 2026".

## 2. Required annual payment

Not applicable to individual estimated tax.

The extension prepayment test is similar. It requires the lesser of 100% of the prior year's tax, if a prior Idaho return was filed, or 80% of the current year's tax. There is no 110% tier and no AGI threshold.

- [F51], for tax year 2025 returns: "100% of the income tax on your 2024 return (if you filed). You can't use this method if you've never filed in Idaho."
- [F51]: "80% of the estimated tax due on your 2025 return."
- [F51] worksheet line 7: "Enter the lesser of line 1 or line 6. If you didn't file a 2024 tax return, then enter the amount from line 6".
- [F51]: if the payment due "is $50 or less, you don't have to pay it now to get the extension."
- [F40-I25] words the prior-year test differently: "100% of what you paid for state income taxes the year before". See item 10.
- The statute governs, and it matches [F51]: the test is the prior year's tax due on the return, not the amount paid. [IC-3033] § 63-3033(a): "or the total tax due on the income tax return for the prior year if a return was filed for the prior year." (corrected in verification)
- The $50 rule is in [IC-3033] § 63-3033(b): "is fifty dollars ($50.00) or less, such payment shall not be required in order to qualify for the extension." (added in verification)

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

There are no individual installment due dates. The extension prepayment is due by April 15 (April 15, 2026 for tax year 2025). [F51]: "You must pay that amount online or postmark it by April 15, 2026."

- Weekend shifting for estimates: not applicable.
- January waiver: not applicable.

## 4. How withholding is treated

Not applicable to estimated tax. In the extension test, withholding and Form 51 payments count as of the return due date, with no installment timing. [F51] lines 8–10 add "Idaho income tax withheld" and "2025 Form 51 payments" and subtract them from the required amount.

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

There is no individual underpayment penalty, so no exceptions are needed.

Penalties that do apply to individuals are on the return:

- late filing, 5% a month, to a maximum of 25% ([ISTC-PEN]; [IC-3046] § 63-3046(c)(1));
- late payment after a timely return, 0.5% a month, to a maximum of 25% ([ISTC-PEN]; [IC-3046] § 63-3046(c)(2));
- the 25% cap is a combined cap that includes the § 63-3033 extension penalty. [IC-3046] § 63-3046(g): "and under section 63-3033, Idaho Code, shall not exceed twenty-five percent (25%) of the tax due". (added in verification)
- the minimum penalty is $10 ([ISTC-PEN]; [IC-3046] § 63-3046(f)). (added in verification)
- no valid extension and tax unpaid by the due date, 2% a month.

Combat-zone relief: [F40-I25]: "No interest or penalty accrues for nonpayment of individual income taxes while you're in a combat zone."

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Not applicable to individuals. [IC-3036A] § 63-3036A(c)(2) allows annualization for corporations only.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

For individuals there is no estimated tax penalty. Two charges can apply to unpaid tax.

**Interest** runs on tax unpaid from the original return due date. [F40-I25]: "at the rate of 6% per year (rate effective for 2026)." The rate is set by [IC-3045] § 63-3045(7)(c) (corrected in verification). It is 2% plus the federal midterm rate in effect on September 15 of the prior year, rounded to a whole number. [IC-3045]: "two percent (2%) plus the rate determined under section 1274(d)". [ISTC-INT] lists 2026 as "6% annual, 0.0050 monthly, 0.00016438 daily". [ISTC-INT] lists 6% for 2024, 2025 and 2027 as well. The 2024 daily factor is 0.00016393, which is 6%/366 for the leap year.

**Extension penalty.** Without a valid extension, the penalty is 2% a month from the original due date. [ISTC-PEN]: "Don't have a valid extension and don't pay tax due by the original due date (2%/month from the original due date to the payment date)."

The statute is [IC-3033] § 63-3033(f) (corrected in verification). The penalty applies only if the payment is below both 80% of this year's tax and 100% of last year's. It falls on the whole balance due, unless the taxpayer shows reasonable cause. There are two cases:

- Tax paid by the extended due date: [IC-3033] § 63-3033(f)(1): "two percent (2%) per month from the original due date to the date of payment."
- Tax not paid by the extended due date: the § 63-3046(c) penalty applies instead, from the original due date. [IC-3033] § 63-3033(f)(2): "the penalty provided in section 63-3046 (c), Idaho Code, from the original due date."

For comparison, corporations pay interest on underpaid estimates. [IC-3046A] § 63-3046A(b): "Interest shall apply from the due date of the quarterly report until the required amount is paid, or until ... the due date of the return". The rate is the § 63-3045 deficiency rate.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- There are no required quarterly payments and no quarterly underpayment penalty for individuals.
- The real cliff is at April 15. The taxpayer must have paid 80% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's by then. Otherwise the extension is invalid and the 2%-a-month penalty applies from the original due date.
- Interest runs only from April 15, at 6% for 2026. It does not run from quarterly dates.
- The prior-year prepayment option needs an Idaho return filed for the prior year. First-time Idaho filers can use only the 80% test.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. The [F40-I25] instructions describe no city, county or school-district income tax. The Tax Commission administers city sales taxes, but no local income taxes.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements between sources

- **Wording of the prior-year extension test.** [F40-I25] says "100% of what you paid for state income taxes the year before". [F51] says "100% of the income tax on your 2024 return". The [F51] worksheet, line 1, uses tax on the return less certain lines, not the amount paid.
- **Form 41ESR.** Its title "Underpayment of Estimated Tax" suggests it could apply to individuals, but it sits under business forms. I checked one older version (EFO00027_10-24-2016.pdf). Its instructions begin "A corporation must make estimated tax payments to the Idaho". That is consistent with [IC-3036A]. The 2025 version was not read.
- **Interest rate.** The 6% figure comes from [F40-I25]. Idaho Code § 63-3045, which sets it, was not fetched.

### Resolved conflicts

- **Prior-year extension test.** The statute governs. [IC-3033] § 63-3033(a) uses "the total tax due on the income tax return for the prior year". That matches [F51] and its worksheet line 1. The [F40-I25] phrase "what you paid" is loose wording. § 63-3033 was last amended in 2014, so no newer law explains the difference.
- **Form 41ESR.** The 2025 version (EFO00027_09-18-2025) is for corporations. [F41ESR-25]: "A corporation must make estimated tax payments to the Tax Commission if it's required to make estimated tax payments to the Internal Revenue Service". It also says "Estimated payments aren't required on tax due on income you report for individual owners."
- **Interest rate.** [IC-3045] § 63-3045(7)(c) sets the rate each calendar year. It is 2% plus the federal midterm rate on September 15 of the prior year, rounded to a whole number. [ISTC-INT] publishes 6% for 2026, matching [F40-I25].
