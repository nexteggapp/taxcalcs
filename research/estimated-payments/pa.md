## 1. Who must make estimated payments

- The test is income-based, not a tax-dollar threshold. For 2026 an individual must pay estimates if expected tax after withholding and credits is at least $430, which is $14,000 of income not subject to employer withholding. Source 1 (TY2026): "can reasonably expect to owe at least $430 ($14,000 of income not subject to employer withholding) in tax after subtracting withholding and credits".
- The statute sets the boundary differently (corrected in verification). Income not subject to withholding must exceed $14,000. Income of exactly $14,000 does not trigger the requirement. Source 8 (§ 325(a)(1)): "can reasonably be expected to exceed the following dollar amount for the applicable taxable year". The statute governs; see the verification section.
- A second condition also applies. Withholding and credits must be expected to fall below the smaller of 90% of 2026 tax or the prior-year safe harbor. Source 1 (TY2026): "can reasonably expect withholdings and credits to be less than the smaller of".
- The income threshold rises by statute: $8,000 (2023 and prior), $9,500 (2024), $11,000 (2025), $14,000 (2026), $17,000 (2027), $20,000 (2028). After 2028 it rises $500 a year. Source 8 (TRC § 325(a)(2)): "the dollar amount under paragraph (1) shall increase annually by five hundred dollars ($500)."
- The 2025 figures were $11,000 of income and $338 of tax. Source 3 (TY2025, REV-1630 Line 3): "Subtract Line 2 from Line 1a. If result is less than $338, stop here."
- The rules cover residents, part-year residents and nonresidents with PA-source income. Source 1 (TY2026): "Resident and part-year resident individuals; and • Nonresident individuals (or residents of other countries)".
- Filing status does not change the threshold. Spouses may file a joint declaration unless an exclusion applies (separated by decree, different tax years, one spouse liable for child support, or required by the department to file separately). Source 1 (TY2026): "A taxpayer and spouse may file a joint return declaration, unless". The statute makes a joint declaration's liability joint and several. Source 8 (§ 325(c)): "the liability with respect to the estimated tax shall be joint and several."
- PA residents working in a reciprocal state without PA withholding must pay estimates. Source 3 (TY2025): "If you are a Pennsylvania resident working in Indiana, Maryland, New Jersey, Ohio, Virginia, or West Virginia".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year option: 90% of the current year's tax. Source 3 (TY2025, Line 1b): "Multiply the amount on Line 1a by 0.90."
- Prior-year option: 100% of prior-year PA taxable income multiplied by the current-year rate (3.07%). It is not the prior year's tax. Source 1 (TY2026): "100 percent of the product of multiplying the net PA taxable income shown on the 2025 PA-40 return by 3.07 percent (0.0307)."
- The form describes this as Exception 1, "prior year income at current year rate". Source 3 (TY2025, Line 12): "Exception 1 – Tax on 2024 income using 2025 tax rate."
- The prior year's Tax Forgiveness Credit is subtracted from the Exception 1 amount. Source 3 (TY2025, Line 12 instructions): "subtract the amount of the Tax Forgiveness Credit on Line 21 of your 2024 return".
- There is no higher prior-year percentage. There is no AGI or income threshold, no 110% tier, and no married-filing-separately variant (corrected in verification: confirmed from the statute, no longer NOT FOUND). § 352(d)(2) states the two tests with no percentage on the prior-year test and no income condition. Source 10 (§ 352(d)(2)): "No addition to tax shall be imposed if the total amount of all payments of estimated tax ... equals or exceeds the lesser of".
- A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option. No income limit appears in § 352(d)(2) or in Sources 1-12.
- The prior-year option requires a filed full-year resident prior-year return. Source 1 (TY2026, Line 10): "If a return was not filed for 2025 or a part-year resident return was filed, the safe harbor method is not permitted"
- The prior-year tax itself is allowed only when the rate did not change. Source 1 (TY2026): "unless the tax rate for both tax years is the same and the taxpayer was a full-year resident".
- A 12-month prior year is not stated as a separate condition. The statute leaves short years to regulation. Source 8 (§ 325(j)): "having a taxable year of less than twelve months in accordance with procedures prescribed in regulations".
- The regulation exists (corrected in verification). 61 Pa. Code § 115.4 covers a current taxable year shorter than 12 months. No declaration is needed for a short year under 4 months, or when the requirement is first met late in a longer short year. Source 16 (§ 115.4(a)): "No declaration of estimated tax shall be required if the short taxable year is one of the following". The regulation still uses a $1,000 income test, which the statute has replaced. Whether a short prior year can support the prior-year safe harbor: NOT FOUND (searched Sources 1-22).
- In the statute the options are "the lesser of" the prior-year-facts amount and 90% of tax on actual income to date. The 90%-of-current-year figure is built into the definition of the underpayment instead. Source 10 (§ 352(d)(1)): "if the estimated tax were equal to ninety per cent of the tax ... shown on the return".

## 3. Due dates and shares

- Four dates: April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the next year. Source 1 (TY2026): "Apr. 15, 2026", "Jun. 15, 2026 Sep. 15, 2026", "Jan. 15, 2027".
- Equal quarters (25% each) apply when the requirement is first met before April 1. A later start adds a catch-up to the first payment: 50/25/25 (first met April 1 to May 31), 75/25 (June 1 to August 31), 100 (after August 31). Source 1 (TY2026, Payment Due Dates Table), first row: "Before April 1, 2026 | 4".
- The statute and regulation differ from this table (corrected in verification). Four installments apply when the requirement is first met on or before April 1, so April 1 itself is in the first group. Source 8 (§ 325(d)(1)): "If the determination is made on or before April 1 of the taxable year". The later groups start after April 1, after June 1 and after September 1. A late start is paid in equal remaining installments: thirds from June 15, halves from September 15. Source 9 (§ 326(a)(3)): "the estimated tax shall be paid in two equal installments". 61 Pa. Code § 115.9(a) says the same. Source 16: "or in three equal installments". The department's 50/25/25 and 75/25 split is not in the statute. See the verification section.
- Dates shift to the next business day for a weekend or holiday. Source 1 (TY2026): "or the next business day if the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or holiday." The statutory basis is the general rule on computing time (added in verification). Source 19 (1 Pa.C.S. § 1908): "Whenever the last day of any such period shall fall on Saturday or Sunday, or on any day made a legal holiday". The 2025 REV-1630 applies this shift: June 15, 2025 was a Sunday. Source 3 (TY2025): "b June 16, 2025". The 2026 dates (Wed, Mon, Tue and Fri) need no shift.
- January payment waiver: a return filed and paid in full by January 31 counts as the January 15 declaration. Source 8 (§ 325(h)(1)): "Such return shall be considered as his or its declaration which was required to be filed no later than January 15." The form treats the payment as the fourth installment. Source 3 (TY2025, Line 6): "paid the total tax due by Jan. 31, 2026, enter the amount paid ... as the fourth installment"
- The statute's January 31 rule is narrower (corrected in verification). It covers only a taxpayer whose declaration was first due January 15, because the requirement was first met after September 1. Source 8 (§ 325(h)): "for which a declaration was required to be filed within the time prescribed by subsection (d)(4) of this section". 61 Pa. Code § 115.9(a)(4) limits it the same way. REV-1630 applies it to every filer. See the verification section.
- A whole-year declaration of $100 or less may be filed by January 15. Source 8 (§ 325(f)): "total estimated tax ... of one hundred dollars ($100) or less may be filed at any time on or before January 15".
- Fiscal-year filers use the 15th day of the 4th, 6th and 9th months, and of the 1st month after year end. Source 3 (TY2025): "for Column a use the 15th day of the fourth month of the tax year".
- A prior-year overpayment is applied to the first installment first, whenever the return is processed. Source 1 (TY2026): "considered to be applied to the first estimated tax payment amount and period regardless of when the previous tax year's return is filed".

## 4. Withholding

- Withholding is treated as paid evenly: one quarter in each period. Section I subtracts all withholding and credits (Line 2) from 90% of tax and divides by four. Source 3 (TY2025, Line 5 instructions): "Divide Line 4 by 4 and enter the amount in each of the four Columns a through d."
- The exception tests also spread withholding evenly. Source 3 (TY2025, Exception 1 Worksheet Line A): "Divide the amount reported in Section I, Line 2 by 4. Enter the amount in each of the four columns."
- Line 2 includes PA withholding, nonresident withholding, Tax Forgiveness, the resident credit and other credits. Source 3 (TY2025): "Line 13 (Total PA Tax Withheld), Line 17 (Nonresident Tax Withheld), Line 21 (Tax Forgiveness Credit)".
- An election to treat withholding as paid on the actual dates: NOT FOUND (searched Sources 1-12; confirmed in verification against 61 Pa. Code Chapter 115 and § 119.23, TRC § 352(d) and the 2026 REV-413 (I)).

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below the threshold. 2025: under $338 after withholding and credits. Source 3 (TY2025, Line 3 instructions): "If less than $338, you did not owe estimated income tax and do not have to complete this form." 2026: under $430. Source 1 (TY2026, Line 17): "If the amount is less than $430, estimated tax payments are not required to be made."
- 100% Tax Forgiveness in the prior year (Act 85 of 2012). Source 3 (TY2025): "taxpayers who qualified for 100 percent Tax Forgiveness in the previous tax year are not subject to the estimated underpayment penalty".
- No liability in the prior year: there is no federal-style rule. The nearest is the "special exception". It applies when no estimates were required the year before, because tax was under the threshold or because of 100% Tax Forgiveness. The taxpayer must then file a declaration and pay in full from the period the requirement is first known. Source 3 (TY2025): "special exceptions apply and you may not owe penalties for the payment periods prior to meeting the estimated payment requirements." Line A of the Special Exception Information must be under $338. Source 3: "If the amount for Line A is $338 or greater ... you do not qualify for the special exception."
- First-year residents and part-year residents: both are subject to the estimate rules. Neither can use the prior-year exception if the prior return was missing or part-year. Source 3 (TY2025): "This exception does not apply if you did not file a return for the prior year or ... filed as a part-year resident." No other rule for new residents was found.
- Farmers (at least two-thirds of gross income from farming): no penalty if all estimated tax is paid by January 15, or the return is filed and fully paid by March 1 (next business day if a weekend or holiday). Source 4 (TY2025): "paid the entire amount due by March 1 of the following year, or you paid all of your estimated tax by Jan. 15, 2026." For 2025 the effective date is March 2, 2026. Source 4: "If the date on Line D is after March 2, 2026". The farmer percentage on REV-1630A is 90% (Line 1b "Multiply the amount on Line 1a by 0.90"). The statute says two-thirds; see answer 10.
- Farmers also get a prior-year exception. All tax on prior-year income at the current rate must be paid by January 15. Source 4 (TY2025): "equal to at least all of the tax due on 2024's taxable income at 2025's tax rate".
- Death: no underpayment for installments due on or after the taxpayer's death. Source 10 (§ 352(d)(1)): "No underpayment shall be deemed to exist with respect to an installment otherwise due on or after the taxpayer's death".
- Retired or disabled waiver: NOT FOUND in Sources 1-12. The answer-center item "Estimated payments for retired taxpayers" (a_id 875) exists but could not be read. Confirmed NOT FOUND in verification: TRC § 352(d) lists only the death exception, and 61 Pa. Code Chapter 115 and § 119.23 have no waiver. The answer center still returned "Technical Difficulties" on 2026-10-20.
- Casualty or disaster waiver: NOT FOUND. Searched Sources 1-12 and pa.gov. Abatement of penalties goes to the Board of Appeals under TRC § 2706 (Source 7). That rule covers penalties in general, not estimates specifically. Confirmed NOT FOUND in verification. Secondary sites report one-off disaster deadline extensions, but no primary department notice was found.

## 6. Annualised income installment method

- For individuals PA uses actual cumulative income, not annualised income. This is Exception 2 on REV-1630, Section II, Line 13 and the Exception 2 Worksheet. There is no separate schedule. Source 3 (TY2025): "Exception 2 - Tax on 2025 income over three, five, eight, and 12 month periods. Enter 90 percent of Exception 2 Line B."
- The periods are January 1 to March 31, to May 31, to August 31, and to December 31. Tax is actual income in each period times 3.07%, with no annualisation factor. Source 3 (TY2025): "Multiply Line A by 3.07 percent (0.0307). This is the tax due."
- The statute matches. It gives "actual income for the months" to individuals and annualisation only to trusts and estates. Source 10 (§ 352(d)(2)(B)): "on the basis of the actual income for the months in the taxable year ending before the month in which the installment".
- REV-1630A is the farmers' form, not an annualisation schedule.
- Exception 2 is claimed only by filing REV-1630. The department's computer applies Exception 1 only. Source 3 (TY2025): "The department will also determine any exceptions to the penalty for any of the estimated tax payment periods using Exception 1".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

- It is an interest charge on each installment's shortfall for the days it is late. It is not a flat percentage. Source 3 (TY2025): "The estimated underpayment penalty is an interest penalty". Source 7: "the daily interest rate for the tax year ... and the number days unpaid or paid late."
- The required installment is (90% of tax minus all withholding and credits) / 4. Overpayments carry forward to later periods. Source 3 (TY2025, Lines 1b-10).
- Interest stops at the return due date, the 15th day of the fourth month after year end. Source 10 (§ 352(d)(1)): "but not beyond the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year."
- Days are split at December 31 so each calendar year's rate applies (REV-1630 Lines 14a-14c, 15a-15c). For 2025 the defaults are 260, 198 and 107 days to December 31, 105 days from January 1 to April 15, and 90 days for the January installment. Source 3 (TY2025): "If Dec. 31 is earlier, enter 260, 198, and 107 respectively."
- A late payment reduces the oldest period's underpayment first. Source 3 (TY2025): "that partial payment is considered a reduction, on the payment date, to the oldest payment period's underpayment."
- How the rate is set: it is the federal (U.S. Treasury) rate in effect on January 1, fixed for that calendar year. Source 11 (Fiscal Code § 806): "the interest rate established by the Secretary of the Treasury ... effective January 1 of such calendar year". TRC § 352(d) adopts the § 806 rate. Source 10: "an amount at the rate established pursuant to section 806".
- The daily rate is the annual rate / 365. Source 7: "The daily interest date equals the annual interest rate divided by 365."
- Current rate: 7% a year, daily 0.000192, charged from January 1, 2025 to December 31, 2026. Source 6 (REV-1611, 11-25): "2025 - 2026 7% 0.000192 1/1/25 - 12/31/26".
- Forms: REV-1630 (individuals), REV-1630A (farmers), REV-1630F (fiduciaries). The result goes on PA-40 Line 27. Source 3 (TY2025): "Interest. Add amounts on Lines 15a, b, and c. Include on Line 27 of Form PA-40."
- The penalty applies even when the return shows a refund. Source 1 (TY2026): "Even if the individual has an overpayment on the PA-40, Personal Income Tax Return."

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is income-based and rises each year: $14,000 of non-withheld income ($430 of tax) for 2026, not $1,000 of tax (Source 1).
- The prior-year safe harbor uses prior-year taxable income at the current rate. It is 100% only, with no 110% tier, and it needs a full-year resident return (Sources 1, 3).
- A late start means catch-up percentages (50/25/25, 75/25, 100), not federal-style annualisation (Source 1).
- Exception 2 uses actual cumulative income without annualising (Source 3).
- The department applies only Exception 1 automatically. Exception 2 must be shown on REV-1630 (Source 3).
- Prior-year 100% Tax Forgiveness removes the penalty entirely (Source 3).
- Payments of $15,000 or more must be made electronically. Otherwise a penalty of 3% of the payment, up to $500, applies. Source 2 (TY2026): "Payments not made electronically will be subject to a penalty equal to 3 percent of the payment amount not to exceed $500."
- No retired or disabled waiver and no casualty waiver were found (answer 5).
- The rate is fixed for the whole calendar year, with no quarterly resets (Source 11).
- REV-1630A uses 90% for farmers, although the PA statute says two-thirds (Sources 4, 10).

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimates

- Local earned income tax (Act 32). It is collected by the tax collector for the taxpayer's home municipality. A resident whose employer does not withhold it pays quarterly estimates to that collector. Source 13 (DCED): "they will be responsible for making quarterly estimated payments on their own directly to the local tax collector(s) for their PA home municipality." DCED also publishes form CLGS-32-3, Taxpayer Quarterly Estimated Payments (linked from Source 13). The DCED sentence is written about employees of out-of-state employers.
- The statute gives local EIT its own dates (added in verification). Net profits are estimated by April 15 and paid in four equal installments: April 15, July 15, October 15 and January 15. Source 18 (LTEA § 502(c)(2)(i)): "on or before July 15 of the current year, October 15 of the current year and January 15 of the succeeding year". These differ from the state's June 15 and September 15 dates.
- Other local income not subject to withholding is reported and paid quarterly on the same four dates. Source 18 (§ 502(c)(3)): "shall make and file with the resident tax officer a quarterly return on or before April 15 of the current year, July 15".
- Local safe harbors: four equal timely payments of 100% of prior-year tax or 90% of current-year tax, each less current-year EIT withheld. Source 18 (§ 502(c)(5)(i)): "Make four equal, timely estimated payments equal to 100% of the prior year's tax less any earned income tax withheld for the current year."
- A final return by January 31 may replace the fourth net-profits installment. Source 18 (§ 502(c)(2)(iii)): "in lieu of paying the fourth quarterly installment of the estimated tax, elect to make and file ... on or before January 31".
- Farmers follow the state rule: a declaration by January 15, or a final return and full payment by March 1 (§ 502(d), Source 18).
- A collector may not penalize without first sending a 30-day corrective-action letter. Source 18 (§ 502(e)(4)): "Impose a penalty on a taxpayer unless it has issued a letter ... to take corrective action within 30 days". The statute sets no penalty rate. CLGS-32-3 leaves the monthly penalty blank for each collector to fill in (Source 22).
- The Philadelphia Earnings Tax is for residents whose employer does not withhold the City Wage Tax. It is administered by the City of Philadelphia Department of Revenue and paid quarterly. Source 15: "The Earnings Tax must be paid quarterly."
- The Earnings Tax quarters are calendar quarters, each due the last day of the following month (added in verification). The 2026 dates are April 30, July 31 and October 31, 2026, and February 1, 2027 (Source 15). The page ties each payment to the earnings of one calendar quarter.
- Philadelphia Net Profits Tax: two estimated payments, each 25% of the prior year's tax. Source 14: "The first payment is due on April 15 of each year, and the second installment is due by June 15."
- Philadelphia School Income Tax: administered by the City Department of Revenue for the School District. Estimated-payment rules: NOT FOUND in the 2025 SIT instructions (https://www.phila.gov/media/20260128151226/2025-School-Income-Tax-SIT-instructions.pdf). Confirmed in verification: the SIT is filed and paid once a year. Source 21: "School Income Tax returns are due on April 15 each year".

## 10. Ambiguities and conflicts

- Farmer percentage. The statute measures a farmer's underpayment against two-thirds of tax. Source 10 (§ 352(d)(1)): "ninety per cent of the tax (two-thirds in the case of an individual described in subsection (e) of section 325)". REV-1630A Line 1b uses 0.90 (Source 4). 61 Pa. Code § 119.23 says 66⅔% (Source 12).
- Late-start installments. The statute says the remaining installments are equal. Source 9 (§ 326(a)(2)): "the estimated tax shall be paid in three equal installments." The department's table gives 50/25/25 and 75/25 (Source 1). REV-1630 still divides the required amount by 4 in every column, unless the special exception applies (Source 3).
- Residency limit on the prior-year exception. The statute's prior-year test has no full-year-residency condition. Source 10 (§ 352(d)(2)(A)): "on the basis of the facts shown on his return for ... the preceding taxable year". The department excludes part-year returns (Sources 1, 3).
- The regulation is stale. 61 Pa. Code § 119.23 (1978) still says 80% and 9% a year, and it allows the prior-year tax as a safe harbor. Source 12: "at the rate of 9% per annum of the underpayment". The statute and forms supersede it.
- The PIT Guide is stale. Source 7 (DSM-12, 12-2023) uses the 2024 figures, $9,500 and $292.
- Errors in the 2026 REV-413 (I). The Line 17 heading reads "TOTAL 2025 REQUIRED ESTIMATED TAX" on a 2026 worksheet. The payment note refers to "Line 35", which does not exist on REV-414 (I).
- Errors in the 2025 REV-1630. The Line 12 instructions say "on your 2023 income" but compute from 2024 income. Line 7 mentions an "April 17th payment period". Line 5 on the form says "the number of payments required for the year (usually four)", while the instructions say "Divide Line 4 by 4".
- Error in the 2025 REV-1630A. The Line D instructions say "Enter the date you filed your 2024 PA-40", while the form says 2025.
- The 2025 PA-40 IN, Line 27, words the test as "more than $11,000 of taxable income". REV-1630 and REV-413 (I) use "$11,000 or more" and a $338 tax floor (Sources 3, 5).
- Correction to the item above (corrected in verification). The 2025 REV-1630 also says "exceed $11,000", not "$11,000 or more". Its Line 3 floor is "$338 or more". The 2026 REV-413 (I) says "$14,000 or more" and "at least $430". The statute says "exceed" (Source 8).
- Error in the 2025 REV-1630A (added in verification). The Line 9 instructions require full-year residence in 2023, not 2024. Source 4: "You must have been a full year resident in 2023 to qualify for this exception."
- REV-1630A applies the $338 floor to 90% of tax less credits (Line 3). REV-1630 applies it to 100% of tax less credits (added in verification; Sources 3, 4).
- The task named TRC §§ 325-327. Section 327 does not exist in the official text (the palegis.us lookup returned "Error VS-8"). The penalty is in § 352(d).
- No 2026 REV-1630 or REV-1630A is published. Penalty mechanics come from the 2025 forms.

### Resolved conflicts

None of TRC §§ 325, 326 or 352, or Fiscal Code § 806, was amended in 2024, 2025 or 2026. The palegis.us text shows the last amendments as 2022 (§ 325), 1971 (§ 326), 2012 (§ 352(d)(2)) and 1982 (§ 806). The Acts lists for 2024-2026 (Source 20) were checked act by act. Act 56 of 2024 is the only Tax Reform Code omnibus act, and it amends other parts of the Code. Acts 45 and 57 of 2025 and Act 21 of 2026 amend the Fiscal Code, and none mentions estimated tax or § 806. So the statute governs every conflict below. No form reflects a newer law.

A. Income threshold boundary. The statute requires income not subject to withholding to "exceed" the amount (§ 325(a)(1)). REV-413 (I) says "$14,000 or more" and "at least $430". REV-1630 says "exceed $11,000" but "$338 or more". Resolution: the requirement starts above $14,000 for 2026. This changes a result only at exactly $14,000.

B. Late-start boundary days. The statute and 61 Pa. Code § 115.9(a) put a requirement first met on April 1 in the four-installment group. They put June 1 in the June 15 group and September 1 in the September 15 group. The REV-413 (I) table moves each of those three days one group later. REV-413 (I) Amount of Payment instructions use a third test, the payment due dates. Source 1: "after the first estimated tax payment date but before the second estimated tax payment date". Resolution: the statute's days govern (on or before April 1; after April 1 and before June 2; after June 1 and before September 2; after September 1).

C. Late-start shares. The statute and § 115.9 require equal remaining installments: thirds from June 15, halves from September 15, all on January 15. The department's table requires 50/25/25 and 75/25. REV-1630 divides by 4 in every column. It then waives earlier periods only under the special exception, which requires the full catch-up payment. Resolution on the law: the statute governs, so a late starter's required installments are equal shares. Unresolved in practice: no public source says the department computes the penalty on equal thirds or halves. This changes the penalty for late starters.

D. January 31 return. § 325(h) and § 115.9(a)(4) let a return filed and paid by January 31 replace the January 15 declaration only when the requirement was first met after September 1. REV-1630 Line 6 treats any final return paid by January 31 as the fourth installment. Resolution: the statute is narrower. The department's form is more generous, and the department applies its own form. A taxpayer outside § 325(h) could owe up to 16 days of interest on the fourth installment under the statute. No public source shows the department charging it.

E. Farmer percentage. § 352(d)(1) measures a farmer's underpayment against two-thirds of the tax. 61 Pa. Code § 119.23 also uses 66⅔%. REV-1630A Line 1b uses 0.90. Resolution: the statute governs, so the farmer measure is two-thirds. REV-1630A overstates a farmer's underpayment. Unresolved in practice: the department's own computation follows REV-1630A, and no public source explains the difference.

F. Residency and the prior-year test. § 352(d)(2)(A) uses "the facts shown on his return for ... the preceding taxable year", with no residency condition. Excluding a taxpayer with no prior return follows from the statute, because there are no facts to use. Excluding a part-year prior return is the department's addition (Sources 1, 3). The 2025 PA-40 IN, Line 27, words the test differently again. Source 5: "or based upon the income as if you filed a full year return, based upon this year's tax rate". Resolution on the law: the statute allows a part-year prior return. Unresolved in practice: the department's forms exclude it, and the PA-40 IN is unclear.

G. Prior-year tax under the threshold. The 2023 PIT Guide lists a separate no-penalty rule. Source 7: "the current year's tax rate minus the amount of your prior year's Tax Forgiveness credit was less than $292". The 2025 REV-1630 allows this only through the special exception, which also requires a declaration and full payment from the period the requirement is first met. The statute has no such rule; § 352(d)(2)(A) already makes the prior-year amount small. Resolution: the REV-1630 is newer and governs over the guide. The statute supports neither as a blanket exemption.

H. Farmers' March 1 on a weekend. § 325(e) says March 1. REV-1630A moves it to the next business day. Resolution: 1 Pa.C.S. § 1908 (Source 19) supports the shift, so March 2, 2026 is correct.

I. Stale regulation. 61 Pa. Code §§ 115.2, 115.4 and 119.23 still use a $1,000 income test, 80% and 9% interest. Source 16 (§ 115.4(a)): "to exceed $1,000". Resolution: the later statute governs over these figures. The regulation's boundary days and equal installments in § 115.9 match the current statute and remain good.

### 2026 changes

- Income threshold rises to $14,000 ($430 of tax) for 2026, under Act 108 of 2022. Already in answer 1.
- The tax rate stays 3.07% for 2026 (Source 1, Line 2).
- The interest rate for 2026 is 7% a year. Source 17: "for the calendar year beginning January 1, 2026, all underpayments of tax ... shall bear interest at the rate of 7% per annum". The rate stays fixed through December 31, 2026. A 2026 underpayment that runs into 2027 will use the 2027 rate for those days, which is not yet published.
- No 2024, 2025 or 2026 act changes the estimated tax thresholds, percentages, dates or interest method (Source 20).
- Act 53 of 2026 amends the Local Tax Enabling Act, effective 60 days after October 8, 2026. It changes collection of delinquent local taxes only, not § 502 estimates.
- No 2026 REV-1630 or REV-1630A is published yet. The 2026 penalty form, day counts and daily rate for 2027 days are unknown.
