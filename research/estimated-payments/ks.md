## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe $500 or more after withholding and credits. A second condition must also hold: withholding and credits are expected to be less than the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's. Nonresidents count only Kansas-source income. No filing-status difference was found. Spouses may pay jointly.

- [ES-26]: "You must make estimated tax payments if your estimated Kansas income tax after withholding and credits is $500 or more".
- [KSA-101](a)(1): "Every individual shall pay estimated tax who can reasonably expect to owe, after withholding and credits, tax of at least $500".
- [ES-26]: "Nonresidents should only consider income from Kansas sources for meeting these conditions."

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax.
- There is no 110% tier and no AGI threshold. High-income taxpayers keep the 100% prior-year option. NOT FOUND: any higher percentage in [ES-26], [K210-25], [K40I-25] or [KSA-107].
- Prior-year return: [ES-26] requires a 12-month return with a tax liability. [KSA-107](b)(2) uses zero if no return was required or the prior-year liability was under $200. See item 10.
- (corrected in verification) The statute governs. Use 100% of last year's tax if a prior-year return showing a liability was filed. Use zero if no prior-year return was required, or the prior-year liability was under $200. [KSA-107] states no 12-month condition. If a prior-year return was required but not filed, only the 90% and annualized tests remain.

Quotes:

- [ES-26]: "are at least 90% (66 2/3% for farmers and fishers) of the tax shown on your return for tax year 2026, OR".
- [ES-26]: "equal or exceed 100% of the tax shown on your 2025 return, (the return must cover a 12-month period with a tax liability), OR".
- [KSA-107](b)(2): "zero if no return was required to be filed or if the tax liability on the individual's return was less than $200".

## 3. Due dates and share due

There are four equal installments of 25% each. The 2026 dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Weekend and holiday dates move to the next regular workday.

The January installment is not required if the return is filed and the tax paid in full by January 31. This does not apply to farmers and fishers.

If the requirement is first met later in the year, fewer installments are due: 1/3 each from June 15, 1/2 each from September 15, or the full amount on January 15.

- [KSA-103](a)(1): "the estimated tax shall be paid in four equal installments."
- [ES-26]: "When the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, substitute the next regular workday."
- [ES-26]: "January 15, 2027 unless your return for the 2026 tax year is filed and the tax fully paid by January 31, 2027."
- [K210-25]: "not required to make a payment for the January 15th quarter if a Form K-40 was filed and the tax was paid in full".

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid evenly. The cumulative amounts by the four dates are 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of the year's withholding. NOT FOUND: any election to use the actual withholding dates, in [K210-25], [ES-26], [K40I-25] or [KSA-107].

- [K210-25] line 5: "Multiply the amount on line 4 by the percentage shown in each column of line 5." The columns show "25% of line 4", "50% of line 4", "75% of line 4" and "100% of line 4".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: [K210-25] applies only if 2025 tax less withholding and credits is $500 or more.
  - [K210-25]: "If your 2025 tax due ... is $500 or more, you may be subject to an underpayment of estimated tax penalty".
  - [KSA-107](a): "such penalty shall not be added if the total amount thereof does not exceed $1."
- No liability in the prior year: [K210-25] says to enter zero as the prior-year tax.
  - [K210-25]: "If you did not file an income tax return for the prior tax year".
  - [K210-25], same sentence: "your income tax balance (line 19, Form K-40) was zero, then enter zero on line 3".
  - [KSA-107](b)(2) uses zero if no return was required or the liability was under $200.
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate rule. The no-prior-return rule above is the closest.
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND.
- Farmers and fishers: this applies if at least two-thirds of gross income comes from farming or fishing. The current-year percentage is 66 2/3%. There is one payment, due January 15. No penalty applies if the return is filed and the tax paid by March 1.
  - [K210-25]: "filed Form K-40 and paid the tax on or before March 1, 2026, you may be exempt".
  - [ES-26]: "Payment is due on or before January 15, 2027 unless your return ... is filed and tax fully paid on or before March 1"
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in [ES-26], [K210-25], [K40I-25] or [KSA-107].
- Casualty or disaster waivers: NOT FOUND in the same documents.

## 6. Annualised income installment method

The method is allowed. It is Exception 2 on Schedule K-210, line 10a, or line 10b for farmers and fishers. You must enclose your own computation. There is no separate schedule.

- Periods: January 1 to March 31, May 31, August 31 and December 31.
- Factors: 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1.
- Percentages: 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%.

Quotes:

- [ES-26]: "90% of the tax shown on your annualized income for the periods January 1, 2026 to March 31, May 31, August 31".
- [K210-25]: "Enclose a schedule showing your computation of annualized income and tax amounts."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like charge on each installment's shortfall, by days late. It runs from the due date until paid, or until the 15th day of the fourth month after year end (April 15), whichever is earlier.

- The rate is the K.S.A. 79-2968 rate: the federal IRC 6621(a)(2) underpayment rate in effect on July 1 of the preceding year, plus one point, fixed for each calendar year.
- The K-210 for tax year 2025 uses 9% for days in 2025 and 8% for days from January 1, 2026. The source is [K210-25] and [K40I-25]; the 8% rate took effect for calendar year 2026.

Compute it on Schedule K-210, Part II.

- [KSA-107](a): "A penalty at the same rate per annum prescribed by K.S.A. 79-2968 (b) ... shall be applied and added".
- [KSA-107](a): "until the 15th day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year".
- [KSA-2968]: "the underpayment rate per annum prescribed and determined under paragraph (2) of subsection (a) of section 6621".
- The same section continues: "which rate is in effect thereunder on July 1 of the year immediately preceding the calendar year ... plus one percentage point".
- [K210-25]: "Penalty is computed to 12/31/25 at 9% and from 1/1/26 to the date the tax was paid or 4/15/26, whichever is earlier, at 8%."
- [K40I-25] line 29, the same 2026 rate for interest on unpaid tax: "compute interest at .6667% per month or 8% per annum".

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is $500, not $1,000.
- There is no 110% rule for high-income taxpayers. 100% of last year always works, subject to the prior-year conditions.
- The rate is the federal underpayment rate plus one point, fixed once a year from the July 1 federal rate. The federal rate resets quarterly.
- The penalty stops on April 15, as the federal one does.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Prior-year rule when there was no return or no liability:
  - [ES-26] says the prior-year return "must cover a 12-month period with a tax liability", which implies that a zero-liability year cannot be used.
  - [K210-25] says to enter zero on line 3 if no prior-year return was filed or the prior-year balance was zero. That gives a required payment of zero.
  - [KSA-107](b)(2) gives zero only if no return was "required to be filed" or the liability was "less than $200". That is broader than zero liability and narrower than "did not file".
- Current-year 90%: [KSA-107](b) lists only the prior-year tax, zero, and annualized income as exceptions. The 90% current-year measure comes from [KSA-107](a)'s definition of the underpayment amount. The forms present both as alternatives.
- Line numbers: [K210-25] line 3 says the prior-year tax is "from line 19, 2024 Form K-40". Line 1 uses "line 18, 2025 Form K-40". The K-40 line numbering changed between years. [K40I-25] line 31 says "prior year's tax liability (line 19 from last year's return)".

### Conflicts resolved

- Prior-year rule when there was no return or no liability. The statute governs. [KSA-107] was last amended by L. 2024, ch. 81, § 17, and [COMP-26] lists no 2025 or 2026 amendment to it. The three sources compare like this:
  - [KSA-107](b)(1) allows the prior-year tax only "if a return showing a liability for tax was filed".
  - [KSA-107](b)(2) allows zero "if no return was required to be filed or if the tax liability on the individual's return was less than $200".
  - [K210-25] goes further than the statute: it enters zero whenever no prior-year return was filed. A taxpayer who was required to file and did not should not get zero under the statute.
  - [ES-26] requires a 12-month prior year. No 12-month condition was found in [KSA-101], [KSA-102], [KSA-103] or [KSA-107]. [KSA-101](d) leaves short years to regulations, and no regulation was found. Whether a short prior year can be used is therefore unresolved.
- Current-year 90%. Not a conflict. [KSA-107](a) measures the underpayment against "90% of the tax shown on the return for the taxable year". [KSA-107](b) adds the prior-year, zero and annualized exceptions. Together they give the alternatives shown on the forms.
- K-40 line numbers. Resolved: the K-40 lost a line in 2025. In [K40I-24], line 19 is "Total tax balance". In [K40I-25], that is line 18, and line 19 is "Kansas income tax withheld". Both [K210-25] references therefore point to the total tax balance.

### 2026 changes

- The 2026 penalty rate is 8%. [K210-25] applies 8% "from 1/1/26". It follows from [KSA-2968].
- The 2026 worksheet rates are 5.2% and 5.58%, the same as the "tax year 2024, and all tax years thereafter" schedule in K.S.A. 79-32,110.
- [COMP-26] lists no 2026 amendment to K.S.A. 79-32,101, 79-32,102, 79-32,103, 79-32,107 or 79-2968. It does list 79-32,110 as amended by 2026 HB 2029, effective July 1, 2026, and 79-32,110b as repealed. The legislature's page for HB 2029 gives its title as "Reconciling multiple amendments to certain statutes". K.S.A. 79-32,110b is a second version of the same rate section, with the same title "Tax imposed; classes of taxpayers; schedules of tax rates". The bill appears to merge the two versions rather than change the rates. The amended text of 79-32,110 was not read.
