## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay if your Connecticut tax, after withholding and any Pass-Through Entity Tax Credit (PE Tax Credit), is $1,000 or more, and you expect withholding plus PE Tax Credit to be less than the required annual payment. The sources state no filing-status difference.

- CT-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "is $1,000 or more; and 2. You expect your Connecticut income tax withheld (including any PE Tax Credit) to be less than".

The PE Tax Credit is Connecticut's credit for pass-through entity tax paid by a partnership or S corporation. It is subtracted like withholding, and it is spread over the quarters as withholding is (source 2, Part 3 lines 12–12b).

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%. Farmers and fishermen: 66 2/3%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage: none. Connecticut has no 110% rule.
- The prior-year option requires a filed prior-year return covering 12 months. The exception: a taxpayer who filed no prior return because they had no liability uses a prior-year tax of zero (question 5).
- A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option.

Quotes:
- Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-701(12), source 4: "if the preceding taxable year was a taxable year of twelve months and the individual filed a return for the preceding taxable year".
- CT-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "100% of the income tax shown on your 2025 Connecticut income tax return if you filed a 2025 ... return that covered a 12‑month period."
- CT-2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2, Box E: "If you check Box E, you must use 90% of your 2025 income tax as your required annual payment." Box E covers a prior-year part-year resident or nonresident with no Connecticut-sourced income.

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% each, cumulative 25% / 50% / 75% / 100% on CT-2210.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day.
- January waiver: none is due if you file by January 31 and pay in full.

Quotes:
- Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(d)(1), source 3: "the amount of any required installment shall be twenty-five per cent of the required annual payment".
- CT-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, your estimated payment will be considered timely if filed on the next"
- CT-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "on or before January 31, 2027, and pay in full ... you will not be charged interest for failing to make the estimated payment"

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each due date. The taxpayer may elect actual withholding dates by checking Box C on CT-2210.

- Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(k), source 3: "an equal part of such tax withheld shall be deemed paid on each due date ... unless the taxpayer establishes the dates".
- CT-2210, TY2025, source 2, Box C: "Your interest is lower by treating your Connecticut income tax withheld from your income as paid on the dates it was actually withheld".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no interest if tax shown less withholding is under $1,000. Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(j)(1), source 3: "the tax, reduced by the tax withheld under this chapter, is less than one thousand dollars." CT-2210 also subtracts the PE Tax Credit (source 2, line 4).
- No liability in the prior year: no interest if the prior year was 12 months with no liability. Throughout that year you must have been a resident, or a nonresident or part-year resident with Connecticut-source income.
  - Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(j)(2), source 3: "did not have any liability for tax for the preceding taxable year and throughout such year the individual was (i) a resident individual".
  - The form implements this as Boxes F and G (source 2).
- First-year residents and those with no prior Connecticut income: a person who was a nonresident or part-year resident in the prior year with no Connecticut-sourced income gets no exception. They must use 90% of current-year tax (Box E). CT-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "If you were a nonresident or part‑year resident and you did not have Connecticut‑sourced income ... you must use 90%".
- Part-year residents: covered as above. DRS suggests the annualised method for movers. CT-2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "If you were a part‑year resident who moved into Connecticut in 2025 ... you may benefit by using the annualized installment method".
- Farmers and fishermen, as defined in IRC §6654(i)(2): one installment due January 15. It is the lesser of 66 2/3% of current-year tax or 100% of a 12-month prior-year filed return's tax. No interest if they file and pay in full by March 1.
  - Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(m), source 3: "(1) There shall be only one required installment for the taxable year, (2) the due date for such installment shall be January fifteenth".
  - Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(m)(3)(A), source 3: "sixty-six and two-thirds per cent of the tax shown on the return for the taxable year".
  - Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(m)(4), source 3: "if, on or before March first of the following taxable year, the farmer or fisherman files a return and pays in full".
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND. Neither §12-722 nor the CT-2210 instructions contain a retirement or disability waiver.
- Casualty or disaster: NOT FOUND. Neither §12-722 nor the CT-2210 instructions contain a casualty or disaster waiver.
- Decedents' estates and certain grantor trusts are exempt for two years after death. Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(n)(2), source 3.

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, on CT-2210 Schedule A (Annualized Income Installment Schedule). DRS also points to Informational Publication 2018(11) and Worksheet CT-1040 AES. The periods and factors match federal (4, 2.4, 1.5, 1). The applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%, set by statute.

- Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(d)(2)(C), source 3: "the applicable percentage for the first required installment is twenty-two and one-half ... forty-five ... sixty-seven and one-half ... ninety."
- CT-2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "If you use the annualized income installment method for any installment due date, you must use this method for all installment due dates."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The charge is interest at a flat 1% per month or fraction of a month on each installment's underpayment. It runs from the due date to the earlier of payment or April 15 of the next year. A month runs from the 16th to the 15th, and any part-month counts as a whole month. The rate is fixed by statute, not indexed.

- Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(a), source 3: "applying interest (1) at the rate of one per cent per month or fraction thereof, (2) to the amount of the underpayment"
- Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-722(b), source 3: the period runs to "The fifteenth day of the fourth month of the next succeeding taxable year, or ... the date on which such portion is paid."
- CT-2210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "A month is measured from the sixteenth day of the first month to the fifteenth day of the next month."

Form: CT-2210, with Schedule B Worksheets A–D computing the interest month by month. If no Part 1 box applies, DRS computes the interest and bills you (source 2).

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The charge is 1% per month, roughly 12% a year, not an indexed daily rate.
- A payment one day late is charged a full month, because part-months count as whole months.
- There is no 110% rule. 100% of the prior year's tax always works when a 12-month prior return was filed.
- No retirement, disability, casualty or disaster waiver exists in the statute or form.
- Someone who moved in from out of state with no prior Connecticut-source income gets no zero-prior-year exception. They must pay 90% of current-year tax.
- PE Tax Credits count like withholding, and are spread over the quarters on CT-2210.
- Connecticut calls the charge "interest", not a penalty.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- The threshold boundary is consistent across sources. The statute exempts tax under $1,000. The CT-1040ES requires payments at "$1,000 or more". CT-2210 line 4 stops if "less than $1,000". So exactly $1,000 is subject to the rule.
- The prior-year return condition. §12-701(12) allows the prior-year option only if the individual "filed a return". (corrected in verification) The CT-2210 instructions allow 100% of 2024 tax in two cases. One is that you "filed a 2024 Connecticut income tax return that covered a 12-month period". The other is that you filed no 2024 return because you had no liability. The second branch comes from the §12-722(j)(2) exception rather than the definition. In that case Boxes F and G stop the computation, so no interest is due.
- The PE Tax Credit in the threshold. The statute's $1,000 test subtracts only "tax withheld under this chapter". The forms also subtract the PE Tax Credit. The statutory basis for that was not traced.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Threshold boundary.** Consistent, as the first pass said. §12-722(j)(1) exempts only "less than one thousand dollars", so exactly $1,000 is subject to interest.
- **Prior-year return condition.** Resolved. §12-701(12) requires a filed 12-month return for the 100% option. A taxpayer who filed nothing because they owed nothing is covered instead by §12-722(j)(2), which removes interest entirely. The forms' two branches match the statute.
- **PE Tax Credit in the threshold and in the installments.** Still unresolved. §12-722(j)(1) and (k) refer only to "tax withheld under this chapter". §12-699(f)(1) grants the PE Tax Credit "against the tax imposed under said chapter". It does not say the credit is a payment of estimated tax or withholding. §12-699a governs the entity's own estimates. No statute found treats the credit as withholding for §12-722. This would change the threshold test and the quarterly credit for anyone with a PE Tax Credit. The DRS treatment is the only source.
