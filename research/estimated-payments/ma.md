## 1. Who must make estimated payments

A taxpayer must pay estimated tax when the tax on income not subject to withholding is expected to be more than $400. This applies to both residents and nonresidents. The 4% surtax on income above $1,107,750 (2026) must be included in the estimate.

- Form 1-ES, TY2026, source 1: "Individual residents and nonresidents who expect to owe more than $400 in taxes on income not subject to withholding."
- Form 1-ES, TY2026, source 1: "The surtax threshold for tax year 2026 is $1,107,750."
- c. 62B § 13, source 4: "for whom the amount of estimated tax is more than $400 shall make payments of estimated tax".

Filing status: the threshold does not vary by filing status. Spouses may make joint payments and divide them if they file separately.

- Form 1-ES, TY2026, source 1: "A husband and wife may make joint payments of estimated tax as long as they are married at the time when the payments are due".

## 2. Required annual payment

The required annual payment is the lesser of:

- **80%** of the current year's tax (66 2/3% for qualified farmers and fishermen), or
- **100%** of the prior year's tax, if a prior-year return was filed and that year was 12 months.

There is no 110% rule and no income threshold. A high-income taxpayer keeps the 100% prior-year option.

- c. 62B § 14(c), source 5: "eighty per cent, or sixty-six and two-thirds per cent in the case of a farmer or fisherman".
- c. 62B § 14(c)(ii), source 5: "one hundred per cent of the tax shown on the return of the taxpayer for the preceding taxable year, provided the taxpayer filed a return".
- M-2210 line 4, TY2025, source 2: "Enter 80% of line 3 or 66.67% of line 3 if you are a qualified farmer or fisherman".
- M-2210 line 5 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "If you did not file a return for 2024, or if your 2024 tax year was less than 12 months, do not complete line 5."

## 3. Due dates and shares

There are four equal installments of 25%, due April 15, June 15 and September 15 of the tax year and January 15 of the next year.

- c. 62B § 14(c), source 5: "The amount of any required installment shall be twenty-five per cent of the required annual payment."
- Form 1-ES, TY2026, source 1: "in equal installments on or before April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027."

Weekend and holiday shift: yes.

- Form 1-ES, TY2026, source 1: "Whenever a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the filing and payment may be made on the next succeeding business day."

January waiver: NOT FOUND. Form 1-ES, M-2210, AP 241 and c. 62B § 14 contain no rule excusing the January 15 installment for filing and paying by January 31. Confirmed in verification: also absent from AP 612, the DOR estimated-payments web page, the 2025 Form 1-NR/PY instructions and the draft 2026 M-2210.

If the requirement first arises later in the year, the 1-ES worksheet puts the first voucher at 25%, 33%, 50% or 100% of the year's estimate, depending on whether it is first due April 15, June 15, September 15 or January 15.

## 4. Withholding

By default, withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each due date. The taxpayer may instead use the actual withholding dates.

- M-2210 line 9, TY2025, source 2: "you may apply an equal part of those taxes as payment on each required installment."
- c. 62B § 14(f), source 5: "unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if the tax after credits and withholding is $400 or less. M-2210, TY2025, source 2: "Your 2025 tax due after credits and withholding is $400 or less." The statute says "less than $400" instead, so the two differ at exactly $400 (corrected in verification). c. 62B § 14(d)(i), source 5: "less the amount of the credits allowed by section 9 of this chapter and section 6 of chapter 62 is less than $400"
- No liability in the prior year: no penalty if the taxpayer was a resident for all 12 months of the prior year and was not liable for tax. M-2210, TY2025, source 2: "You were a resident of Massachusetts for the full 12 months of the previous taxable year and were not liable for taxes."
- First-year residents: no specific rule. The no-liability exception requires prior-year residence for the full 12 months, so it does not cover a new resident. DOR estimated-payments page (updated 06/18/2026, source 8): "There is no safe harbor provision for first-year filers."
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND (no special rule beyond the above). Confirmed in verification: the 2025 Form 1-NR/PY instructions repeat the same exceptions with no part-year rule.
- Farmers and fishermen: 66 2/3% instead of 80%, and no penalty at all if they file and pay in full by March 1. To qualify, at least two-thirds of gross income must come from farming or fishing, on either the current or the prior-year return. M-2210, TY2025, source 2: "You were a qualified farmer or fisherman who filed and paid in full with your return by March 1, 2026."
- Retired or disabled: a waiver is available for a taxpayer who retired after age 62 or became disabled in the tax year or the year before, if the underpayment was due to reasonable cause. M-2210, TY2025, source 2: "You retired in 2024 or 2025 after reaching age 62, or you became disabled".
- Casualty or disaster: waiver available. M-2210, TY2025, source 2: "Your underpayment was by reason of casualty, disaster or unusual circumstance".

## 6. Annualised income method

Allowed, using Part 3 of Form M-2210. Because the base is 80% rather than 90%, the applicable percentages are 20%, 40%, 60% and 80% (federal: 22.5%, 45%, 67.5%, 90%). The annualisation multipliers are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1. The periods are Jan 1 to March 31, May 31, August 31 and December 31.

The surtax is computed on a separate line (corrected in verification). Part 3 subtracts the full-year surtax threshold from the period's actual, unannualised taxable income (line 16). It then multiplies the excess by the annualisation amount (line 18) and by 4% (line 19). The threshold printed is $1,083,150 for TY2025 and $1,107,750 on the draft TY2026 form.

- M-2210, TY2025, source 2: "If you do not receive taxable income evenly throughout the year, you may wish to annualize your income".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like charge on each installment's shortfall, for the days from the due date until paid or April 15, whichever is earlier. It is simple interest: the statute excludes daily compounding for this charge. M-2210 splits the days by calendar quarter so that each quarter's rate can be applied.

- c. 62B § 14(a), source 5: "an amount determined at the rate but without daily compounding and on a per annum basis established under section thirty-two of chapter 62C".

Rate rule: the federal short-term rate plus 4 percentage points, set each quarter.

- c. 62C § 32(a), source 6: "the Federal short-term rate determined under section 6621(b) of the Internal Revenue Code ... plus four percentage points".

Rates:

- TY2025: 8% in every M-2210 line from April 15, 2025 to April 15, 2026 (source 2). TIR 26-5 sets 7% for 2026 Q2, so the form's 8% overstates the rate for April 1 to 15, 2026 (corrected in verification; see conflict C3).
- 2026, from TIR 26-5 (dated 09/01/2026, source 7): Q1 8%, Q2 7%, Q3 8%, Q4 8%.
- TY2026: the draft 2026 M-2210 (dated 09/25/2026, source 9) prints 7%, 8%, 8% and "X%" for the four periods. The 2027 Q1 rate is not yet published.

Form: Form M-2210 (Form M-2210F for fiduciaries).

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise

- The current-year safe harbor is 80%, not 90%.
- The prior-year safe harbor is always 100%. There is no 110% rule.
- The threshold is $400, not $1,000.
- The annualisation percentages are 20%, 40%, 60% and 80%.
- There is no January 31 file-and-pay relief.
- The retirement waiver requires age 62.
- The rate is the federal short-term rate plus 4 points, set quarterly.
- Taxpayers subject to the 4% surtax must make all payments electronically.

## 9. Local income taxes

None found. Form 1-ES, M-2210 and AP 241 mention no city or county income tax.

## 10. Ambiguities and conflicts

- c. 62C § 32 compounds interest daily, but c. 62B § 14(a) says the estimated-tax charge is "without daily compounding". M-2210 computes it as simple interest.
- The 1-ES worksheet and AP 241 say a taxpayer whose requirement arises after April 1 pays the year's estimate over the remaining dates. But c. 62B § 14(c) fixes each required installment at 25% of the required annual payment, and M-2210 line 8 divides "by the number of installments required for the year". Whether a late start reduces the earlier installments for penalty purposes is not stated explicitly.
- The statute and M-2210 describe the farmer/fisherman March 1 rule as a full exemption. M-2210 line 4 also gives a 66.67% rate for farmers who miss March 1. The installment pattern for those farmers is four installments of 25% (corrected in verification). c. 62B § 14(c) defines the 66 2/3% figure as part of the "required annual payment", and every required installment is 25% of that payment. There is no separate single-January-payment rule for farmers.

### Conflicts resolved

- C1. Compounding. c. 62C § 32(a) compounds daily. c. 62B § 14(a) says "without daily compounding" for this charge. The DOR web page also says "compounded daily". The specific statute governs, so the charge is simple interest. M-2210 computes it as simple interest (underpayment × days ÷ 365 × rate).
- C2. The $400 floor. § 14(d)(i) exempts tax "less than $400". M-2210, AP 241, the DOR web page and the 1-NR/PY instructions exempt "$400 or less". § 13 requires estimates only when estimated tax is "more than $400". The difference affects only a balance of exactly $400. No public source reconciles it. DOR administers "$400 or less".
- C3. Rate for April 1 to 15, 2026. TY2025 M-2210 line 23 applies 8% to all days from January 1 to April 14, 2026. TIR 26-5 sets 7% for 2026 Q2. The penalty uses the rate established under c. 62C § 32 (§ 14(a)), so 7% governs April 1 to 15, 2026. Arithmetic for an underpayment U unpaid to April 15, 2026: the form charges U × 104/365 × 8%. Quarter by quarter it is U × (90/365 × 8% + 14/365 × 7%). The form overstates by U × 14/365 × 1%, or $3.84 per $10,000. Separately, line 19 counts days "before 4/15/26", while line 15 counts to the line 14 date. April 15 itself falls in no rate line.
- C4. Second 2026 due date. The DOR web page says "Second installment is due on or before June 16, 2026." § 14(c), 1-ES TY2026 and the draft 2026 M-2210 all say June 15, 2026. June 15, 2026 is a Monday and not a legal holiday. The statute governs: June 15, 2026. The web page is treated as a typo.
- C5. Late-arising requirement (UNRESOLVED). § 14(c) fixes "four required installments", each 25% of the required annual payment. AP 241 § 241.3 and 1-ES make a late starter pay over the remaining dates. M-2210 line 8 divides "by the number of installments required for the year". No statute, regulation or AP says whether a late start reduces the earlier required installments. Under the statute alone, the earlier installments stay at 25%, and the annualised method is the statutory way to reduce them. This changes the penalty for any late starter who does not annualise.
- C6. Farmers who miss March 1. § 14(c)(i) sets the required annual payment at 66 2/3% of the year's tax for a farmer or fisherman. Every required installment is 25% of that payment. So the pattern is four installments, each 25% of the lesser of 66 2/3% of current tax or 100% of prior-year tax.
- Statute amendments. The current texts of c. 62B §§ 13 and 14 and c. 62C § 32 were read on malegislature.gov on 2026-10-20. A session-law search on malegislature.gov found no 2025 or 2026 act amending them. St. 2026, c. 101 § 38 overrides them only for one TY2025 relief (below).

### 2026 changes

- Surtax threshold rises to $1,107,750 for TY2026, from $1,083,150 for TY2025 (1-ES TY2026; M-2210 Part 3).
- Underpayment rate for 2026 by quarter: 8%, 7%, 8%, 8% (TIR 26-5). The 2027 Q1 rate is not published; the draft 2026 M-2210 shows "X%".
- New c. 63E, inserted by St. 2026, c. 101 § 21, applies to taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 (§ 45). A pass-through entity may elect a 4% excise on members' income above the surtax threshold. Members get a refundable credit: "proportionate to each qualified member's share of the tax due and paid ... multiplied by 0.9". c. 63E § 7 lets DOR require estimated payments "in a manner consistent with chapter 62B". No DOR guidance on 63E estimated payments was found as of 2026-10-20. The working draft TIR of 06/23/2026 does not address them.
- TY2025 relief, St. 2026, c. 101 § 38. No interest or penalties apply to TY2025 underpayments caused by the act's decoupling from P.L. 119-21 (§§ 163(j), 168(n), 174, 174A, 179, 1400Z-2). The taxpayer must file a conforming return within 90 days of enactment. The act was approved June 12, 2026, so the deadline is September 10, 2026. The working draft TIR says this covers "underpaid estimated payments".
- Initiative Petition 25-18 would have cut the rate for 2027. The working draft TIR reports that the Supreme Judicial Court held it ineligible for the 2026 ballot on June 18, 2026. It does not affect TY2026.
- No change in 2026 to the $400 threshold, the 80% and 100% percentages, the due dates, or the rate formula.
