## 1. Who must make estimated payments

A taxpayer must pay if both of these are true:

- they expect to owe at least $500 after withholding and credits, and
- withholding and credits will be less than the smaller of 60% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax.

The rules apply to residents and nonresidents alike. There is no difference by filing status. Married taxpayers may pay estimated tax jointly or separately. Either choice is compatible with a joint or separate return. [TF2019-3] advises separate payments, but the law permits joint ones (corrected in verification).

- [HAR-18-235-97](d)(1)(A): "Married taxpayers may file estimated tax either separately or jointly, even if only one spouse has estimated tax liability".
- [HRS-235-97](a)(1): "a single payment voucher may be submitted by them jointly".

- [TF2019-3], tax year 2026: "You expect to owe at least $500 in tax for the current year, after subtracting your withholding and credits."
- [TF2019-3], on who is covered: "Hawaii residents, and ... Hawaii nonresidents, including resident and nonresident aliens."
- [TF2019-3], on spouses: "If you are married, you should make separate estimated tax payments for yourself and your spouse, even if you will be filing a joint return."

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 60% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax. The prior-year return must cover all 12 months.
- If no prior-year return was filed, or it covered less than 12 months, only the 60% option is available.
- There is no 110% tier and no AGI threshold. High-income taxpayers keep the 100% prior-year option. The statute sets the required annual payment itself (corrected in verification).
- [HRS-235-97](f)(4): "the required annual payment shall be the lesser of sixty per cent of the tax shown on the return" or "one hundred per cent" of the prior year's tax.

Quotes:

- [TF2019-3] worksheet line 11a: "Multiply line 10 by 60%".
- [TF2019-3] worksheet line 11b: "Enter 100% of the tax shown on your tax return for the preceding year. Your tax return for the preceding year must cover all 12 months."
- [N210-I25], tax year 2025: "less than the smaller of: 1. 60% of your 2025 tax, or 2. 100% of your 2024 tax."
- [N210-I25] line 7: "If you did not file a return for 2024, or if your 2024 tax year was less than 12 months, do not complete line 7."

Joint and separate returns: if the taxpayer files jointly in only one of the two years, they combine or split the prior-year tax under federal Publication 505 and check box E.

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

There are four equal installments, due on the 20th rather than the 15th: April 20, June 20, September 20 and January 20. For tax year 2026 that is April 20, 2026; June 20, 2026; September 20, 2026; and January 20, 2027. Each installment is one-fourth of the required annual payment.

- [TF2019-3]: "You can pay all of your estimated tax by April 20 of the current year or in four equal amounts".
- [TF2019-3] worksheet note: "pay 1/4 of the amount on line 13 on April 20 (current year), June 20 (current year), September 20 (current year), and January 20 (next year)."

Weekend and holiday shifting applies.

- [TF2019-3]: "If the due date for an estimated tax payment falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the payment will be on time if you make it on the next day".
- [N210-I25]: a payment made on the next working day "will be considered paid on the payment due date."

January waiver: the January 20 payment is not required if the return is filed and paid in full by January 31.

- [TF2019-3]: "You do not have to make the payment due January 20 of the following year if you file your current year tax return by January 31".
- [N210-I25]: "If you file your return and pay the tax due by January 31, 2026, you will have no penalty for the January installment, and the periods of underpayment for earlier installments will end on January 20, 2026."

A taxpayer who first has income later in the year pays a larger share at once ([TF2019-3] Q11):

| First has income | First payment due | Share of the year's estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Apr 1 – May 31 | June 20 | one-third |
| Jun 1 – Aug 31 | Sept 20 | one-half |
| After Aug 31 | Jan 20 | all |

Nonresident aliens without Hawaii wage withholding pay one-half by June 20, one-fourth by September 20 and one-fourth by January 20.

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in equal amounts on each due date by default. The taxpayer may elect actual withholding dates by checking box D on N-210.

- [N210-I25] line 10: "For withheld income tax on wages, you are considered to have paid one-fourth of these amounts on each payment due date unless you can show otherwise."
- [N210-F25] Part I, box D: "you treat it as paid for estimated tax purposes when it was actually withheld instead of in equal amounts on the payment due dates."

The withholding counted includes amounts withheld on Individual Housing Account (IHA) distributions, on nonresident sales of Hawaii real property, and by pass-through entities for nonresident owners. See [N210-I25] line 4.

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: tax minus withholding and credits is less than $500. [N210-I25]: "The total tax shown on your 2025 return minus the amount of tax you paid through withholding and tax credits is less than $500."
- No liability in the prior year. This is available only to someone who was a full-year Hawaii resident in that year, with no tax liability and a 12-month tax year. [N210-I25]: "(1) You were a full-year Hawaii resident in 2024 ... (2) You had no tax liability for 2024, and (3) Your 2024 tax year covered a 12-month period." "No liability" includes not having been required to file. The residence test comes from the forms; the adopted federal text requires only U.S. residence (corrected in verification). See Verification, conflict 2.
- First-year residents and part-year residents: no exception. They are expressly excluded from the no-prior-liability exception. [N210-I25]: "If you were a nonresident or a part-year resident in 2024, you do not meet the exception under number 2."
- Farmers and fishermen: at least two-thirds of gross income from farming or fishing in the prior or current year.
  - Option one: pay the whole estimate by January 20, as a single required installment in column (d).
  - Option two: file and pay all tax by March 1, which means no penalty.
  - [N210-I25]: "You filed your Hawaii return and paid the entire tax due by March 1, 2026."
  - [TF2019-3]: "Pay all of your estimated tax by January 20 of the following year".
  - The required amount is the general required annual payment ([N210-I25] line 9, column (d): "enter the full amount on line 8 of Part II"). No separate 66 2/3% rate was found in Hawaii text. The statute adopts the federal farmer rule, so this rests on the department's reading (corrected in verification). See Verification, conflict 1.
- Retired or disabled: a waiver may be requested on N-210 by checking box B with documentation. It applies to someone who retired after age 62 or became disabled in the prior or current year, with reasonable cause and no willful neglect. [N210-I25]: "You retired after reaching age 62 or became disabled in 2024 or 2025".
- Casualty or disaster: waiver by request using box B. [N210-I25]: "You didn't make a payment because of a casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstance and it would be inequitable to impose the penalty."
- Decedents' estates and certain grantor trusts: excepted for two years after death.
- Combat zone service: estimated payments are deferred. [TF2019-3]: "paying your estimated taxes will be deferred."

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed, using Form N-210 Schedule A. The taxpayer checks box C and attaches N-210. If Schedule A is used for one due date, it must be used for all of them.

- The periods end 3/31, 5/31, 8/31 and 12/31.
- The annualization factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1.
- The applicable percentages are 15%, 30%, 45% and 60%, which is 60% spread over four quarters.
- The standard deduction is entered in full in each column, not annualized.
- [N210-I25]: "If you use Schedule A for any payment due date, you must use it for all payment due dates."
- For planning, [TF2019-3] points to the federal Publication 505 worksheet, with Hawaii substitutions.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is interest-like, at 2/3 of 1% per month or part of a month (0.00667 a month, about 8% a year). It applies to each installment's underpayment.

- Months run from the 21st of one month to the 20th of the next. Any part of a month counts as a full month.
- The period ends at the earlier of the payment date or April 20 of the following year.
- The rate is fixed, not indexed. Its statutory basis is HRS § 235-97(f) (corrected in verification).
- [HRS-235-97](f): "at the rate of two-thirds of one per cent a month or fraction of a month upon the amount of the underpayment".
- The penalty is computed on Form N-210 Part IV, lines 18–21. Line 17 is the Part III overpayment line (corrected in verification).

Quotes:

- [N210-I25] Part IV: "the penalty is imposed on the amount of the underpayment of estimated tax at the rate of 2/3% (.00667) a month or part of a month."
- [N210-I25] line 19: "A month ... is the period from the 21st day of the current calendar month through the 20th day of the following calendar month."
- [N210-I25]: "counting the number of months after the due date of the installment to and including the date of payment or April 20, 2026, whichever is earlier."
- [N210-F25] line 20: "Number of months on line 19 x .00667 x" the underpayment.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The current-year safe harbor is 60%, not 90%.
- The prior-year safe harbor is always 100%. There is no 110% rule.
- The threshold is $500.
- All due dates fall on the 20th: April 20, June 20, September 20 and January 20. The return is also due April 20.
- The penalty counts whole months at 2/3% each. A payment one day late costs a full month (0.667%). The federal penalty applies the section 6621 underpayment rate "for the period of the underpayment" ([IRC-6654](a)), not a fixed monthly rate (corrected in verification).
- The no-prior-liability exception requires full-year Hawaii residence in the prior year, according to the department's forms. See item 10.
- The department advises spouses to make separate estimated payments, though the rule permits joint ones (corrected in verification).
- Hawaii has no estimated-tax-specific voucher. Payments go online or with the generic N-200V.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. The Hawaii Department of Taxation sources above describe no county or city income tax.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements between sources

- **Farmers and fishermen percentage.** Federal law lets farmers pay 66 2/3%. [N210-I25] simply uses the line 8 required annual payment, the smaller of 60% or 100%, as a single January installment. A separate farmer percentage is NOT FOUND. Verification found that HRS § 235-97(f)(4) adopts IRC § 6654(i), which federally uses 66 2/3%; see Verification, conflict 1 (corrected in verification).
- **"At least $500" versus "less than $500".** [TF2019-3] says estimates are required if you expect to owe "at least $500", and the exception applies to tax "less than $500". The two agree, so exactly $500 is required. HAR § 18-235-97(c)(1) confirms: payments are required if liability after credits "is $500 or more" (confirmed in verification).
- **Statute not verified.** Resolved in verification. HRS § 235-97(f) and (f)(4) state the 2/3% monthly rate and the 60%/100% rule.
- **Waiver detail.** Resolved in verification. The waiver grounds are those of IRC § 6654(e)(3), which HRS § 235-97(f)(4) adopts and HAR § 18-235-97(j) cites. That section lists exactly casualty or disaster, and retirement after 62 or disability. These match [N210-I25].
- **Payment form.** Resolved in verification. N-200V Rev. 2025 is at files.hawaii.gov/tax/forms/current/n200v_i.pdf. No 2026 revision was found.

### Conflicts resolved

1. **Farmers and fishermen percentage.** HRS § 235-97(f)(4) adopts IRC § 6654(i). Federally, § 6654(i)(1)(C) substitutes "66 2/3 percent" for "90 percent" in the required annual payment. Hawaii's proviso replaces that whole definition with 60% or 100%, so there is no "90 percent" left to substitute. The department applies the general 60%/100% amount (N-210 line 9, column (d)). I treat the form as governing. No Hawaii text states a 66 2/3% rate. This is a reading of the statute, not an express rule.
2. **No-prior-liability residence test.** HRS § 235-97(f)(4) adopts IRC § 6654(e)(2). Its text requires the person to be "a citizen or resident of the United States" for the prior year. HAR § 18-235-97(b)(2)(B) states no residence test. The department's forms require full-year Hawaii residence. No public source reconciles these. The department's forms are what it administers, so they govern in practice. A new Hawaii resident with no prior-year tax would be refused the exception by the forms but might qualify under the adopted federal text.
3. **110% high-income rule.** IRC § 6654(d)(1)(C) is inside the adopted § 6654(d). The Hawaii proviso sets the required annual payment to 60% or 100% outright. The N-210 line 7 instruction says "Enter 100% of your 2024 tax" with no income test. Resolved: no 110% rule.
4. **"At least $500" versus "less than $500".** HAR § 18-235-97(c)(1) says payments are required if the liability after credits "is $500 or more". Exactly $500 is required. Resolved.
5. **Joint estimated payments.** TF2019-3 Q9 advises separate payments. HRS § 235-97(a)(1) and HAR § 18-235-97(d) permit joint ones. The law governs; the TF sentence is advice. N-200V credits each payment to the one social security number on the voucher.
6. **Waiver grounds.** HAR § 18-235-97(j) limits waivers to IRC § 6654(e)(3). Those grounds match N-210. Resolved.

### 2026 changes

HRS § 235-97 was not amended in 2024 or 2025. Its history ends at L 2003, c 14, and the same compilation shows 2025 amendments to other sections (e.g. § 235-2.3, L 2025, c 123).

Department of Taxation Announcement No. 2026-06 (July 31, 2026), "Tax Law Changes from the 2026 Regular Legislative Session", lists no change to § 235-97, the estimated tax penalty, due dates, the $500 threshold or the 60%/100% rule. https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/news/announce/ann26-06.pdf

The 2026 acts that touch individual income tax start in 2027 or later, except Act 35:

- Act 24 (SB 3125) changes brackets "for tax years 2027 and 2029". It does not change 2026 brackets. Its § 235-51 changes apply to "taxable years beginning after December 31, 2026" (SB3125 CD2, section 9).
- Act 118 (SB 2881) allows elective withholding on Form 1099-R distributions. It "applies to taxable years beginning after December 31, 2026".
- Act 217 (SB 2552) raises IHA deduction limits from 2027. It does not change estimated tax rules.
- Act 35 (HB 2329) moves IRC conformity to the IRC "as amended as of December 31, 2025" for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2025. HRS § 235-97(f)(4) adopts the § 6654 subsections "as of the date set forth in section 235-2.3(a)". So tax year 2026 uses § 6654 as of December 31, 2025. I read § 6654 only in the 2023 edition of the U.S. Code. uscode.house.gov returned errors, so I could not confirm whether those subsections changed in 2024 or 2025. Hawaii's own percentages, threshold, dates and rate are in § 235-97 itself and do not depend on this.

Result: no 2026 change to thresholds, percentages, dates or the penalty rate was found.
