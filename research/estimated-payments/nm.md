# New Mexico

New Mexico calls the charge a "penalty", but it computes it as interest.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Every individual required to file a PIT-1. In practice, no penalty applies unless tax minus withholding is $1,000 or more. Full-year residents, part-year residents and nonresidents are all covered. No filing-status differences were found.

- [NM-ES25], 2025: "Every individual who must file a personal income tax return under the Income Tax Act also must pay estimated income tax."
- [NMSA-12.2](H)(1): no penalty if the difference between the tax shown on the return and withholding "is less than one thousand dollars ($1,000)".
- [NM-FYI320]: "All full-year and part-year residents, nonresidents and persons physically present in New Mexico for 185 days … are subject to this requirement."

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90%. Prior year: 100%.
- There is no 110% tier. No higher prior-year percentage appears at any income level, and no taxpayer loses the prior-year option because of high income.
- The prior-year option requires a 12-month prior year and a filed New Mexico return.

Quotes:

- [NMSA-12.2](B)(1): "(a) ninety percent of the tax shown on the return of the taxable year …; or (b) one hundred percent"
- [NMSA-12.2](B)(1)(b): the prior-year option applies only "if the preceding taxable year was a taxable year of twelve months and the taxpayer filed a New Mexico tax return".
- [NM-ES25], 2025: "100% of the tax on the prior year's return, if you filed a return during the prior year for a full 12-month year."

## 3. Due dates and shares

- Due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. Each installment is 25%.
- If a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or state or federal holiday, it moves to the next business day.
- No penalty applies to the January installment if the return is filed and the tax paid in full by January 31.

Quotes:

- [NM-41272], 2025, instruction 8: "multiply the required annual payment in line 7 by 0.25 (25%). Enter this amount in each column of line 12."
- (corrected in verification) The 25% share comes from the form. [NMSA-12.2](C) says only that "There shall be four required installments". Its catch-up rule (50% / 75% / 100%) implies 25% per installment but does not state it.
- [NM-41272], 2025: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or a Federal/State holiday the due date is the next business day."
- [NMSA-12.2](I): no penalty on the fourth installment if "on or before January 31 of the following taxable year the taxpayer files a return … and pays in full".

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each due date. The taxpayer may instead elect actual withholding dates by filing Form RPD-41272 with exception code 1. The election can be made separately for wage withholding and other withholding.

- [NMSA-12.2](F): "An equal part of the amount of withheld tax shall be deemed paid on each due date" It continues: "unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld."
- [NM-41272], 2025: "This provision may apply separately to wage and pension withholdings and any other amounts withheld under the Withholding Tax Act."
- (corrected in verification) Pass-through and oil and gas withholding counts for the person it was withheld from, not the person who withheld it. [NMAC-3.3.12] 3.3.12.15 says a remitter or pass-through entity "may not credit the amounts it withheld" from others against its own estimated tax.

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- **Tax below $1,000.** [NM-41272], 2025: no penalty if "Your tax for the current year, less total rebates and credits, less income tax withheld is under $1,000".
- **No liability in the prior year.** This applies only to full-year residents. [NMSA-12.2](H)(2): "the taxpayer did not have a tax liability for the preceding taxable year and the taxpayer was a resident of New Mexico". The prior year must also be 12 months.
- **First-year residents.** They are fully exempt. [NMSA-12.2](M): "The provisions of this section do not apply to first-year residents." [NM-FYI320] adds that this holds "regardless of the date on which they become residents."
- **Part-year residents.** NOT FOUND as a separate rule. Part-year residents are subject to the requirement per [NM-FYI320].
- **Farmers and ranchers.** They qualify if they expect at least two-thirds of gross income from farming or ranching, or had that share in the prior year. A joint return counts the spouse's income in the test. They may pay the whole required annual payment by January 15, or file and pay in full by March 1. A penalty applies only if they underpay by more than one-third. Fishermen are not mentioned.
  - [NMSA-12.2](E): "A penalty under Subsection G of this section shall not be imposed unless the rancher or farmer underpays the tax by more than one-third."
  - [NM-41272], 2025: farmers enter "100% of the required annual payment on line 12, column d".
  - (corrected in verification) Farmers must enter exception code 5 on the return even when the March 1 rule leaves no penalty. [NM-41272], 2025: "Enter 5 if you are a farmer or rancher and meet these requirements."
- **Retired or disabled.** No specific exception exists. (corrected in verification) Confirmed absent from [NMSA-12.2], [NM-41272], [NM-ES25], [NM-FYI320], [NM-WEB] and [NMAC-3.3.12]. The general waiver below is the only route. [NMAC-3.1.11] 3.1.11.11(B) lists disability "because of injury or prolonged illness" as an indication of nonnegligence. That rule is written for the § 7-1-69 civil penalty, and no source says it governs the § 7-2-12.2(H)(4) waiver.
- **Casualty, disaster or other waiver.** There is no casualty-specific rule. [NMSA-12.2](H)(4) gives a general waiver: no penalty if "the secretary determines that the underpayment was not due to fraud, negligence or disregard of rules and regulations."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

There is no true annualised installment method in the current forms or the 2011 statute. Instead there is a "late start" method: the "Exception for income that varies during the year", exception codes 2–4 on Form RPD-41272.

- This method applies only if income through March 31 was too small to require a payment.
- The first required payment then catches up: 50% in June, 75% in September, or 100% in January.
- Quote: [NMSA-12.2](C)(1): "fifty percent of the required annual payment must be paid on or before June 15".
- Quote: [NM-41272], 2025: "You may only use this method if actual income through March 31 is too small to require an estimated payment."

[NM-FYI320] (2014) describes something closer to annualisation. See item 10.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

- **Method.** The penalty is interest-like: the underpayment times the daily rate times the days late, for each installment. It runs until the payment date or April 15, whichever is earlier. Payments apply to the earliest installment first. TRD computes it by default. The taxpayer files Form RPD-41272 only to use an exception method.
  - [NM-ES25], 2025: "penalty due = estimated tax payment x daily interest rate x number of days late"
  - [NM-41272], 2025: "Complete and submit RPD-41272 only if you are using an exception method to calculate the penalty".
- **Rate rule.** The rate in [NMSA-12.2](G) is the one "specified in Subsection B of Section 7-1-67 NMSA 1978". TRD says this is the IRC individual underpayment rate, which changes quarterly. The rate for the first quarter after the tax year also covers April 1–15.
  - [NM-41272], 2025: "The penalty is calculated at the annual rate established each quarter for individual income tax purposes under the U.S. Internal Revenue Code (IRC)."
  - [NM-41272], 2025: "the interest rate that applies to the first quarter following the tax year also applies to the first 15 days".
- **Current rate.** 7% per year (0.019178082% per day) for July 1, 2026 – December 31, 2026. It was 6% for April 1 – June 30, 2026, and 7% for January 1, 2025 – March 31, 2026, per [NM-RATES]: "July 1, 2026 – December 31 , 2026 7% 0.019178082%".
- The penalty is not subject to the general 20% cap. [NM-ES25]: "The penalty for underpayment of estimated tax may exceed the maximum 20% that applies to an underpayment penalty for tax."

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The required annual payment matches federal, but there is no 110% rule.
- First-year residents owe no estimated tax.
- The prior-year zero-liability exception is limited to full-year New Mexico residents.
- There is no true annualisation. The "varying income" method only delays the start date.
- Farmers face a penalty only if they underpay by more than one-third.
- TRD computes the penalty itself, and the form is filed only when an exception method lowers it.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- **Annualisation.** [NM-FYI320] (Rev. 6/2014) describes a "modified annualization procedure" using the federal annualisation factors and cumulative 25/50/75/100% installments. [NM-41272] (2025) and [NMSA-12.2](C) describe only the late-start method.
- **Line 4 of RPD-41272.** [NM-41272] line 4 compares the 2025 tax with "the income tax withheld on 2024 PIT-1". This is probably a typo for 2025. The instructions at the top of the form refer to "the tax withheld and reported on your PIT-1 for the tax year".
- **PIT-1 line number.** [NM-41272] tells filers to enter the exception code on "PIT-1, page 2, line 35" in one place and on "line 34" in another. [NM-ES25] says line 35.
  - (corrected in verification) Resolved by [NM-PIT1], 2025: line 34 is "Penalty on underpayment of estimated tax" and line 35 is the exception-code box. [NM-41272]'s one "line 34" reference to the code box is a typo.
- **Statute version.** The statute text is taken from the 2011 session law. NOT FOUND: whether § 7-2-12.2 has been amended since.
- **No 2026 PIT-ES instructions.** NOT FOUND. TRD says the existing ones are not year-specific.

### Resolved conflicts

- **Annualisation.** [NMSA-12.2](C) governs. It provides only the late-start schedule, keyed to when the taxpayer "becomes required to pay estimated tax". [NM-41272] (2025) matches it. [NM-FYI320] (2014) describes a "modified annualization procedure". It recomputes the required payment each quarter from year-to-date income, which is one way of deciding when the taxpayer becomes liable. It is not a statutory reduction of installments. Use the statute and the 2025 form.
- **Line 4 of RPD-41272.** The "2024 PIT-1" reference is a typo. [NMSA-12.2](H)(1) compares the year's tax with withholding "for that taxable year". The form's own header note says "the tax withheld and reported on your PIT-1 for the tax year".
- **PIT-1 line number.** Resolved by [NM-PIT1]: line 34 holds the penalty and line 35 the exception code.
- **Statute version.** No amendment is listed for 2024–2026 sessions. 2025 HB 218 amended the neighbouring § 7-2-12 and § 7-2-12.1, but not § 7-2-12.2.
- **$1,000 test on [NM-WEB].** [NM-WEB] says TRD computes a penalty if the difference between tax and "estimated tax payments and withholding tax paid is more than $1,000". [NMSA-12.2](H)(1) governs. It measures tax minus withholding only, and relieves only a difference "less than one thousand dollars". At exactly $1,000 the penalty can apply. Estimated payments do not count in this test.
- **Oil and gas withholding on [NM-WEB].** [NM-WEB] says not to include tax withheld under the Oil and Gas withholding act. [NMSA-12.2](F) deems that withholding paid for the person withheld from. [NMAC-3.3.12] 3.3.12.15 reconciles the two: the remitter may not count it against its own estimated tax.
