## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay if you expect to owe at least $500 after withholding and credits, and your withholding plus credits will be less than the smaller of 90% of 2026 tax or 100% of 2025 tax. The threshold is $250 for married/RDP filing separately.

- 540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "you expect to owe at least $500 ($250 if married/RDP filing separately) in tax for 2026 (after subtracting withholding and credits)".
- 540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "You do not have to make estimated tax payments if you are a nonresident or new resident ... and did not have"

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%, including alternative minimum tax (AMT).
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% if prior-year California AGI was more than $150,000, or more than $75,000 for married/RDP filing separately. This rule does not apply to farmers or fishermen.
- High-income taxpayers lose the prior-year option. With current-year California AGI of $1,000,000 or more ($500,000 married/RDP filing separately), you must use 90% of current-year tax.
- The prior-year option requires a filed prior-year return covering 12 months.

Quotes:
- 540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "whose 2025 California adjusted gross income is more than $150,000 (or $75,000 if married/RDP filing separately) must figure estimated tax based on ... 110%".
- 540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "This rule does not apply to farmers or fishermen."
- 540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Taxpayers with 2026 California adjusted gross income equal to or greater than $1,000,000 ... must figure estimated tax based on their tax for 2026."
- FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "If you did not file a tax return for 2024, or if your 2024 taxable year was less than 12 months"

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Shares are not equal: 30% / 40% / 0% / 30%. Nothing is due on September 15.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day.
- January payment waived: you need not make the fourth payment if you file the 2026 return by January 31, 2027 and pay the balance.

Quotes:
- 540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "30% of the required annual payment for the 1st required installment, 40% ... for the 2nd ... no installment is due for the 3rd".
- FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "When the estimate due date falls on a weekend or holiday, the deadline ... is extended to the next business day."
- 540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "If you file your 2026 tax return by January 31, 2027, and pay the entire balance due, you do not have to"

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in the same 30% / 40% / 0% / 30% split as the installments, not in equal quarters. You can elect actual dates if you can show the amounts and dates (FTB 5805 Part I, Question 3).

- FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3, Worksheet II line 2: "Enter in column (a) and column (d) ... line 3 multiplied by 30%, enter -0- in column (c) and 40% for column (b)."
- FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "For withheld California income tax, you are considered to have paid the required amounts on each payment due date unless you can show otherwise."
- FTB 5805, TY2025, source 2, Question 3: "Was your California withholding not withheld in equal installments and are you able to show the actual amounts withheld per period"

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount, in either year: no penalty if tax after credits and withholding was under $500 ($250 married/RDP filing separately) for either 2024 or 2025.
  - FTB 5805, TY2025, source 2: "less credits (including the withholding credit) but not including estimated tax payments for either 2024 or 2025 was less than $500".
- No liability in the prior year: no penalty if the prior-year return covered 12 months, or would have if required, and showed no liability. FTB 5805, TY2025, source 2: "Your 2024 return was for a full 12 months ... and you did not have any tax liability on that return."
- First-year residents: no estimates are required for a new resident with no prior-year California liability (540-ES quote in question 1). The penalty does apply to nonresidents and new residents, subject to the exceptions above. FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "The penalty for the underpayment of estimated tax applies to nonresidents and new residents."
- Part-year residents: no separate exception. Form 540NR filers use FTB 5805 with a prorated-tax worksheet (Worksheet I, source 3).
- Farmers and fishermen: qualify if at least two-thirds of gross income for the current or prior year is from farming or fishing. They pay 66 2/3% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax, in one installment due January 15. There is no penalty if they file and pay by March 1. Farmers with California AGI of $1,000,000 or more must use the current-year percentage.
  - FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "Farmers and fishermen are required to make one estimate payment. For calendar year taxpayers, the due date is January 15, 2026."
  - FTB 5805F, TY2025, source 4, line 4: "Multiply line 3 by 662/3% (.6667)".
  - FTB 5805F, TY2025, source 4, line 10: "If your California AGI is equal to or greater than $1,000,000/$500,000 for married filing a separate return, use line 4."
- Retired or disabled: waiver available. FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "You retired after age 62 or became disabled in 2024 or 2025 and your underpayment was due to reasonable cause."
- Casualty or disaster: waiver available. FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "due to a casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstance and it would be against equity and good conscience to impose the penalty."
- FTB error (added in verification): no penalty to the extent an erroneous FTB levy, processing action or collection action caused the underpayment. R&TC §19136.7(a), source 6: "created or increased as the direct result of an erroneous levy, erroneous processing action, or erroneous collection action".
- Group-return nonresidents (added in verification): from tax year 2026, §19136 does not apply to a nonresident who elects to be included in a group return under §18537. R&TC §19136(j), source 6: "this section shall not apply to a nonresident individual electing to file in a group return".
- New-law exception: no penalty to the extent a law enacted and operative in the same year caused the underpayment. FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "created or increased by any provision of law that is chaptered during and operative for the taxable year of the underpayment."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, on FTB 5805 Part III (Annualized Income Installment Method Schedule).

The periods and factors (4, 2.4, 1.5, 1) match federal. The applicable percentages do not: they are 27%, 63%, 63%, 90%, reflecting the 30/40/0/30 schedule. The regular installment on line 19 is 30%, 40%, 0% and 30% of the required annual payment.

- FTB 5805, TY2025, source 2, line 15: applicable percentages "27% 63% 63% 90%".
- FTB 5805, TY2025, source 2, line 19: "Enter 30% of the amount shown on ... line 6 in columns (a & d), enter 40% ... in column b, enter -0- in column c."
- FTB 5805, TY2025, source 2: "If you use the annualized income installment method for one payment due date, you must use it for all payment due dates."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like charge on each installment's underpayment for the days unpaid. It uses more than one rate when the underpayment spans rate periods. The period ends at the earlier of the payment date or April 15 of the next year.

- FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "The penalty is figured for the number of days that the underpayment remained unpaid."
- FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "If an underpayment remained unpaid for more than one rate period, the penalty ... will be figured using more than one rate period."

How the rate is set: semiannually by the FTB. The instructions say the July 1 and January 1 rates are determined in March and September.
- FTB 5805 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "will be determined by the FTB in March 2026 and September 2026, respectively."
- (corrected in verification) Statutory rule, found: §19136(b) replaces the §6621 rate with the §19521 rate. §19521 uses the IRC §6621 underpayment rate (federal short-term rate plus 3 points), set semiannually. The January rate applies July through December, and the July rate applies January through June. The penalty is not compounded daily.
  - R&TC §19521(a)(2), source 6: "(A) The rate for January shall apply during the following July through December, and (B) The rate for July shall apply".
  - R&TC §19521(b)(2), source 6: "Paragraph (1) shall not apply for purposes of computing the amount of any addition to tax under Section 19136".

Rates for the 2025 computation period (source 3): "8% 4-15-25 through 6-30-25" and "7% 7-1-25 through 4-15-26".

The FTB rates page (source 5) lists the estimate penalty rate. Its footnote says the "Interest rate" column covers "Personal income tax underpayment and overpayment, corporation underpayment, and estimate penalty rate":

| Period | Rate |
|---|---|
| 07/01/2026 – 12/31/2026 | 7% |
| 07/01/2025 – 06/30/2026 | 7% |
| 01/01/2025 – 06/30/2025 | 8% |

Short method: calendar-year filers may use Part II lines 7–13 if they made no estimated payments other than withholding, or paid estimated tax on the due dates. Source 3: "You paid estimated tax on the required due dates." For 2025 the factor is .05028767, less .00019 per day paid before April 15, 2026 (FTB 5805, TY2025, source 2, lines 11–12).

Form: FTB 5805, or FTB 5805F for farmers and fishermen. The penalty goes on Form 540 line 113 or Form 540NR line 123. FTB will compute the penalty and bill you if you do not file the form. Source 3: "If you owe a penalty, the Franchise Tax Board (FTB) figures the penalty for you and sends you a bill".

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- Installments are 30% / 40% / 0% / 30%. Nothing is due September 15, and 70% is due by June 15. Withholding is deemed paid in the same split.
- The threshold is $500 ($250 married/RDP filing separately), not $1,000.
- No prior-year safe harbor for current-year California AGI of $1,000,000 or more ($500,000 MFS). These taxpayers must pay 90% of current-year tax. This applies to farmers too.
- No penalty if tax after withholding and credits was under $500 in either the current or the prior year. Federal tests only the current year.
- New residents with no prior-year California liability need not make estimates.
- The annualised method uses 27% / 63% / 63% / 90%, not 22.5% / 45% / 67.5% / 90%.
- The required annual payment includes the 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax on taxable income over $1,000,000. Source 3: "imposes an additional 1% tax on taxable income over $1,000,000 and is included in the calculation of the estimated tax."
- Mandatory e-pay applies once an estimate or extension payment exceeds $20,000, or the return's tax exceeds $80,000. Non-compliance carries a 1% penalty (540-ES instructions, TY2026, source 1).
- No penalty to the extent a same-year law change caused the underpayment.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Withholding wording. Form question 3 asks whether withholding was "not withheld in equal installments". The worksheet treats withholding as 30% / 40% / 0% / 30%, not equal quarters. The worksheet instruction is the operative one, so the form's wording is loose.
- Which year's AGI applies. The 110% test uses prior-year (2025) California AGI per the 540-ES. The $1,000,000 test uses current-year (2026) AGI. The FTB 5805 Important box mixes both in one sentence for 2025.
- Stale footer. The 2026 540-ES instructions PDF carries the page footer "Form 540-ES Instructions 2025" on page 1.
- January file-and-pay date. The 540-ES says "by January 31, 2027". The 2025 FTB 5805 instructions say "before February 1, 2026". These are equivalent, but phrased differently.
- No statutory basis. The FTB pages give no statutory citation for the rate-setting rule, and the statute site was unreachable. (corrected in verification) Resolved: the rule is R&TC §19521 (question 7).

### Conflicts resolved

- **Withholding wording.** Resolved in favour of the worksheet. §19136(e)(2)(A) replaces "an equal part" in §6654(g)(1) with "the applicable percentage", meaning 30/40/0/30. The form's question 3 wording is loose. The rule is not.
- **Which year's AGI applies.** Resolved. The 110% test uses prior-year AGI: §6654(d)(1)(C)(i), adopted by §19136(a), reads "for the preceding taxable year". §19136.2 suspended it only for 1998. The $1,000,000 test uses current-year AGI: §19136.3(a) reads "shown on the return of the individual for the taxable year". Both the 540-ES and the 5805 are consistent with this.
- **$1,000,000 rule and farmers.** Resolved: it applies to farmers. §19136.3 modifies §6654(d)(1)(B), and the farmer rule in §6654(i)(1)(C) is built on §6654(d)(1)(B). The FTB 5805F agrees.
- **January file-and-pay wording.** These are the same rule. §6654(h) uses January 31.
- **Rate determination timing.** No conflict. The statute fixes which month's federal rate applies. The FTB's "March and September" is when it announces the rate.
