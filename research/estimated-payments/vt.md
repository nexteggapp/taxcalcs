## 1. Who must make estimated payments

The Vermont documents do not state a dollar threshold for who must pay. They say a taxpayer must pay if they expect to owe tax or have income not subject to withholding. A $500 figure appears only as the level below which no penalty or interest is charged (question 5). No filing-status difference was found.

- IN-114 Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Generally, you must pay estimated income tax if you expect to owe an income tax liability or have income not subject to withholding".
- Estimated Income Tax web page, source 6: you must make estimated payments if "you think that your withholding will be less than 90% of your tax liability."
- 32 V.S.A. §5852(a), source 9: every individual subject to tax, other than farmers and fishers, "shall make installment payments of the taxpayer's estimated tax liability for each taxable year."

The IN-114 also requires self-employed people physically working in Vermont to include the Child Care Contribution (0.11% of that self-employment income) in their estimated payments.

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage for high income: none. No 110% rule was found in sources 1–11.
- The prior-year option requires a filed prior-year return covering 12 months (statute).
- High-income taxpayers keep the prior-year option.

Quotes:
- 32 V.S.A. §5851(2), source 8: "if the preceding year was a taxable year of 12 months and the taxpayer filed a return for such preceding taxable year."
- IN-114 voucher, TY2026, source 2: "an amount equal to 100% of your prior year tax liability or 90% of your current year tax liability, whichever is less."
- IN-152 lines 1–3, TY2025, source 3: "90% of 2025 VERMONT TAX MINUS CREDITS", "100% of 2024 VERMONT TAX MINUS CREDITS", "Enter the lesser of Line 1 or Line 2."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% of the required annual payment each.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day, by Department practice. State Holiday Closures page, source 15: "Any tax due date that falls on a holiday is automatically extended to the next business day." Timely Filing page, source 16: "When the due date falls on a weekend, the due date is the next business day." The statute covers Sunday only. 32 V.S.A. §3004, source 14: an act due on a Sunday "shall be valid if done on the following Monday." No 2026 due date falls on a weekend or a Vermont holiday. (corrected in verification) The IN-111 instructions also say mailed returns are on time if received "within three business days after the due date".
- January payment waived by early filing: NOT FOUND. No such rule appears in sources 1–11.

Quotes:
- IN-114 Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Estimated income tax payments are made in four equal amounts by the due dates shown below".
- 32 V.S.A. §5852(a), source 9: "The amount of each payment shall be 25 percent of the required annual payment."
- IN-152 line 4, TY2025, source 3: "Tax payments required per quarter (Multiply Line 3 by .25)".

## 4. How withholding is treated

The worksheets treat withholding as paid in equal quarters, 25% on each due date. They offer no box to use actual withholding dates. The statute, however, allows the actual dates if the taxpayer establishes them (see question 10).

- IN-152 line 5a, TY2025, source 3: "From 2025 Form IN-111, add Lines 26a, 26d, and 26e, then multiply by 25% (0.25) per quarter".
- 32 V.S.A. §5852(c), source 9: "An equal part of such amount shall be deemed paid on each due date ... unless the taxpayer establishes the dates"

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty or interest if tax due less withholding is under $500. IN-111 Instructions line 31, TY2025, source 5: "If the tax liability due, less withholding, is less than $500, you will not be subject to penalty or interest charges." The $500 is measured before estimated payments: current-year tax less withholding and credits. Source 17's example: "this year's tax from Line 26 less withholding and credits is not less than $500." (corrected in verification) See question 10 for the "more than $500" wording on the voucher.
- No liability in the prior year: NOT FOUND as a separate exception. Under the statute's definition (source 8), a filed 12-month prior-year return showing zero tax makes the required annual payment zero.
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND in sources 1–11.
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND in sources 1–11.
- Farmers and fishermen: fully exempt from estimated payments if at least two-thirds of income is from farming or fishing as defined federally. No percentage or date was found because no payment is required. IN-114 Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Individuals receiving two-thirds of their income from farming or fishing, as defined by the IRS, are not required to make estimated income tax payments." The IN-111 instructions (source 5) refer to the federal definition in 26 U.S.C. §6654(i)(2).
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in sources 1–11.
- Casualty or disaster: NOT FOUND in sources 1–11.

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed, but only for taxpayers who annualize federally. The Vermont installment is the federal Form 2210 Schedule AI installment scaled by the ratio of the Vermont required annual payment to the federal one. Use Worksheet IN-152A and attach federal Form 2210 pages 1–4.

- IN-114 Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Individuals who make annualized estimated tax payments with the IRS may also make annualized estimated payments in Vermont."
- IN-152A line 6, TY2025, source 4: "Vermont payment due. (Divide Line 5 by Line 4, then multiply by Line 3.)"

## 7. How the penalty is computed

Two charges on each quarter's shortfall, added together:

1. Interest for the days late, from the due date to the date paid or April 15 of the next year, whichever is earlier. Each calendar year's days use that year's rate.
2. A penalty of 1% of the shortfall for each month or part of a month late, capped at 25% by statute.

- Interest rate rule: the Commissioner sets one annual rate each year under 32 V.S.A. §3108, equal to the average prime rate for the 12 months ending September 30, rounded up to the nearest quarter percent. §3108(a), source 13: the rate equals "the average prime rate charged by banks during the immediately preceding 12 months commencing on October 1 of the prior year, rounded upwards". Interest Rates page, source 7: "Rounding up to the nearest quarter of a percent, the annual rate for the overpayment and underpayment of tax for 2026 will be 7.75%."
- Rates in effect: 8.50% for 2025 and 7.75% for 2026, effective January 1, 2026 (source 7; IN-152 lines 9a and 9b, source 3).
- Penalty rule: 32 V.S.A. §3202(b)(2), source 11: "a penalty that shall be equal to one percent of the outstanding tax liability for each month, or portion thereof".
- IN-152 line 12, TY2025, source 3: "a portion of a month counts as a whole month".
- Forms: Worksheet IN-152, or IN-152A for annualizers. The total goes on IN-111 line 31.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- A separate 1%-per-month penalty is charged on top of interest. A shortfall one day late is charged a full month's 1%.
- The threshold is $500, not $1,000.
- No 110% rule for high incomes.
- The worksheet does not let a taxpayer use actual withholding dates.
- Farmers and fishers are fully exempt from estimated payments, not just given a single January date.
- No federal-style exception for zero prior-year tax, and no retirement, disability or casualty waiver was found.
- Self-employed people must include the Child Care Contribution in estimated payments.
- Annualizing is available only to taxpayers who annualize federally.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. Sources 1–6 mention no city, county or school-district income tax. No primary source was found that states this explicitly.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- The $500 threshold, three wordings. The IN-111 Instructions say no penalty if the tax due less withholding "is less than $500". The IN-114 Instructions say "If the tax liability due is less than $500" and do not mention withholding. (corrected in verification) The IN-114 voucher says penalties apply if you "owe more than $500 when you file". Neither says what happens at exactly $500. Neither says whether the $500 is measured before or after estimated payments.
- No statutory $500 for individuals. 32 V.S.A. §5852 states no dollar threshold. The "$500.00 minimum tax requirement" appears in §5857, which sits next to §5856 (corporate declarations) and describes a declaration schedule (15th day of the 4th, 6th, 9th and 12th months) that does not match the individual dates. Which statute supports the $500 individual threshold was not found.
- Withholding timing. The statute (§5852(c)) lets a taxpayer prove actual withholding dates. The IN-152 and IN-152A force 25% per quarter.
- Prior-year safe harbor in the statute. §5851(2) uses 100% of prior-year tax. §5859(c)(1), the interest exception, instead uses "the tax computed at the rate applicable to the taxable year but otherwise on the basis of the facts shown on the return for" the preceding year. The worksheets follow §5851.
- Period end. §5859(b)(2)(A)(i) ends the underpayment period on the date "a U.S. income tax return is required to be filed for that year by that corporation". The worksheets end it on April 15 for individuals.
- Annualization percentage. §5852(b) refers to "an amount equal to the 24 percent of the taxpayer's required payment for federal income tax purposes". IN-152A instead scales each federal Schedule AI installment by a ratio. The statute's "24 percent" was not explained in any source found.

### Conflicts resolved

- **What the $500 is measured against.** Resolved. Source 17's worked examples measure current-year tax less withholding and credits, before estimated payments. In one example, tax is $1,000, withholding $0 and estimates $600, and interest and penalty apply.
- **Exactly $500.** Unresolved. The IN-111 and IN-114 Instructions exempt only "less than $500". The voucher charges only those who "owe more than $500". No statute sets the individual threshold.
- **Statutory source of the $500.** Unresolved. §§5851–5852 (subchapter 5, individuals) state no dollar amount. The "$500.00 minimum tax requirement" is in §5857, in subchapter 5A ("Quarterly Filing and Payment"). Its companion §5858 uses a 4th/6th/9th/12th-month schedule that does not match the individual dates.
- **Withholding timing.** The statute governs. §5852(c) lets the taxpayer establish actual withholding dates, though the worksheets have no line for it.
- **Prior-year safe harbor.** Unresolved. §5851(2) uses 100% of prior-year tax. The §5859(c)(1) interest exception uses prior-year facts at current-year rates. The two differ only when rates or law changed between the years. §3202(b)(2) charges the 1% penalty for failures under "subchapter 5 or 5A", so both subchapters reach individuals. The worksheets follow §5851.
- **Period end.** Resolved in effect. §5859(b)(2)(A)(i) ends the period on the federal return due date, worded for "that corporation". For a calendar-year individual that date is April 15, which is what IN-152 uses.
- **Annualization "24 percent".** Unresolved. §5852(b) is unchanged since 2015 and no source explains it. IN-152A scales the federal installment by a ratio.
- **Interest rate on source 17 (new).** Source 17 says "Interest of 0.5% per month". §3108 and IN-152 give 8.50% for 2025 and 7.75% for 2026 (about 0.65% a month). The statute governs; the web page is out of date.
- **Weekend rule (new).** §3004 extends only a Sunday due date. The Department extends weekends and holidays (sources 15 and 16). No 2026 installment date is affected.
