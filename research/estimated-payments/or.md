## 1. Who must make estimated payments

The threshold is $1,000 of tax after credits and withholding (source 1, tax year 2026).
"You must make estimated tax payments if you expect to owe tax of $1,000 or more after all credits and withholding."

Refundable credits count in the threshold test (source 1, 2026).
"You estimate that your tax after withholding and credits (including refundable credits) will be $1,000 or more"

The instructions give the same $1,000 for every filing status. No filing-status variant appears in sources 1, 2, 5 or 6.

The $1,000 amount is set by rule (source 11, OAR 150-316-0465(1)(a)).
"No declaration of estimated tax is required if the estimated tax as defined ORS 316.557 is less than $1,000."

The statute also has gross-income tests that vary by filing status (source 9, ORS 316.563(1)): more than $1,000 of non-wage gross income, or gross income over $20,000 (single, head of household, surviving spouse, or married with a non-wage-earning spouse), $10,000 (married, both spouses with wages) or $5,000 (married, not entitled to a joint declaration).
"$5,000 in the case of a married individual not entitled under ORS 316.567 to file a joint declaration"
The DOR instructions do not mention these gross-income tests. See answer 10.

Estates and trusts are not required to pay (source 1, 2026; source 9, ORS 316.559).
"Unlike the federal system, estates and trusts are not required to make Oregon estimated tax payments."

Nonresidents need pay only if wages are subject to Oregon withholding or they have Oregon business income (source 9, ORS 316.569).
"No declaration shall be required of a nonresident individual under ORS 316.557 to 316.589 unless:"

Married couples may pay jointly, except a nonresident noncitizen spouse, legal separation, or different tax years (source 1, 2026; source 9, ORS 316.567(2)).
"If you expect to file separate returns, make separate estimated tax payments."

## 2. Required annual payment

The required annual payment is the lesser of 90% of current-year tax and 100% of prior-year tax (source 1, 2026).
"Your required annual payment is the lesser of: • Ninety percent (90%) of the tax after all credits to be shown on your 2026 return; or"
"One hundred percent (100%) of the tax after all credits shown on your 2025 return (also known as “safe harbor”)."

The statute leaves the prior-year percentage to DOR rule (source 9, ORS 316.587(8)(b)), and the rule sets it at 100% (source 11, OAR 150-316-0493(3)(a)(B)).
"One hundred percent of the tax shown on the prior year's return, if qualified."

There is no higher prior-year percentage above an AGI threshold. No 110% rule, and no married-filing-separately variant, appears in sources 1, 2, 5, 9 or 11. A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option in any of these sources.

The prior-year option requires a filed prior-year return, filed before the current-year return, for a 12-month year (source 11, OAR 150-316-0493(3)(a)(B)).
"the prior year’s return must be filed before the current year’s return, and the prior tax year must consist of 12 months."

A part-year-resident prior-year return qualifies; a short-year return does not (source 1, 2026).
"Your 2025 return can’t be a short-year return, but it may be a part-year return."

With no prior-year Oregon return, only the 90% figure applies (source 1, 2026, worksheet line 20b).
"If you didn’t file a 2025 Oregon return, enter the amount from line 20a on line 21."

The statute adds a third measure: 90% of annualized tax (source 9, ORS 316.587(8)(c)). See answer 6.

When spouses switch between joint and separate returns, the prior-year tax is allocated or combined (source 11, OAR 150-316-0495 and 150-316-0497).
"the tax liability for the prior year may be used even though the current year is a single or separate return"

## 3. Due dates and shares

Calendar-year 2026 dates are April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027 (source 1, 2026; source 4).
"Quarter 1: April 15, 2026. • Quarter 2: June 15, 2026. • Quarter 3: September 15, 2026. • Quarter 4: January 15, 2027."

The installments are equal: 25% each (source 1, 2026; source 11, OAR 150-316-0493(4)(a)(A)).
"Regular Installment: The required installment payment for each period is 25 percent of the required annual payment."

The full amount may instead be paid by the first due date (source 1, 2026).
"You have the option to pay all of your 2026 estimated tax by the due date of the first installment or in four equal installments."

Weekend and holiday shifting follows Oregon legal holidays, not federal ones (source 1, 2026).
"If your payment due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or an Oregon legal holiday, the payment is due the next business day."
For tax year 2025 this moved the second date to June 16, 2025 (source 2, 2025).

If the requirement is first met after April 1, fewer equal installments apply: 3 (after April 1 and before June 2), 2 (after June 1 and before September 2), or 1 (after September 1) (source 1, 2026). The trigger dates are in ORS 316.577(1)-(3); the number of installments is in ORS 316.579(3)-(5) (source 9). (corrected in verification)
"You can begin making installment payments after the first installment date if the income ... isn’t received until after April 1"

Fiscal-year dates are the 15th day of the 4th, 6th and 9th months and 15 days after year end (source 1, 2026).
"Fourth 15 days after last day of tax year"

The DOR instructions do not waive the January installment for an early-filed return. This is NOT FOUND in sources 1, 2, 5 and 6. The statute has a related January 31 provision (source 9, ORS 316.579(7)). See answer 10.
"If a taxpayer does not file a declaration but files a return on or before January 31 of the succeeding year and pays in full"

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each installment date, unless the taxpayer shows the actual dates (source 2, 2025).
"Withholding is treated as if it were paid in equal amounts on the due date for each required installment payment unless you can show that it was paid differently."

The statute gives the same rule and allows the actual-dates alternative (source 9, ORS 316.587(6)).
"unless the taxpayer establishes the date on which all amounts were actually withheld"

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

Tax below $1,000. No interest if tax after credits minus withholding is under $1,000 (source 2, 2025).
"If the amount on line 5 is less than $1,000, stop here! You don’t owe underpayment interest."
The statute is ORS 316.588(1) (source 9).

No liability in the prior year (Exception 2). The taxpayer must have been a full-year resident with a 12-month year and $0 tax or no filing requirement (source 2, 2025; source 9, ORS 316.587(5)(a)).
"If you were a nonresident or a part-year resident for tax year 2024, you can’t claim Exception 2."

First-year residents. NOT FOUND as a separate exception in sources 1, 2, 5, 9 and 11. A new resident with no prior Oregon return can use only the 90% current-year figure (answer 2).

Part-year residents. There is no exemption. The required annual payment is divided by the number of installment periods in which the taxpayer was a resident or had Oregon business income (source 2, 2025).
"Divide the amount on line 10 of the required annual payment worksheet by the number of installment periods when you were an Oregon resident"
A part-year prior-year return still supports the 100% safe harbor (source 11, OAR 150-316-0493(3)(b)).

Farmers and fishermen (Exception 1). Oregon exempts them entirely. There is no reduced percentage and no special January date. The test is two-thirds of gross income from farming or fishing, including oyster farming, in the prior year or estimated for the current year (source 1, 2026; source 9, ORS 316.573).
"or will be at least two-thirds of your estimated gross income for 2026, you don’t have to make Oregon estimated tax payments."
Gross income counts sources inside and outside Oregon (source 11, OAR 150-316-0475).

Retired or disabled (Exception 3). The taxpayer retired at 62 or older, or became disabled, in the current or prior year, and had reasonable cause (source 2, 2025; source 9, ORS 316.587(5)(c); source 11, OAR 150-316-0489).
"During 2024 or 2025, you retired at age 62 or older or became disabled; and • There was reasonable cause"

Casualty, disaster or unusual circumstances (Exception 4). Decided case by case, per installment period (source 2, 2025; source 9, ORS 316.587(5)(b); source 11, OAR 150-316-0487).
"Unemployment isn’t an unusual circumstance, but the loss of your tax records from a fire, flood, or other natural disaster might qualify."

First-year S corporation shareholders (Exception 5). Applies to a nonresident, or a prior-year part-year resident, on S corporation income in the first year of the election (source 2, 2025; source 9, ORS 316.587(5)(d)).
"The income is for the first year that S corporation status was elected"

Federal pensions for service before October 1, 1991 need no estimated payments (source 1, 2026).
"If you retired from U.S. government service before October 1, 1991, you don’t need to make Oregon estimated tax payments on your federal pension."

## 6. Annualized income installment method

It is allowed. The worksheet is the Annualized Income Worksheet in the Form OR-10 Instructions (source 2, 2025; source 1, 2026).
"This worksheet is found inside Form OR-10 Instructions."

The taxpayer checks a box on the return: Form OR-40 box 44b, OR-40-N box 69b, or OR-40-P box 68b (source 2, 2025).
"Check the box on your return to indicate that you annualized your income: Form OR-40, box 44b"

The periods end March 31, May 31, August 31 and December 31. The multipliers are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1. The applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90% (source 2, 2025, worksheet lines 4 and 23; source 11, OAR 150-316-0493(4)(a)(B)).
"22.5% for the first period; (ii) 45% for the first and second periods; (iii) 67.5% for the first, second and third periods"

Any shortfall from annualizing is added to the next installment (source 11, OAR 150-316-0493(4)(c)).
"must be added to the required installment payment for the next succeeding period."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

It is interest, not a penalty. It accrues daily on each installment's shortfall until paid or the return due date, whichever is earlier (source 9, ORS 316.587(1) and (3); source 1, 2026).
"Underpayment interest is charged on each underpaid installment until the date the installment is paid or the due date of the return, whichever comes first."

The ORS chapter 314 underpayment penalties do not apply (source 9, ORS 316.587(1)).
"The penalty provisions contained in ORS chapter 314 for underpayment of tax shall not apply to underpayments of estimated tax"

DOR computes it on a running balance between events: withholding, required installments, rate changes and payments (source 2, 2025).
"Underpayment interest is calculated on a running balance of estimated tax due from one event to the next."

Payments go to the earliest unpaid installment first (source 9, ORS 316.579(10) and 316.587(4)).
"Any payment of estimated tax received by the Department of Revenue shall first be applied to underpayments of estimated tax due for any prior installment"

Each line's interest is rounded to the nearest whole dollar (source 2, 2025).
"Round each amount to the nearest whole dollar."

The rate is the general ORS 305.220 interest rate (source 9, ORS 316.587(1)). Each year in July, DOR compares its rate with the IRS underpayment rate for the third quarter plus one percent. If they differ by one point or more, the new rate takes effect January 1 (source 10, ORS 305.220(3); source 12, OAR 150-305-0144).
"If the Oregon rate is one percent or more different from the modified federal rate, the director will revise the Oregon rate to the federal rate plus one percent."

The current rate is 8% a year, effective January 1, 2026 (source 12, OAR 150-305-0140(1), REV 9-2025). The 2025 rate was 9%.
"For deficiencies and delinquencies owing on or after January 1, 2026, ... will bear interest at the rate of eight percent annually."
The OR-10 worksheet uses daily rates of 0.000247 for 2025 and 0.000219 from January 1, 2026, on a 365-day year (source 2, 2025).
"For 2025, the annual interest rate is 9 percent. For 2026, the annual interest rate is 8 percent."

The rate for 2027 is NOT FOUND. OAR 150-305-0140 (source 12) lists rates only through January 1, 2026. The DOR rulemaking page (source 17) listed no interest-rate rule for 2027 on 2026-10-20. ORS 305.220(3)(a) requires notice "not less than three months before the proposed effective date" (source 10). (corrected in verification)

The form is Form OR-10 (source 3, 2025). The total goes on Form OR-40 line 44, OR-40-N line 69, or OR-40-P line 68.
"Use this form to report the interest on underpaid estimated tax or to claim an exception to paying estimated tax."

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

There is no 110% prior-year rule for high incomes. The prior-year safe harbor is 100% at every income level (answer 2).

Self-employment tax, FICA and household employment tax are left out of the Oregon calculation (source 1, 2026).
"Don’t include Social Security tax (FICA), self-employment tax, or household employment tax in your calculations."

Farmers and fishermen meeting the two-thirds test owe no estimated tax at all (answer 5).

Weekend and holiday shifting uses Oregon legal holidays (answer 3).

Estates and trusts make no Oregon estimated payments (answer 1).

The kicker counts as a credit in "tax after credits" in the OR-10 worksheet. For 2025, line 2 of the required annual payment worksheet includes Form OR-40 line 32, the kicker line (sources 2 and 7).
"Enter your total 2025 credits from Form OR-40, lines 32 and 37 through 39"
For the 2024 prior-year figure, the worksheet uses OR-40 lines 36 through 39, and line 38 of the 2024 form is the kicker line (sources 2 and 8).
"38. Oregon surplus refund. Enter your kicker amount"
So a kicker lowers both the $1,000 test and the 90% figure for that year. It also lowers the next year's 100% safe harbor. That last point is my inference from the worksheet line references; no source states it in words.

The kicker exists only on odd-numbered-year returns (source 5, 2025).
"The kicker is available only on the return for an odd-numbered year."
For 2025 it is 9.863 percent of 2024 tax liability (source 6, 2025).

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

Metro Supportive Housing Services (SHS) personal income tax and Multnomah County Preschool for All (PFA) personal income tax both require quarterly estimated payments. The City of Portland Revenue Division administers both (source 14).
"The City of Portland Revenue Division administers the tax on behalf of Metro."
"The City of Portland Revenue Division administers the tax on behalf of Multnomah County."

For tax years 2021 to 2025, the threshold was $1,000 of current-year and prior-year liability for each program. From tax year 2026 it is $5,000 (source 14).
"Beginning in tax year 2026 and for all subsequent years, this threshold increases to $5,000."

TriMet and Lane Transit District self-employment taxes are administered by Oregon DOR. They have no estimated payments (source 13, 2025).
"Am I required to make estimated payments? No. File and pay your taxes by the due date of the return"

These were not researched further.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

The $1,000 boundary is worded differently. Sources 1 (page 1), 2, 5 and 6 say "$1,000 or more". OAR 150-316-0493(3)(d) says payments are not required if the amount is "less than $1,000". But source 1 page 6 says interest applies only if the difference "is more than $1,000". So a balance of exactly $1,000 is ambiguous.

The statute's gross-income tests (ORS 316.563(1): $1,000 non-wage; $20,000, $10,000 or $5,000 by filing status) are absent from all DOR instructions. The instructions apply only the $1,000 tax test. How the two interact is not explained.

ORS 316.577 sets the first declaration date as "April 18 ... or the due date of the return for the prior taxable year ... whichever is earlier". DOR publishes April 15. In practice the earlier date is normally April 15.

The rules for the prior-year return are worded differently. OAR 150-316-0493 says the prior-year return must be "filed before the current year’s return". OAR 150-316-0495 and 150-316-0497 (filing-status changes) say "filed timely including extensions". Source 5 says only that the return must cover "all 12 months".

ORS 316.579(7) treats a return filed by January 31 and paid in full as the declaration or its amendment. Its effect on underpayment interest is not stated. The DOR worksheets do not implement it.

Source 1 lets a taxpayer whose income starts after April 1 make 3, 2 or 1 equal installments. But source 2 computes regular required installments as the annual amount divided by four, except for part-year residents and annualizers. Using the start-later schedule without annualizing could still produce interest under the OR-10 worksheet.

Source 1 page 1 lists a third test, "Ninety percent of the tax on your 2026 annualized income", alongside the 90% and 100% tests. The required annual payment worksheet (line 21) uses only the 90% and 100% figures. Annualizing changes the installment amounts, not the annual figure.

Source 2 says interest on tax unpaid after the return due date "is computed separately". That interest is outside Form OR-10.

### Resolved conflicts

- The $1,000 boundary. Resolved. The statute governs. ORS 316.563(2) and 316.588(1) waive the requirement and the interest only when the amount "is less than" the rule amount (source 9). OAR 150-316-0465(1)(a) and 150-316-0493(3)(d) also say "less than $1,000" (source 11). Exactly $1,000 is therefore subject to the requirement and to interest. The "more than $1,000" wording on source 1 page 6 is the outlier. Neither section was amended in 2026 (source 15).
- The gross-income tests. Partly resolved. ORS 316.563(1) requires a declaration only when a gross-income test is met, and 316.563(2) removes it when estimated tax is under $1,000. Both conditions must hold for the declaration requirement. ORS 316.587 and 316.588, which impose the interest, do not refer to the gross-income tests. Whether interest can apply to someone who meets the $1,000 test but no gross-income test is not stated in any source found.
- The April 18 date. Resolved. ORS 316.577 uses the earlier of April 18 or the prior-year return due date. ORS 314.385(1)(a) sets that return due date as the federal return due date (source 16). The first declaration is therefore due on the federal return due date, normally April 15. This matches source 1.
- The prior-year return condition. Not resolved. ORS 316.587(8)(b) requires only a 12-month prior year and a filed return (source 9). OAR 150-316-0493(3)(a)(B) adds "filed before the current year's return". OAR 150-316-0495 and 150-316-0497, which apply only when filing status changes, require a return "filed timely including extensions". OAR 150-316-0497 also requires that both spouses' prior returns "have a tax liability". Each rule governs its own case. No source reconciles them.
- The January 31 provision. Resolved. ORS 316.579(7) treats the return as the declaration or its amendment. ORS 316.587 contains no January 31 waiver of interest (source 9). Interest depends on required installments under ORS 316.587(8), not on whether a declaration was filed. The DOR worksheets are consistent with this.
- The start-later schedule. Not resolved. ORS 316.587(8) defines the required installment as "the amount of the installment that would be due", which follows the ORS 316.579 schedule (source 9). That supports 3, 2 or 1 installments for a taxpayer first required after April 1. Form OR-10 divides by four except for part-year residents and annualizers (source 2). The statute should govern, but OR-10 does not show how to apply it.
- The annualized third test. Resolved. ORS 316.587(8)(c) makes it a per-installment measure (source 9). It changes installment amounts, not the annual figure.
- Interest after the return due date. Resolved. ORS 316.587(3)(a) ends estimated tax interest on the 15th day of the fourth month (source 9). Later interest falls under ORS 305.220 as a delinquency (source 10).
