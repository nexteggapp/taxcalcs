## 1. Who must make estimated payments

For tax year 2026 and later, you must pay estimated tax if you expect $1,000 or more of Iowa tax on income not subject to withholding. Through tax year 2025 the threshold was $200.

Married couples filing a joint Iowa return estimate together. Couples filing separately (filing status 3) each make their own payments under their own SSN.

- [ES-26]: "Every individual or married couple filing a joint Iowa return that expects to have a tax liability of $1,000 or more".
- [CODE-422.16] 12.a.(1): "if their Iowa income tax liability can reasonably be expected to amount to one thousand dollars or more for the year."
- [EST-PAGE]: "Taxpayers who estimate they will owe $200 ( $1,000 for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026) or more".
- [EST-PAGE]: "Spouses must make separate estimated payments if they file separate Iowa returns (status 3)."

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax.
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% if prior-year federal AGI plus Iowa net modifications exceeded $150,000, or $75,000 for married filing separate federal returns.
- High-income taxpayers do not lose the prior-year option. They use 110% of it.
- (corrected in verification) The income measured for the 110% test is worded differently. [2210-25] adds "Iowa net modifications reported on IA 1040, line 3". [ES-26] adds "bonus depreciation adjustment/section 179 from IA 1040 Schedule 1, line 9 and all other Iowa net income decoupling".
- 12-month year: the prior year must have covered 12 months, or would have, had a return been required. A filed prior-year return is not required by that wording.

Quotes:

- [2210-25]: "90% of the tax shown on the 2025 return; or".
- [2210-25]: "100% of the tax shown on the 2024 return if your 2024 federal AGI plus any Iowa net modifications ... is $150,000 or less".
- [2210-25]: "110% of the tax shown on the 2024 return if your 2024 federal AGI ... is greater than $150,000".
- [2210-25]: "(or greater than $75,000 for married filing separate federal returns)."
- [2210-25]: "Your 2024 return must have covered (or would have, had you been required to file) a period of 12 months."
- [ES-26]: "Pay 110% of the prior year's Iowa tax liability ... if prior year's federal adjusted gross income (AGI) ... exceeded $150,000".

## 3. Due dates and share due

There are four equal installments of 25% each, on dates different from federal. The 2026 dates are April 30, June 30 and September 30, 2026, and January 31, 2027. A date on a weekend or a holiday under Iowa Code 421.9A moves to the next business day.

If the requirement is first met later in the year, fewer installments are due. The first falls on the next due date.

No separate file-by-January-31 waiver was found. The fourth installment is itself due January 31.

(corrected in verification) January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. Under the weekend rule the fourth 2026 installment is due Monday, February 1, 2027. [ES-26] prints the unshifted date 01/31/2027.

(corrected in verification) The installment count when the requirement is first met later is in [2210-25]: requirement first met April 2 to June 1, three installments from June 30; June 2 to September 1, two from September 30; after September 1, one on January 31.

- [ES-26] line 17: "Multiply line 16 by 25% (.25). This is your quarterly estimated payment amount."
- [ES-26] payment schedule: "Installment 1 04/30/2026 Installment 2 06/30/2026 Installment 3 09/30/2026 Installment 4 01/31/2027".
- [ES-26]: "If a due date ends on a weekend or holiday as defined in Iowa Code section 421.9A, substitute the next regular business day".
- [CODE-422.16] 12.b.(1): "on or before the last day of the fourth month of the taxpayer's tax year".

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each installment date. You may instead use the actual withholding dates if you can establish them.

- [2210-25] line 20: "You may consider an equal part of the Iowa income tax withheld during the year as paid on each required installment date".
- [L35-25]: "Iowa tax withholding is credited equally in each installment period."

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: for 2025, no penalty if the balance after credits, or after withholding, is under $200. For 2026 the general threshold is $1,000 ([EST-PAGE], [CODE-422.16]). No 2026 IA 2210 is published yet.
  - [2210-25] line 6: "Subtract line 5 from line 3. If less than $200, stop. You do not owe a penalty."
- No liability in the prior year: no line for this exists on [2210-25]. [CODE-422.16] 12.d.(3) applies the federal exceptions. See item 10.
  - (corrected in verification) No separate line is needed. A 12-month prior year with $0 tax is entered as $0 on [2210-25] line 7, so line 8 is $0 and the form stops: "If less than or equal to line 5, stop. You do not owe IA 2210 penalty." [RULE-308.6](1)a confirms that a prior-year liability is not required.
- Low-income exemption from tax: the 2026 thresholds are $24,000 single or $32,000 other filing statuses for age 65 or older, and $9,000 or $13,500 under 65. [ES-26]: "Taxpayers 65 years of age or older: You are exempt if: (1) You are single and your Iowa taxable income is $24,000 or less".
- First-year and part-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate exception. The 12-month wording above, "or would have, had you been required to file", is the only related text.
- Nonresidents: two cases exempt the nonresident from estimates on that income.
  - Income included in a composite return or subject to an entity-level PTET election. [ES-26]: "the nonresident member is not required to pay estimated tax on their distributive share of income from that pass-through entity."
  - Wages from Iowa services. These are withheld instead. [EST-PAGE]: "Nonresidents who earn wages from services performed in Iowa do not make estimated payments to Iowa."
- Farmers and fishers: this applies if two-thirds of gross income comes from farming or fishing.
  - The current-year percentage is 66.7% ([2210F-25] line 4).
  - Pay once by January 15 and file by April 30, or file and pay in full by March 1. Use IA 2210F.
  - [ES-26]: "Pay the estimated tax in one payment on or before January 15, 2027 and file the Iowa income tax return by April 30, 2027".
  - [ES-26]: "File the Iowa income tax return and pay the tax due in full on or before March 1, 2027."
- Retired or disabled: may be waived if the person retired at 62 or later, or became disabled in the tax year or the preceding year, with reasonable cause. [2210-25]: "The penalty may also be waived if the taxpayer retired at age 62 or later, or became disabled in the tax year".
- Casualty or disaster: may be waived. [2210-25]: "may be waived if the underpayment was due to casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances."
- Rule text: [RULE-308.6](2)a: "The underpayment was due to casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances and imposition of the penalty would be against equity and good conscience."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

The method is allowed. Use the IA 2210 Schedule AI "Iowa Annualized Income Installment Method" (45-011) and check the box on IA 1040 line 35. You must then use the regular method, not the short method.

- [ES-26]: "Include IA 2210 Schedule AI Iowa Annualized Income Installment Method (45-011) with your IA 1040".
- [2210-25]: "You must use the regular method ... if you made any estimated payments late or if you choose to annualize your income".
- [RULE-308.6](1)b: "90 percent of the tax on the taxpayer's annualized income."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is a daily interest-like charge on each installment's shortfall. It runs from the due date to the payment date, ending no later than April 30 of the next year.

- The rate is the IDR interest rate under Iowa Code 421.7: the average prime rate for the preceding October to September, rounded to a whole percent, plus two points.
- The rate is 10% a year (0.027397% a day) for 2025 and for 2026. The 2026 rate took effect January 1, 2026 [RATE-26].

Compute it on Form IA 2210, or IA 2210F for farmers and fishers. An optional short method uses a factor of .0667122.

- [CODE-422.16] 12.d.(3): "the amount to be added ... is an amount determined at the rate in effect under section 421.7."
- [2210-25] line 24: "10% per annum = 0.027397% per day (01/01/26 - 12/31/26)".
- [RATE-26]: "Starting January 1, 2026, the interest rate for taxpayers with overdue payments will be: 10.0% annually 0.8% monthly 0.027397% daily".
- [RATE-26]: "Iowa law requires that this average be rounded to the nearest whole percent and two percentage points to be added to it."
- [L35-25]: "The IA 2210 penalty is calculated as a per day interest charge".

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- Due dates fall at month end: April 30, June 30, September 30 and January 31. Iowa's return is also due April 30.
- The 2026 threshold rose from $200 to $1,000.
- The farmer and fisher single payment is due January 15, which is earlier than the regular fourth installment of January 31.
- The 110% test adds Iowa net modifications to federal AGI.
- Spouses filing status 3, married filing separately on the Iowa return, must each make and claim their own estimates. [2210-25]: "Estimated payments are credited to only one individual."
- The IA 2210 starts from IA 1040 line 18. That line is before the line 19 school district and EMS surtax, so the surtax appears to be excluded from the penalty base. See item 10.

## 9. Local income taxes

These exist. The school district surtax and the county Emergency Medical Services (EMS) income surtax are computed on the state IA 1040, line 19, and administered by IDR. No separate local estimated payment system was found. The 2026 IA 1040ES worksheet has no surtax line.

- [L19-25]: "Counties may impose a countywide Emergency Medical Services (EMS) income surtax."
- [L19-25]: "Multiply the amount on line 18 by the surtax rate and enter the result."

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Prior-year exception: [2210-25] has no line for a no-prior-year-liability exception. [CODE-422.16] 12.d.(3) says "the exceptions in the Internal Revenue Code also apply", which would bring in the IRC 6654(e)(2) exception. [RULE-308.6](1)a says the old "must have had a tax liability" condition was eliminated for years after 1986: "the requirement that the return for the prior tax year must have had a tax liability is eliminated". Whether a prior year with $0 tax gives a required payment of $0 is not stated directly.
- Surtax in the base: [L35-25] says "you must include your Iowa income and lump-sum taxes when calculating the penalty". [2210-25] line 1 uses IA 1040 line 18, which excludes the line 19 surtax. Whether the surtax counts toward the 90% and 100% tests is not addressed.
- Current-year 90%: [ES-26] does not mention the 90% current-year option. It lists 100% or 110% of the prior year, or the worksheet estimate. [2210-25] and [RULE-308.6] use 90%. [EST-PAGE] lists only the prior-year and annualized tests.
- Farmer date: the farmer single-payment date of January 15 comes from the IRC. [CODE-422.16] 12.a.(2) says the IRC exceptions for farmers apply. It sits oddly with Iowa's January 31 fourth installment.
- 2026 penalty form: no 2026 IA 2210 is published. The $1,000 threshold for the penalty form is inferred from [EST-PAGE] and [CODE-422.16], not from a 2026 IA 2210.

### Conflicts resolved

- No-liability prior year. Resolved: a 12-month prior year with $0 tax gives a required annual payment of $0. [CODE-422.16] 12.d.(3) says "the exceptions in the Internal Revenue Code also apply". [RULE-308.6](1)a removed the liability condition and kept the 12-month condition: "the return must have covered a 12-month period". On [2210-25], $0 on line 7 makes line 8 $0, and the form stops.
- Current-year 90%. Resolved: the 90% test applies. [CODE-422.16] 12.d.(3) adopts the IRC 6654 method, which includes 90%. [2210-25] line 4 applies it: "Multiply line 3 by 90% (.90)." [ES-26], [EST-PAGE] and [RULE-308.6](1) leave it out, but they are guidance and an older rule. The statute was not amended on this point in 2024, 2025 or 2026.
- Surtax in the base. Partly resolved. [L35-25] names "Iowa income and lump-sum taxes" and not the surtax. [ES-26] lines 4 to 6 add the lump-sum tax into the estimate and have no surtax line. Whether IA 1040 line 18 includes the lump-sum tax was not checked. No source puts the line 19 school district or EMS surtax in the penalty base, and [2210-25] line 1 excludes it. No statute or rule addresses it.
- Farmer date. Not a conflict. [ES-26], [2210-25], [EST-PAGE] and [L35-25] all give January 15. [CODE-422.16] 12.a.(2) imports the IRC farmer rules, which use January 15.
- Threshold at exactly $1,000. Resolved: $1,000 is covered. [CODE-422.16] 12.a.(1) says "one thousand dollars or more". [ES-26] line 16 says "exceeds $1,000", but its own line 15 says "equals or exceeds $1,000".
- 2026 penalty form. Confirmed: [FORMS] lists no 2026 IA 2210. The 2026 penalty-form threshold is still inferred from [CODE-422.16].

### 2026 changes

- The $1,000 threshold, from 2025 Acts, ch 148, §59, applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 ([CODE-422.16] section history).
- The 2026 penalty rate stays at 10% a year, with 0.8% a month and 0.027397% a day ([RATE-26]).
- The 2026 worksheet uses a flat 3.8% rate. [ES-26] line 4: "Multiply line 3 by 3.8% (.038)".
- No 2026 session amendment was found. [CODE-422.16-CUR] has the same text and section history as the 2026 code edition, and the current Iowa Code 421.7 was last amended by 2022 Acts, ch 1061.
