## 1. Who must make estimated payments

The threshold is more than $500 of tax after credits, minus Ohio withholding.
The 2026 instructions use the same threshold for every filing status.
Source: IT 1040ES instructions, tax year 2026 (source 1).
Quote: "estimated Ohio tax liability (total tax minus total credits) less Ohio withholding is more than $500."

The statute sets the same $500 test.
Source: ORC 5747.09(B) (source 5).
Quote: "if the amount payable as estimated taxes, less the amount to be withheld from the taxpayer's compensation, is more than five hundred dollars."

Joint filers must file joint declarations under the statute.
Source: ORC 5747.09(B) (source 5).
Quote: "Taxpayers filing joint returns pursuant to section 5747.08 of the Revised Code shall file joint declarations of estimated taxes."

Since September 5, 2023, each payment posts to one spouse's account only.
Source: FAQ question 2 (source 9). (corrected in verification: the date is in the FAQ, not the 2026 instructions)
Quote: "As of September 5, 2023, Ohio individual and school district income tax payments are posted under one taxpayer's account."
Couples sure of a joint return may pay the combined amount under either spouse's account. (corrected in verification)
Source: FAQ question 2 (source 9).
Quote: "All payments posted to either account will be applied on the joint return filed."
Married taxpayers unsure of their filing status should pay separately.
Source: IT 1040ES instructions, tax year 2026 (source 1).
Quote: "Married taxpayers unsure of their filing status for 2026 should make separate estimated payments based on their individual estimated Ohio tax liabilities."

## 2. The required annual payment

Each required installment is 25% of the lesser of 90% of current-year tax and 100% of prior-year tax.
Source: ORC 5747.09(A)(4) (source 5).
Quote: "twenty-five per cent of the lesser of ... Ninety per cent of the tax liability for the taxable year; ... One hundred per cent"

The prior-year option needs a filed prior-year return and a 12-month prior year.
Source: ORC 5747.09(A)(4) (source 5).
Quote: "applies only if the taxpayer filed a return under section 5747.08 ... and if the preceding taxable year was a twelve-month taxable year."

The 2025 IT/SD 2210 and the FAQ require the prior-year return to be timely filed.
Source: IT/SD 2210, tax year 2025, page 2, line 6 (source 3).
Quote: "Did you timely file a 2024 Ohio income tax return? If yes, continue to line 7. If no, skip to line 8"

There is no higher prior-year percentage for high incomes.
The statute and the 2025 form name only 100% of prior-year tax.
No AGI threshold, no married-filing-separately variant, and no loss of the prior-year option were found.
Searched: ORC 5747.09, 2025 IT/SD 2210, 2026 IT 1040ES, FAQ (sources 1, 3, 5, 9).
Quote (2026 worksheet line 11): "Enter the lesser of line 9 or line 10 (If you did not file a 2025 IT 1040, enter the amount from line 10)"

## 3. Due dates and the share due by each

The 2026 due dates are April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027.
Source: IT 1040ES instructions, tax year 2026 (source 1).
Quote (one line of the list): "4th quarter - Jan. 15, 2027"

The statute states cumulative shares of current-year tax: 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%.
Source: ORC 5747.09(C)(1)-(4) (source 5).
Quote: "twenty-two and one-half per cent ... forty-five per cent ... sixty-seven and one-half per cent ... ninety per cent of the tax liability"

The penalty form uses equal quarters: 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of the required annual payment.
These equal the statutory shares when the 90% current-year basis applies.
Source: IT/SD 2210, tax year 2025, page 2, Part II column headings (source 3).
Quote: "4/15/25 – 25%  6/16/25 – 50%  9/15/25 – 75%  1/15/26 – 100%"

Weekend and holiday due dates move to the next business day.
The 2025 form shows June 16, 2025, because June 15, 2025 was a Sunday.
Source: IT/SD 2210 instructions, tax year 2025, page 4 (source 3).
Quote: "If a due date falls on a weekend or holiday, the payment is due the next business day."

There is no general waiver of the January payment for filing early.
Only farmers and fishermen have one (see answer 5).
Searched: ORC 5747.09, 2025 IT/SD 2210, 2026 IT 1040ES, FAQ, 2025 IT 1040 booklet (sources 1, 3, 4, 5, 9).

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in equal amounts on each due date.
The taxpayer may instead use the actual withholding dates by establishing all of them.
Source: ORC 5747.09(B) (source 5).
Quote: "considered as paid in equal amounts on each payment date unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld"

The 2025 form only implements the equal-amounts method.
Source: IT/SD 2210, tax year 2025, page 2, line 11 (source 3).
Quote: "Multiply the total tax withheld from compensation by the percentage indicated at the top of each column"

How to claim the actual-dates method on the form: NOT FOUND.
Searched: 2025 IT/SD 2210 and its instructions, FAQ, 2025 IT 1040 booklet (sources 3, 4, 9).

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

Tax below an amount: no penalty if current-year tax minus withholding is $500 or less.
Source: IT/SD 2210, tax year 2025, page 2, line 8 (source 3).
Quote: "Is line 2 less any withholding $500 or less? If yes, STOP, you have no interest penalty."

No liability in the prior year: no such exception exists.
A first-time filer also cannot use the prior-year safe harbour.
Source: FAQ question 7 (source 9).
Quote: "Ohio law does not contain a specific safe harbor for first time filers."

First-year residents: no exception.
New residents should start paying by the next due date after moving in.
Source: FAQ question 7 (source 9).
Quote: "New residents who have income not subject to withholding should begin making estimated payments by the next due date after moving into Ohio."
The statute sets the first declaration date for a new taxpayer.
Source: ORC 5747.09(B) (source 5).
Quote: "on or before the fifteenth day of the fourth month after the taxpayer becomes subject to tax for the first time."

Part-year residents: NOT FOUND.
No part-year rule appears in ORC 5747.09, the 2025 IT/SD 2210, the FAQ or the 2025 booklet (sources 3, 4, 5, 9).

Farmers and fishermen: no percentage is stated.
They qualify if at least two-thirds of estimated gross income is from farming or fishing.
They must use the same method they use federally.
They owe no estimates if they file and pay in full by the first day of the third month after year end (March 1).
Otherwise they may pay the estimated tax in full by the 15th day of the first month after year end and file on time.
Source: Ohio Adm. Code 5703-7-04(B) and (C) (source 7).
Quote: "on or before the first day of the third month following the close of the taxpayer's taxable year, both files ... and makes payment in full"
The 2025 form gives the dates as March 1, 2026, or January 1, 2026 with filing by April 15, 2026 (see answer 10).
Source: IT/SD 2210 instructions, tax year 2025, page 4 (source 3).
Quote: "You make payment in full of the tax due on or before January 1, 2026 and you file your return on or before April 15, 2026."
The rule's January 15 date governs over the form's January 1 date (corrected in verification; see the Verification section).

Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND.
No such waiver appears in ORC 5747.09, the 2025 IT/SD 2210, the FAQ or the 2025 booklet (sources 3, 4, 5, 9).

Casualty or disaster waivers: no standing rule found.
The Tax Commissioner may abate the penalty in whole or in part.
Source: ORC 5747.09(D) (source 5).
Quote: "The tax commissioner may abate, in whole or in part, the interest penalty imposed under division (D) of this section."
This abatement power is new. (corrected in verification)
HB 96 added it, effective September 30, 2025, for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025.
Source: HB 96 as enrolled, Section 801.40 (legislature.ohio.gov, search-prod.lis.state.oh.us/api/v2/general_assembly_136/legislation/hb96/07_EN/html/).
Quote: "The amendment by this act of section 5747.09 ... applies to taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025."
Ohio has granted ad hoc relief, for example in 2020.
Source: Department news release, March 27, 2020 (source 17).
Quote: "have also been granted additional time to file and pay without penalty or interest."

Active-duty servicemembers with an IRS extension do not owe the penalty for the extension period.
Source: 2025 IT 1040 booklet, military section (source 4).
Quote: "These servicemembers do not owe interest, penalties, or the interest penalty in connection with this extension period."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

The statute allows it as reasonable cause.
Source: ORC 5747.09(E)(1) (source 5).
Quote: "determined by annualizing the income received during the year up to the end of the month immediately preceding the month in which the payment is due"

The form for it was withdrawn from tax year 2017.
The 2025 IT/SD 2210 has no annualisation schedule.
Source: Ohio Tax Alert, Updates to Ohio Form IT/SD 2210 (source 13).
Quote: "The long-form used for the annualized calculation of the interest penalty is no longer available."

How a taxpayer claims annualisation now: NOT FOUND.
Searched: 2025 IT/SD 2210, FAQ, 2025 IT 1040 booklet (sources 3, 4, 9).

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is interest on each installment's underpayment for the period it is late.
It is not a flat percentage.
Source: ORC 5747.09(D) (source 5).
Quote: "at the rate per annum prescribed by section 5703.47 of the Revised Code upon the amount of underpayment for the period of underpayment"

The penalty is discretionary from tax year 2025. (corrected in verification)
HB 96 changed "shall be added" to "may be added".
Source: ORC 5747.09(D), September 30, 2025 version, compared with the September 28, 2018 version (source 5).
Quote (current): "an interest penalty may be added to the taxes for the tax year"

The rate is the federal short-term rate for July, rounded to a whole percent, plus 3 points.
It is set on October 15 for the following calendar year.
Source: ORC 5703.47(B) (source 6).
Quote: "rounded to the nearest whole number per cent, plus three per cent, shall be the interest rate per annum"

The rate is 7% for calendar year 2026.
Source: Administrative Journal Entry dated October 15, 2025 (source 12).
Quote: "the interest rate prescribed by R.C. 5703.47 for calendar year 2026 is seven percent (7%)."
The rate was 8% for 2025.
Source: IT/SD 2210 instructions, tax year 2025, page 4 (source 3).
Quote: "based on the statutory interest rate (8% for 2025 and 7% for 2026) and the number of days the estimated payment was late."
The rate for calendar year 2027 is not yet published. (corrected in verification)
The 2026 penalty needs it, because the fourth-quarter period runs from January 15 to April 15, 2027.
The department's interest rate page lists rates only through 2026 as of 2026-10-20 (source 11).
No 2027 journal entry was found at the URL pattern of the 2026 one, and no 2026 IT/SD 2210 is posted yet.

The daily formula divides by 365.25.
Source: IT/SD 2210 instructions, tax year 2025, page 4 (source 3).
Quote: "Rate = interest rate X number of days the payment is late ÷ 365.25"

The form applies a preset rate factor to the cumulative underpayment at each due date.
The 2025 factors are 0.013580, 0.019932, 0.026311 and 0.017248.
Source: IT/SD 2210, tax year 2025, page 2, line 15 (source 3).
My arithmetic, not stated on the form: each factor covers the days from that due date to the next one, and the last runs to April 15.
For example, 0.08 × 62 ÷ 365.25 = 0.013580 (April 15 to June 16, 2025).
The third factor splits at January 1: 107 days at 8% plus 15 days at 7%.

The form is IT/SD 2210, Part II.
Source: FAQ question 6 (source 9).
Quote: "Part II of form IT/SD 2210 is used to calculate the interest penalty."

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is $500, not $1,000 (sources 1, 5).
- There is no 110% prior-year rule for high incomes (sources 3, 5).
- The prior-year safe harbour needs a timely filed prior-year return, per the form (source 3).
- There is no exception for having no prior-year liability (source 9).
- There is no waiver of the January installment for filing early, except for farmers and fishermen (sources 3, 7).
- There is no retirement or disability waiver (NOT FOUND in sources 3, 4, 5, 9).
- The annualised method is in the statute but has no current form (sources 5, 13).
- The rate is fixed for a calendar year, not reset each quarter (source 6).
- The daily rate divides by 365.25 (source 3).
- The form charges interest up to April 15 in fixed periods, not to each actual payment date (source 3; see answer 10).
- The penalty can be computed on combined state and school district tax (source 3; see answer 9).
- One form, IT/SD 2210, covers individuals, school district filers, trusts and pass-through entities (source 3).
- From tax year 2025 the penalty is discretionary and the commissioner may abate it (source 5; HB 96). (corrected in verification)

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

School district income tax has its own estimates on the SD 100ES worksheet.
The Department of Taxation administers it.
The threshold and due dates match the state tax.
Source: SD 100ES instructions, tax year 2026 (source 14).
Quote: "estimated school district tax liability (total tax minus total credits) less school district withholding is more than $500."

The IT/SD 2210 covers the school district tax too, and may be computed on a combined basis.
Source: IT/SD 2210 instructions, tax year 2025, page 4 (source 3).
Quote: "complete page 2 using your combined liability for individual and school district income tax and your total taxes paid"

Municipal income taxes have their own estimated payments, with a $200 threshold in statute.
Source: ORC 718.08(B)(1) (source 16).
Quote: "every taxpayer shall make a declaration of estimated taxes ... if the amount payable as estimated taxes is at least two hundred dollars."

Each city or a third-party administrator runs the municipal tax.
RITA and CCA are such administrators, but no state page naming them was found.
Businesses may opt in to state administration of the municipal net profit tax.
Source: Department of Taxation, Municipal Net Profit Tax page (source 15).
Quote: "administered by each municipal corporation that imposes a tax or by a third-party administrator working on behalf of the municipal corporation."

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

1. Farmers' early-payment date.
   The rule says the 15th day of the first month after year end (January 15).
   The 2025 form says January 1, 2026.
   Rule quote (source 7): "on or before the fifteenth day of the first month following the close of the taxpayer's taxable year"
   Form quote (source 3): "payment in full of the tax due on or before January 1, 2026"
2. Farmers' income test.
   The rule tests estimated gross income and requires the federal method.
   The form says "receive at least 2/3 of your gross income" and omits the federal-method condition (sources 3, 7).
3. "Timely" prior-year return.
   The statute requires only a filed return.
   The form and FAQ require a timely filed one (sources 3, 5, 9).
4. End of the penalty period.
   The statute runs it "to the date on which the payment is made" (source 5).
   The FAQ says "until the later of when the installment is paid or the return is due" (source 9).
   The form uses fixed periods ending April 15 (source 3).
5. Withholding dates.
   The statute allows actual withholding dates.
   The form allows only equal amounts (sources 3, 5).
6. Annualisation.
   The statute allows it, but no current form supports it (sources 5, 13).
7. Penalty abatement.
   The statute lets the commissioner abate the penalty (source 5).
   A 2025 department news item says it "is required by law and cannot be removed." (source 10).
8. Joint payments.
   The statute requires joint declarations (source 5).
   The estimated payments page says joint filers "make joint estimated payments" (source 8).
   The 2026 instructions say payments post to one SSN and unsure couples should pay separately (source 1).
9. Statute and form percentages.
   The statute states 22.5/45/67.5/90% of current-year tax but defines each installment as 25% of the lesser amount.
   The form uses 25/50/75/100% of the lesser amount (sources 3, 5).
   The two agree on the current-year basis; the statute's (C) shares do not mention the prior-year basis.
10. Fiscal-year filers must adjust the form's dates and rates themselves (source 3).

### Conflicts resolved

1. Farmers' early-payment date. The rule governs: January 15 (the 15th day of the first month after year end).
   OAC 5703-7-04 is a binding rule, effective November 28, 2024.
   The 2025 form is later but reflects no newer law.
   FAQ question 8 supports the rule. Quote: "their estimated payment due date and return due date is the same for Ohio income tax purposes."
2. Farmers' income test. The rule governs: two-thirds of estimated gross income, plus use of the federal method.
   The form is a short summary of the same rule.
3. "Timely" prior-year return. Not resolved.
   ORC 5747.09(A)(4) and (E)(2) require only that a return was filed. Quote: "the taxpayer filed a return under section 5747.08 of the Revised Code for that year."
   The form (line 6) and FAQ questions 5 and 7 require a timely filed return.
   No rule, information release or 2024-2026 amendment resolves this.
   The conflict changes the result for a taxpayer whose prior-year return was filed late.
4. End of the penalty period. Not resolved.
   ORC 5747.09(D) ends the period on the payment date.
   FAQ question 6 ends it at the later of the payment date and the return due date.
   The form credits a payment only at the next column date, and its last period ends April 15.
   The form is what the department computes.
   The statute would charge less when a late payment arrives between due dates.
5. Withholding dates. The statute governs and allows actual dates.
   The form has no line for them. How to claim them is NOT FOUND.
6. Annualisation. The statute governs and allows it as reasonable cause (ORC 5747.09(E)(1)).
   No current form or instruction supports it. How to claim it is NOT FOUND.
7. Penalty abatement. Resolved: the statute governs.
   HB 96 made the penalty discretionary and added the abatement power, for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025 (V2, Section 801.40).
   The news item (source 10) is dated May 7, 2025, before the amendment took effect on September 30, 2025.
   It describes the prior law, under which the penalty "shall be added" (V1).
8. Joint payments. Resolved: no conflict.
   FAQ question 2 says joint filers may pay the combined amount under either spouse's account, and all of it applies to the joint return.
   Payments are not split between spouses who later file separately (source 1).
9. Statute and form percentages. Resolved: no conflict for the penalty.
   The penalty uses the "required installment" of ORC 5747.09(D), which is 25% of the lesser amount.
   The (C) shares equal 25/50/75/100% of the 90% current-year amount.
10. Fiscal-year filers. Not a conflict. Source 3 states the rule.
11. Cumulative or per-quarter underpayment (found in verification). Not resolved by text.
    ORC 5747.09(D)(1)-(4) define each underpayment as "the required installment less the amount of taxes paid by the date prescribed for that payment".
    Read literally, that compares one 25% installment with all payments to date.
    The form compares cumulative required amounts (25/50/75/100%) with cumulative payments.
    The statute's next sentence applies a payment to an earlier underpayment only after the current installment is covered.
    The form is the department's reading.
12. 2026 worksheet tax table (found in verification). The statute governs.
    Both copies of the 2026 IT 1040ES worksheet (sources 1 and 2) show the 2025 brackets, with 3.125% over $100,000.
    ORC 5747.02(A)(3)(c) sets a single 2026 bracket (V4). Quote: "For taxable years beginning in 2026 and thereafter, $332.00 plus 2.75% of the amount in excess of $26,050."
    The worksheet overstates estimated 2026 tax above $26,050. It does not change the penalty rules.

### 2026 changes

- HB 96 amended ORC 5747.09, effective September 30, 2025, for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025.
  The penalty "may be added" instead of "shall be added".
  The commissioner may abate it in whole or in part.
  No other wording in 5747.09 changed (V1 compared with source 5).
  Thresholds, percentages and due dates are unchanged.
- No later bill amending ORC 5747.09 has been enacted.
  HB 889 and SB 22 mention ORC 5747.09 but are only introduced.
- The 2026 interest rate is 7% (source 12; source 11).

  It sets the fourth-quarter factor and part of the third-quarter factor for tax year 2026.
