# District of Columbia: individual estimated income tax and underpayment interest

DC calls the charge "underpayment interest", not a penalty.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

A DC resident or domiciliary who expects to owe more than $100 of DC tax on income not covered by withholding. There is no difference by filing status. Spouses or registered domestic partners may file joint vouchers, but not if separated under a decree or if they have different taxable years or periods of residency.

- [ES26], tax year 2026: "required to file a District of Columbia (DC) income tax return and, you expect to owe $100 or more in taxes."
- [DCC-1812.08] § 47-1812.08(i)(1): "if the person can reasonably be expected to receive gross income not subject to the withholding provisions of this section that will result in a tax liability of more than $100."
- [OTR-WEB]: "You live in DC and your gross income isn't fully withheld, and You expect your DC tax liability will be greater than $100.00."
- The statute applies only to persons "residing or domiciled in the District". It excludes certain federal elected officials, their staff and presidential appointees unless domiciled in DC.
- Joint vouchers, [ES26]: "No joint declaration may be made if spouses have separated under a decree of divorce or separate maintenance or if they have different taxable years, or periods of residency."

Whether the threshold is exactly $100 is unresolved. See item 10.

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 110% of last year's tax for everyone, at every income level. There is no 100% tier and no AGI threshold.
  - The statute's 100% wording applies only to tax years before 2012.
- Requirements for the prior-year option:
  - [DCC-4203] requires a filed prior-year return covering 12 months.
  - The forms instead require full-year DC residence in the prior year.
  - [D2210] says that if no 2024 DC return was filed, use only the 90% amount.
- High-income taxpayers keep the 110% option. No source removes it.

Quotes:

- [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(b)(1)(B)(ii)(II): "For tax years beginning after December 31, 2011, 110% of the tax shown on the return of the individual for the preceding taxable year if the individual filed a return for the preceding taxable year consisting of 12 months".
- [ES26], tax year 2026: "At least 90% of your 2026 income tax; or ... 110% of your 2025 DC income tax for a 12-month period."
- [D2210], tax year 2025: "You must have been a DC resident during all of 2024 to use the 110% exception."
- [D2210] line 5: "If you did not file a DC return for 2024, use only the Line 2 amount."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

There are four equal installments, each 25%, due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. For tax year 2026 the dates are April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027.

- [ES26]: "Voucher 1: April 15, 2026; Voucher 2: June 15, 2026; Voucher 3: September 15, 2026; and Voucher 4: January 15, 2027".
- [D2210] line 6: "Multiply Line 5 amount by 25% (.25) for the amount required for each periodic payment."
- [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(b)(1)(B): "Twenty-five percent of the lesser of".

Late start (added in verification): the statute moves the first declaration later when the duty first arises mid-year. [DCC-1812.08] § 47-1812.08(i)(4): "if the requirements of paragraph (1) of this subsection are first met: (A) after April 1st and before June 2nd". The declaration is then due June 15, September 15 or January 15. No source says how the required installments are re-sized in that case.

Weekend and holiday shifting applies. [ES26]: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the voucher is due the next business day." Whether "legal holiday" means DC holidays or federal ones is NOT FOUND. DC Emancipation Day (April 16) has historically moved the April return date, but no source here says so for estimates.

January payment: if the return is filed by January 15, the fourth voucher is not sent. [ES26]: "If you file your DC individual tax return on or before January 15, you must pay the balance of any tax due with your return before you make your last estimated tax payment". It adds: "Do not send in your last voucher." [DCC-1812.08] § 47-1812.08(i)(6) treats a return filed and paid in full by January 15 as the declaration or its amendment. Unlike the federal rule, this is not stated as a waiver of interest on the fourth installment.

## 4. How withholding is treated

By statute, withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each due date. The taxpayer may instead establish the actual dates withheld, but then all withholding must be dated that way.

- [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(d)(3): "An equal part of such amount shall be deemed paid on each due date for the payment of estimated tax for the taxable year".
- It continues: "unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld, in which case the amounts withheld shall be deemed payments of estimated tax on the dates on which such amounts were actually withheld."

[D2210] line 8 asks for "DC withholding and estimated tax paid each period" on a cumulative basis. It does not say whether withholding is entered evenly or by actual date. See item 10.

## 5. Exceptions with no interest

- Tax below an amount: tax after credits, withholding and timely estimated payments is less than $100. [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(e)(1): "reduced by applicable credits and payments of estimated tax that are timely made, is less than $ 100".
  - (corrected in verification) The form drops the timeliness condition. [D2210]: "The tax due for 2025 minus income tax withheld and/or estimated tax payments is less than $100". [OTR-WEB] says "Your current year's tax liability is less than $100.00". See item 10.
- No liability in the prior year. [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(e)(2): "The individual did not have any liability for tax for the preceding taxable year".
  - [D2210] adds a full-year residence condition: "You had no DC income tax liability for the tax year 2024 and in that year, you lived in DC the entire 12 months".
- A 10% rule appears on the form only. [D2210]: "Your remaining tax due after totaling all credits, estimated tax payments and withholding, is less than 10% of your total DC tax liability for the year." See item 10.
- First-year and part-year residents: no explicit exception. NOT FOUND in [ES26], [D2210], [D40-25] and [DCC-4203].
  - Someone who was not a full-year resident last year cannot use the 110% option or the no-liability exception on the form. They must meet 90% of the current year.
  - Joint vouchers are barred for spouses with different "periods of residency" ([ES26]).
  - Annualization for a part-year return is restricted by statute. See item 6.
- Farmers and fishermen: no special rule. NOT FOUND in [ES26], [D2210], [DCC-4203] or [DCC-1812.08].
- Retired or disabled: excepted if the Mayor determines the taxpayer retired after 62 or developed a disability in the year or the year before, and the underpayment was due to reasonable cause. [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(e)(3): "The taxpayer retired after having attained 62 years of age or developed a disability". This exception is in the statute but not on [D2210].
- Casualty or disaster. [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(e)(4): "by reason of casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances, the imposition of the addition to tax would be against equity and good conscience". This is not on [D2210].
- Death. [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(e)(5): "The taxpayer dies during the taxable year." This is not on [D2210].

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed. The statute and the form describe it differently.

- Statute ([DCC-4203] § 47-4203(b)(2)):
  - Cumulative 22.5%, 45% and 67.5% of tax on income annualized for the first 3, 5 and 8 months, then 90% at the fourth installment.
  - "The annualized income method shall not apply to individuals filing a return for part of a taxable year except under regulations as the Mayor may prescribe."
- Form ([D2210] line 7, with a checkbox "Check here if you are using the 'Annualized Income' method"):
  - A simpler proration: "determine the percentage for each period (divide the period income by the full year's income). Multiply Line 5 by each period's percentage".
  - No separate schedule exists.

## 7. How the interest is computed

It is interest on each installment's shortfall for the period it is unpaid, not a flat percentage.

- The rate is the general underpayment rate. By statute it has been 10% per year, compounded daily, since January 1, 2003. It is fixed in statute, not indexed.
- The period runs from the due date to the earlier of payment or April 15 of the next year (the 15th day of the 4th month).
- Statutory rounding: an underpayment unpaid for part of a month is treated as paid at the end of that month.
- The form is D-2210. It applies fixed per-period factors of .0175, .0265, .0351 and .0259 to cumulative underpayments.
- If the taxpayer does not file D-2210, OTR computes the interest automatically.

Quotes:

- [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(c)(1): "an amount of interest determined by applying the underpayment rate set forth in § 47-4201 to the amount of the underpayment for the period of the underpayment."
- [DCC-4201] § 47-4201(d)(2): "10% per year, compounded daily, beginning January 1, 2003."
- [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(c)(2)(B): "provided, that an underpayment that is unpaid during part of a month shall be considered to be paid at the end of the month."
- [ES26], tax year 2026: "underpayment interest on the unpaid amount of tax at the rate of 10% per year compounded daily".
- [D2210] line 10: "Underpayment interest factors .0175 .0265 .0351 .0259". The instructions say only "These are the underpayment interest factors by period."
- [D2210]: "If you do not wish to calculate the interest, the Office of Tax and Revenue (OTR) will calculate it when your return is processed".

My inference, not a source statement (corrected in verification): each factor equals the effective annual rate of 10% compounded daily (10.5156%) times the days to the next due date over 365, truncated to four decimals. For tax year 2025 the periods are 61, 92, 122 and 90 days, giving .0175, .0265, .0351 and .0259 exactly. True daily compounding over those days would give .0169, .0255, .0340 and .0250. The form also ignores the statute's month-end rounding and ends each period at the next due date, not the payment date. See item 10.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The threshold is $100, not $1,000.
- The prior-year option is always 110%. There is no 100% tier, even at low incomes.
- The prior-year option and the no-liability exception need full-year DC residence in the prior year. That is the form's wording; the statute says a 12-month return.
- The charge is interest at a fixed 10% compounded daily, not the federal quarterly rate. The statute rounds partial months up to month end.
- The form's annualized method is a simple income proration, not the federal Schedule AI computation.
- The form allows a "less than 10% remaining" exception that federal law does not have.
- The retirement, disability, casualty and death exceptions exist only in the statute. They are not on the form.
- Payments over $5,000 for a period must be electronic. [D40-25]: "If the amount of the payment due for a period exceeds $5,000, you must pay electronically". This is from the tax year 2025 booklet. It is not found in [ES26].

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None. DC is a single taxing jurisdiction with no separate city, county or school-district income tax. No source above mentions any local income tax.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements between sources

- **Threshold at exactly $100.** [ES26] says "$100 or more". The statute says "more than $100" for the filing duty. [DCC-4203] excuses tax "less than $100".
- **The 10% exception.** [D2210] excuses remaining tax "less than 10% of your total DC tax liability for the year", which is not in [DCC-4203]. It seems to restate the 90% test on an annual basis, ignoring installment timing. [D2210] also says interest is computed per period, so the two statements conflict when payments were late but the annual total was met.
- **Prior-year condition.**
  - [DCC-4203] requires a filed 12-month prior-year return.
  - [D2210] and [OTR-WEB] require full-year DC residence in the prior year for the 110% option.
  - [DCC-4203] § 47-4203(e)(2), the no-liability exception, has no 12-month or residence condition. [D2210] adds one. (corrected in verification) [OTR-WEB] does not: "There was no DC tax liability for the preceding tax year".
- **The 110% item in the exception list.** [D2210] lists "Your DC estimated tax payments plus any DC income tax withheld equals at least 110% of your 2024 DC income tax liability". It is not stated as timely per installment.
- **Annualized method.** The form's proration method differs from the statute's 22.5/45/67.5/90% annualization.
- **Withholding timing on D-2210.** Not stated. The statute's default is even spreading with an all-or-nothing actual-dates election.
- **Interest factors.** [D2210] gives fixed factors with no derivation. (corrected in verification) They match 10.5156% × days/365, not daily compounding and not month-end rounding. See item 7. NOT FOUND: an official explanation.
- **"Legal holiday" for the weekend rule.** It is not defined whether this means DC or federal holidays.
- **January 15 filing.** The rule is stated as an instruction ("Do not send in your last voucher"). No source says whether interest on an earlier shortfall is waived.

### Conflicts resolved

None of §§ 47-4201, 47-4203 or 47-1812.08(i) was amended in substance in 2024, 2025 or 2026. The statute therefore governs over the forms in each conflict below.

- **Threshold at $100.** The duty to declare arises above $100 (§ 47-1812.08(i)(1)). [ES26] and [D40-25] say "$100 or more", which is wrong at exactly $100. The interest exception needs a balance under $100 after timely payments (§ 47-4203(e)(1)). A balance of exactly $100 is not excused.
- **$100 exception timing.** The statute counts only timely payments. [D2210] and [OTR-WEB] omit that. The statute governs.
- **10% exception.** It is not in § 47-4203. [OTR-WEB] lists a 10% safe harbor for businesses only, and § 47-4204 does not contain one either. It has no statutory basis for individuals. Whether OTR applies it in practice is unresolved.
- **Prior-year condition.** The statute requires a filed 12-month prior-year return for the 110% option and nothing for the no-liability exception. The forms add full-year residence. The statute governs. Unresolved: whether a part-year resident's prior return counts as "consisting of 12 months".
- **110% item in the form's exception list.** The statute applies it per installment at 25% each (§ 47-4203(b)(1)(B)). The form's own lines 6 to 9 do the same. Resolved as per installment.
- **Annualized method.** The statute's 22.5/45/67.5/90% schedule governs. No regulation authorizing the form's proration was found. Unresolved, and it changes the required amounts.
- **Withholding timing.** Resolved by § 47-4203(d)(3): equal parts, unless actual dates are shown for all withholding.
- **Interest factors.** The statute requires 10% compounded daily over the actual underpayment period, with month-end rounding. The form's factors do neither (item 7). The two methods give different amounts. How OTR computes the automatic assessment is not published. Unresolved.
- **January 15 filing.** § 47-1812.08(i)(6) makes the return the declaration. § 47-4203(e) has no waiver for earlier shortfalls. Resolved: interest on earlier installments still applies.
