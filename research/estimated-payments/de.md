## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Anyone, resident or nonresident, whose Delaware tax after withholding and credits is expected to exceed $800. There is no difference by filing status. Spouses filing jointly or combined (filing status 2 or 4) may file a joint declaration. Spouses filing separately on separate forms (filing status 3) should file separate declarations.

- [PIT-EST-I26], tax year 2026: "shall make a declaration of estimated tax for the taxable year if the tax due in excess of withholding can reasonably be expected to exceed $800.00."
- [PIT-RES-I25], tax year 2025 instructions about 2026: "the Delaware tax liability less Delaware withholding and credits can reasonably be expected to exceed $800".
- [DC-1169] § 1169(a): "if the estimated tax can reasonably be expected to exceed $800."
- Joint declarations, [PIT-EST-I26]: "spouses who expect to file a joint tax return or a combined tax return (filing status 2 or filing status 4)".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax. Farmers and fishermen use 66 2/3%.
- Prior year: 100% of the prior year's tax.
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% if prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000, or $75,000 for married filing separately (filing status 3).
- High-income taxpayers keep the prior-year option at 110%. No source removes it.
- 12-month requirement (corrected in verification): [PIT-RES-I25] makes its exceptions conditional on the current-year (2025) return covering 12 full months, not the prior-year return. The "no liability last year" exception in [PIT-EST-I26] instead needs the preceding year to be a twelve-month period. [PIT-UND-I25] states no 12-month condition at all. No source requires the prior-year return to have been filed.

Quotes:

- [PIT-EST-I26], tax year 2026: "(a) is at least 90 percent (66 2/3% for farmers and fishermen) of the amount due on the income tax return for the taxable year, or (b) 100% of the tax shown on the prior year's return".
- [PIT-EST-I26]: "(110% if the federal adjusted gross income for the previous tax year is in excess of $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separate))".
- [PIT-UND-I25], tax year 2025: "110% if your 2024 Delaware AGI is more than $150,000 or $75,000 if Married Filing Separate Returns, Filing Status 3".
- [PIT-UND-I25] says the 110% rule applies only "If you are not a farmer or fisherman".
- [PIT-RES-I25], tax year 2025: "You will not owe the penalty if your 2025 tax return was for a period of 12 full months AND ONE of the following applies".

Federal versus Delaware AGI for the 110% test is unresolved. See item 10.

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

There are four equal installments, each 25% of the required annual payment. The due dates are April 30, June 15, September 15 and January 15. The first date is April 30, not April 15, and matches Delaware's April 30 return due date.

- [PIT-EST-I26], tax year 2026: "in equal installments on or before April 30, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the following year."
- [PIT-RES-I25], tax year 2025, lists the 2026 periods. Jan 1–Mar 31 is due April 30, 2026. Apr 1–May 31 is due June 15, 2026. Jun 1–Aug 31 is due September 15, 2026. Sep 1–Dec 31 is due January 15, 2027.
- [PIT-UND-I25] Part 1, line C: "This is your required annual payment, which should have been made in four equal payments." Line 23: "Enter 1/4th of your Required Annual Payment".

Weekend and holiday shifting is set by Delaware's own statute, not the federal rule. [DC-551] § 551(d): an act due on a "Saturday, Sunday or a legal holiday in this State" is timely "on the next succeeding day". The 2025 form follows it. [PIT-UND-F25] line 39 lists the 2025 due dates as "5/1/25 6/16/25 9/15/25 1/15/26", with June 16 because June 15, 2025 was a Sunday. The 2024 form did not follow it (corrected in verification). [PIT-UND-F24] line 39 lists "6/15/24" although June 15, 2024 was a Saturday. For tax year 2026 the question does not arise: April 30, June 15 and September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027 all fall on weekdays.

The statute ties dates to federal dates but lets the agency set others. [DC-1169] § 1170(a): "on or before the dates prescribed by the laws of the United States ... except that the State Tax Commissioner may establish other dates".

January waiver: none for general taxpayers. NOT FOUND in [PIT-EST-I26], [PIT-UND-I25] or [PIT-RES-I25]. Farmers and fishermen have their own January 15 / March 1 rule (item 5).

Late starters: [PIT-EST-I26] item 7 lets a taxpayer who first becomes required after April 1 file by June 15, September 15 or January 15. The estimate is then paid "in equal installments on the remaining payment dates."

## 4. How withholding is treated

The two penalty methods treat withholding differently, and neither offers an election.

- Short method: withholding is subtracted as one annual total, so in effect it is spread evenly. [PIT-UND-I25] line G: "Enter the total amount of Delaware tax withheld."
- Annualized method (Part 3/4): withholding is counted in the period in which it was actually withheld. [PIT-UND-I25] line 30: "FOR EACH PERIOD ONLY. Enter the amount of Delaware withholding. In Column 1, enter the withholding for the period January 1st through March 31st."
- S corporation payments and refundable business credits are spread evenly. [PIT-UND-I25] line 29: "Apply S Corp payment and Refundable Business Credits evenly to each quarter by dividing by four (4)".

An election to treat withholding as paid on the actual dates outside the annualized method (like federal Form 2210 Part IV): NOT FOUND in [PIT-UND-I25].

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if the required annual payment minus withholding is $800 or less. [PIT-UND-F25] line E: "If $800 or less, stop here. You do not owe the penalty."
- No liability in the prior year, provided it was a 12-month year. [PIT-EST-I26]: "Payment of estimated tax is not required if there was no tax liability for the preceding year, provided such year was a twelve-month period."
- First-year residents: no separate rule found. NOT FOUND in [PIT-UND-I25], [PIT-EST-I26] and [PIT-RES-I25]. The part-year exception below would cover a resident's first year if they moved in during it.
- Part-year residents are fully excepted. [PIT-UND-I25], tax year 2025: "You were a Part-Year resident during 2025".
- Farmers and fishermen (at least two-thirds of gross income from farming or fishing):
  - Pay the whole estimated tax by January 15, or file and pay in full by March 1.
  - Their current-year percentage is 66 2/3%.
  - [PIT-UND-I25]: "paid the total estimated tax due by January 15, 2026, or filed your return by March 1, 2026 AND paid the total tax due at that time."
  - [PIT-EST-I26] item 5 says a farmer may file the declaration by January 15 and the return by April 30, or file and pay in full by March 1.
- Retired or disabled: a waiver on request after assessment, not an automatic exception. [PIT-UND-I25], reasons for a waiver: "In 2024 or 2025 you retired after age 62 or became disabled."
- Casualty or disaster: waivable on request after assessment. [PIT-EST-I26] item 11: "may be waived if the underpayment is due to casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances". [PIT-UND-I25] says waivers are requested in writing to the Tax Conferee, P.O. Box 8714. Taxpayers claiming a waiver should not attach PIT-UND: "You will be given a chance to request a waiver after the penalty has been assessed."

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed, using Form PIT-UND Part 3 ("Annualized Installment Method"). The taxpayer checks the box "Taxpayer is Using the Annualization of Income Method".

- The periods end 3/31, 5/31, 8/31 and 12/31.
- The annualization multipliers are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1.
- The cumulative percentage multipliers are .225, .450, .675 and .900, which is 90% spread over four quarters.
- The standard deduction is entered at its full annual amount in every column. It is not annualized.

[PIT-UND-I25]: "This method should be used if you do not qualify to use the Short Method (e.g., unequal or late estimated payments) or by individuals whose income is earned in an uneven fashion".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The statute sets the penalty at 1.5% per month or fraction of a month on each installment's underpayment, for the period of the underpayment. Form PIT-UND computes it as a daily charge of 0.05% (0.0005) for each day late, ending at the earlier of the payment date or April 30 of the next year.

The rate is fixed in the statute. It is not indexed and not tied to an interest rate. The general ½%-per-month interest rule does not apply to estimated tax.

The form is PIT-UND. There are two computation routes:

- Short method (Part 2): the total underpayment × 12%, less 0.05% per day for any part paid before April 30.
  - It may be used only when there were no estimated payments, or four equal payments all made on time.
  - It may not be used if any capital gain tax payments were made on the taxpayer's behalf.
- Regular method (Parts 3–5): computed per installment, by days late.

Quotes:

- [DC-535] § 535(b): "there shall be added to the tax an amount equal to 1 1 / 2 % per month, or fraction thereof, of the amount of such underpayment".
- [DC-535] § 535(b) continues: "The Director shall determine the amount of an underpayment of estimated tax by an individual in a manner consistent with the internal revenue laws".
- [PIT-UND-I25] line 40: "Compute the number of days between the due date on Line 39 and the date the amount on Line 37 is paid or April 30, 2026 whichever is earlier."
- [PIT-UND-I25] line 41: "Multiply Line 40 by 0.0005."
- [PIT-UND-I25] Part 2, lines J and K: "Multiply Line I by 0.12." Then subtract "0.0005 times the amount on Line l" for each day paid before April 30, 2026.
- [DC-535] § 533(a): interest "shall [not] apply to any failure to pay estimated income tax under § 1170". The exact text is "nor shall this section apply to any failure to pay estimated income tax under § 1170".

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The threshold is $800, not $1,000.
- The first installment is due April 30, not April 15.
- The penalty is a fixed 1.5% per month (about 18% a year), not the federal quarterly-adjusted interest rate. It is computed at 0.05% per day and stops at April 30.
- Part-year residents are excepted entirely.
- Retirement after 62 or disability, and casualty or disaster, are not claimed on the form. The taxpayer waits for the assessment and then writes to request a waiver.
- A Short Method charges a flat 12% of the total underpayment.
- Farmers and fishermen follow the same shape as federal: 66 2/3%, pay by January 15 or file and pay by March 1. The only difference is the April 30 return date. Delaware also exempts farmers and fishermen from the 110% rule.
- A nonresident estimates tax "as though all of your income is Delaware income" and then prorates. [PIT-EST-I26]: "a non-resident computes Delaware income tax as though all of your income is Delaware income."
- Federal Form 2210 is not accepted in place of PIT-UND. [PIT-UND-I25]: "Do not submit a Federal Form 2210 instead of a Delaware Form PIT-UND."

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements between sources

- **Federal or Delaware AGI for the 110% test.** [PIT-EST-I26] says "federal adjusted gross income for the previous tax year". [PIT-UND-I25] and [PIT-RES-I25] say "Delaware AGI".
- **Penalty measure.** The statute ([DC-535] § 535(b)) says "1 1/2% per month, or fraction thereof", so one day late would cost a full 1.5%. The form uses 0.05% per day, so one day late costs 0.05%.
- **End of the penalty period.** The statute does not say the individual penalty stops at April 30. The form stops it at April 30. The statute does stop the corporate penalty at the 4th month, in § 535(c)(3).
- **Short Method due dates misprinted.** [PIT-UND-I25] Part 2 lists the dates as "(June 15th, July 15th, September 15th, and January 15th)". That conflicts with the April 30 first date everywhere else.
- **Penalty start date.** [PIT-UND-F25] line 39 lists the first due date as 5/1/25, the day after April 30. The other three columns are the due dates themselves: 6/16/25, 9/15/25, 1/15/26. Days late are therefore counted one day differently for Q1.
- **Cut-off for Q4 payments.** [PIT-UND-I25] line 29 counts fourth-period payments through "January 16, 2026", one day after the January 15 due date.
- **Statutory threshold wording.** [DC-1169] § 1169(a) says "the estimated tax can reasonably be expected to exceed $800". [PIT-UND-F25] line E stops the computation at "$800 or less", which is consistent. But [PIT-RES-I25]'s worksheet says estimates are not required if the balance "is less than $800". That leaves exactly $800 ambiguous.
- **"10%" statement.** [PIT-RES-I25] says "You may owe this penalty if the amount you owe (Line 41, Balance Due) is more than 10% of the tax shown on your return". That reads as an informal rule of thumb, since the 90% test implies a 10% margin. It is not stated as a rule anywhere else.
- **Prior-year return filed.** No source says whether the prior-year option requires a filed prior-year return.

### Resolved and unresolved conflicts

- **Federal or Delaware AGI for the 110% test.** Unresolved. [PIT-EST-I26] says "federal adjusted gross income". [PIT-UND-I25], [PIT-RES-I25] and [PIT-NON-I25] all say Delaware AGI. The tax year 2024 PIT-UND and 2025 PIT-EST instructions show the same split, so this is not a one-year typo. The statute names no AGI. § 535(b) only says the underpayment is determined "in a manner consistent with the internal revenue laws". PIT-UND is the form that computes the penalty, so its Delaware AGI wording is the better guess, but no public source settles it.
- **1.5% per month or fraction versus 0.05% per day.** Unresolved in law. No 2024–2026 amendment touches § 535(b). The only recent change to § 535, 84 Del. Laws c. 301 § 16 (approved July 17, 2024), amended subsection (e) for marijuana taxes. The statute reads as a full 1.5% for any part of a month. The Division's own form charges 0.05% a day, which is 1.5% for a 30-day month and never more than the statute. A calculator that matches the form will match what the Division asks taxpayers to file.
- **Penalty ending at April 30.** Resolved as consistent. § 535(b) says only "for the period of the underpayment". The federal penalty period ends at the return due date. April 30 is Delaware's return due date under § 1168.
- **Short Method dates "June 15th, July 15th, September 15th, and January 15th".** Resolved as a misprint. The same text appears in the 2024 instructions. The statute, [PIT-EST-I26] item 8 and form line 39 all use April 30 as the first date.
- **Q1 penalty start 5/1 versus the April 30 due date.** Unresolved. Both the 2024 and 2025 forms print 5/1 for Q1 and the due date itself for the other quarters. Line 40 counts days "between the due date on Line 39" and payment. The effect is one day of penalty (0.05%) on Q1.
- **Q4 payments counted through January 16.** Unresolved, and a further conflict found. [PIT-UND-I25] line 29 says "through January 16, 2026". [PIT-UND-F25] Part 4 heads the column "9/16/25 - 1/15/26". The 2024 instructions also say January 16.
- **Exactly $800.** Resolved. § 1169(a) requires a declaration only if estimated tax will "exceed $800", so exactly $800 needs none. [PIT-UND-F25] line E agrees ("$800 or less ... You do not owe the penalty"). The [PIT-RES-I25] worksheet's "less than $800" is looser wording, and the statute governs.
- **The "10%" statement.** Resolved as informal guidance. It appears only in [PIT-RES-I25] page 4 under "You may owe this penalty". No form line uses it.
- **Prior-year return filed.** Confirmed: no source addresses it.
- **New: which year must be 12 months.** [PIT-RES-I25] tests the current-year return, [PIT-EST-I26] the preceding year, and [PIT-UND-I25] neither. § 1170(b) leaves short years to the State Tax Commissioner's rules. No published rule was found.
- **New: what "estimated tax" subtracts.** § 1169(b) subtracts only "credits allowable for tax withheld". [PIT-RES-I25] and the [PIT-EST-I26] worksheet also subtract other credits. The forms' version yields a lower or equal amount for the $800 test.
- **New: worked example in PIT-UND line 43 note.** It counts "48 days" and "93 days", which match neither the 5/1 and 6/16 dates on the form nor April 30 and June 15. It is illustrative only.
