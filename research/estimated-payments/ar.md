## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must file a declaration if you can reasonably expect estimated tax to exceed $1,000 after withholding and credits. The sources state no filing-status difference.

- AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "if the taxpayer can reasonably expect their estimated tax to be more than one thousand dollars ($1,000)."
- AR1000ES worksheet, TY2026, source 1, line 8: "If $1,000 or more, file the Declaration Voucher. ... If less than $1,000 no Declaration Voucher is required."
- AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "can reasonably expect the estimated tax to be $1,000 or more and income tax withholdings for the income year do not equal 90%"

Whether exactly $1,000 is in or out differs between these documents. See question 10.

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100% (the prior year's net tax).
- Higher prior-year percentage: none. Arkansas has no 110% rule.
- The prior-year option requires a filed return for a 12-month preceding year that showed a liability.
- A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option.

Quotes:
- AR2210, TY2025, source 2, line 6: "Required annual payment. Enter the smaller of line 2 or line 5". Line 2 is 90% of 2025 net tax. Line 5 is 2024 net tax.
- AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "The Underestimate Penalty is computed on the lesser of the current year's tax liability or the previous year's tax liability."
- AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3 (decoded): "where such return showing a liability for tax was filed by the taxpayer for the preceding income year of twelve (12) months."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes, one-fourth each. A taxpayer who first becomes liable later in the year pays 1/3, 1/2, or all of it in the remaining installments.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes. A payment is timely if postmarked on the next business day after a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday.
- January payment waived: the fourth installment is not needed if you file and pay by January 31, or the last day of the first month after year-end for fiscal years.

Quotes:
- AR1000ES vouchers, TY2026, source 1: due dates "04/15/2026", "06/15/2026", "09/15/2026", "01/15/2027".
- AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "Enter one-fourth (1/4) of line 8 of the worksheet on 'Amount of This Payment' space of voucher."
- AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "If you first become liable to file a declaration on June 15, 2026: Enter on voucher one-third (1/3) of line 8."
- AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "the payment will be considered timely filed if it is postmarked on the next succeeding business day."
- AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3, Exception 2 (decoded): "the taxpayer may file an income tax return and pay the tax on or before January 31" in lieu of "the fourth (4th) quarter installment".

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in four equal amounts on the due dates. No election to use actual withholding dates was found in sources 1–5.

- AR2210, TY2025, source 2, line 8: "Estimated tax paid and tax withheld. Enter 1/4 (.25) of line 3, AR2210 in each column."
- AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3, example: "Payments reported on Line (3) shall be deemed to have been paid in four (4) equal payments on the quarterly due dates."

(corrected in verification) The form's line 8 is labelled "Estimated tax paid and tax withheld", but it tells you to enter 1/4 of line 3. Line 3 is "2025 Arkansas income tax withheld" only. As printed, estimated payments enter nowhere in Part III. The instructions' worked example treats line 3 as all payments ("Payments", $2,000), spread evenly. The form and its instructions disagree. See question 10.

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

The AR2210 lists six numbered exceptions. You enter the exception number on the return (Form AR1000F/AR1000NR, box 52A).

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if the tax shown on the return is less than $1,000. The form's line 4 stops at "$1,000 or less" after withholding.
  - AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "No penalty shall be imposed for a tax year if the tax shown on the return for such tax year is less than $1,000".
  - AR2210, TY2025, source 2, line 4: "If the result is $1,000 or less, stop here."
- No liability in the prior year: no penalty if the prior year was 12 months, you had no liability, and you were an Arkansas resident throughout that year. AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3, Exception 3: "(c) the taxpayer was a resident of Arkansas throughout the preceding tax year."
- First-year residents: no separate exception. Exception 3 excludes anyone not resident throughout the prior year.
- Part-year residents: no separate exception. AR1000NR filers use the same AR2210 (source 2 line references).
- Farmers: qualify if at least two-thirds of gross income is from farming. Fishermen are not mentioned. The single payment is due the 15th day of the second month after year-end (February 15). Alternatively, file and pay by the 15th day of the third month (March 15). No reduced farmer percentage (such as 66 2/3%) was found.
  - AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "pay the estimated tax on or before the fifteenth (15th) day of the second (2nd) month after the close of the income year."
  - AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "file an income tax return and pay the full amount of tax on or before the fifteenth (15th) day of the third (3rd) month".
- Retired or disabled: no penalty if the Commissioner determines you retired after age 62 or became disabled in the tax year or the prior year, with reasonable cause. AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3, Exception 5: "retired after having attained age 62, or (ii) became disabled in the tax year ... or in the tax year preceding".
- Casualty or disaster: no penalty if the Commissioner so determines. AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3, Exception 4: "by reasons of casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances the imposition of such penalty would be against equity and good conscience."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, on Form AR2210A, which replaces the AR2210 (exception "6"). The factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5, 1, and the percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5%, 90%. Spouses filing separately on the same return (filing status 4) annualise each spouse's income separately.

- AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3, Exception 6: "The penalty is computed using Form AR2210A which must be submitted with Form AR1000F/AR1000NR".
- AR2210, TY2025, source 2: "(To claim exception 6, do not complete Form AR2210. For exception 6, use Form AR2210A only.)"
- AR2210A, TY2025, source 4, line 2: "Enter spouse's income. (Filing status 4 only)".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is a fixed 10% per year, charged like interest on each installment's underpayment for the days late. The days end at the earlier of the payment date or April 15 of the next year. The rate is fixed and is not tied to an interest index.

- AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1: "a penalty of ten percent (10%) per annum shall be added to the amount of the underestimate."
- AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3, page 2: "days from the due date of the quarterly payment to the date payment is actually made divided by 365, times ten percent (10%)"
- AR2210, TY2025, source 2, line 16: "Underpayment from line 13 X Number of days from line 15 / 365 X .10".
- Statutory citation, AR2210 instructions, TY2025, source 3: "The penalty provided in ACA §26-18-208 (Supp.1997) for failure to make correct payments of estimated income tax". The statute text is NOT FOUND.
- Form: AR2210, or AR2210A for annualised income. The penalty goes on Form AR1000F/AR1000NR line 52B.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The penalty is a flat 10% a year. It is not the federal short-term rate plus 3%.
- There is no 110% rule. 100% of prior-year tax always works, if the prior year was a 12-month year with a filed return.
- The no-prior-year-liability exception requires that you were an Arkansas resident all of the prior year.
- Farmers get later dates than federal. The payment is due February 15 (federal: January 15). Alternatively file and pay by March 15 (federal: March 1). Fishermen are not mentioned.
- The standard AR2210 has no election to treat withholding as paid when actually withheld.
- The voucher worksheet uses a $2,470 standard deduction per taxpayer and tax credits of $29 per person. Spouses' tax is computed in separate columns (AR1000ES, TY2026, source 1).

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- The threshold boundary is inconsistent:
  - The AR1000ES instructions say "more than one thousand dollars ($1,000)".
  - The AR1000ES worksheet says "If $1,000 or more, file".
  - The AR2210 instructions say "$1,000 or more".
  - The AR2210 line 4 says stop if "$1,000 or less".
  - The AR2210 instructions say no penalty if tax shown "is less than $1,000".
  - Subject 603 says "If you owe at least $1,000 of tax when you file ... and the amount ... is more than 10% of the tax shown"
- Estimated payments are spread evenly, or left out. AR2210 line 8 tells filers to enter 1/4 of line 3 in each column, and line 3 is only withholding. (corrected in verification) Taken literally, the form leaves estimated payments out entirely. The instructions' example treats all payments on "Line (3)" as paid "in four (4) equal payments". The worked example shows a $2,000 payment total divided by four. So the regular method appears to ignore when estimated payments were actually made. This could be a simplification or an omission; the sources do not say which.
- The prior-year return condition. The 12-month, filed-return-showing-liability condition appears in the instructions' declaration paragraph. The AR2210 form simply asks for 2024 net tax with no such condition.
- Subject 603 (2020) lists "The estimated tax paid is as much as was due on your return for the preceding income tax year" as a stand-alone waiver. The 2025 AR2210 instead builds prior-year tax into the required annual payment.
- The farmer percentage is not stated, so it is unclear whether farmers must pay 90% or a lower percentage by the February 15 date.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Threshold boundary ($1,000 exactly).** Not resolvable from public sources, because the statute could not be read. The penalty side is consistent: the instructions say no penalty if tax shown "is less than $1,000". The declaration side is not: the AR1000ES says "more than $1,000", but the worksheet and AR2210 instructions say "$1,000 or more". AR2210 line 4 ("$1,000 or less, stop") also conflicts with the "less than $1,000" exception. The line 4 test uses tax after withholding, while the exception uses tax shown, so they measure different things. Which governs at exactly $1,000 depends on the statute text in Ark. Code Ann. title 26, chapter 51.
- **Estimated payments in Part III.** Unresolved. The form's line 8 uses only withholding (line 3). The instructions' example treats line 3 as all payments, spread evenly. Neither uses actual payment dates. This changes the penalty for anyone who made estimated payments. It needs the statute or DFA guidance.
- **Prior-year condition, Subject 603, farmer percentage.** Unresolved for the same reason. DFA Regulation 1998-1 (corporation income tax) Rule 1.26-51-912 says "it must be either 100% of the prior year's tax liability or 90%". It does not address farmers. It is a corporation regulation, so it does not settle the individual rule.
