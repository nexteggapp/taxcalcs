No 2026 TC-40 instructions were published at the time of access. The TC-40 facts below are from the 2025 instructions.

Utah has no individual estimated tax and no quarterly underpayment penalty. Its only prepayment rule is a test applied on the original return due date, and its penalty applies only to taxpayers who use the automatic six-month filing extension. The answers below describe that rule, because it is the nearest Utah equivalent.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Nobody. Utah does not require quarterly estimated payments. Prepayments are voluntary, made any time with TC-546 or online.

- TC-40 instructions, TY2025, source 1: "Utah does not require quarterly estimated tax payments."
- TC-40 instructions, TY2025, source 1: "You can prepay at any time at tap.utah.gov, or by mailing your payment with form TC-546".

A taxpayer who wants to use the automatic extension to file must have prepaid a minimum amount by the original due date (question 2). No dollar threshold or filing-status difference applies.

## 2. Required annual payment

This is the extension prepayment requirement, due in full by the original return due date.

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage for high income: none. No 110% rule was found in sources 1–5.
- Filed prior-year return: the TC-40 instructions say a taxpayer with no Utah liability in the prior year, or filing for the first time, must use 90% of the current year. Pub 58 says a taxpayer who filed a prior-year return showing zero tax need not prepay. A 12-month prior-year requirement: NOT FOUND in sources 1–5.
- High-income taxpayers keep the prior-year option.

Quotes:
- Utah Code §59-10-516(2)(b), source 4: "the lesser of: (i) 90% of the total tax reported on the return for the current taxable year; or (ii) 100%" of the prior year's total tax liability.
- TC-40 instructions, TY2025, source 1: "if you did not have a Utah tax liability in 2024 or if this is your first year filing."
- Pub 58, source 3: "You do not need to prepay ... if you filed a return the previous year and the tax was zero."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

There are no installments. The full required amount is due on the original due date of the return (April 15, 2026 for tax year 2025). The share is 100% on one date.

- TC-546, Rev. 10/26, source 2: "Pay the amount on line 8 on or before the return due date."
- TC-40 instructions, TY2025, source 1: "You must prepay by the original due date".
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day. TC-40 instructions, TY2025, source 1: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the due date is the next business day." The sentence is printed under the fiscal-year item but is worded for any due date. The instructions add that "The due date may change if the IRS changes the due date of the federal return." (corrected in verification)
- January waiver: not applicable.

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding counts as a prepayment toward the single test on the original due date. Because there is only one test date, the timing of withholding within the year does not matter, and there is no election.

- TC-40 instructions, TY2025, source 1: "You may prepay through withholding (W-2, TC-675R, 1099-R, etc.), payments applied from previous year refunds, credits and credit carryovers".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: NOT FOUND. No small-amount exception exists in sources 1–5.
- No liability in the prior year: no prepayment needed if a prior-year return was filed showing zero tax (Pub 58, quoted in question 2). If no prior-year return was filed, the taxpayer must prepay 90% of the current year. Pub 58, source 3: "If you did NOT file a return the previous year, you must prepay 90 percent of the current year's tax liability."
- First-year filers: must prepay 90% of the current year (TC-40 instructions, quoted in question 2).
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND in sources 1–5.
- Farmers and fishermen: NOT FOUND. No special rule in sources 1–5.
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND as a specific exception.
- Casualty or disaster: no specific exception found. Penalties may be waived for reasonable cause. Pub 58, source 3: "We may waive, reduce or compromise any penalties or interest, based on reasonable cause." Pub 58 cites §59-1-401(13), but in the version effective 1/1/2026 the waiver is subsection (14): "upon reasonable cause shown, the commission may waive, reduce, or compromise any of the penalties or interest" (source 5). (corrected in verification)
- Structural exception: a taxpayer who files and pays in full by the original due date never meets this penalty. A taxpayer who files after the extension due date gets the late-filing and late-payment penalties instead. Pub 58, source 3: "The insufficient prepayment penalty will then not apply."

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Not applicable. There are no installments to annualize. NOT FOUND in sources 1–5.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The extension penalty is 2% of the unpaid tax per month of the extension period, computed daily, for at most six months (12%). Interest is charged separately at the Utah rate from the original due date until paid.

- Utah Code §59-1-401(5)(b), source 5: "the penalty per month during the period of the extension ... is an amount equal to 2% of the tax due on the return".
- Pub 58, source 3: "The penalty is 2 percent per month, calculated on a daily basis, until the date the return is filed or the extension expires."
- Pub 58 formula, source 3: "(underpayment) x .24* x (number of days outstanding) / 365", with "* 2 percent rate per month up to a maximum of six months, or 12 percent (annual 24 percent)".
- Interest rate rule: federal short-term rate for the fourth quarter of the prior year plus two points. Pub 58, source 3: "two percentage points above the federal short-term rate."
- Interest rate in effect: 6% for 2026. TC-40 instructions, TY2025, source 1: "The 2026 interest rate is 6 percent." Pub 58 (Rev. 11/25), source 3, lists "Jan. 1, 2025 - Dec. 31, 2026 ... 6%".
- Form: no separate form. TC-546 has a worksheet for the minimum prepayment. Pub 58 explains the calculation. The amount goes on TC-40 line 40.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- No quarterly estimated payments at all, and no quarterly underpayment penalty.
- A taxpayer who files by April 15 owes no prepayment penalty however little was paid during the year. Only interest and the ordinary late-payment penalty apply to the balance.
- The prepayment penalty applies only while the return is on extension, and at 2% a month it is much steeper than a federal interest-style charge.
- No 110% rule for high incomes.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. Sources 1–5 mention no city, county or school-district income tax. No primary source was found that states this explicitly.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Zero prior-year tax. The statute (source 4) gives only the lesser of 90% current or 100% prior, which for a zero prior year is zero. The TC-40 instructions say a taxpayer with no 2024 Utah liability must prepay 90% of 2025 tax. Pub 58 says a taxpayer who filed a prior-year return with zero tax need not prepay. The TC-40 instructions and Pub 58 contradict each other for a filer whose prior-year return showed zero tax.
- Penalty base. The statute applies the penalty to "the total balance due" (§59-10-516(3)) and to "the tax due on the return, unpaid as of the day on which the return is due" (§59-1-401(5)(b)). Pub 58 calls the base the "underpayment". Whether the base is the shortfall below the 90%/100% minimum or the whole unpaid balance is not stated plainly.
- Daily versus monthly. The statute says 2% "per month". Pub 58 says the penalty is "calculated on a daily basis" at 24% a year.
- Interest-rate period. Pub 58 shows one 6% period running "Jan. 1, 2025 - Dec. 31, 2026", while describing the rate as set each calendar year.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Zero prior-year tax.** The statute governs, and it favours the taxpayer. §59-10-516(2)(b) uses the lesser of 90% of current tax or "100% of the total tax liability for the taxable year immediately preceding". It sets no condition that a return was filed or that the liability was above zero. A prior-year liability of zero therefore makes the minimum zero. The TC-546 worksheet gives the same result: line 5 is the lesser of line 3 and line 4, "Last year's Utah tax liability (as filed, amended or audited)". Pub 58 agrees for a filer with zero tax. The TC-40 instruction (90% if no 2024 liability) has no basis in the statute. Pub 58's 90% rule for a non-filer also has no statutory basis if the prior-year liability was in fact zero. §59-10-516 has not changed since 2021: its current version is still the 2021 one. Unresolved: how the Commission assesses in practice. This would change the number for a first-year filer.
- **Penalty base.** Resolved by statute. The base is "the tax due on the return, unpaid as of the day on which the return is due" (§59-1-401(5)(b)). That is the whole unpaid balance on the original due date, not the shortfall below the 90%/100% minimum. TC-546 and the TC-40 instructions say the same ("2 percent of the unpaid tax"). Missing the minimum is only the trigger.
- **Daily versus monthly.** Not resolvable from public sources. The statute says "per month during the period of the extension" and says nothing about part months. Pub 58 prorates daily at 24% a year. The Commission's published method is daily.
- **Interest-rate period.** Resolved. Pub 58's table merges consecutive years with the same rate, for example "Jan. 1, 2021 – Dec. 31, 2022 ... 2%". §59-1-402(2) sets one rate per calendar year. The rate was 6% for 2025 and again 6% for 2026.
