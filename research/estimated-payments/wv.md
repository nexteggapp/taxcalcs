## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must make quarterly payments if your estimated tax minus West Virginia withholding is at least $600, unless that amount is less than 10% of your estimated tax. No filing-status difference was found.

- IT-140ESI, source 1: "is at least $600, unless that liability is less than ten percent of your estimated tax."
- The 2025 IT-140 booklet, source 2, states the same rule, worded "less than ten percent (10%) of your estimated tax". (corrected in verification)

The statute uses a different test. W. Va. Code §11-21-55(a), source 3: a declaration is required if AGI "can reasonably be expected to exceed $400 plus the sum of the West Virginia personal exemptions".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90% (66⅔% for qualified farmers).
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage for high income: none. No 110% rule was found in sources 1–6.
- The prior-year option requires a prior-year return covering a full 12 months. The statute also requires that the prior-year return showed a liability.
- High-income taxpayers keep the prior-year option.

Quotes:
- IT-140 booklet, TY2025, source 2: "100% of your prior tax liability (if you filed a prior year return that covered a full 12 months)."
- W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(d)(1), source 5: "if a return showing a liability for tax was filed by the taxpayer for the preceding taxable year".
- IT-210 line 8, TY2025, source 2: "IF LINE 7 IS ZERO AND LINE 3 IS MORE THAN $5,000, ENTER THE AMOUNT SHOWN ON LINE 6".

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates: April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the next year. For 2026 that is April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% each. A taxpayer who first meets the requirement later pays in three, two or one equal installments.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day.
- January payment waived: yes, if the return is filed and the tax paid in full by January 31 (statute), or by February 1 (2025 booklet). See question 10.

Quotes:
- IT-140ESI, source 1: "Current year quarterly installments are typically due April 15, June 15, and September 15 with the fourth quarter due January 15".
- IT-140ESI, source 1: "If the due date falls on Saturday, Sunday, or a legal holiday, the payment is due the next business day."
- W. Va. Code §11-21-56(a)(1), source 4: "the estimated tax shall be paid in four equal installments."
- IT-210 line 15, TY2025, source 2: "Enter one-fourth of line 8, Part I, of Form IT-210 in each column."
- W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(i), source 5: if the return is filed and paid in full "on or before the last day of the first month following the end of the taxable year", no addition applies to "the fourth required installment".
- IT-140 booklet, TY2025, source 2: "If you file your tax return and pay any tax due on or before February 1, no fourth quarter penalty is due."

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid one-fourth on each due date. The taxpayer can instead show the actual dates, by checking the box on IT-210 Part I. The statute lets wage withholding and other withholding be treated separately.

- IT-210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "considered to have paid one-fourth of these amounts on each payment due date, unless you check the box on line 11"
- W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(g)(2), source 5: the taxpayer "may apply subdivision (1) of this subsection separately with respect to: (A) Wage withholding; and (B) All other amounts withheld".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if tax minus West Virginia withholding is less than $600. W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(e)(1), source 5: "reduced by the credit allowable for withheld tax, is less than $600."
- No liability in the prior year: no penalty only if all of these hold: the prior year was 12 months, the taxpayer was a US citizen or resident all that year, and current-year liability is less than $5,000. W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(e)(2)(D), source 5: "The individual's West Virginia personal income tax liability for the current taxable year is less than $5,000."
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate rule. The zero-prior-year exception requires US (not West Virginia) residence, so a person moving from another state may qualify if the other conditions hold.
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND as an exception. The IT-140ESI tells part-year residents to prorate tax by the West Virginia income ratio when estimating.
- Farmers (two-thirds of gross income from farming): one installment due January 15, of 66⅔% of current-year tax. No penalty if the return is filed and paid by March 1. Fishermen are not mentioned.
  - IT-140 booklet, TY2025, source 2: "You are only required to make one payment for the taxable year (due January 15)."
  - IT-140 booklet, TY2025, source 2: "The amount of estimated tax required to be paid is sixty-six and two-thirds percent (66 ⅔%) instead of ninety percent (90%)."
  - W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(j)(4)(A), source 5: substitute "March 1" for "the thirty-first day of January".
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND as a specific waiver in sources 1–6.
- Casualty or disaster: the Tax Commissioner may waive. W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(e)(3), source 5: "by reason of casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances the imposition of such addition to tax would be against equity and good conscience."
- Reasonable cause: the booklet offers a waiver for reasonable cause, with a written statement on the "Request for Waiver of Estimated Penalty" page. IT-140 booklet, TY2025, source 2: "all or part of the penalty may be waived if the West Virginia Tax Division determines that reasonable cause has been shown."

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed. Use Part II of Form IT-210, the Annualized Income Worksheet. Factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1. Applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%.

- IT-210 Part II, TY2025, source 2: "PART II: ANNUALIZED INCOME WORKSHEET".
- W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(d)(2)(C)(i), source 5: income is annualized "in the same manner that taxable income is annualized for federal income tax purposes".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

An interest-like addition on each installment's shortfall for the days late. The period runs to the date paid or the return's original due date, whichever is earlier. Each calendar year's days use that year's rate. A short method is available for those with no estimates, or with four equal on-time estimates.

- Rate rule (after 2016): each year the Tax Commissioner sets the rate at the adjusted prime rate plus 3 points, as of the first business day of December, effective January 1. W. Va. Code §11-10-17a(c), source 6: "the adjusted prime rate charged by banks (rounded to the nearest hundredth of a percent) plus three percentage points per annum".
- Rates in effect: 12.25% for calendar 2025 (daily .000336) and 11.5% for calendar 2026 (daily .000315). IT-210 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "For January 1, 2026 to December 31, 2026 the rate is 11.5%."
- Penalty statute: W. Va. Code §11-10-18a(a), source 5: an amount "determined by applying the rate established under section seventeen or seventeen-a".
- Payments are applied to the oldest underpayment first.
- Form: IT-210, Underpayment of Estimated Tax by Individuals (Part III short method; Part IV regular method). If the IT-210 is not filed, the Tax Division computes the penalty and bills.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The threshold is $600, and there is an extra test: no payments are needed if the shortfall is under 10% of estimated tax.
- The zero-prior-year exception is lost if current-year tax is $5,000 or more. The IT-210 then requires 90% of current-year tax.
- No 110% rule for high incomes.
- The rate is prime plus 3 points (11.5% for 2026), far above the federal rate.
- The farmer rule names farmers only, not fishermen.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. Sources 1–2 mention no city, county or school-district income tax. No primary source was found that states this explicitly.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Instructions out of date. The only IT-140ESI found is dated Sept 2023. It says the rate "is determined by the State Tax Commissioner at six month intervals, but cannot be less than eight percent". The current statute (§11-10-17a(c)) sets one rate a year at prime plus 3, and the 8% floor applies only to "taxable years beginning prior to January 1, 2017". Its rate schedules are also older than the 2025 booklet.
- January waiver date. The statute says the last day of January. The IT-140ESI says "by January 31". The 2025 booklet says "on or before February 1". The IT-210 instructions also say "you will not owe a penalty for the payment due January 18", which matches no other date and looks like a typo.
- Who must pay. The statute (§11-21-55(a)) uses non-wage AGI over $400 plus exemptions. The instructions use tax after withholding of at least $600, with a 10%-of-estimated-tax exception. The 10% exception was not found in the statute.
- Return as declaration. §11-21-55(h) treats a return filed and paid by February 15 as the declaration, a different date from the January 31 penalty waiver in §11-10-18a(i).
- Days count. The IT-210 Line 10 instruction gives 364 days for column (a) in one sentence and 365 in the chart and example just above it.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Stale IT-140ESI rate text.** The statute governs. §11-10-17a(c) sets one rate a year at adjusted prime plus 3 points. The section was last amended in 2015 (HB 3006). The 2025 booklet's 12.25% and 11.5% follow it.
- **January waiver date.** The statute governs: "the last day of the first month following the end of the taxable year" (§11-10-18a(i)), shifted to the next business day under the IT-140ESI weekend rule. January 31, 2026 was a Saturday, so the shifted date was Monday, February 2, 2026. The booklet's "February 1" was a Sunday and matches neither the statute nor the weekend rule. For tax year 2026, January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the date is Monday, February 1, 2027. "January 18" is a typo: no rule gives that date, and January 18, 2026 was a Sunday.
- **Who must pay.** Unresolved. The statute's test is non-wage AGI over $400 plus exemptions. The instructions' $600 test matches only the penalty exception (§11-10-18a(e)(1)). The 10% test is in no statute found. It may be in 110CSR21 §55, but the Secretary of State's copy of that rule is a scanned cover sheet (source 7, UNCHECKABLE). The penalty outcome does not depend on it: the $600 exception is statutory.
- **Return as declaration.** Resolved. §11-21-55(h) (February 15) decides whether a declaration was filed. §11-10-18a(i) (January 31) decides the penalty. The penalty rule governs any penalty number.
- **Day count.** Resolved. April 15, 2025 to April 15, 2026 is 365 days. The 364 is an error.
- **Statute cross-reference (new).** §11-10-18a(j)(4) tells farmers to apply "Subsection (h)" with "March 1" in place of "the thirty-first day of January". That phrase is in subsection (i); (h) covers pass-through withholding. The intent is clear from the substituted words, and the booklet applies March 1.
