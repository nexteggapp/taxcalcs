## 1. Who must make estimated payments

All four conditions must apply for 2026 (source 1, 2026).

- The taxpayer must be required to pay federal estimated tax for 2026: "You are required to pay estimated federal income tax for the 2026 tax year."
- The 2025 North Dakota net tax liability must be $1,000 or more: "Your net tax liability for the 2025 tax year was equal to or more than $1,000."
- A taxpayer not required to file a 2025 North Dakota return counts as zero: "your net tax liability for 2025 is zero for this purpose."
- The expected 2026 balance after withholding must be $1,000 or more: "an amount equal to or more than $1,000 for the 2026 tax year."
- Withholding must be expected to fall short of the required annual payment (answer 2).

The statute says the same about the prior-year gate (source 4, N.D.C.C. 57-38-62(1)): "whose net tax liability for the preceding taxable year was less than one thousand dollars is not required to pay estimated tax".

The $1,000 threshold is the same for every filing status. No source gives a different amount for married filing separately.

Married filing jointly: each spouse is treated as subject to federal section 6654 (source 4, 57-38-62(1)): "must each be deemed to be subject to the federal provision."

Married filing separately: each spouse pays separately (source 1, 2026): "you and your spouse must make separate estimated tax payments based on your separate incomes."

## 2. Required annual payment

The required payment is the lesser of 90% of the current year's net tax liability or 100% of the prior year's (source 4, 57-38-62(1)(a)-(b)).

- Current year: "equals ninety percent of the taxpayer's current taxable year's net tax liability."
- Prior year: "equals one hundred percent of the taxpayer's net tax liability for the immediately preceding taxable year."

Source 1 (2026) repeats this as lines 11 to 13 of the worksheet.

Higher prior-year percentage above an income threshold: none. No North Dakota source mentions 110% or an AGI threshold. The prior-year percentage is 100% at every income level, including married filing separately.

A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option. No source limits it by income.

The prior-year option needs a filed return requirement, and is unavailable to someone who moved in during the prior year (source 4, 57-38-62(1)(b)(1)): "does not apply to any taxpayer who was not required by this chapter to file a return for the immediately preceding taxable year".

The same subsection also excludes "an individual who moved into this state during the immediately preceding taxable year".

There is no 12-month prior-year requirement for individuals. The statute applies a full-year test only to "an estate or trust that was not in existence for the entire immediately preceding taxable year" (source 4).

Change in filing status (source 4, 57-38-62(1)(b)(2)-(3)):

- Joint last year, separate this year: each spouse uses the liability "which would have been computed for the immediately preceding taxable year if separate state returns had been required to be filed."
- Separate last year, joint this year: the couple uses "the sum of their separate net tax liabilities for the immediately preceding taxable year."

Farmers use 66 2/3% instead of 90% (answer 5).

## 3. Due dates and shares

Four equal installments of 25% each (source 4, N.D.C.C. 57-38-63): "no less than one-quarter of the estimated tax ... on April fifteenth, June fifteenth, and September fifteenth of the taxable year, and January fifteenth".

2026 dates (source 1, 2026): April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.

Fiscal years use the 15th day of the 4th, 6th and 9th months and the 1st month of the next year (source 4, 57-38-63).

Weekend and holiday shifting applies (source 1, 2026): "If any installment due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the installment is not due until the next business day."

North Dakota's legal holidays are its own list, which includes Good Friday (source 8, N.D.C.C. 1-03-01): "The Friday next preceding Easter Sunday and commonly known as Good Friday." No 2026 or 2027 estimate date falls on Good Friday.

January payment waiver: yes, but it is tied to the federal filing, not the North Dakota filing (source 2, 2025, Part 1 Item C). It applies "because you filed your 2025 federal income tax return and paid the remaining federal income tax due by February 1, 2026."

Under Item C the taxpayer enters 25% of the required payment in the first three quarters and zero in the fourth (source 2, 2025): "Enter zero in the 4th quarter column."

Part-year and late-starting taxpayers pay in equal shares over the remaining dates (answer 5).

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid evenly, unless the taxpayer shows the actual dates (source 2, 2025, line 8 instructions): "Unless you can show otherwise, one-fourth of the total amount withheld is deemed to have been withheld by each payment due date."

The taxpayer can therefore use actual withholding dates. No source describes a formal election beyond "unless you can show otherwise".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

Tax below an amount. No interest is due if the estimated tax is under $1,000 per return (source 4, 57-38-62(3)): "no interest is due if the estimated tax of an individual, estate, or trust is less than one thousand dollars per income tax return filed." Source 2 (2025) applies the same test on lines 4 and 5.

No prior-year liability. If no 2025 return was required, 2025 liability is zero, which is below $1,000, so no estimates are required (source 3, 2025): "If you are not required to file a North Dakota return for 2025, you do not have to pay estimated tax for 2026."

Prior-year liability under $1,000. No estimates are required (source 2, 2025, line 5): "If less than $1,000, no estimated tax payment was required for 2025; do not file this form".

First-year residents (moved in during the prior year). They lose the 100% prior-year option and must meet 90% (source 1, 2026): "If you moved into North Dakota during 2025 and had no income from North Dakota prior to the move, part b does not apply". See answer 10 for how the statute and Schedule ND-1UT word this differently.

Part-year residents and nonresidents with no North Dakota income until after April 15. They pay equal shares over the remaining due dates (source 1, 2026): "you must pay the required estimated tax in equal payments over the remaining installment due dates following the receipt of income from North Dakota sources."

Source 2 (2025, Item D) sets the split for that case:
- Income first received April 1 to May 31: one-third in each of quarters 2, 3 and 4.
- Income first received June 1 to August 31: one-half in each of quarters 3 and 4.
- Income first received September 1 to December 31: all in quarter 4.

Quote: "enter one-third of the amount on line 6 in the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th quarter columns."

Farmers. A taxpayer who qualifies as a farmer for federal estimate purposes uses 66 2/3% of the current year's liability (source 1, 2026): "Substitute 66 2/3% if a qualified farmer."

A farmer may pay everything in one installment (source 1, 2026): "you may pay the full amount of your estimated tax due by January 15, 2027."

A farmer who files the federal return and pays federal tax in full by March 1 owes no interest for skipping state estimates (source 5, 81-03-04-02(3)(a)): "files a federal income tax return by March first of the following tax year, and pays the federal tax in full by that same date".

The regulation defines a farmer for this waiver as one who "derives over two-thirds of gross income from farming" (source 5). The waiver applies even if the state return is filed after March 1 (source 5): "The individual does not have to file a state income tax return or pay any state income tax due on or before March first".

A second farmer waiver covers a farmer who files the federal return after March 1. The farmer must make the January 15 federal installment and pay the state estimate due January 15 (source 5, 81-03-04-02(3)(b)): "The first three payments due on April fifteenth, June fifteenth, and September fifteenth of the current tax year are not required". (added in verification)

Fishermen: NOT FOUND. Sources 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 mention farmers only.

Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND. Sources 1 to 5 contain no retirement or disability waiver.

Casualty or disaster. North Dakota automatically adopts IRS disaster extensions (source 6, undated): "North Dakota automatically recognizes the federal extension for North Dakota tax purposes."

The commissioner may also waive interest for good cause (source 4, N.D.C.C. 57-38-45(5)): "The tax commissioner may for good cause shown waive all or any part of any civil penalty or interest".

Small amounts on a zero-balance return. If the return shows no overpayment and no tax due, interest under $5.00 need not be paid with the return (source 2, 2025): "if the interest due on Schedule ND‑1UT is $5.00 or more, the interest due ... must be included in the balance due".

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, but only if the taxpayer also used it for federal purposes (source 2, 2025, Item B): "This exception applies only if you used the annualized income installment method to calculate the amount of your underpayment and penalty for federal estimated income tax purposes".

It is computed on Schedule ND-1UT, Part 1 Item B and line 7, starting from the federal Form 2210 annualized figures (source 2, 2025): "use the annualized federal taxable income determined on the 2025 Federal Form 2210 to calculate an annualized North Dakota taxable income."

A copy of the federal annualized worksheet from Form 2210 must be attached (source 2, 2025).

The regulation states the same (source 5, 81-03-04-02(3)(c)): "utilizes the annualized income installment method for federal purposes as provided in section 6654".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

North Dakota charges interest, not a penalty, on each installment's shortfall for the days late (source 4, 57-38-62(3)): "The provisions of section 57-38-45, except those provisions relating to the imposition of a penalty, apply in case of ... underpayment of estimated tax."

Interest runs from the installment due date to the earlier of payment or the return due date (source 4, 57-38-62(3)): "interest accrues on a per annum basis from the due date of an installment to the fifteenth day of the fourth month".

The rate is a fixed 12% per year (source 2, 2025): "Interest of 12% per year is charged on an underpayment or late payment of estimated tax." Source 1 (2026) states the same rate for 2026: "Interest is calculated at the rate of 12% per year from the installment due date".

The rate is fixed, not reset quarterly. Statutory basis: 57-38-45(1)(a) sets "the rate of twelve percent per annum" for extensions. See answer 10 on the link between the two sections.

The form computes days late divided by 365, times 12%, times the underpayment (source 2, 2025, lines 16b to 16e): "Divide line 16b by 365 and round to three decimal places".

Payments apply first to the current quarter, then to the earliest earlier shortfall (source 2, 2025): "the excess must be applied to offset an outstanding underpayment, if any, from a previous quarter, starting with the earliest underpayment."

The form is Schedule ND-1UT (SFN 28704). The result goes on Form ND-1, line 37 (source 2, 2025): "Enter this amount on Form ND-1, line 37."

A taxpayer who owes this interest cannot file Form ND-EZ (source 2, 2025): "you may not file Form ND-EZ; you must file Form ND‑1."

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- North Dakota requires estimates only from taxpayers who must pay federal estimates (source 1, 2026, condition 1). Source 2 (2025) says not to complete Schedule ND-1UT otherwise.
- There is a prior-year gate: no estimates are required if the prior-year North Dakota net tax liability was under $1,000 (source 4, 57-38-62(1)).
- A taxpayer with no North Dakota return required last year owes no estimates this year (source 3, 2025).
- The prior-year percentage is always 100%. There is no 110% rule for high incomes (source 4; no mention in sources 1 to 3).
- Someone who moved into North Dakota last year cannot use the prior-year option (source 4, 57-38-62(1)(b)(1)).
- The charge is interest at a fixed 12% a year, not the federal quarterly-adjusted rate (sources 1 and 2).
- Underpayment interest on estimates stops at the return due date (generally April 15) (source 1, 2026; source 4, 57-38-62(3)). Tax still unpaid after that date is late-payment tax on the return, which carries interest of 1% a month under 57-38-45(1)(b) (source 4): "interest at the rate of one percent per month or fraction of a month during which the tax remains unpaid". (corrected in verification)
- The farmer waiver keys off filing the federal return by March 1, not the state return (source 5).
- The January installment waiver keys off the federal return filed and paid by February 1 (source 2, 2025, Item C).
- The annualized method is available only if it was used on the federal Form 2210 (source 2, 2025).
- North Dakota legal holidays include Good Friday, which is not a federal holiday (source 8).

## 9. Local income taxes

None found. Home rule cities may levy only the taxes their statute lists, and income tax is not among them (source 7, N.D.C.C. 40-05.1-06(5)). The list is "excises, fees, charges, franchise and license taxes, sales and use taxes, farm machinery gross receipts taxes".

The same subsection bars new tax types (source 7): "Ordinances enacted after August 1, 2017, may not allow for the collection and levy of any tax not otherwise specified under this section."

No official source states outright that there is no local income tax. Home rule counties (chapter 11-09.1) were downloaded and contain no reference to income tax.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- First-year residents. The statute removes the prior-year option for anyone "who moved into this state during the immediately preceding taxable year" (source 4). Form ND-1ES (2026) and the booklet (2025) add "and had no income from North Dakota prior to the move" (sources 1, 3). Schedule ND-1UT (2025) line 6 drops that qualifier: "if you moved into North Dakota during the 2024 tax year, enter the amount from line 2" (source 2). Which of the two governs a mover with prior North Dakota income is unclear.
- Booklet year typo. The 2025 booklet's condition 3 for 2026 estimates reads "at least $1,000 in North Dakota income tax for 2025" (source 3). Form ND-1ES says "for the 2026 tax year" (source 1). The booklet appears to be wrong.
- Small-tax threshold in the regulation is stale. N.D. Admin. Code 81-03-04-02(3)(d) waives interest when liability "exceeds the taxpayer's withholding by less than five hundred dollars" (source 5, last amended 2002). The statute and the forms use $1,000 (sources 1, 2, 4).
- Interest rate source. N.D.C.C. 57-38-62(3) applies the interest provisions of 57-38-45 "on a per annum basis" but does not name a rate (source 4). Section 57-38-45 has both 12% per annum (extensions, 1(a)) and 1% per month (late payment, 1(b)). The forms use 12% per year (sources 1, 2). The statute does not say explicitly which subdivision applies to estimates.
- January waiver date. Schedule ND-1UT (2025) Item C uses February 1, 2026, and refers to the federal 4th-quarter exception (source 2). This research did not check that date against the federal Form 2210 instructions.
- Fiscal-year and short-year rules are in the statute (57-38-63) but not repeated for short years on the forms. A short year under 120 days needs no estimates (source 4): "In the case of a tax year that is for a period of less than one hundred twenty days, no estimated tax payment is due."

### Resolved conflicts

C1. First-year residents with North Dakota income before the move.
The statute removes the prior-year option for every individual "who moved into this state during the immediately preceding taxable year" (source 4, 57-38-62(1)(b)(1)). It has no exception for prior North Dakota income. Schedule ND-1UT line 6, the form that computes the interest, follows the statute (source 2). Form ND-1ES and the booklet add the qualifier "had no income from North Dakota prior to the move" (sources 1 and 3). The 2025 session did not amend 57-38-62 (source V1). The statute governs. A mover with prior North Dakota income also cannot use the 100% prior-year option. The ND-1ES wording is more lenient than the law. A taxpayer who relied on it could ask for a good-cause waiver under 57-38-45(5).

C2. Booklet year typo.
The booklet's condition 3 says "for 2025" (source 3, page 8). Form ND-1ES says "for the 2026 tax year" (source 1). The statute defines "estimated tax" as tax "for the current taxable year" less credits, including withholding (source 4, 57-38-62(4)). The booklet is wrong. The test uses the 2026 year.

C3. Regulation's $500 limit against the statute's $1,000.
The regulation waives interest when liability exceeds withholding "by less than five hundred dollars" (source 5, 81-03-04-02(3)(d), last amended 2002). The statute says no interest is due if the estimated tax is under $1,000 per return (source 4, 57-38-62(3)). Every case under $500 is also under $1,000, so the two do not produce different results. The statute's $1,000 governs. Schedule ND-1UT line 4 uses $1,000 (source 2).

C4. Interest rate.
57-38-62(3) applies the interest provisions of 57-38-45 "on a per annum basis" (source 4). Section 57-38-45 has 12% per annum in 1(a) and 1% per month in 1(b) (source 4). Both equal 12% a year. "Per annum basis" overrides the month-or-fraction counting in 1(b). So the rate is 12% a year under either reading. The day-count method (days divided by 365, rounded to three decimals) comes only from the form (source 2, line 16c). No statute or regulation sets it.

C5. January waiver date.
Schedule ND-1UT Item C uses "February 1, 2026" (source 2). February 1, 2026 is a Sunday. The federal rule uses January 31 (source V3): "If you file your return and pay the tax due by January 31, 2026". January 31, 2026 is a Saturday. The federal weekend rule moves it to Monday, February 2, 2026 (source V3): "the act is considered to be performed timely if it's performed no later than the next day that isn't a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday." Item C is defined by the federal exception: it applies "if you did not have to make the 4th quarter estimated federal income tax payment" (source 2). North Dakota has no statute for this waiver. Unresolved detail: whether a federal return filed and paid on February 2, 2026 qualifies for Item C. The federal exception would cover it. The ND form's literal date would not. A data format should store the test as "federal 4th-quarter exception applies", not a fixed North Dakota date.

C6. Short tax years (not on the forms).
57-38-63 has two short-year rules (source 4). A year under 120 days needs no estimates. In any other short year that ends before the remaining due dates, "the final estimated tax payment is due on the fifteenth day of the last month of the short tax year." The forms do not repeat either rule. The statute governs.

C7. Non-filers in the prior year (found in verification).
57-38-62(1)(b)(1) says the prior-year option "does not apply to any taxpayer who was not required by this chapter to file a return for the immediately preceding taxable year" (source 4). That implies such a taxpayer may still owe estimates at 90%. But the same subsection opens with a gate that applies "Notwithstanding any other provision of this section": prior-year liability under $1,000 means no estimates. Form ND-1ES and the booklet treat a non-filer's prior-year liability as zero, so the gate applies (sources 1 and 3). The "notwithstanding" wording makes the gate govern. Non-filers owe no estimates. The non-filer clause in (1)(b)(1) has no practical effect for individuals.

### 2026 changes

No law changes the estimate rules for tax year 2026.

- 2025 regular session: the session laws' sections-affected table lists no change to 57-38-45, 57-38-62, 57-38-63 or 1-03-01 (source V1). The only chapter 57-38 sections it lists are 57-38-01.42, 57-38-30.3, 57-38-59 and 57-38-60 to 57-38-60.3.
- 57-38-59 now bars withholding from wages that qualify for the military pay subtraction, unless the taxpayer asks (source 4, 57-38-59(4)). Those wages are subtracted from taxable income, so this does not change any estimate rule.
- January 2026 and September 2026 special sessions: each passed one tax bill, both on the property tax primary residence credit (HB 1626 and SB 2405, source V2). Neither touches income tax.
- Income tax rates for 2026 are unchanged at 0%, 1.95% and 2.50%. Only the bracket amounts are indexed (source 1, 2026 rate schedules; source 3, 2025 rate schedules).
