## 1. Who must make estimated payments

An individual must pay estimated tax when both of these hold:

- the annual liability exceeds $200, and
- withholding covers less than 80% of the liability.

No filing-status differences were found.

- Form 80-106, TY2025, source 1: "Every individual taxpayer who does not have at least eighty percent (80%) of his/her annual tax liability prepaid through withholding must make estimated tax payments if his/her annual tax liability exceeds two hundred dollars ($200)."
- Reg. 35.III.11.21.100(1), source 4: the same wording.

## 2. Required annual payment

The required annual payment is the lesser of:

- **80%** of the current year's tax, or
- **100%** of the prior year's tax.

There is no 110% rule and no income threshold.

- Form 80-320 line 2, TY2025, source 2: "Multiply the amount on line 1 by 80% and enter the result".
- Form 80-320 line 4, TY2025, source 2: "Enter the lesser of line 2 or line 3".

Filed return and 12-month year: the regulation requires both for the prior-year option.

- Reg. 35.III.11.21.100(3), source 4: "provided a return was filed and the return covered a period of twelve (12) months."

If the taxpayer was not required to file a resident return last year, the regulation's prior-year test uses last year's income at the current year's rates.

- Reg. 35.III.11.21.100(3), source 4: "the estimated tax payments must be equal to or more than the tax liability computed on prior year's income based on Mississippi current year's rates and exemption."

Form 80-320 instead says to use line 2 (80% of current tax) when the prior-year tax is zero and no estimates were paid. See answer 10.

High-income taxpayers do not lose the prior-year option.

## 3. Due dates and shares

There are four equal installments of 25%, due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. The whole estimate may instead be paid by April 15.

- Reg. 35.III.11.21.100(2), source 4: "the estimated tax may be paid in four equal installments".
- Form 80-320 line 5, TY2025, source 2: "Required installments. Enter 1/4th (.25) of line 4".

Weekend and holiday shift: NOT FOUND. Searched sources 1-4 for "Saturday", "weekend" and "holiday".

January waiver: NOT FOUND (no file-by-January-31 rule).

## 4. Withholding

Form 80-320 puts all withholding in the first column (April 15). Withholding is not spread over the four due dates, and no election to spread it was found.

- Form 80-320 line 6, TY2025, source 2: "Income tax withheld (column A only) and estimated tax paid".
- Form 80-320 line 6 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "For column (A) only, enter the total overpayment from prior year, estimated tax paid and/or withholding as of payment due date."

See answer 10.

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no interest if the year's liability is $200 or less. Form 80-320 line 1, TY2025, source 2: "If your 2025 Mississippi Income Tax Liability is $200 or less, do not complete the remainder of this form".
- No liability in the prior year: no exception found. Form 80-320 line 4, TY2025, source 2: "If line 3 is zero and your 2025 Mississippi income tax liability (line 2) exceeds $200 and no estimate payments for the 2025 tax year were made, enter the amount from line 2."
- First-year and part-year residents: NOT FOUND as specific exceptions. The regulation's alternative for taxpayers "not required to file a Mississippi resident return for the prior year" (answer 2) is the nearest rule.
- Requirement first met after a due date: interest is computed only in the columns that apply. Form 80-100, TY2025, source 3: "Filing requirements met after the payment due date."
- Farmers and fishermen (at least two-thirds of gross income): no interest if 80% of the estimate is paid by January 15, or the return is filed and paid by March 1. Form 80-100, TY2025, source 3: "80% of estimated tax is paid by the 15th day of the first month after the close of the income year".
- Retired, disabled, casualty or disaster: NOT FOUND in sources 1-4.

## 6. Annualised income method

NOT FOUND for individuals. Form 80-320 has no annualisation section. Rule 100 does not mention annualisation, although the next rule (Rule 101, corporate estimated payments) allows it for corporations.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The charge is called "interest". It is a flat rate of 1/2% per month on the cumulative shortfall for each period. Each period runs from the payment due date until paid or the next due date, whichever is earlier.

The shortfall carries forward into the next column, so a continuing underpayment keeps accruing in later periods. The 4th column runs to April 15. Per the Form 80-320 example, the months counted are 2, 3, 4 and 3 for the four columns.

- Form 80-320 line 9, TY2025, source 2: "compute interest at 1/2% per month from payment due date until paid or next payment due date, whichever is earlier".
- Form 80-100, TY2025, source 3: "will result in an assessment of interest at the rate of 1/2% per month on underpayment of tax from the date payment is due until paid".

Rate rule: fixed by statute, not indexed. § 27-7-327 sets 1/2% per month for underestimates assessed on or after January 1, 2019. The 1% in Rule 100 is the pre-2015 base rate (see answer 10). (corrected in verification)

- § 27-7-327(e), source 6: "One-half of one percent (1/2 of 1%) per month for such taxes assessed on or after January 1, 2019."

Partial months: NOT FOUND. The Form 80-320 example counts whole months only (2, 3, 4 and 3), and no source says how a part month is counted. (corrected in verification)

Form: Form 80-320. The interest goes on Form 80-105 line 30 (resident) or Form 80-205 line 32 (nonresident or part-year).

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise

- The current-year safe harbor is 80%.
- The prior-year safe harbor is always 100%. There is no 110% rule.
- The threshold is $200, and it is tested on the whole year's liability.
- The requirement is triggered when withholding covers less than 80% of the liability.
- All withholding is credited to the first installment.
- The rate is a flat 1/2% per month, set in statute. The worksheet example counts whole months; part-month treatment is not stated. (corrected in verification)
- The prior-year safe harbor is in the regulation and the form, not in § 27-7-327, which states only the 80% rule. (corrected in verification)
- There is no annualisation, no weekend rule and no January 31 relief in the sources found.

## 9. Local income taxes

None found in the DOR sources reviewed. The forms and instructions mention no city or county income tax.

## 10. Ambiguities and conflicts

- Interest rate: Rule 100 (source 4, PDF modified May 26, 2026) says "interest of one percent (1%) per month". The TY2025 Form 80-320 and Form 80-100 both say 1/2% per month. Resolved: § 27-7-327 as amended in 2014 sets 1/2% from January 1, 2019, so the forms are right and Rule 100 is out of date. (corrected in verification)
- Withholding timing: the line label says withholding goes in "column A only", but the instruction says "withholding as of payment due date". The Form 80-320 example puts $1,500 in column A, labelled as including withholding and payments, and shows only estimated payments in later columns. Whether withholding after April 15 is all credited to April 15 is not stated explicitly.
- Prior-year zero: Rule 100(3) offers last year's income at current rates for taxpayers who did not have to file. Form 80-320 line 4 says to use 80% of current tax when line 3 is zero and no estimates were paid. The form does not mention the regulation's alternative.
- Threshold base: the 80-106 and Rule 100 test "annual tax liability exceeds $200" together with the 80% withholding test. The 80-320 tests only liability "$200 or less", before withholding.
- No 2026 Form 80-106 was posted; the TY2025 voucher (Rev. 09/25) is the latest found.

### Conflicts

- **Interest rate (1% vs 1/2%).** Resolved: 1/2% per month governs. 2014 HB 799 Section 7 amended § 27-7-327 to step the rate down to 1/2% "for such taxes assessed on or after January 1, 2019". The bill history shows "Approved by Governor" on April 10. Rule 100 still prints the old 1% base rate. A search found no later amendment of § 27-7-327, and the TY2025 forms agree with 1/2%.
- **Withholding timing.** Not resolved. Form 80-320 puts withholding in column A only. Neither a statute nor a rule says how withholding after April 15 is timed. The form is the only rule found, so all withholding is treated as paid on April 15.
- **Prior-year zero.** Not resolved. Rule 100(3) lets a taxpayer who did not have to file use last year's income at this year's rates. Form 80-320 line 4 uses 80% of this year's tax instead. § 27-7-327 has no prior-year exception at all, so both rules rest on DOR authority. Result: for such a taxpayer the required payment is either 80% of current tax (form) or tax on prior income at current rates (rule).
- **Threshold base.** No real conflict. Form 80-320 line 1 is an early exit for liability of $200 or less. A taxpayer with 80% withheld still owes nothing, because all withholding is credited in column A.
- **Form line numbers.** Booklet line 30 says it comes "from Form 80-320, Line 19". Form 80-320 line 19 says to enter its total on line 36 / 37, and line 11 says line 30 / 32. Line 11 to line 30 is the consistent reading. This changes where the amount is entered, not the amount.
