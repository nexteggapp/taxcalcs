## 1. Who must make estimated payments

The threshold is estimated Rhode Island tax of $250 or more in excess of allowable credits.
It applies to residents and nonresidents, whether or not a federal estimate is required.
- [S1] 2026 RI-1040ES: "can be reasonably expected to be $250 or more in excess of any credits allowable against his or her tax".
- [S6] § 44-30-55(a) uses the same words: "two hundred and fifty dollars ($250) or more in excess of any credits allowable".
- [S11] 280-RICR-20-55-1 § 1.5(A) repeats the $250 threshold.

The RI-1040ES worksheet subtracts credits on line 8 and withholding on line 10 [S1]. (corrected in verification)

Filing status: there is no separate threshold by filing status.
Married couples may pay jointly, with joint and several liability.
- [S1] 2026 RI-1040ES: "Married individuals may make joint payments as if they were one taxpayer".
- [S6] § 44-30-55(b) bars a joint declaration if the spouses are separated by decree "or if they have different taxable years".
- [S11] § 1.5(C): spouses who paid jointly and file separately "must decide on how the estimates and credits are to be divided between them".

## 2. Required annual payment

Current-year percentage: 80% of the current-year tax.
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, Part 1 line 2: "Enter 80% of the amount shown on line 1".

Prior-year percentage: 100% of the prior-year tax.
The required annual payment is the smaller of the two.
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 5: "Enter your 2024 RI tax"; line 6: "Enter the SMALLER of line 2 or line 5".
- [S1] 2026 RI-1040ES worksheet line 7: "Enter the SMALLER of lines 5 or 6" (2026 computed tax vs. 2025 tax).

Higher prior-year percentage for high income: NOT FOUND.
None of [S1], [S2], [S3], [S4], [S6], [S7] or [S8] mentions 110% or an AGI threshold.
The forms show a flat 100% of prior-year tax for everyone.
No married-filing-separately variant exists because there is no threshold.

Loss of the prior-year option for high income: NOT FOUND. No source restricts it.

Filed return or 12-month year for the prior-year option: no requirement is stated for the general 100% option.
A 12-month prior year and full-year residency are required only to enter zero for prior-year tax (see item 5).
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 5 instructions: "your 2024 federal tax return was (or would have been had you been required to file) for a full 12 months, then enter zero (0)".

## 3. Due dates and shares

Tax year 2026 due dates are April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027.
- [S1] 2026 RI-1040ES: "1st Estimate Due......April 15, 2026 ... 4th Estimate Due......January 15, 2027".

Each installment is one quarter of the required annual payment.
- [S7] § 44-30-56(a): "Then due dates of equal installments are: ... April 15; June 15; September 15; January 15 of following year."
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 13: "Divide the amount shown on line 6 by four (4) and enter the result in each column."

Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or Rhode Island legal holiday.
The rule uses Rhode Island legal holidays, not federal or District of Columbia holidays.
- [S10] § 44-30-91(b): "falls on Saturday, Sunday, or a Rhode Island legal holiday ... timely if it is performed on the next succeeding day".
None of the four 2026 dates falls on a weekend, so the 2026 coupons show no shifted date [S1].
None of the four 2026 dates is a Rhode Island legal holiday under § 25-1-1 [S15]. (added in verification)
The Division applies the shift in practice: its 2024 table lists the normal second 2024 installment date as June 17, 2024, because June 15 was a Saturday [S14]. (added in verification)
- [S16] ADV 2026-15 confirms the second 2026 installment: "Personal income tax – estimate ... Form RI-1040ES ... June 15, 2026".

January installment waiver: yes, but the two Division documents give different dates (see item 10).
- [S1] 2026 RI-1040ES: "You do not have to make your January 15, 2027 payment if you file your 2026 return by February 15, 2027".
- [S6] § 44-30-55(e) treats a return filed "on or before the fifteenth day of the second month after the close of his or her taxable year" with full payment as the declaration.
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 16 instructions: "If you filed your return on or before January 31, 2026 and paid the tax balance in full, then enter that amount in column D."

For tax year 2026 the statutory waiver date moves to Tuesday, February 16, 2027. (corrected in verification)
February 15, 2027 is the third Monday of February, which is a Rhode Island legal holiday.
- [S15] § 25-1-1 lists "the third Monday of February (as Washington's Birthday)".
- [S10] § 44-30-91(b) moves an act due on a Rhode Island legal holiday to the next day that is not a weekend or holiday.
The 2026 RI-1040ES still prints "February 15, 2027" [S1].

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid evenly, one quarter on each due date, unless the taxpayer shows otherwise.
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 16 instructions: "You are considered to have paid Rhode Island withholding tax evenly during the year unless you can show otherwise."
- [S2] same: "you are considered to have paid one-fourth of your Rhode Island withholding on each installment due date."

The words "unless you can show otherwise" are the only provision for using actual dates.
No separate election form or procedure was found.

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

Tax below an amount: no charge if current-year tax minus Rhode Island withholding is $250 or less.
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 4: "Subtract line 3 from line 1. If the result is $250.00 or less, do not complete the rest of this form".

No liability in the prior year: yes, with conditions on residency and a 12-month year.
The test is worded as no federal tax liability (see item 10).
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 5: "If you had no federal tax liability for 2024 and you were a Rhode Island resident during all of 2024".
- [S8] § 44-30-84(b) adopts "the exceptions specified in 26 U.S.C. § 6654(e)(1) or (2)" applied to "the corresponding Rhode Island tax amounts and returns".

First-year residents: NOT FOUND. No rule specific to new residents appears in [S1] through [S11].

Part-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate rule.
The only part-year effect found is that the prior-year-zero exception requires full-year residency in the prior year [S2].
[S5] is the booklet for nonresidents and part-year residents. It repeats the general rule that "a taxpayer" who expects to owe more than $250 must pay estimates. It has no part-year rule. (corrected in verification)

Farmers and fishermen: two-thirds of gross income from farming or fishing qualifies.
Filing and paying by March 1 means no charge at all.
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210: "Gross income from farming or fishing is at least two thirds of your annual gross income".
- [S2] same: "Filed Form RI-1040 or RI-1040NR and paid the tax due on or before March 1, 2026."
Otherwise, a qualifying farmer uses 66.67% in place of 80% and one installment, January 15 (Column D).
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210: "substitute 66.67 % for 80% on Part I, line 2 and complete Part 1 and Part 3, Column D".
Farmers and fishermen must use RI-2210, not RI-2210A [S3].

Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in any Rhode Island form or instruction.
The statute incorporates only § 6654(e)(1) and (2), not the federal (e)(3) waiver [S8].
The regulation says federal exceptions "may be followed" in general [S11] (see item 10).
- [S11] § 1.6(A)(1): "In general, the Federal exceptions under which taxpayers will not be charged for underpayment of estimated tax may be followed".

Casualty or disaster waivers: NOT FOUND for tax year 2025 or 2026.
The statute allows waiving interest for one circumstance, a state closing of banks and credit unions.
- [S8] § 44-30-84(a)(2): waiver allowed if "the underpayment results from the state's closing of banks and credit unions".
That waiver is in subsection (a), and estimated-tax interest is in subsection (b), so whether it reaches estimates is unclear.
The 2024 RI-2210 [S13] shows a one-year schedule change ("For tax year 2024 do not enter anything in Column A.").
The cause was disaster relief. (corrected in verification)
After storms and flooding, the Division moved the first two 2024 installments to July 15, 2024, for every taxpayer in the state [S14].
- [S14] ADV 2024-16: "Personal income tax - ... Form RI-1040ES ... June 17, 2024 ... July 15, 2024".
- [S14] same: the Division allowed "all individuals and entities with Rhode Island returns and payments normally due on April 15, 2024" to pay by July 15.
This was a postponement of due dates by the Division, not an adoption of the federal § 6654(e)(3) waiver.
No similar relief for tax year 2025 or 2026 was found. The advisories index on tax.ri.gov is behind a bot challenge, so this rests on web searches and on ADV 2026-15 [S16], which lists the normal June 15, 2026 date.

Estates (not individuals): estates have no estimate requirement for the first two years after death [S2].

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed. It is Part 5 of Form RI-2210, the Annualized Income Installment Worksheet.
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210: "TAXPAYER IS USING THE ANNUALIZATION OF INCOME METHOD (RI-2210, Part 5)".
- [S2] line 14 instructions: "if you complete and use line 14 for any installment you must complete it and use it for all installments."
The periods end 3/31, 5/31, 8/31 and 12/31.
The annualization factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1.
The applicable percentages are 20%, 40%, 60% and 80% [S2, Part 5, lines 23–27].
RI-2210A cannot be used with annualization [S3].

## 7. How the penalty is computed

It is interest on each installment's shortfall for the days it is late, not a flat percentage.
The Division calls it "underestimating interest".
- [S8] § 44-30-84(b): "computed on the amount by which his or her actual payments and credits ... are less than eighty percent (80%) of the installment".
- [S2] 2025 RI-2210, line 19b: "Number of days on line 19a X 12% X underpayment on line 17" divided by 365.
Interest runs until the payment date or April 15 of the following year, whichever is earlier.
- [S8] § 44-30-84(b): "for the period the failure continues, until the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year."

Rate rule: on each January 1, the tax administrator sets the rate for that calendar year at prime (as of October 1 of the prior year) plus 2%.
Since January 1, 2023, the rate for non-trust-fund taxes has a floor of 12% and a cap of 21%.
- [S9] § 44-1-7(b): "adding two percent (2%) to the prime rate, which was in effect on October 1 of the preceding year".
- [S9] § 44-1-7(b)(2): "in no event shall the rate of interest exceed twenty-one percent (21%) per annum nor be less than twelve percent (12%)".

Current rate: 12.00% per year for calendar year 2026, set by the Division on November 26, 2025.
- [S12] ADV 2025-23: "the interest rate on delinquent tax payments of other tax types shall be 12.00% per annum."
The 2025 rate was also 12.00% [S12].
The 2025 RI-2210 uses 12% for all periods: "The interest rate to be used is 12% per annum" [S2].
The form splits the days after December 31 onto separate lines: line 20 for January 1–15 and line 21 for January 15–April 15 [S2].
The 2025 form does not say why. Its instructions say to use "the applicable interest rates" [S2]. (corrected in verification)
The 2024 form stated its rate for one calendar year only: "12% per annum from January 1, 2025 to December 31, 2025" [S13]. This is consistent with a rate that can change on January 1 [S9].
Every rate in this item is the non-trust-fund rate. The 18% trust-fund rate covers only the taxes in §§ 44-19-35 and 44-30-76 [S9], and the RI-2210 uses 12% [S2]. (added in verification)
The 2026 rate is 12% because prime on October 1, 2025 was 7.25% [S12]. Prime plus 2% is 9.25%, below the 12% floor [S9]. (added in verification)
The resident booklet states the same rate: "failure to pay adequate estimated tax, is subject to interest at the rate of 12% (0.1200) per annum" [S4]. (added in verification)

Forms:
- RI-2210 (full computation, Parts 3–4) [S2].
- RI-2210A, a shortcut, for taxpayers who made no estimates or four equal on-time estimates and did not annualize [S3].
The shortcut multiplies the underpayment by 7.9808%.
It then subtracts the underpayment × days paid before 4/15/2026 × 0.000329 [S2, S3].
- [S3] 2025 RI-2210A, line 10: "Multiply line 9 by 7.9808% (0.079808)".
The factor follows from the 12% rate. It is 12% × (365 + 304 + 212 + 90) ÷ 365 ÷ 4 = 7.9808%. (added in verification)
The four day counts run from each 2025 due date to April 15, 2026. This is a verification calculation, not a Division statement.
The daily rate 0.000329 is 12% ÷ 365, rounded.
The result goes on RI-1040 line 15b [S4] or RI-1040NR line 18b [S5]. (corrected in verification)

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The current-year safe harbor is 80%, not 90% [S2].
- There is no 110% prior-year rule for high incomes; it is 100% of prior-year tax for everyone [S1, S2].
- The threshold is $250, not $1,000 [S1, S6].
- The rate is fixed per calendar year (12% for 2025 and 2026) rather than reset each quarter [S9, S12].
- Weekend and holiday shifting uses Rhode Island legal holidays [S10].
- RI-2210A offers a shortcut that uses a fixed 7.9808% factor [S3].
- The January installment can be skipped by filing by February 15 (RI-1040ES), not January 31 as federally (see item 10) [S1]. For tax year 2026 that date is February 16, 2027, because February 15 is a Rhode Island legal holiday [S10, S15]. (corrected in verification)
- The prior-year-zero exception is worded as "no federal tax liability", not no Rhode Island liability [S2, S3].
- The federal retirement/disability and casualty waivers are not adopted in the statute [S8].

## 10. Ambiguities and conflicts

- January waiver date. The 2026 RI-1040ES [S1] and § 44-30-55(e) [S6] say file and pay by February 15. The 2025 RI-2210 instructions [S2] say January 31, 2026. The 2024 RI-2210 [S13] also says January 31.
- Threshold wording. The RI-1040ES and statute say "$250 or more" [S1, S6]. The RI-1040 and RI-1040NR instructions say "more than $250" [S4, S5]. The RI-2210 excuses a result of "$250.00 or less" [S2].
- Where the prior-year safe harbor comes from. § 44-30-84(b) states only the 80% test and the § 6654(e)(1)–(2) exceptions [S8]. The 100%-of-prior-year option appears only on the forms [S1, S2].
- Small-tax exception amount. § 44-30-84(b) adopts the § 6654(e)(1) small-tax exception using "the corresponding Rhode Island tax amounts". The statute does not say whether the amount is the federal $1,000 or Rhode Island's $250. The forms use $250 [S2].
- No-liability test. The RI-2210 and RI-2210A refer to "no federal tax liability for 2024" [S2, S3]. § 44-30-84(b) points to the corresponding Rhode Island amounts [S8].
- Retired, disabled and casualty waivers. The statute adopts only § 6654(e)(1) and (2) [S8]. The regulation says federal exceptions "may be followed" in general [S11]. The RI-1040ES says the law "follows similar provisions of the Internal Revenue Code with respect to exceptions" [S1]. None of these says whether the § 6654(e)(3) waivers apply.
- $2 minimum. § 44-30-84(a)(1) says interest under $2.00 is not payable [S8]. That sentence is in subsection (a), not subsection (b) on estimates, so whether it applies to estimate interest is unclear.
- Shortcut factor. The 7.9808% factor and 0.000329 daily rate (12%/365) are stated without derivation [S2, S3]. The 2024 form used 0.000191 per day [S13] and a different installment schedule. Its reason is not stated.
Verification derived the 2025 factor from the 12% rate (see item 7). The 2024 schedule came from storm relief [S14]. The 2024 daily rate 0.000191 is still unexplained. (corrected in verification)
- Booklet versions. Draft November 2025 versions of the RI-1040 and RI-1040NR instructions are also on tax.ri.gov. The December 2025 versions were used [S4, S5]. Their estimate text matches the drafts.

### Conflicts resolved

No statute in this file was amended in 2024, 2025 or 2026. Verification read every public law linked from the official lists for those years (1,397 law pages) [S18]. None amends §§ 44-30-55, 44-30-56, 44-30-84, 44-30-91 or 44-1-7. The FY 2025 and FY 2026 budget bills [S19] and the FY 2027 budget [S17] only cite § 44-1-7 as a rate. So no form reflects a newer law, and the statute governs wherever the two differ in substance.

- January waiver date. The statute says the 15th day of the second month [S6], which is February 15. For tax year 2026 the holiday rule makes it February 16, 2027 [S10, S15]. The RI-1040ES agrees with the statute on the date [S1]. The RI-2210 uses January 31 [S2]. The statute governs the date. Scope is a separate question. § 44-30-55(e) applies only when the declaration was "not otherwise required to be filed prior to the close" of the year [S6]. The RI-2210 limits the rule the same way [S2]. The RI-1040ES states no limit [S1]. The statute governs the scope too. Still unresolved: § 44-30-55(e) makes the return the declaration but does not say that interest on a missed January 15 installment is waived. Which date the Division's own computation applies cannot be settled from public sources. A data format should hold the statutory date and the form's January 31 date separately.
- Threshold wording. The statute governs the requirement: $250 "or more" [S6]. The booklets' "more than $250" is looser wording of the same rule [S4, S5]. The no-charge test is a separate rule on the RI-2210: tax minus withholding of $250.00 "or less" [S2]. So a taxpayer at exactly $250 must pay estimates but owes no charge.
- Source of the 100% prior-year option. Still unresolved. § 44-30-84(b) measures the shortfall against 80% of the installment and adopts only § 6654(e)(1) and (2) [S8]. The prior-year option appears only on the forms [S1, S2, S3]. No law or regulation was found that creates it. The forms are what the Division applies.
- Small-tax exception amount. Still unresolved in the statute [S8]. The forms use $250 [S2, S3].
- No-liability test. The statute governs. It applies § 6654(e)(2) to "the corresponding Rhode Island tax amounts and returns" [S8]. On that reading the test is no Rhode Island liability for the prior year. The forms say "no federal tax liability" [S2, S3]. No later law explains the form wording.
- Retired, disabled and casualty waivers. The statute does not adopt § 6654(e)(3) [S8]. The regulation lets the Division follow federal exceptions but does not require it [S11]. Whether the Division grants an (e)(3) waiver on request cannot be settled from public sources. The Division has postponed due dates for a disaster by advisory [S14].
- $2 minimum. Still unresolved. The sentence is in § 44-30-84(a)(1), not (b) [S8]. No form or regulation mentions it for estimates.
- Shortcut factor. Resolved. The 2025 factor 7.9808% equals 12% interest for the days from each due date to April 15, 2026, averaged over four equal installments (see item 7). The 2024 factor 6.9762% matches the July 15, 2024 storm schedule [S13, S14]. It uses 366-day years for 2024 days and 365 for 2025 days. The 2024 daily rate 0.000191 matches neither 12% ÷ 365 nor 12% ÷ 366 and remains unexplained.

### 2026 changes

- Interest rate: 12.00% for calendar year 2026, confirmed from ADV 2025-23 [S12]. It is unchanged from 2025.
- Thresholds, percentages and due dates for tax year 2026: no change. No 2024–2026 public law amends the governing sections [S18, S19]. ADV 2026-15 lists the normal June 15, 2026 date [S16].
- Waiver date: February 16, 2027 for tax year 2026, because of the Washington's Birthday holiday (see item 3). This comes from the existing statutes, not a new law.
- High-income surtax, tax year 2027 onward. The FY 2027 budget adds a surtax on taxable income over $1,000,000 [S17]. It is 1% for 2027, 2% for 2028 and 3% from 2029. For estates and trusts the threshold is $36,427. It does not apply to tax year 2026. It will raise required payments for affected taxpayers from 2027, with no change to the estimate rules.
- Tax amnesty. The FY 2027 budget sets a 75-day amnesty ending February 15, 2027, for periods ending on or before December 31, 2025 [S17]. It does not cover tax year 2026.
- Statute site lag. The official statute pages do not yet show 2026 laws; the § 44-30-2.6 page has no surtax text. So the statute pages alone do not prove the absence of a 2026 change. The public laws were searched for that reason.
