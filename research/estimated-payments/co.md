## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay if you expect to owe more than $1,000 in net Colorado tax after withholding and credits. The sources state no filing-status difference.

- DR 0104EP, TY2026, source 1: "you must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe more than $1,000 in net tax for 2026, after subtracting any withholding or credits"

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 70%. Farmers and fishermen: 50%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100% if prior-year federal AGI was $150,000 or less ($75,000 or less married filing separately).
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% above that AGI threshold.
- The threshold uses federal AGI, not Colorado income, from the preceding year.
- Both prior-year options require a 12-month preceding year and a filed Colorado return.
- A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option. It rises to 110%.

Quotes:
- DR 0104EP, TY2026, source 1: "70% of the actual net Colorado tax liability".
- DR 0104EP, TY2026, source 1: "the federal adjusted gross income on that return was $150,000 or less (or, if married filing separately, $75,000 or less)".
- DR 0104EP, TY2026, source 1: "110% of the preceding year's net Colorado tax liability. This amount applies only if the preceding year was a 12-month year".
- DR 0104EP, TY2026, source 1: "If at least 2/3 of your income is from farming or fishing, 50% of the actual net Colorado tax liability."
- DR 0204, TY2025, source 2, line 5(b): "If your previous year federal adjusted gross income is greater than $150,000 (greater than $75,000 if married filing separate), enter 10% of line 5(a)."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% each.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day.
- January waiver: no penalty is computed for the fourth installment if the return is filed and tax paid by January 31.

Quotes:
- Part 6 guide, source 3: "Estimated tax payments are due in four equal installments on the following dates. April 15 ... June 15 ... September 15 ... January 15".
- DR 0204, TY2025, source 2, line 7: "Divide the amount on line 6 by four."
- DR 0104EP, TY2026, source 1: "If the due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, payment will be due the next business day."
- DR 0204 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "If the tax return is filed and any tax due is paid by January 31 no penalty will be computed in column four."

## 4. How withholding is treated

DR 0204 line 9 asks for "Amount of income tax withheld from wages and/or nonresident real estate transactions" in each quarterly column. Neither the form nor its instructions say whether to enter actual amounts per quarter or one quarter of the total.

(corrected in verification) The statute answers it. Withholding is deemed paid in equal parts on each due date, unless you establish the actual dates. Wage withholding and other withholding may be treated separately.

- C.R.S. §39-22-605(8)(a), source 6: "an equal part of such amount shall be deemed paid on each due date ... unless the taxpayer establishes the dates".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if current-year tax after credits, less withholding, is $1,000 or less. DR 0204 calls the $1,000 a "Statutory exemption". DR 0204, TY2025, source 2, Exception 2, line 3: "Subtract line 2(c) from line 1. If 2(c) is larger, enter zero and no penalty is due."
- No liability in the prior year (corrected in verification): no penalty if the prior year was 12 months, you had no liability, and you were a Colorado resident throughout it. C.R.S. §39-22-605(7)(b)(III), source 6: "The individual was a resident of Colorado throughout the preceding taxable year."
- First-year residents: no exception. (corrected in verification) The statute confirms it: the zero-prior-year exception requires Colorado residence throughout the prior year. The prior-year options require a filed Colorado return, so a first-year resident with no prior Colorado return is left with 70% of current-year tax.
- Part-year residents: no exception. (corrected in verification) The whole section applies to them. C.R.S. §39-22-605(13), source 6: "All of the provisions of this section shall also apply to nonresident or part-year resident taxpayers."
- Farmers and fishermen: qualify if at least two-thirds of gross income for the current or prior year is from farming or fishing. No penalty if they file and pay in full by March 1. Otherwise they make a single payment due January 15. The required payment is the smaller of 50% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax; the 110% add-on does not apply.
  - DR 0204, TY2025, source 2, Exception 1: "you pay the full amount of tax due on or before March 1, you are not subject to the estimated tax penalty."
  - DR 0204 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "Estimated tax payments from a farmer or fisherman are due in a single payment by January 15 and only column four is used".
  - DR 0204, TY2025, source 2, line 5(b): "If you checked the box for exception number 1 above enter 0."
- Retired or disabled (corrected in verification): waiver available if the executive director finds you retired after 62 or became disabled in the year or prior year, with reasonable cause. C.R.S. §39-22-605(7)(d)(I), source 6: "retired after having attained age sixty-two or became disabled".
- Casualty or disaster (corrected in verification): no casualty-specific waiver. There is a general good-cause waiver instead. C.R.S. §39-22-605(7)(c), source 6: "the underpayment was due to good cause shown by the taxpayer."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, but only if you also annualise for federal purposes. It is done on DR 0204 Part 4. The periods end March 31, May 31, August 31 and December 31. The factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5, 1. The applicable percentages are 17.5%, 35%, 52.5% and 70%, reflecting the 70% rule.

- DR 0204 instructions, TY2025, source 2: "may elect to use the annualized income installment method ... if they elect annualized installments for the payment of their federal income tax."
- DR 0104EP, TY2026, source 1: "If you use an annualized method to file estimated payments for your federal income tax, you may file using this method"

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like charge on each installment's underpayment for the days late. It ends at the earlier of the payment date or April 15. The form splits it at December 31, because Colorado's interest rate is set per calendar year.

- 2025 form, first segment: due date to the earlier of payment or December 31, at 12%. DR 0204, TY2025, source 2, line 15: "Underpayment on line 12 multiplied by 12% multiplied by number of days on line 14 divided by 365 (366 for leap year)".
- 2025 form, second segment: the later of December 31 or the due date, to the earlier of payment or April 15, at 11%. DR 0204, TY2025, source 2, line 18: "Underpayment on line 12 multiplied by 11% multiplied by number of days on line 17 divided by 365".

How the rate is set: CDOR's penalties page (source 4) cites "§ 39-21-110.5, C.R.S. Rate of interest to be fixed" and "§ 39-21-109, C.R.S.". It gives a regular and a discounted rate for each calendar year:

| Year | Regular rate | Discounted rate |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 12% | 9% |
| 2026 | 11% | 8% |

The 2025 DR 0204 uses the regular rates (12% for 2025, 11% for early 2026). (corrected in verification) The rate is the Wall Street Journal prime rate plus three points, rounded to the nearest full percent. It is fixed each July 1 and takes effect the next January 1. The discounted rate does not apply to estimated tax, because §39-21-109, which grants the discount, excludes it. CDOR's table also lists 10% for 2027.
- C.R.S. §39-21-110.5(2), source 6: "the annual rate of interest shall be the prime rate, as reported by the 'Wall Street Journal', plus three points".
- C.R.S. §39-21-109(5), source 6: "This section shall not apply to any failure to pay estimated Colorado income tax."
- C.R.S. §39-22-605(3), source 6: the penalty applies "the rate of interest established under section 39-21-110.5" and is "the only penalty imposed".

Form: DR 0204. The penalty goes on DR 0104 line 46.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The current-year safe harbour is 70%, not 90%. Farmers and fishermen use 50%, not 66 2/3%.
- The 100% prior-year option is only for prior-year federal AGI of $150,000 or less. Above that it is 110%, as federal. Both need a filed Colorado return for a 12-month prior year.
- The annualised method is available only if you also annualise federally.
- The penalty rate is Colorado's regular interest rate: 12% for 2025 and 11% for 2026. That is well above the federal rate.
- The rate changes only on January 1, not quarterly.
- (corrected in verification) The zero-prior-year-liability exception requires Colorado residence throughout the prior year, not US residence.
- (corrected in verification) There is a retirement and disability waiver, and a general good-cause waiver, but no casualty-specific waiver. None of these appears on DR 0204.
- (added in verification) Exactly $1,000 of tax after withholding is not exempt (question 10).

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

Several Colorado cities levy occupational privilege taxes ("head taxes"). For example, Denver's is administered by the City and County of Denver Department of Finance (source 5). It is a flat monthly amount per employee, not a tax on income. (corrected in verification) Source 5 was read. The employee tax is $5.75 a month, withheld by the employer, for employees paid at least $500 a month in Denver. Employers pay $4.00 a month per taxable employee and per owner. The guide describes no estimated payments. Source 5: "liable for the Employee OPT to be withheld by the employer at a rate of $5.75 per month." Whether other cities' head taxes require individual estimated payments is NOT FOUND.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- What income threshold. DR 0104EP says the prior-year 100% rule depends on "the federal adjusted gross income on that return". DR 0204 says "previous year federal adjusted gross income". Both use federal AGI, even though the test concerns the Colorado return.
- Withholding timing is unstated on the forms. (corrected in verification) The statute deems it paid in equal parts unless actual dates are shown (question 4).
- Whether exactly $1,000 is in or out. DR 0104EP says pay if you owe "more than $1,000". DR 0204 subtracts a $1,000 "statutory exemption" and charges no penalty at zero. (corrected in verification) The statute governs, and exactly $1,000 is not exempt. The exception applies only if tax less withholding "is less than one thousand dollars" (C.R.S. §39-22-605(7)(a)). The "exceeds $1,000" phrase attributed to the guide page does not appear on that page.
- Farmers' prior-year option. DR 0204 zeroes the 110% add-on for farmers but still lets them use 100% of prior-year tax. DR 0104EP lists only "50% of the actual net Colorado tax liability" for farmers, without mentioning a prior-year alternative. (corrected in verification) Resolved in favour of DR 0204. C.R.S. §39-22-605(10)(c) substitutes 50% for 70% "without regard to paragraph (c)", the 110% rule, so the 100% prior-year option stays.
- Which rate applies. CDOR publishes both regular and discounted rates. The DR 0204 uses the regular rate, and the sources do not explain why the discount does not apply. (corrected in verification) Resolved: the discount comes from §39-21-109(1.5), and §39-21-109(5) excludes estimated tax. The regular rate applies.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Exactly $1,000.** The statute governs. §39-22-605(7)(a) waives the penalty only if tax less withholding "is less than one thousand dollars". So exactly $1,000 is subject to the penalty. DR 0104EP's "more than $1,000" and DR 0204's line 3 both suggest the opposite. The statute was last amended in 2022 (SB 22-233) and was not changed in 2025 or 2026 (Red Books), so no newer law supports the forms. This changes the outcome only for tax of exactly $1,000.
- **Federal AGI in the 110% test.** Confirmed, not a conflict. §39-22-605(6)(c)(I) says "the taxpayer's federal adjusted gross income shown on the return ... for the preceding taxable year".
- **Farmers' prior-year option.** DR 0204 is right. §39-22-605(10)(c) uses 50% "and without regard to paragraph (c)", so 100% of prior-year tax remains available.
- **Regular versus discounted rate.** The regular rate applies. The discount is in §39-21-109(1.5), and §39-21-109(5) says that section does not apply to estimated tax.
- **Weekend shifting.** The forms are broader than the statute's wording. §39-22-605(5)(c) moves a due date only for a "legal federal holiday", to the "adjusted federal due date". The forms say weekend or legal holiday, which matches the federal practice the statute points to. This does not change a number.
