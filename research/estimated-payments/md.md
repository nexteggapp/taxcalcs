## 1. Who must make estimated payments

An individual must declare and pay estimated tax when the tax on income not covered by withholding is expected to exceed $500. The statute sets the threshold as half of the federal § 6654(e)(1) amount, which is $1,000, so $500. The tax counted includes the county (local) income tax, because the worksheet estimates "Maryland and local income tax".

- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "your gross income would be expected to develop a tax of more than $500 in excess of your Maryland withholding."
- Tax-General § 10-815(a), source 5: "to exceed one-half the amount specified in § 6654(e)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code".

Wagering income has a separate rule. A person who receives $500 or more of unwithheld gambling or lottery winnings must file a declaration and pay in full within 60 days.

- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "You must file a declaration with payment in full within 60 days of receiving $500 or more of income from awards, prizes, lotteries or raffles".
- Tax-General § 10-820(d), source 6: "within 60 days after the individual receives income from wagering". (added in verification)

Filing status: the threshold is the same for every filing status. A married couple may file a joint declaration.

- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "A married couple may file a joint declaration."

## 2. Required annual payment

The required annual payment is the lesser of 90% of the current year's tax or 110% of the prior year's tax. The 110% figure applies to every taxpayer. There is no 100% option and no income threshold. A first-time filer has no prior-year option and must use 90% of the current year.

- 502UP, TY2025, source 3: "(1) 110% of last year's taxes, or (2) 90% of the current year's taxes."
- 502UP line 10, TY2025, source 3: "Enter the lesser of Line 8 or 9b. If first-time filer, enter Line 8".
- Tax-General § 13-602(a)(2), source 8: "less than 110% of the tax paid for the prior taxable year".

High-income taxpayers: there is no separate rule; 110% applies to all. Married filing separately: no variant found.

12-month prior year: NOT FOUND. Neither 502UP, § 13-602 nor COMAR 03.04.01.02 requires the prior year to be a 12-month year. The only stated condition is the first-time-filer instruction above.

The PVW frames the prior-year option as conditional on rising income, which differs from 502UP (see answer 10). Resolved in verification: the statute and COMAR 03.04.01.02(A)(4)(b) state the 110% test with no income condition, so 110% is always available. (corrected in verification)

- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "if you will receive more income than you did in 2025, you must pay at least 110% of your prior year tax".

## 3. Due dates and shares

There are four equal installments of 25%, due April 15, June 15 and September 15 of the tax year and January 15 of the next year.

- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "You must pay at least one-fourth of the total estimated tax on Line 15 of this form on or before April 15, 2026."
- Tax-General § 10-902(a)(1), source 7: "at least 25% of the estimated income tax shown on the declaration".

Weekend and holiday shift: yes, to the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. A statute states this for every tax act, so it covers estimated payments. Legal holidays include Maryland and federal holidays. (corrected in verification)

- Tax-General § 1-201(b), source 11: "performance of the act is considered timely if the act is performed on the next succeeding day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday."
- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "If any due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the return and payment must be filed by the next business day."

For tax year 2026 none of the four due dates falls on a weekend. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the January 31 option moves to Monday, February 1, 2027. (added in verification)

January waiver: the fourth payment is not needed if the return is filed and the balance paid by January 31.

- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "you may file and pay the balance of tax due on or before January 31, 2027."
- Tax-General § 10-820(b)(3), source 6: "If an individual elects to pay the income tax in full on or before January 31".

The 502UP column headings for 2025 show the actual dates, unshifted: April 15, 2025; June 15, 2025; Sept 15, 2025; Jan 15, 2026.

## 4. Withholding

The 502UP counts withholding cumulatively "from the beginning of the year to the due date" of each column. It does not say withholding is spread evenly, and it does not offer an even-spread election. See answer 10.

- 502UP line 14, TY2025, source 3: "the sum of any estimated payments you have made and any Maryland tax withheld from the beginning of the year to the due date".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no interest if the unpaid tax is less than $500. The statute and COMAR say "less than $500" of unpaid tax. 502UP says "$500 or less" of liability after withholding. The statute governs, so exactly $500 unpaid can carry interest. (corrected in verification) Tax-General § 13-602(c), source 8: "if the amount of unpaid income tax is less than one-half the amount specified in § 6654(e)(1)". 502UP, TY2025, source 3: "The tax liability on gross income after deducting Maryland withholding is $500 or less".
- No liability in the prior year: NOT FOUND as an exception. Searched 502UP, PVW, the resident booklet, COMAR 03.04.01.02, and §§ 10-815, 13-602 and 13-702. By the 502UP arithmetic alone, a filer whose prior-year line 34 tax was $0 gets line 9b = $0, so line 10 is $0 and no interest is due. A first-time filer cannot use this. This is an inference from the form, not a stated rule. (added in verification)
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND, except that a "first-time filer" uses 90% of the current year only (answer 2).
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND.
- Income taxable by another state: no interest if at least 90% of taxable income is taxable by another state and the underpayment is no greater than the local tax. 502UP, TY2025, source 3: "If at least 90% of your taxable income is taxable by another state, and the underpayment is not greater than the local tax, no interest is due".
- Farmers and fishermen (gross income from farming or fishing at least two-thirds of total): one declaration with full payment by January 15, or file and pay the return by March 1. There is no reduced percentage like the federal 66 2/3%. PVW, TY2026, source 2: "Your 2026 declaration and full payment of the estimated tax are due on or before January 15, 2027." If neither is done, interest is computed on the whole required payment in column 4 only.
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND.
- Casualty or disaster: no specific rule. A general reasonable-cause waiver exists for both interest and penalty. (corrected in verification) Tax-General § 13-606(a), source 12: "For reasonable cause, a tax collector may waive interest on unpaid tax." § 13-714(a) says the same for penalties.
- For 2025 only, interest and penalties caused by the 2025 rate changes are waived. The waiver is in Section 26 of Chapter 604 of 2025, source 14. It does not apply to 2026. Resident booklet, TY2025, source 4: "The Comptroller will waive interest or penalties on underpaid estimated income taxes for calendar year 2025 if the underpayment is the result of the liability created by the tax rate changes."

## 6. Annualised income method

There is no federal-style annualised method. Instead, 502UP lines 11-13 scale the required payment by the share of the year's income received by the end of each period (Jan 1-Mar 31, Jan 1-May 31, Jan 1-Aug 31, Jan 1-Dec 31). With even income the shares are 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%. A taxpayer whose interest comes out to zero this way enters code 301 on the return.

- 502UP, TY2025, source 3: "You may elect to report your income based on the actual dates of receipt."
- 502UP, TY2025, source 3: "If the interest calculated on Line 18 is zero because your income was not received evenly throughout the year, enter code number 301".

The statute's federal annualisation reference (§ 10-902(e)) applies only to fiduciaries.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is called "interest". Each column applies a factor to that period's cumulative shortfall, for the time until the next due date. Because the columns are cumulative, an unpaid amount carries into every later column, so interest runs until it is paid or until April 15. The form uses fixed factors for each column: 0.0192, 0.0289, 0.0381 and 0.0266.

The printed factors are day counts, not whole months. Each equals actual days divided by 365, times the annual rate in force for those days. Column 3 splits at January 1 across two rates. Arithmetic for TY2025: 61/365 × 11.4825% = 0.0192. 92/365 × 11.4825% = 0.0289. (107/365 × 11.4825%) + (15/365 × 10.8133%) = 0.0381. 90/365 × 10.8133% = 0.02666, printed 0.0266. The 0.0001 gap in column 4 is unexplained (truncation would fit, but would break column 1). (corrected in verification)

If a late payment was made before the next due date, the factor is the number of months late divided by 12, times the annual rate. A partial month counts as a full month. This whole-month rule matches the statute, § 13-604(a): "for each month or fraction of a month".

- 502UP, TY2025, source 3: "The factors represent the interest rate for the portion of the year between the due date of each quarter and the due date of the next quarter."
- 502UP, TY2025, source 3: "Treat a partial month as one month."

Rate rule: the Comptroller sets the annual rate by October 1 for the next calendar year. The rate is the greater of 9% (for 2023 and later) or 3 points above the average prime rate for the State's previous fiscal year. The monthly rate is one-twelfth of the annual rate.

- Tax-General § 13-604(b), source 9: "3 percentage points above the average prime rate of interest quoted by commercial banks to large businesses during the State's previous fiscal year".

Current rates from 502UP (TY2025, source 3): 0.9568% per month (11.4825% a year) for due dates before January 1, 2026. 0.9011% per month (10.8133% a year) after December 31, 2025.

- "Interest is computed at a rate of 0.9011% per month after December 31, 2025."

The 2027 rate is 9.995% a year (0.8329% a month). The Comptroller published it on September 4, 2026. (corrected in verification)

- Maryland Register 53:18, source 15: "For the 2027 calendar year, the annual interest rate on refunds and moneys owed to the State will be 9.995%."

For TY2026, column 3 (September 15, 2026 to January 15, 2027) spans 10.8133% and 9.995%. Column 4 (January 15 to April 15, 2027) uses 9.995%. The TY2026 Form 502UP was not yet published on 2026-10-20 (2026/502up.pdf returned 404). By the day-count method above, the factors would be 0.0181, 0.0273, 0.0358 and 0.0246. These are projections, not published figures.

There is also a statutory penalty of up to 25% of the amount underestimated. 502UP computes only interest (see answer 10).

- Tax-General § 13-702(a), source 10: "a tax collector shall assess a penalty not exceeding 25% of the amount underestimated".

Form: Form 502UP (attached to Form 502, 505 or 515). The interest goes on Form 502 line 51.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise

- The prior-year safe harbor is always 110%, for every income level. There is no 100% option.
- The threshold is $500 and includes county tax.
- Annualisation is replaced by an income-share method, and code 301 must be entered when it removes the interest.
- The statute charges interest by the month, and a partial month counts as a full month. The printed 502UP factors are day-based, but the late-payment adjustment uses whole months. Federal § 6654(a) applies the § 6621 rate "for the period of the underpayment". (corrected in verification)
- The rate floor is 9%, so the rate is far above the federal rate (about 11.5% for 2025, 10.8133% for 2026, 9.995% for 2027).
- Farmers and fishermen must pay 100% by January 15. There is no 66 2/3% reduction.
- A separate 25% penalty is authorised by statute.

## 9. Local income taxes

Maryland's 23 counties and Baltimore City levy a local income tax. It is administered by the Comptroller on the state return and paid through the same Form PV estimates, not separately.

- PVW, TY2026, source 2: "Maryland counties and Baltimore City levy an income tax on residents that is a percentage of taxable net income."

## 10. Ambiguities and conflicts

- The PVW conditions 110% on having more income than last year ("if you will receive more income than you did in 2025"). 502UP and § 13-602 make 110% the prior-year test for everyone, with no condition.
- Withholding timing is not stated. The cumulative "withheld from the beginning of the year to the due date" wording suggests actual dates, but no even-spread default or election is mentioned.
- § 13-702 authorises a penalty of up to 25% in addition to the § 13-602 interest. 502UP mentions "penalty and interest" in its preamble but computes only interest. How often the penalty is actually assessed was NOT FOUND.
- The 502UP factors do not match the stated monthly rate times the number of months. For example, column 4 (Jan 15 to Apr 15, 3 months at 0.9011%) would be 0.0270, but the form prints 0.0266. Resolved in verification: the factors are actual days / 365 times the annual rate (answer 7). (corrected in verification)
- The PVW states the weekend rule under "When to file" next to the extension text. Resolved in verification: Tax-General § 1-201(b) applies the rule to every tax payment, so it covers quarterly estimates. (corrected in verification)

### Conflicts resolved

1. 110% conditional on rising income (PVW) versus unconditional (502UP). The statute governs. § 13-602(a)(2)(ii) and COMAR .02(A)(4)(b) carry no income condition. No 2024-2026 amendment was found. The PVW sentence is guidance only.
2. Weekend rule scope. § 1-201(b) covers "the last day to pay a tax" under the whole article. It applies to estimates.
3. $500 boundary. § 13-602(c) and § 13-702(c) say unpaid tax "less than" $500. COMAR says the underpayment "is less than $500". 502UP says liability after withholding "is $500 or less". The two also measure different things: unpaid tax versus liability net of withholding. The statute governs. A design should key on unpaid tax < $500.
4. Factors versus monthly rate. The arithmetic is in answer 7. Columns 1-3 match days/365 exactly. Column 4 is 0.0001 lower than days/365 gives. Statute § 13-604(a) charges a monthly rate per "month or fraction of a month". The form's day-based factors are a Comptroller simplification. The statute would give 0.0191, 0.0287, 0.0377 and 0.0270 for TY2025. This stays UNRESOLVED from public sources. It changes interest by up to about 0.0004 of the shortfall per column. A form-faithful format must store the printed factors.
5. 25% penalty. § 13-702(a) says "shall assess a penalty not exceeding 25%". COMAR .02(A)(4)(a) says "shall assess interest and penalty". 502UP computes only interest. Whether and how the penalty is assessed in practice was NOT FOUND.
6. New in verification: prior-year base. § 13-602 says "110% of the tax paid for the prior taxable year, reduced by the credit allowed under § 10–703". COMAR adds § 10-703.2. 502UP line 9a uses prior-year line 34, less Part CC credits on Form 502CR. The statute's "tax paid" and the form's tax "developed" are not reconciled in any source found.
7. New in verification: the 502UP period-3 late-payment text says "paid earlier than December 15, 2025". The next due date is January 15, 2026. This is an internal inconsistency in the form. It is unresolved.

### 2026 changes

- The 2027 interest rate is 9.995%. It applies to the TY2026 column 3 days after December 31, 2026, and to all of column 4.
- The 2026 rate of 10.8133% is confirmed by 502UP and the TY2025 booklet.
- The 2025 rate-change waiver (Ch. 604 of 2025, § 26) covers calendar year 2025 only. No 2026 waiver was found.
- No 2024-2026 amendment was found to §§ 10-815, 10-820, 10-902, 13-602, 13-604 or 13-702. The current statute text on mgaleg shows the same thresholds, percentages and dates. Ch. 735 of 2025 re-enacted § 13-604 without amendment. The searches cannot prove that no amendment exists.
- The TY2026 PVW carries new 2026 rate brackets (top rates 6.25% and 6.50%) and new Anne Arundel and Frederick local rates. These change the tax being estimated, not the estimate rules.
- A 2% tax on net capital gain applies where federal AGI exceeds $350,000, from tax year 2025. The Comptroller's May 1, 2026 release says individuals "may need to adjust their personal estimated payments accordingly".
- The TY2026 Form 502UP and the 2026 booklet were not yet published on 2026-10-20 (both URLs returned 404).
