## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Estimated payments are required when the expected tax, after withholding and refundable credits, is $500 or more. The threshold is the same for every filing status. No filing-status differences were found.

- Estimated Tax page, 2026, source 1: "You must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe $500 or more in Minnesota income tax after subtracting your withholding and refundable credits."
- Minn. Stat. § 289A.25 subd. 4, source 3: "reduced by the credits allowable is less than $500."

## 2. Required annual payment

The required annual payment is the lesser of:

- 90% of the current year's tax (66⅔% for farmers and commercial fishermen; the form rounds this to 66.7%, 0.667) (corrected in verification), or
- 100% of the prior year's tax, or 110% if prior-year federal AGI exceeded $150,000.

The $150,000 threshold has no married-filing-separately variant in the statute or on the form. For a part-year resident or nonresident, "AGI" means the AGI assigned to Minnesota.

- M15 instructions, TY2026, source 2: "100% of your prior year's total tax liability (110% if your federal adjusted gross income is more than $150,000)".
- § 289A.25 subd. 5(2)(i), source 3: "for an individual who is not a Minnesota resident for the entire year, the term 'adjusted gross income' means the Minnesota share".

Filed return and 12-month year: both are required for the prior-year option.

- § 289A.25 subd. 5(2), source 3: "if a return showing a liability for the taxes was filed by the taxpayer for the preceding taxable year of 12 months."
- M15 line 5, TY2026, source 2: "If you did not file a 2025 return, or it did not cover the full 12-month period, skip line 5".

Nonresidents and part-year residents must have had at least $1 of Minnesota tax in the prior year to use the prior-year option.

- Estimated Tax page, 2026, source 1: "you must have had at least $1 of Minnesota tax liability to use 100% of your prior year's tax."

High-income taxpayers do not lose the prior-year option; they use 110%.

## 3. Due dates and shares

There are four equal installments of 25%, due April 15, June 15 and September 15 of the tax year and January 15 of the next year.

- § 289A.25 subd. 5, source 3: "The amount of any installment required to be paid shall be 25 percent of the required annual payment".

Weekend and holiday shift: yes, to the next business day.

- Estimated Tax page, 2026, source 1: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the Minnesota Department of Revenue must receive your payment by the next business day."

The web page's wording "must receive" is not the whole rule. Minnesota has a postmark rule. A mailed payment counts as paid on its US Postal Service postmark date. An electronic payment counts as paid on its confirmation time-and-date stamp. Private meter postmarks do not count. (corrected in verification)

- § 270C.395 subd. 1, source 9: "the date of the United States postmark stamped on the cover in which the document or payment is mailed".
- § 645.151, source 10: due on a weekend or holiday, a payment "is timely delivered or filed if it is delivered or filed on the next succeeding day".

January waiver: no addition to tax on the fourth installment if the return is filed and the tax paid in full by January 31.

- § 289A.25 subd. 9, source 3: "then no addition to tax is imposed under subdivision 2 with respect to any underpayment of the fourth required installment".
- Estimated Tax page, 2026, source 1: "You do not need to make the January 15 payment if you file your Minnesota income tax return and pay the entire amount by January 31."

## 4. Withholding

By default, withholding is treated as paid evenly on the four due dates. The taxpayer may show the actual dates instead. Refundable credits are also treated as payments and spread evenly the same way.

- M15 line 14, TY2026, source 2: "You are considered to have paid Minnesota withholding evenly during the year unless you show otherwise."
- § 289A.25 subd. 8, source 3: "an equal part of those amounts is considered paid on the installment date ... unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which the amounts were actually withheld".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if the tax minus withholding and credits is under $500. M15 line 3, TY2026, source 2: "If less than $500, STOP HERE; you do not owe an underpayment penalty".
- No liability in the prior year: no penalty, but only if all three conditions are met: no prior-year liability, a full-year Minnesota resident in the prior year, and a 12-month prior year. M15, TY2026, source 2: "You did not have a Minnesota tax liability on line 17 of your 2025 Form M1".
- First-year residents: no special exemption. The no-liability exception requires full-year prior residence, so it does not cover them. § 289A.25 subd. 6(a)(3), source 3: "the individual was a resident of Minnesota throughout the preceding taxable year."
- Part-year residents: the required annual payment uses Minnesota assignable AGI. The prior-year option needs at least $1 of prior-year Minnesota liability (answer 2).
- Farmers and commercial fishermen (at least two-thirds of gross income): one installment at 66⅔%, due January 15, or none if the return is filed and paid by March 1. M15, TY2026, source 2: "If you filed Form M1 and paid your entire income tax by March 1, 2027, or paid two-thirds of your income tax by January 15, 2027, you do not have to complete this schedule."
- Farmer definition (corrected in verification): the statute uses IRC § 6654(i)(2). That test is met by two-thirds of gross income from farming or fishing in the current year, or on the prior year's return. The department's publications state only the current-year test. 26 U.S.C. § 6654(i)(2)(B), source 12: "shown on the return of the individual for the preceding taxable year is at least 66 2/3 percent".
- Retired, disabled, casualty or disaster: Minnesota follows the IRS. If the IRS waives the federal penalty, Minnesota does too, and the taxpayer includes a copy of the federal request with Form M1. M15, TY2026, source 2: "If the IRS does not require you to pay additional charges for underestimating your federal tax because you are newly retired or disabled, or because of a casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances, do not complete Schedule M15." Statute: § 289A.25 subd. 7, source 3, which cross-references IRC § 6654(e)(3). Under the statute the commissioner makes the determination, not the IRS. The form's instruction to rely on the IRS decision is the department's practice. (corrected in verification) § 289A.25 subd. 7: "to the extent the commissioner determines that the provisions of section 6654(e)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code apply."

## 6. Annualised income method

Allowed, using the "Worksheet - Annualized Income Installment" on the back of Schedule M15. The worksheet starts from federal Form 2210 Schedule AI lines 1-11 with Minnesota modifications. The periods and percentages are the same as federal: 3, 5, 8 and 12 months, and 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%.

- M15, TY2026, source 2: "If you use this method for one payment due date, you must use it for all payment due dates."
- § 289A.25 subd. 5(3), source 3: "The applicable percentage of the tax is 22.5 percent in the case of the first installment, 45 percent for the second installment".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like "amount in lieu of interest". It is charged on each installment's shortfall, for the days from the due date until paid or April 15 of the next year, whichever is earlier.

- M15 line 18, TY2026, source 2: "Number of days between the payment due date and the date on line 17".

**Rate rule.** A single rate applies to the whole tax year: the rate in effect on the January 1 that begins the year. That rate is set by October 15 of the prior year, equal to the prime rate averaged over the six months ending September 30 and rounded to the nearest full percent. The rate does not change mid-year, and the amount does not itself bear interest after the return due date.

- § 270C.40 subd. 4, source 4: "The amount in lieu of interest for that taxable year shall be the amount determined in subdivision 5 for January 1 on which begins the taxable year".
- § 270C.40 subd. 5, source 4: "equal to the prime rate charged by banks during the six-month period ending on September 30 of that year, rounded to the nearest full percent."

**Current rate.** 7% for 2026: on M15 TY2026 line 20, and in the Penalties and Interest table ("2026 ... 7%", source 5). The table gives 8% for 2024-2025. Under § 270C.40 subd. 4 that makes the TY2025 rate 8%. The TY2025 M15 prints 7% instead (corrected in verification; see the conflicts in the verification section).

**Short method.** An optional short method is available when payments were all withholding, or four equal timely amounts. For 2026 it multiplies the underpayment by 2%, then subtracts 0.000192 per day for amounts paid before April 15, 2027. The 0.000192 daily factor equals 7% ÷ 365. The 2% factor does not match 7%: at 7% the equivalent factor is about 4.66%. 2% matches a 3% rate. (corrected in verification; arithmetic in the verification section)

Form: Schedule M15. The penalty goes on Form M1 line 27.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise

- The 110% threshold of $150,000 has no $75,000 married-filing-separately variant.
- Nonresidents and part-year residents need at least $1 of prior-year Minnesota tax to use the prior-year option.
- The no-prior-liability exception requires a full prior year of Minnesota residence.
- Refundable credits count as payments spread evenly over the year.
- A single annual rate is fixed at the start of the year and does not change quarterly.
- In practice waivers follow the IRS's decision rather than a separate state request. The statute leaves the decision to the commissioner. (corrected in verification)
- The threshold is $500.
- The form's short-method factor (2%) does not match its own 7% rate. (added in verification)

## 9. Local income taxes

None found. The Minnesota sources reviewed (sources 1, 2, 5 and 6) mention no city, county or school-district income tax. State law prohibits local income taxes (added in verification). § 477A.016, source 11: "No county, city, town or other taxing authority shall increase a present tax or impose a new tax on sales or income."

## 10. Ambiguities and conflicts

- The only M15 found is the TY2026 "FINAL DRAFT — 10/1/26". The TY2025 schedule could not be fetched (404 at both URLs found), so the TY2025 rate comes only from the "2024-2025 ... 8%" row of source 5. (corrected in verification: the TY2025 M15 is at source 7 and prints 7%, not 8%. See the verification section.)
- The statute's 110% test reads "the adjusted gross income shown on the return" (Minnesota statute), while the M15 and web page say "federal adjusted gross income". For full-year residents these are presumably the same. For nonresidents, the statute substitutes the Minnesota share.
- § 289A.25 subd. 10 defines a farmer or fisherman by reference to IRC § 6654(i)(2). The department's page and M15 say instead "at least two-thirds of your annual gross income". Whether prior-year income can qualify, as the federal definition may allow, is not stated in the Minnesota publications. IRC § 6654(i)(2) itself was not checked.

### Conflicts resolved

1. **TY2025 rate: 8% (statute) vs 7% (TY2025 M15).** § 270C.40 subd. 4 fixes the rate at the one for the January 1 that begins the tax year. The department lists 8% for 2025 (source 15: "The interest rate for 2025 is 8%"). The formula agrees: April–September 2024 monthly prime was 8.50% five times and 8.30%, averaging 8.47%, which rounds to 8% (source 16). The TY2025 M15 line 20 prints 7%, and its daily factor 0.000192 equals 7% ÷ 365. The TY2024 M15 printed 8% with 0.000219, which equals 8% ÷ 365. The statute has not been amended since 2005 (source 14). The statute governs, so TY2025 is 8% in law. Still unresolved: whether the department assesses TY2025 at the 7% its form computes. The form's 7% equals the calendar-2026 rate. For TY2026 this does not matter, because 2026 and the computed 2027 rate are both 7%.

2. **Short-method factor.** For TY2026, an underpayment of each installment held to April 15, 2027 runs 365, 304, 212 and 90 days, an average of 242.75 days. The equivalent factor is 7% × 242.75 ÷ 365 = 4.655%. The form prints 2%. A 2% factor fits a 3% rate (3% × 242.75 ÷ 365 = 1.995%), which was the rate for 2021–2022. The TY2024 and TY2025 forms also print 2%, at 8% and 7%. The statute charges the rate "upon the amount of the underpayment for the period of the underpayment" (§ 289A.25 subd. 2). The regular method follows that rule. The short method as printed charges less than half of it. A data format should compute from the rate and days, not store the 2% factor.

3. **66.7% vs 66⅔%.** The statute governs. The form's 0.667 is 0.033 percentage points higher.

4. **"Adjusted gross income shown on the return" vs "federal adjusted gross income".** No conflict. Section 289A.25 does not define AGI separately. The M15 uses federal Form 1040 line 11 for full-year residents. For nonresidents and part-year residents, the M15 line 5 instructions match subd. 5(2)(i). One gap remains: subd. 5(2)(i) does not say whether "not a Minnesota resident for the entire year" refers to the prior year or the current year.

5. **Farmer definition.** The statute governs through IRC § 6654(i)(2), which includes a prior-year test. Minnesota's IRC reference date is now May 1, 2026 (Laws 2026, ch. 128, art. 1, s. 1; it was May 1, 2023). The amendment notes in source 12 show no change to § 6654(i)(2) in that period.

6. **"Must receive" vs postmark.** § 270C.395 governs. A timely USPS postmark or electronic confirmation stamp is timely payment.

7. **Who grants the § 6654(e)(3) waiver.** The statute gives it to the commissioner. The form says to follow the IRS decision. Both apply: the form is how the commissioner exercises the determination in practice.
