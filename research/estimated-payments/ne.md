## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Residents and nonresidents whose tax, after personal exemption credits, is expected to exceed withholding and other credits by $500 or more. No filing-status differences were found for the threshold.

- [NE-ES26], 2026: "is expected to exceed their income tax withholding and other credits by $500 or more."
- [NRS-2769](1)(a): no payment is required "if the estimated tax can reasonably be expected to be less than five hundred dollars".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90%, or 66⅔% for those engaged in farming, ranching or fishing.
- Prior year: 100% of the prior year's tax. This requires a full 12-month prior year.
- High income: 110% of the prior year's tax if prior-year federal AGI was over $150,000. The threshold is $75,000 if married filing separately. Farmers, ranchers and fishermen are excluded from the 110% rule.
- High-income taxpayers keep the prior-year option at 110%; they do not lose it.

Quotes:

- [NE-ES26], 2026: "90% (66 ⅔% for those engaged in farming, ranching, and fishing) of the income tax liability for the year"
- [NE-ES26], 2026: "100% of the income tax shown on the Nebraska return for the full 12‑month preceding tax year, if federal AGI was $150,000 or less"
- [NE-ES26], 2026, worksheet line 16b: "110% of that amount if you are not engaged in farming or fishing and the AGI shown on that return is more than $150,000"
- [NE-ES26], 2026, describes the 110% as federally derived: "110% (the applicable federal percentage) of the preceding year's income tax reported on a Nebraska return, if 2025 federal AGI was above $150,000."
- [NE-2210N-26], 2026, line 7 instructions: "If the 2025 income tax year was for less than 12 months, do not complete this line."

The 12-month rule is stated. NOT FOUND: an explicit requirement that the prior-year return was actually filed.

(corrected in verification) The filed-return requirement is stated. [NE-2210N-26], 2026, general instructions: "100% of your 2025 income tax liability (if you filed a 2025 return that covered a full 12 months)".

(corrected in verification) The 110% rule and the 66⅔% farmer rule have a regulatory source. [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.01: "If federal adjusted gross income is more than $150,000, ($75,000 for married filing separately), then the applicable federal percentage" applies. The same regulation sets 90% (66-2/3% for farmers and fishermen) and 100% of the preceding year's tax. [NRS-2790](3) authorises this by tying the computation to federal law.

## 3. Due dates and shares

- Due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15, in equal installments of 25% each.
- If a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, it moves to the next business day.
- If the obligation first arises later in the year, the first payment catches up: ½ of the required amount at the June installment, ¾ at the September installment, or all of it at the January installment.
- The January installment is waived if the return is filed and the tax paid by January 31.
- By statute, the due dates follow the federal dates unless the Tax Commissioner sets others.

Quotes:

- [NE-ES26], 2026: "in equal installments on or before April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027."
- [NE-ES26], 2026: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, taxpayers have until the next business day to make the payment."
- [NE-2210N-26], 2026, line 10: "Enter 25% of line 8 in each column"
- [NE-2210N-26], 2026: "If you file your return and pay the income tax due by January 31, 2027 … you will not owe a penalty"
- [NRS-2769](5): estimated tax "shall be paid on or before the dates prescribed by the laws of the United States for payment of estimated federal income tax".
- (corrected in verification) The catch-up is worksheet guidance in [NE-ES26], line 19 ("enter ½ of the amount on line 18" for a first payment in June). Form 2210N still charges each column at 25% (line 10). [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.01 likewise divides the required amount "by the number of installment dates". A later-arising obligation avoids the earlier penalty only through annualisation.

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid evenly, one quarter on each due date, unless the taxpayer shows the actual dates. For nonresidents, tax paid on their behalf by a pass-through entity or fiduciary is treated as paid on the last day of the entity's tax year, unless shown otherwise.

- [NE-2210N-26], 2026, line 11: "One-fourth of the Nebraska income tax withheld … is considered paid on each required installment date, unless you establish other dates"
- [NE-2210N-26], 2026: pass-through payments are "considered paid on the last day of the organization's taxable year, unless you establish other dates".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- **Tax below $500.** [NE-2210N-26], 2026: no penalty if tax "minus applicable credits and the amount of income tax you paid through income tax withholding is less than $500."
- **No liability in the prior year.** [NE-2210N-26], 2026: "You had no Nebraska income tax liability for 2025, you were a U.S. citizen or resident for the entire year". The 2025 return also had to be "for a full 12 months".
- **First-year residents.** These are covered by the first-time filer exception. [NE-2210N-26], 2026: "You are a first-time filer for Nebraska income tax purposes".
- **Part-year residents.** NOT FOUND as a separate rule. The forms apply to "every resident and nonresident". (corrected in verification) Confirmed: [NRS-2769](1) and [NE-REG20] REG-20-001.01 also say only "Every resident and nonresident individual".
- **Farmers, ranchers and fishermen.** They qualify if at least two-thirds of gross income in either the current or the prior year comes from farming, ranching or fishing. They may pay all estimated tax by January 15 at 66⅔%. Filing Form 1040N and paying in full by March 1 removes the penalty entirely.
  - [NE-ES26], 2026: "you may pay all of your estimated income tax on or before January 15, 2027 and still avoid a penalty".
  - [NE-2210N-26], 2026: no penalty if "A Form 1040N is filed and the Nebraska income tax due is paid on or before March 1."
- **Retired or disabled.** This is a discretionary waiver, not an automatic exception. [NE-2210N-26], 2026: "In 2025 or 2026, you retired after reaching age 62, or became disabled, and your underpayment was due to reasonable cause."
- **Casualty or disaster.** This is also a discretionary waiver. [NE-2210N-26], 2026: "Underpayment due to casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstance where it would be inequitable to impose the penalty".
- (corrected in verification) Both waivers are in [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.03. REG-20-007.07 says DOR may ask for "police reports, insurance company reports or documentation relating to retirement or disability status".

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed. The taxpayer checks a box at the top of Form 2210N. Paper filers attach their own schedule modelled on the federal one. NOT FOUND: any Nebraska annualisation worksheet.

- [NE-2210N-26], 2026: "Nebraska income tax payments equal or exceed 90% of your Nebraska income tax liability based on annualized income"
- [NE-2210N-26], 2026: "Paper filers attach a separate schedule showing your computation similar to the federal Annualized Income Installment Method Schedule."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

- **Method.** The penalty is interest-like: a percentage per year on each installment's shortfall for the days late. It runs to the payment date, the next due date or April 15, whichever is earliest. Payments apply to the earliest underpayment first. The computation is on Form 2210N, lines 19–23, and the result goes on Form 1040N, line 57.
  - [NE-2210N-26], 2026, line 22a: "Penalty (8% per year on the line 19 amount for the number of days in 2026 on line 21)"
  - [NE-2210N-26], 2026, line 18: "Your payments are applied first to any underpayment balance on an earlier installment."
- **Rate rule.** [NRS-2790](3) says the underpayment is determined "in accordance with the laws of the United States". It names no rate. The rate printed on the form is 8%, and [NE-2210N-25] for 2025 also used 8%.
  - (corrected in verification) The rate rule is in the regulation. [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.03: "The penalty is imposed at the same rate specified for interest in section 45-104.02". It runs to the return due date "without regard to any extensions of time", or the payment date if earlier.
- **Link to the delinquent-tax rate.** The 8% matches the delinquent-tax interest rate in [NE-RR]: "For the two-year period beginning January 1, 2027 through December 31, 2028, the rate … will be 8% per year." The [NE-RR] table also shows "1-1-2025 and after 8%". That rate is set under [NRS-104.02](2): "the average short-term borrowing rate for the federal government during July of the previous year … plus three percentage points." It is redetermined every other year and changes only if the move is at least two points. The forms do not say that the Form 2210N rate comes from [NRS-104.02]. That link is my inference from the matching numbers. See item 10.
  - (corrected in verification) The link is no longer an inference; [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.03 states it. The rate is 8% for all of 2026, and [NE-RR] keeps it at 8% for 2027–2028. [NE-RR] says to use "Simple interest".

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is $500, not $1,000.
- The penalty rate is a flat 8% per year printed on the form. It does not change quarterly.
- Being a first-time Nebraska filer removes the penalty entirely.
- Retirement at 62 or older, or disability, earns only a discretionary waiver, not an automatic exception.
- Fishermen are grouped with farmers and ranchers.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- **Prior-year rule.** [NE-2210N-26]'s general instructions list only "100% of your 2025 income tax liability (if you filed a 2025 return that covered a full 12 months)". They do not mention 110%. Line 7 of the same form does apply 110% above $150,000 AGI.
- **Source of the 8% rate.** The 8% rate is printed on the form, but no Nebraska source states the rule that sets it. [NRS-2790](3) points to federal law. The number matches the [NRS-104.02] delinquent-tax rate in [NE-RR] instead of the federal § 6621 rate.
- **Which AGI the 110% test uses.** [NE-ES26] says "if 2025 federal AGI was above $150,000". This is prior-year AGI. The MFS variant on worksheet line 16b depends on the current-year filing status.
- **Folder naming.** DOR's "2026" folder holds tax-year-2026 forms (Form 2210N) together with the 2027 Form 1040N-ES. The 2026 Form 1040N-ES is in the "2025" folder. The rule text in [NE-ES26] and [NE-ES27] is identical apart from the year.

### Resolved conflicts

- **Prior-year rule and 110%.** [NE-2210N-26]'s general instructions omit 110%, but its line 7 applies it. [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.01 governs and includes the 110% rule. Use line 7.
- **Source of the 8% rate.** Resolved by [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.03: the penalty uses the [NRS-104.02] rate. That rate is 8% for 2025–2028 per [NE-RR].
- **Which AGI the 110% test uses.** Prior-year federal AGI ([NE-ES26]; [NE-2210N-26] line 7: "your federal adjusted gross income (AGI) in 2025"). The married-filing-separately threshold depends on the current-year status ([NE-ES26] line 16b: "if your filing status for 2026 is married, filing separately"). [NE-REG20] does not say which year.
- **Threshold in the regulation.** [NE-REG20] REG-20-001.01 and REG-20-007.06B still say $300. [NRS-2769](1)(a), amended in 2008, says $500, and the 2026 forms agree. The statute governs; the 2006 regulation is out of date.
- **First-time filer exception.** It appears on [NE-ES26] and [NE-2210N-26] but not in [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.06 or the statutes. The forms are DOR's current published position. UNRESOLVED whether it rests on any rule. It matters only for a first-time filer with prior-year income elsewhere.
- **US citizenship condition.** The forms require US citizenship or residence for the zero-liability exception. [NE-REG20] REG-20-007.06A does not. The forms follow federal law, which [NRS-2790](3) adopts.
- **Folder naming.** Confirmed. The 2026 Form 1040N-ES is under /2025/, and the 2027 edition under /2026/. Their rule text is identical apart from the years.
