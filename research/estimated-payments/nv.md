# Nevada

Tax year researched: 2026. All sources accessed 2026-10-20.

## Sources

1. Constitution of the State of Nevada, Article 10, Section 1(9), current text on the Nevada Legislature site. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Const/NvConst.html
2. Nevada Revised Statutes chapter 363C (Commerce Tax), current text, NRS 363C.020 and 363C.200. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-363C.html

## 1. Does Nevada tax individual income for tax year 2026?

**No.** The constitution bars any income tax on the wages or personal income of natural persons. That covers wages, interest, dividends, capital gains and any other personal income.

- Source 1, Art. 10, Sec. 1(9): "No income tax shall be levied upon the wages or personal income of natural persons."

## 2. Estimated payment rules

Not applicable. No individual income-type tax exists.

## 3. Local income taxes

None. The constitutional bar in Art. 10, Sec. 1(9) is not limited to the state, so it applies to any income tax on natural persons.

## 4. Ambiguities

- **Business income is expressly taxable.** The same subsection continues: "taxes may be levied upon the income or revenue of any business in whatever form it may be conducted for profit in the State." (Source 1).
- **Commerce Tax reaches some sole proprietors.** The Commerce Tax is a gross-revenue tax, not an income tax. It applies to a "business entity" with Nevada gross revenue above $4,000,000. That includes a natural person who files Schedule C, E or F for the business.
  - Source 2, NRS 363C.020(2)(b): "A natural person, unless that person is engaging in a business and is required to file … a Schedule C"
  - Source 2, NRS 363C.200(1): the tax applies to "each business entity whose Nevada gross revenue in a taxable year exceeds $4,000,000"
  - The return is due on the 45th day after the taxable year ends (NRS 363C.200(2)). Rates by industry and payment rules were not researched.
- **No Nevada Department of Taxation page was read.** The constitution was used as the primary source.
