## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay if you expect to owe at least $500 after withholding and credits, and your withholding plus credits will be less than the smaller of 90% of 2026 tax or 100% of 2025 tax. The forms show no filing-status difference in the $500 threshold.

- Form 40ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "You expect to owe at least $500 in tax for 2026, after subtracting your withholding and credits."
- Form 40ES worksheet, TY2026, source 2, line 11: "If less than five hundred dollars ($500), no estimated tax is required to be filed".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% if AGI is more than $150,000, or more than $75,000 for married filing separately.
- The prior-year return must cover 12 months. The 2210AL instructions also say that with no prior-year return the prior-year option is unavailable.
- A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option. The percentage rises to 110%.

Quotes:
- Form 40ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "b. 100% of the tax shown on your 2025 tax return. Your 2025 tax return must cover all 12 months."
- Form 40ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "If your Alabama AGI for 2025 was more than $150,000 ($75,000 if ... Married Filing a Separate Return) substitute 110%".
- Form 2210AL Instructions, TY2025, source 4: "If you didn't file a return for 2024 or if your 2024 tax year was less than 12 months, don't complete line 7."

Which year's AGI sets the 110% threshold is inconsistent between the two documents. (corrected in verification) The statute settles it: prior-year AGI. §40-18-80 adopts §6654(d)(1)(C)(i), which uses "the adjusted gross income shown on the return of the individual for the preceding taxable year". See question 10.

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes. The penalty form uses 25% of the required annual payment in each column.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day, including State holidays.
- A taxpayer whose requirement first arises later in the year pays the estimate in fewer, larger installments (1/3, 1/2, or all of it).
- January waiver (corrected in verification): yes, by statute. §40-18-80 adopts §6654(h). No penalty applies to the fourth installment if the return is filed and paid in full by January 31. ADOR's 40ES and 2210AL do not mention this. Farmers are covered in question 5.
  - 26 U.S.C. §6654(h), source 9: "on or before January 31 of the following taxable year, the taxpayer files a return ... and pays in full".

Quotes:
- Form 40ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "paid in full or in equal installments on or before April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027."
- Form 40ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "If the 15th falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or State holiday, the due date will then be considered the following business day."
- Form 2210AL, TY2025, source 3, Part III line 1: "enter 25% (.25) of Part I, line 8, Form 2210AL, in each columns".
- Form 40ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1, voucher schedule: requirement met after 4-1-2026 and before 6-2-2026, due 6-15-2026, "1/3 of line 11".

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid in equal amounts on the four due dates. The taxpayer can elect to treat it as paid on the dates actually withheld, by checking box D.

- Form 2210AL, TY2025, source 3, box D: "treating the Alabama income tax withheld from your income as paid on the dates it was actually withheld, instead of in equal amounts".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if tax after other payments, minus withholding, is less than $500. Form 2210AL, TY2025, source 3, line 6: "If less than $500, stop; you do not owe a penalty."
- No liability in the prior year (corrected in verification): no penalty, by statute. §40-18-80 adopts §6654(e)(2). The conditions are a 12-month prior year, no liability for tax in that year, and US citizenship or residence throughout it. Section 40-18-2 (Alabama income tax) is substituted for the federal tax, so "liability" means Alabama liability. Neither the 2210AL nor its instructions implements this exception.
  - Code of Ala. §40-18-80(a), source 7: "Individuals shall pay estimated income tax in accordance with 26 U.S.C. § 6654 except:"
  - 26 U.S.C. §6654(e)(2)(B), source 9: "the individual did not have any liability for tax for the preceding taxable year".
- First-year residents (corrected in verification): no exception names them. Under the §6654(e)(2) exception above, someone who owed no Alabama tax for a 12-month prior year, and was a US citizen or resident throughout it, owes no penalty. That reading is mine, from the statute. ADOR does not state it.
- Nonresident aliens (added in verification): §6654(j) applies through §40-18-80. That means three installments (June 15, September 15, January 15), with 50% due in the first.
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND as an exception. Form 2210AL attaches to "FORM 40 OR FORM 40NR", so nonresidents are covered.
- Farmers and fishermen: qualify if at least two-thirds of gross income is from farming or fishing. Part IV uses 66 2/3% of current-year tax, or 100% of prior-year tax, with one payment due January 15. Farmers need no estimate if they file by March 1 and pay in full.
  - Form 40ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "farmers file their tax return on or before March 1, 2027, and pay the total tax ... they need not file estimated tax."
  - Form 2210AL, TY2025, source 3, Part IV line 4: "Multiply line 3 by 66 2/3% (0.667)".
  - Form 2210AL, TY2025, source 3, Part IV line 12: "Number of days from January 15, 2026, to the date on line 11."
- Retired or disabled: waiver available if you retired after age 62 or became disabled in the year or the prior year, with reasonable cause. Form 2210AL Instructions, TY2025, source 4: "you retired after reaching age 62 or became disabled, and your underpayment was due to reasonable cause".
- Casualty or disaster: waiver available. Form 2210AL Instructions, TY2025, source 4: "The underpayment was due to a casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstance, and it would be inequitable to impose the penalty."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, on Schedule AI of Form 2210AL. The periods match federal (1/1–3/31, 1/1–5/31, 1/1–8/31, 1/1–12/31). The annualisation factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5, 1. The applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5%, 90%.

- Form 2210AL, TY2025, source 3, box C: "your penalty is reduced or eliminated when figured using the annualized income installment method. You must figure the penalty using Schedule AI".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like charge. It is each installment's underpayment times the days late, divided by 365, times the rate. The days run from the installment due date to the earlier of the payment date or April 15 of the following year.

- The 2025 form prints the rate as a fixed 7% (.07). Form 2210AL, TY2025, source 3, line 11: "Underpayment X No. of days on line 10 X .07 / 365".
- How the rate is set: ADOR's interest page says interest follows 26 U.S.C. §6621 under Code of Ala. §40-1-44. Source 5: "the Alabama Department of Revenue will calculate interest on underpayments and overpayments (where applicable) at the same annual rate". Source 5 lists 7% for every quarter of 2025, and for 2026: 7% (Q1), 6% (Q2), 7% (Q3), 7% (Q4).
- Statutory basis: the 2210AL instructions cite §40-2A-11 and §40-18-80. Form 2210AL Instructions, TY2025, source 4: "calculate underpayment of estimated tax penalty as prescribed in Section 40-2A-11 and 40-18-80 of the Code of Alabama 1975."
- (corrected in verification) The statute text was found (source 7). §40-18-80 adopts §6654(a), which charges "the underpayment rate established under section 6621" for the period of the underpayment. §40-1-44(a) (source 8) also ties Alabama interest to the §6621 underpayment rate. §40-2A-11 contains no estimated-tax rule. Its only relevant part is subsection (h), a general reasonable-cause waiver of penalties.
- (corrected in verification) A rate change during the penalty period should split the computation, because §6654(a) applies the rate for each period. The 2025 form prints one 7% rate through April 15, 2026, but ADOR's table shows 6% from April 1, 2026. Under the statute, the days from April 1 to April 15, 2026 would be charged at 6%. See question 10.
- Form: Form 2210AL. The penalty goes on Schedule ATP, Part II, line 1 (Form 40) or Form 40NR, line 30.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is $500, not $1,000.
- A State holiday shifts a due date, as well as weekends. Source 1: "If the 15th falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or State holiday".
- The 2025 form uses one flat annual rate for the whole computation (7%). It is not split by rate period, although ADOR's own rate table shows 6% for Q2 2026. (corrected in verification) The statute applies the §6621 rate for each period, so the form simplifies the law.
- (added in verification) The federal January 31 file-and-pay rule and the zero-prior-year exception apply by statute, but ADOR's forms print neither.
- ADOR sends no statement of estimated payments received. Source 2: "The Alabama Department of Revenue does not send notices of amounts paid on estimated tax."
- Farmers may skip estimates by filing and paying by March 1. This matches federal.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Which year's AGI triggers 110%. The 2026 40ES instructions use prior-year AGI ("If your Alabama AGI for 2025 was more than $150,000"), which matches federal. The 2025 2210AL instructions use current-year AGI: "If your adjusted gross income (AGI) for 2025 was more than $150,000" for the 2025 penalty year.
- Line numbering. The 2210AL instructions refer to the required annual payment as "Part I, Line 6". On the 2025 form it is line 8, and line 6 is the $500 test.
- An undefined category. The 2210AL instructions name a special percentage for "qualified individuals with small businesses" but give no rule for it.
- The waiver instructions are internally inconsistent. They say to "Check box A in Part I" (box A is in Part II, or Part IV Section I). They say to "Complete Part IV, lines 1 through line 12" for all filers. They say to "Attach Form 2210A", a form not otherwise described.
- Farmers' prior-year test. Part IV line 7 uses 100% of prior-year tax. The 110% high-income rule is not stated for farmers.
- The rate. The 2025 form prints 7% for the whole year, which matches ADOR's 2025 rates. The source does not say how a mid-year rate change would be handled.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Which year's AGI triggers 110%.** The statute governs: prior-year AGI. §40-18-80 adopts §6654(d)(1)(C)(i), which reads "for the preceding taxable year". The 40ES (2026) agrees. The 2210AL instructions' "AGI for 2025" for the 2025 penalty year is wrong. §40-18-80 has not been amended since Act 2010-685 (ALISON history), so no newer law explains the instructions. Still unresolved: the statute does not say whether "adjusted gross income" means federal AGI or Alabama AGI. The 40ES says "Alabama AGI".
- **Farmers' prior-year test.** Resolved: 100%, never 110%. §6654(i)(1)(C) computes the farmer installment "without regard to subparagraph (C) of subsection (d)(1)", and §40-18-80 adopts it.
- **"Qualified individuals with small businesses."** No such rule exists in current §6654 (source 9 read in full from (d)(1) to (d)(2)). The phrase is stale wording and carries no rule.
- **The rate across a rate change.** The statute governs. §6654(a) applies the §6621 rate for the period of the underpayment, so April 1–15, 2026 days should be charged at 6%. The 2025 form charges 7% throughout. The difference is at most 15 days at 1 point.
- **Line numbering and the waiver steps** (Q10 items 2 and 4). These are drafting errors on the form; no source resolves them. They do not change any number.
