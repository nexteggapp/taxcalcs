## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay estimated tax in two cases:

- You expect more than $5,000 of income not subject to Kentucky withholding, or adjusted gross income at the filing threshold.
- Your estimated tax is more than $500.

(corrected in verification) These are not two separate cases. [KRS-141.305](1) requires payments if either income test in (1)(a) or (1)(b) is met. [KRS-141.305](2) then removes the requirement when the estimated tax is $500 or less. So payments are required when an income test is met and the estimated tax is more than $500.

In practice: you expect to owe at least $500 after withholding and refundable credits, and withholding will be below the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's. No filing-status difference was found in the threshold.

(corrected in verification) Exactly $500 is not covered. The statute and [2210K-25] line 5 both exclude "$500 or less". The [ESI-26] phrase "at least $500" does not govern.

- [KRS-141.305](1)(a): "Gross income from sources other than wages upon which Kentucky income tax will be withheld can reasonably be expected to exceed five thousand dollars".
- [KRS-141.305](2): "No estimated tax shall be required if the estimated tax liability can reasonably be expected to be five hundred dollars ($500) or less."
- [ESI-26]: "You expect to owe at least $500 in tax for 2026, after subtracting your withholding and refundable credits."

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax. The prior-year return must cover all 12 months.
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% if prior-year Kentucky AGI was more than $150,000, or more than $75,000 if married filing separately. This does not apply to farmers.
- High-income taxpayers do not lose the prior-year option. They use 110% of it.
- Married couples filing separately on a combined return add their incomes together to test the 110% threshold [TAM].

Quotes:

- [ESI-26]: "90% of the tax to be shown on your 2026 tax return; or (b) 100% of the tax shown on your 2025 tax return."
- [ESI-26]: "Your 2025 tax return must cover all 12 months."
- [ESI-26]: "If your Kentucky adjusted gross income for 2025 was more than $150,000 ($75,000 if your filing status for 2026 is married filing separately)".
- [TAM]: "Married couples in Kentucky that file separately on a combined form should add their incomes together to determine if they meet the 110% threshold."

## 3. Due dates and share due

There are four equal installments of 25% each, on the federal dates. The 2026 dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. A payment date on a holiday or weekend moves to the next working day. The 2025 voucher shows the shift: June 16, 2025.

The January voucher is not required if the return is filed and paid by February 1, 2027. The instructions attach this only to cases where the requirement first arises after September 1. See item 10.

- [ESI-26]: "Installments for calendar year 2026 are due April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027."
- [TAM]: "If a payment date falls on a holiday or weekend, the applicable payment date is the next working day."
- [2210K-25] line 9: "Required Installments. Enter 25% (.25) of Part II, line 8 in each column".
- [ESI-26]: "If the change occurs after September 1, 2026".
- [ESI-26], same sentence: "the voucher is not required if the 2026 Kentucky income tax return is filed and ... paid on or before February 1, 2027."

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid evenly, 25% per period. NOT FOUND: an election to use actual withholding dates, in [2210K-25], [ESI-26] or [TAM]. See item 10.

- [2210K-25] line 10: "If you have Kentucky income tax withheld, multiply the total by 25 percent (.25) and enter in columns A through D."

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if 2025 tax less withholding and refundable credits is $500 or less. [2210K-25] line 5: "If the result is $500 or less, stop here; you do not owe a penalty."
- No liability in the prior year: there is an exception for a citizen or resident alien with no prior-year liability, if the prior year was a full 12 months.
  - [2210K-25]: "You had no liability for 2024, you were a U.S. Citizen or resident alien for the entire year".
  - [2210K-25], same item: "your 2024 tax return ... was for a full 12 months."
  - [ESI-26]: "You had no liability for 2025 if your total tax was zero or you didn't have to file a Kentucky tax return."
- First-year and part-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate rule. The no-liability exception above covers someone with no prior-year Kentucky liability.
- Farmers and fishermen: this applies if at least two-thirds of gross income in the current or prior year came from farming or fishing.
  - The current-year percentage is 66 2/3%, and the 110% rule does not apply.
  - Pay all estimated tax by January 15, or file and pay in full by March 1. For tax year 2025 that date was March 2, 2026.
  - [ESI-26]: "Pay all of your estimated tax by January 15, 2027."
  - [ESI-26]: "File your 2026 Form 740 or Form 740–NP by March 1, 2027, and pay the total tax due."
- Decedent's estate, and a trust owned by the decedent, within two years of death: exempt. [2210K-25]: "You're filing a decedent's estate return for any tax year ending before the date that is two years after the decedent's death."
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in [2210K-25], [ESI-26] or [TAM].
- Casualty or disaster: NOT FOUND as a specific exception. (corrected in verification) The reasonable-cause rules in 103 KAR 1:040 include a catastrophic event that destroys records, and death or serious illness. 103 KAR 1:040 Section 1(5): "Destruction or unavailability of taxpayer records by a catastrophic event." A general reasonable-cause waiver exists. [KRS-141.305](3)(e): "Any waiver of penalties shall be performed as provided in KRS 131.175." [TAM]: "Waivers of penalty must be based on reasonable cause per KRS 131.010(9)."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

The method is allowed. Use Form 2210-K, Part III.

- Periods are the federal ones: 1/1 to 3/31, 5/31, 8/31 and 12/31.
- Factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1, and percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%.
- If you use it for one due date, you must use it for all of them.

Quotes:

- [2210K-25]: "If you use Part III for any payment due dates, you must use it for all payment due dates."
- [ESI-26]: "you may be able to lower or eliminate the amount of your required estimated tax payment for one or more periods".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like charge on each installment's shortfall, by days late. It is computed in the federal IRC 6654 way, but at a Kentucky rate. Shortfalls carry forward until paid. The last measurement date is April 15 of the following year.

- The DOR sets the rate under KRS 131.183. The "tax interest rate" is the adjusted prime rate rounded to a whole percent, reset annually from the September prime rate and effective January 1.
- [2210K-25] uses 10% for calendar 2025 and 9% for calendar 2026.
- [RATE-26] sets the 2026 tax interest rate at 7%. Unpaid taxes accrue at 9%, which is that rate plus 2%. So the form's 9% matches the rate plus 2%. See item 10.

Compute it on Form 2210-K, Part II, lines 9 to 23.

- [KRS-141.305](3)(d): "The tax interest rate identified under KRS 131.183 shall be used to determine the underpayment rate".
- [2210K-25] line 21: "The percentage rate for calendar years 2025 is 10 percent and 2026 is 9 percent."
- [RATE-26]: "established the Tax Interest Rate effective January 1, 2026, to be 7 %".
- [RATE-26]: "taxes payable to the Commonwealth that have not been paid ... will accrue interest at the rate of 9 % pursuant to KRS 131.183(2)(a)2."
- [KRS-131.183](1)(b)1.b (cited as (1)(a)1.b before verification; corrected in verification): the rate is reset "if the adjusted prime rate charged by banks during September of that year ... is at least one (1) percentage point more or less".

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is $500, not $1,000.
- There is a second, income-based trigger: non-wage income above $5,000.
- The 110% test uses Kentucky AGI, not federal AGI. Married couples filing separately on a combined return add their incomes together for that test.
- The rate is a Kentucky prime-based annual rate, fixed per calendar year. It is not the federal quarterly rate.
- The farmer date is March 1, as it is federally.

## 9. Local income taxes

These exist. Kentucky cities, counties and school boards levy occupational license taxes on wages and net profits. The Department of Revenue does not administer them, and they are not paid with Form 740-ES. Whether each local tax has its own estimated payments was not researched.

- [DOR-BUS]: "The Department of Revenue does not administer local occupational, net profits or gross receipts taxes."

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Penalty rate: [KRS-141.305](3)(d) points to "the tax interest rate identified under KRS 131.183". That rate is 7% for 2026 [RATE-26]. [KRS-131.183](1)(a) excludes the estimated-tax addition from the general interest charge: "Except for the addition to tax required when an underpayment of estimated tax occurs under KRS 141.044 and 141.305". [2210K-25] nevertheless uses 9% for 2026 (and 10% for 2025), which is the tax interest rate plus 2%.
- January waiver: [ESI-26] attaches the file-by-February-1, 2027 relief to a footnote on requirements first arising after September 1. Federal relief covers everyone who files by January 31. Whether Kentucky gives it to all taxpayers is unclear. [KRS-141.305](3) adopts IRC 6654 "except" provisions that do not apply to Kentucky.
- Withholding election: neither [2210K-25] nor [ESI-26] offers the federal election to treat withholding as paid when withheld. [KRS-141.305](3) says payments are "calculated in the same manner" as IRC 6654, which includes that election.
- Federal waivers: the federal casualty, disaster, retirement and disability waivers of IRC 6654(e)(3) are not mentioned. [KRS-141.305](3)(e) routes all waivers through KRS 131.175 instead.
- Weekend dates on the 2025 form: [2210K-25] prints the second 2025 installment as "6/15/25". [ESV-25] prints "June 16, 2025", the weekend-shifted date.
- 2026 voucher: no 2026 Form 740-ES voucher was found. The 2026 dates come from [ESI-26]. (corrected in verification) The 2026 voucher exists at https://revenue.ky.gov/Forms/740es%202026.pdf, form 42A740ES (10/25). It prints April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027.

### Conflicts resolved

- Penalty rate for 2026: 7% or 9%. NOT resolved; it changes the number.
  - [KRS-141.305](3)(d) uses "The tax interest rate identified under KRS 131.183". [RATE-26] names 7% as "the Tax Interest Rate" for 2026.
  - [KRS-131.183](1)(b)1.a defines that rate as the adjusted prime rate "rounded to the nearest full percent as adjusted by subsection (2) of this section". Subsection (2)(a) adds 2%.
  - [KRS-131.183](1)(a) takes the estimated-tax addition out of ordinary interest, and (5) calls it a penalty.
  - [2210K-25] line 21 applies 9% for 2026, which is 7% plus 2%.
  - The statute can be read either way. DOR's form, which is what DOR assesses, uses 9%. No regulation or ruling settling it was found.
- January 31 relief. Partly resolved. [KRS-141.305](3) applies IRC 6654 "at the same time and calculated in the same manner", which includes the federal file-by-January-31 rule. [ESI-26] mentions February 1, 2027 only in the after-September-1 footnote. February 1, 2027 is January 31, 2027 (a Sunday) moved to the next working day. Whether DOR gives the relief to every filer is not stated in any DOR document.
- Withholding election. NOT resolved. [KRS-141.305](3) imports the IRC 6654 method, which lets you treat withholding as paid when withheld. [2210K-25] line 10 offers only the 25% spread.
- Federal casualty, disaster, retirement and disability waivers. Resolved: they do not carry over as such. [KRS-141.305](3)(e): "Any waiver of penalties shall be performed as provided in KRS 131.175." [KAR-1:040] lists the reasonable-cause grounds, which include catastrophic destruction of records and death or serious illness. They do not include retirement or disability.
- Weekend dates on the 2025 form. Resolved: June 16, 2025 governs. [TAM]: "If a payment date falls on a holiday or weekend, the applicable payment date is the next working day." [2210K-25] prints the unshifted date.
- $500 threshold at exactly $500. Resolved: the statute governs. [KRS-141.305](2) says "five hundred dollars ($500) or less". [2210K-25] line 5 agrees. Its own instructions say "less than $500", and [ESI-26] says "at least $500". KRS 141.305 has not been amended since 2019.
