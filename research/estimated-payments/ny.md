# New York

One estimated-tax system covers four taxes:

- New York State personal income tax
- New York City resident tax
- Yonkers tax (resident surcharge and nonresident earnings tax)
- the Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Mobility Tax (MCTMT) on self-employment earnings

All four are paid on Form IT-2105, and the penalty for all four is computed together on Form IT-2105.9.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Anyone who expects to owe at least $300 of New York State, New York City or Yonkers tax after withholding, credits and pass-through payments. The $300 test applies to each tax separately. For the MCTMT, any amount owed requires estimates. No filing-status differences were found.

- [NY-2105I], 2026: "at least $300 of either New York State, New York City, or Yonkers tax for 2026."
- [NY-2105I], 2026: for the MCTMT, payments are required "if you expect to owe any MCTMT for the tax year".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90%, or 66⅔% for farmers and fishermen.
- Prior year: 100%. A filed return for a full 12-month year is required.
- High income: 110% of the prior year's tax if prior-year NYAGI was over $150,000. The threshold is $75,000 if married filing separately in the current year. The same 110% applies if net self-employment earnings allocated to the MCTD were over $150,000. Farmers and fishermen are excluded from the 110% rule.
- High-income taxpayers keep the prior-year option at 110%; they do not lose it.

Quotes:

- [NY-2105I], 2026: "110% … if you are not a farmer or a fisherman and the New York adjusted gross income … is more than $150,000"
- [NY-2105I], 2026: "You must have filed a return for 2025 and it must have been for a full 12-month year".
- [NY-2105.9I], 2025, Line 16 worksheet: "If your New York adjusted gross income … for 2024 is more than $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately for 2025) enter 110%".

## 3. Due dates and shares

- Due dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027, in equal quarters.
- If the requirement first arises later in the year, payments start on the next due date. [NY-2105I] gives the dates but not the catch-up percentages.
  - (corrected in verification) [NY-2105I], 2026, worksheet "Computation of installments" gives them: first installment paid June 15 is 50% of the estimate, September 15 is 75%, and January 15 is the full amount. This is payment guidance. [NY-685](c)(4) still sets each required installment at "twenty-five percent of the required annual payment". [NY-2105I] tells a taxpayer whose requirement arises after March 31 to use the annualised income installment method to reduce the earlier installments.
- The January payment is waived if the return is filed and the balance paid by January 31.
- Weekend and holiday shifting is stated only in the fiscal-year paragraph. See item 10.
  - (corrected in verification) [NY-GCL] supplies the general rule: an act due on "a Saturday, Sunday or a public holiday ... may be done on the next succeeding business day".

Quotes:

- [NY-2105I], 2026: "pay it in four equal installments on April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027."
- [NY-2105I], 2026: "If you file your 2026 New York State income tax return on or before January 31, 2027, and pay the full balance" … then no January payment is required.
- [NY-2105I], 2026, fiscal-year paragraph: "If any due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, your return is due on the next regular workday."
- [NY-2105I], 2026: if the spouse died within 30 days before April 15, the first installment gets a 90-day extension, using special condition code D9.

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid in four equal installments, unless the taxpayer proves otherwise.

- [NY-2105.9I], 2025: withholding is "considered payments of estimated tax in equal installments on the four installment dates, unless you prove otherwise."

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- **Tax below $300.** [NY-2105.9I], 2025: "the tax due (after deducting tax withheld) is less than $300." Where more than one tax applies, the threshold is $300 times the number of taxes among NYS, NYC and Yonkers. The MCTMT counts as 0. [NY-2105.9I] Line 15 worksheet: "Multiply line e by $300".
- **No liability in the prior year.** The prior year must have been 12 months. During it the taxpayer must have been a New York resident, or a nonresident or part-year resident with New York source income. [NY-2105.9I], 2025: "If you were a nonresident or part-year resident and had no New York source income, you do not qualify for exception (2)".
- **First-year residents.** There is no exception. Someone who moves to New York from elsewhere had no New York source income in the prior year, so does not qualify under the rule above.
- **Part-year residents.** Covered only by the prior-year rule above.
- **Farmers and fishermen.** They qualify if at least two-thirds of federal gross income came from farming or fishing in either the current or the prior year. They have one installment: 66⅔% by January 15, or they file and pay by March 1. They do not use Form IT-2105.9; they submit federal Form 2210F instead.
  - [NY-2105I], 2026: "There is only one required installment date, January 15, if two-thirds or more of your federal gross income"
- **Retired or disabled.** [NY-2105.9I], 2025: "you retired in 2024 or 2025 after reaching the age of 62, or you became disabled, and the underpayment was due to reasonable cause".
- **Death.** No penalty is due for "an installment of estimated tax is due on or after an individual's death".
- **Casualty or disaster.** (corrected in verification) A waiver exists in the statute. [NY-685](d)(4)(A): none is imposed if, "by reason of casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances", the addition "would be against equity and good conscience". It is a Department determination, not an automatic exception. [NY-2105.9I] does not list it.
- **Statutory basis of the other exceptions.** (corrected in verification) [NY-685](d)(1) is the $300 rule, (d)(2) the no-prior-year-liability rule, (d)(3) the death rule and (d)(4)(B) the retired-or-disabled waiver. The retired-or-disabled rule also requires a Department determination of "reasonable cause and not to willful neglect".

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed. The worksheets are in [NY-2105.9I]: Worksheet 1 for NYS, 2 for NYC, 3 for Yonkers, and 4a–4c for the MCTMT. The applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%. If annualisation is used for one due date, it must be used for all of them. Form IT-2105.9 must be filed with the return even if no penalty is owed.

- [NY-2105.9I], 2025: "If you use Worksheet 1 … for any payment due date, you must use it for all payment due dates."
- [NY-2105I], 2026: "be sure to file Form IT‑2105.9 with your 2026 tax return, even if no penalty is owed."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

- **Method.** The penalty is interest-like: the rate times the days late on each installment's underpayment. Days are counted within each installment penalty period. The penalty is computed on Form IT-2105.9, using the Part 2 short method or the Part 3 regular method.
  - [NY-2105.9I], 2025: the period runs from the due date "to and including the date of payment, the last date in the penalty rate period or the next installment due date"
  - The short method is not allowed if any payment was late or annualisation is used.
- **Rate rule.** The rate is the federal short-term rate plus 5.5 percentage points, with a floor of 7.5%. It is adjusted quarterly. The rate for the third month after the tax year also covers April 1–15.
  - (corrected in verification) [NY-685](c)(1) applies "the underpayment rate established under subsection (j) of section six hundred ninety-seven", or 7.5% if none is set. [NY-697](j) sets that rate at the federal short-term rate "plus (ii) five and one-half percentage points", with a 7.5% floor. [NY-697](j) compounds interest daily, but says that rule "shall not apply" to the § 685(c) estimated-tax addition. The estimated-tax penalty is simple interest.
  - [NY-2105.9I], 2025: "The penalty rate will be the federal short-term rate plus five and one half percentage points, but not less than 7.5%."
  - [NY-2105.9I], 2025: "The federal short-term rate for the first month of the previous calendar quarter, rounded to the nearest whole percent, will be used".
- **Rates for 2025.** 9.5% for every period from April 15, 2025 through April 15, 2026, per [NY-2105.9I].
- **Rates for 2026.** The DTF rate pages give the Tax Law § 697(j) income tax underpayment rate:

  | Quarter | Rate |
  |---|---|
  | Q1 2026 | 9.5% |
  | Q2 2026 | 8.5% |
  | Q3 2026 | 9.5% |
  | Q4 2026 (10/1–12/31/2026) | 9.5% |

  [NY-RATE-Q4]: "For purposes of section 697(j) of the Tax Law, the underpayment rate for this period is 9.5%." These pages do not label this rate as the estimated-tax penalty rate. See item 10.
  - (corrected in verification) The link is now sourced. [NY-685](c)(1) adopts the § 697(j) underpayment rate, so these quarterly figures are the 2026 estimated-tax penalty rates. Under the April 1–15 rule, the Q1 2027 rate will also cover April 1–15, 2027. It is not yet published.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is $300, applied per tax. There is no threshold at all for the MCTMT.
- New York City, Yonkers and MCTMT liabilities are added together into one required annual payment and one penalty computation.
- MCTMT earnings above $150,000 can trigger the 110% rule even when NYAGI does not.
- There is no first-year-resident relief.
- The penalty rate is New York's own: the federal short-term rate plus 5.5 points, with a 7.5% floor. I did not check how this compares with the federal rate.
- Farmers and fishermen use federal Form 2210F rather than a New York form.

## 9. Local income taxes

- **New York City and Yonkers.** Their taxes are paid with the state estimate on the same Form IT-2105. The voucher has separate amount boxes for each tax, with a total. The penalty for all of them is computed on the combined Form IT-2105.9. The Yonkers resident estimate is 16.75% of the NYS amount. The Yonkers nonresident earnings tax is computed on Form Y-203.
  - [NY-2105I], 2026: "separately enter the amounts for New York State, New York City, Yonkers, and MCTMT; then enter the total"
  - [NY-2105I], 2026: "The current rate for Yonkers is 16.75% (.1675)."
- **NYC business taxes.** The NYC unincorporated business tax and general corporation tax are separate. [NY-2105I], 2026: "These taxes are administered directly by the NYC Department of Finance."
- **Other localities.** NOT FOUND. No other New York local income tax with estimated payments is mentioned.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- **Weekend shifting for calendar-year filers.** [NY-2105I] states the Saturday/Sunday/holiday rule only in the fiscal-year paragraph, and refers to the "return" rather than the payment. No 2026 calendar-year date falls on a weekend, so this does not matter for 2026. The 2025 penalty tables in [NY-2105.9I] use "June 15, 2025" as a due date, even though that day was a Sunday.
- **Rate pages versus the penalty formula.** The DTF quarterly rate pages give the § 697(j) underpayment interest rate. [NY-2105.9I] and [NY-IP] give the same formula, short-term plus 5.5 with a 7.5% floor, for the estimated-tax penalty. That the two rates are equal in 2026 is my inference; no source states it. The 2026 Form IT-2105.9-I, which would print the rates, was NOT FOUND. The URL https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/2026/inc/it2105_9i_2026.pdf returned 404, and the "current_forms" copy is still the 2025 edition.
- **Wording of the zero-liability exception.** [NY-2105I] words it as "you had no tax liability for the full 12-month preceding tax year". [NY-2105.9I] adds the condition about residence or New York source income.

### Resolved conflicts

- **Weekend shifting for calendar-year filers.** [NY-GCL] governs every statutory deadline: an act due on a weekend or public holiday "may be done on the next succeeding business day". No 2026 installment date falls on a weekend. January 31, 2027, the file-and-pay date for the fourth installment, is a Sunday. Under [NY-GCL] it should move to Monday, February 1, 2027. No DTF source states this for 2027. [NY-2105.9I]'s use of "June 15, 2025" (a Sunday) as a due date is a form simplification.
- **Rate pages versus the penalty formula.** Resolved. [NY-685](c)(1) applies the § 697(j) underpayment rate. The DTF quarterly pages publish that rate. They are the estimated-tax penalty rates.
- **Wording of the zero-liability exception.** [NY-685](d)(2) governs. It requires a 12-month prior year with no liability and, throughout it, residence or "a nonresident or part-year resident who had New York source income". [NY-2105.9I] matches it. [NY-2105I]'s shorter wording omits the residence condition.
