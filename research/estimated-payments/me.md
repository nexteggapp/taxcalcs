## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Both of the following must be true:

- Estimated Maine tax for the year, over withholding and credits, is $1,000 or more.
- Prior-year tax liability was also $1,000 or more.

The prior-year test does not apply to an "unusual event" (see item 2). No filing-status difference was found.

- [ES-26]: "estimated Maine income tax for the year, over and above tax withheld and allowable credits, is $1,000 or more".
- [ES-26], continuing: "and if your tax liability for the prior year was also $1,000 or more."
- [MRS-5228](2)(D): "The person had less than $1,000 tax liability under this Part for the preceding taxable year."
- [MRS-5228](2)(D), next sentence: "This paragraph does not apply with respect to an unusual event."

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax. The prior year must be a 12-month year.
- There is no 110% tier and no AGI threshold. NOT FOUND: any higher percentage in [ES-26], [2210-25] or [MRS-5228].
- Unusual event rule: an unusual event is non-withheld taxable income in an installment period that exceeds the same period of the prior year by $500,000 or more. For that excess you must pay 90% of the tax, and the prior-year option is lost for it. The rule applies regardless of AGI.
- Filed return: a filed prior-year return is not stated as a requirement.

Quotes:

- [ES-26]: "a) An amount equal to the preceding year's tax liability, if the preceding year was a taxable year of 12 months; or".
- [ES-26]: "b) An amount equal to 90% of the tax liability for the current taxable year."
- [ES-26]: "exceeds the Maine taxable income received during the same estimated tax installment period of the prior tax year by $500,000 or more."
- [ES-26]: "This special rule prohibits you from utilizing the prior year tax liability rule under paragraph a above".

## 3. Due dates and share due

There are four equal installments of 25% each. The 2026 dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027.

Weekend shifting: NOT FOUND as a stated rule. The 2025 form prints the second 2025 installment as June 16, 2025, which implies shifting.

(corrected in verification) The rule is in statute. A due date under Title 36 that falls on a weekend or Maine legal holiday moves to the next day that is neither. 36 M.R.S. §153(2): "the performance of that act is timely if it occurs on the next succeeding day which is not a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday". https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec153.html

The fourth installment carries no penalty if the return is filed and paid in full by January 31. This is new statutory text, effective September 24, 2025.

(corrected in verification) Subsection 9 was amended, not newly added: its history reads "PL 2025, c. 113, Pt. C, §12 (AMD)". The earlier wording was not checked. The statute requires both filing the return and paying in full. For tax year 2026 the date is January 31, 2027, a Sunday, so §153(2) moves it to February 1, 2027.

- [ES-26]: "pay in four equal installments on April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027."
- [2210-25] line 7: "Multiply the amount on line 6 by .25 (to compute the amount for line 9)."
- [2210-25] line 8 column B: "June 16, 2025".
- [MRS-5228](9): "a penalty may not be imposed with respect to any underpayment of the 4th required installment".
- [2210-25]: "Do not calculate the penalty for the 4th required payment if ... the tax is paid in full by January 31."

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid evenly, 25% per column. NOT FOUND: an explicit election to use actual withholding dates. The statute allows installments to follow the "actual distribution of tax liability and allowable credits" when documented. See item 10.

- [2210-25] line 10a: "Tax withheld. Enter 25% (0.25) of line 3 in each column."

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if this year's tax, after allowable credits and withholding, is under $1,000. [2210-25]: "Subtract line 3 from line 1. If the result is less than $1,000, do not complete or file this form."
- Low or no prior-year liability: no penalty if prior-year tax was under $1,000, except for unusual event income. [2210-25] line 5: "If less than $1,000, do not complete or file this form."
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate rule. The prior-year rule above (under $1,000) is the closest.
- Part-year residents: NOT FOUND.
- Short prior year: the 100% option is unavailable. [2210-25] line 5: "If short year, enter the amount from line 2."
- Farmers and persons who fish commercially: this uses the federal IRC 6654(i)(2) test.
  - The current-year percentage is 66 2/3%.
  - There is one installment, due January 15.
  - No penalty applies if the return is filed and the tax paid by March 1.
  - [ES-26]: "your estimated tax may be paid in a single installment due on or before January 15, 2027."
  - [ES-26]: "If you file your 2026 individual income tax return on or before March 1, 2027, and pay the total tax at that time".
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in [ES-26], [2210-25] or [MRS-5228].
- Casualty or disaster: NOT FOUND as a specific exception. There is a general waiver for cause. [MRS-5228](5): "For cause, the State Tax Assessor may waive or abate all or any part of the penalty."
- Unusual event income from a pass-through entity: the penalty is waived, or more time given, if all four of these hold.
  1. You are an owner in a pass-through entity.
  2. You had no control over the distribution.
  3. You did not receive the payment.
  4. You pay by the next installment due date after receipt.
  - [MRS-5228](11): "the assessor shall waive the penalty under subsection 5 or grant a reasonable extension of time".

## 6. Annualised income installment method

The method is allowed. Use the "Annualized Income Installment Worksheet" and check line 17 of Form 2210ME. You must file the form even if the penalty is zero.

- Periods are the federal ones: 1/1 to 3/31, 5/31, 8/31 and 12/31.
- Factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1, and percentages 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90% [AI-25].

Quotes:

- [2210-25]: "To calculate your actual liability, complete the Annualized Installment Worksheet for each payment due date and check the box on line 17".
- [MRS-5228](5)(A): "The taxpayer establishes by adequate record the actual distribution of tax liability and allowable credits".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest charge on each installment's shortfall, counted in months, not days.

- A part of a month counts as a whole month.
- Interest compounds monthly.
- The period ends when the shortfall is paid, or at the 15th day of the fourth month after year end, whichever is earlier.

The rate is the 36 M.R.S. §186 rate: the Wall Street Journal prime rate on September 1 of the prior year, rounded up to a whole percent, plus one point. The rate is .8333% a month for 2025 and .75% a month (9% a year) for 2026, per [2210-25].

Compute it on Form 2210ME, Part II. Use the precomputed table of rates there; fiscal-year filers may not use it.

- [MRS-5228](5): "A penalty shall accrue automatically on underpayments of the required installment amount".
- [MRS-5228](5), same sentence: "at the rate provided pursuant to section 186."
- [MRS-186]: "equals the prime rate as published in the Wall Street Journal on the first day of September of the preceding calendar year".
- The same sentence of [MRS-186] continues: "rounded up to the next whole percent plus one percentage point."
- [2210-25]: "For calendar year 2026, the penalty rate is .75% per month or fraction thereof, compounded monthly."
- [2210-25] line 13: "A part of a month is considered a whole month."

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- No estimate is required if last year's tax was under $1,000. This is broader than the federal zero-liability exception.
- There is no 110% rule.
- The unusual event rule replaces it. A $500,000 jump in non-withheld income for a period, compared with the same period last year, must be covered at 90% of current tax. The prior-year safe harbor does not cover it.
- The penalty counts whole months and compounds monthly. One day late costs a full month's interest.
- For 2026, a 2% income tax surcharge applies to Maine taxable income over $1,000,000 (single), $750,000 (married filing separately) or $1,500,000 (joint or head of household). Estimates must account for it [ES-26].
- (corrected in verification) From tax year 2026 Maine has an elective pass-through entity tax (36 M.R.S. chapter 815, subchapter 3). That entity-level tax is excluded from an individual's "estimated tax". [MRS-5228](1)(B): "exclusive of a withholder's liability for taxes withheld and the tax imposed by chapter 815, subchapter 3". The surcharge is not excluded.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Prior-year liability wording: [2210-25] says the penalty generally applies if "your 2024 tax liability before tax credits, income tax withholding or any other tax payment(s) for the tax year was $1,000 or more". Its line 5 measures 2024 tax after refundable credits (line 24 minus lines 25c, 25d and 25e). [MRS-5228](2)(D) just says "less than $1,000 tax liability".
- Withholding by actual date: the federal election to treat withholding as paid when withheld is not offered on [2210-25]. [MRS-5228](5)(A) lets installments follow the documented "actual distribution of tax liability and allowable credits". Whether that covers withholding timing is not stated.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: no document states it. Only the June 16, 2025 date on [2210-25] shows it.
- January 31 relief: [MRS-5228](9) was amended by P.L. 2025, c. 113, effective September 24, 2025 [GEN-25]. The statute ties the relief to "the last day of the first month of the following taxable year". [ES-26] does not mention this relief.

### Conflicts resolved

- Prior-year liability wording. Narrowed, not fully resolved; it can change whether the $1,000 test is met.
  - [MRS-5228](2)(D) tests "less than $1,000 tax liability under this Part for the preceding taxable year".
  - The current-year test in (2)(C) subtracts "allowable credits", which (1)(A) defines as payments to the Bureau (withholding and estimates). The prior-year test in (2)(D) does not subtract them. So the [2210-25] instruction, which measures the prior-year liability before withholding and payments, matches the statute.
  - [2210-25] line 5 also subtracts the refundable credits on 1040ME lines 25c to 25e. Whether that is right depends on whether those credits are "tax liability" or payments, and the statute does not say. Line 5 is how Maine Revenue Services (MRS) computes it.
- Withholding by actual date. Partly resolved. [MRS-5228](5)(A) lets installments follow the "actual distribution of tax liability and allowable credits", and "allowable credits" include withholding ((1)(A)). So the statute allows withholding timing to be shown by adequate record. [2210-25] has no line for this and spreads withholding 25% a quarter. Whether MRS accepts actual withholding dates outside the annualized worksheet is not stated.
- Weekend and holiday shifting. Resolved by [MRS-153](2).
- January 31 relief. Resolved. [MRS-5228](9), as amended by P.L. 2025, c. 113, requires filing the return and paying in full by the last day of the first month. [GEN-25] mentions only payment. The statute governs. [ES-26] (revised July 2026) does not mention the relief.

### 2026 changes

- The 2% surcharge applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, with dollar thresholds indexed from 2027 ([ES-26] Note (1)). It is part of "estimated tax" under [MRS-5228](1)(B).
- A pass-through entity tax election is available for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 ([MRS-5199-C]), enacted by P.L. 2025, c. 650, Pt. N. The entity's tax is excluded from the owner's "estimated tax" ([MRS-5228](1)(B), amended by the same law).
- The penalty rate for 2026 is .75% a month, compounded monthly ([2210-25]).
- The fourth-installment relief in [MRS-5228](9) took effect September 24, 2025. It applies to the January 2026 installment and later ones.
- No 2026 change to the $1,000 tests, the 90% and 100% percentages, the $500,000 unusual-event amount or the due dates was found. The section history of [MRS-5228] ends with P.L. 2025, c. 650.
