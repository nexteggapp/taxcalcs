Wisconsin calls the charge "underpayment interest", not a penalty.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay if you expect to owe at least $500 for 2026 after withholding and credits, and you expect withholding to be less than the smallest of 90% of 2026 tax, 100% of 2025 tax, or 90% of 2026 tax on annualized income. No filing-status difference was found. Full-year residents, part-year residents and nonresidents are all covered.

- 1-ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "if you expect to owe, after subtracting your withholding and credits, at least $500 in tax for 2026".
- 1-ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Full-year residents, part-year residents, nonresidents, estates, and trusts are subject to the estimated tax requirement."

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage for high income: none for individuals. No 110% rule was found in sources 1–5. (Estates and trusts with taxable income of $20,000 or more lose the prior-year option.)
- The prior-year option requires a filed prior-year return covering 12 months. If the prior-year return was adjusted or amended, use the latest figure.
- High-income individuals keep the prior-year option.

Quotes:
- 1-ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "(2) 100% of the tax shown on your 2025 income tax return, assuming the return covered 12 months".
- Wis. Stat. §71.09(13)(b), source 4: "does not apply if the preceding taxable year was less than 12 months or if the taxpayer did not file a return"
- Schedule U Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "If your 2024 return was adjusted either by the department or by the filing of an amended return, use the adjusted amounts".

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% each. A taxpayer who first meets the requirement later pays 1/2, 3/4 or all at the first required date.
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day. The 2025 Schedule U shows the second due date as June 16, 2025 (June 15, 2025 was a Sunday).
- January payment waived: yes, if the return is filed and the full balance paid by January 31, and no estimated tax was owed for the September or earlier dates.

Quotes:
- 1-ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "in four equal installments on or before April 15, 2026, June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027."
- Wis. Stat. §71.09(13)(a), source 4: "the amount of each installment required under sub. (12) is 25 percent of the lower of the following amounts".
- 1-ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Note: If any due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, use the next business day." This note is printed under the fiscal-year paragraph. The statute applies the rule to every due date. Wis. Stat. §990.001(4)(b), source 6: an act due "on a Sunday or legal holiday" may be done "on the next secular day". §990.001(4)(c) does the same for a Saturday payment when the office is closed. (corrected in verification)
- 1-ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "by January 31, 2027, pay the entire balance due, and did not owe estimated tax for the September 2026 or prior installment date".

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid one-fourth on each due date. The taxpayer can instead enter the amounts actually withheld in each period and enter exception code 08.

- Schedule U Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "You are considered to have paid one-fourth of any withheld Wisconsin income tax on each payment due date unless you can show otherwise."
- Schedule U Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "If your tax was not withheld evenly throughout the year, you may fill in the amounts actually withheld during each period."

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

Each exception has a code entered on the underpayment interest line of the return.

- Tax below an amount: no interest if tax minus withholding is less than $500. Wis. Stat. §71.09(11)(a), source 4: "The tax shown on the return ... minus amounts withheld under subch. X, is less than $500."
- No liability in the prior year (code 02): only for someone who was a full-year Wisconsin resident in the prior year, with a 12-month year and zero tax. Wis. Stat. §71.09(11)(b), source 4: "the taxpayer had no liability ... for that year and the taxpayer was a resident of this state for all of that year."
- First-year residents: not covered by code 02. Schedule U Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "Do not use code '02' if you ... were a nonresident or part-year resident of Wisconsin for 2024".
- Part-year residents: no exception. They are subject to the requirement (question 1) and are excluded from code 02.
- Farmers and fishers (code 04): two-thirds of gross income (joint gross income if filing jointly) from farming or fishing in the current or prior year. They may pay all estimated tax by January 15, or file and pay in full by March 1 and make no estimated payments. On Schedule U the whole required annual payment goes in the January column. No reduced percentage was found; the required annual payment stays 90%/100%.
  - 1-ES Instructions, TY2026, source 1: "Pay your 2026 estimated tax in full by January 15, 2027; or" "File your 2026 income tax return on or before March 1, 2027, and pay the total tax due."
  - Schedule U Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "fill in the amount from line 8 in the last column of line 16. Fill in 0 (zero) in the first three columns."
- Retired or disabled (code 06): waiver if the taxpayer retired after age 62 or became disabled in the current or prior year. The instructions add a reasonable-cause condition; the statute does not. Wis. Stat. §71.09(11)(d), source 4: the taxpayer "retired during the taxable year or during the preceding taxable year after having attained age 62 or becoming disabled". The statute's only limit is "a showing by the department under s. 73.16 (4)". (corrected in verification) Schedule U Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "In 2024 or 2025 you retired after age 62 or became disabled and your underpayment was due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect".
- Casualty, disaster or unusual circumstance (code 05): waiver. Wis. Stat. §71.09(11)(c), source 4: "because of casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances it is not equitable to impose interest."
- Federally declared disaster or terroristic or military action (code 10): no interest if the taxpayer qualifies for a federal extension under IRC §7508A (Wis. Stat. §71.09(11)(e), source 4).
- Active duty outside the continental United States (code 07). Wis. Stat. §71.09(2), source 4: "This section does not apply to any person on active duty with the U.S. armed forces while stationed outside the continental United States."
- Estates and grantor trusts within two years after death (code 03).

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed. Use Schedule U Part IV, the Annualized Income Installment Method Worksheet, and enter exception code 08 (or 09 if a net operating loss is involved). Periods end March 31, May 31, August 31 and December 31. Factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1. Applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%.

- Schedule U Instructions, TY2025, source 3: "Use the worksheet in Part IV to figure the amount to fill in on line 16 of Schedule U."
- Wis. Stat. §71.09(13)(d), source 4, sets the 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90% percentages.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

Interest on each installment's shortfall for the days late, at a fixed statutory 12% a year. The period runs from the due date to the date paid or the 15th day of the 4th month after year-end, whichever is earlier. A short method applies to taxpayers with no estimated payments, or with four equal on-time payments.

- Rate rule: fixed by statute, not indexed. Wis. Stat. §71.84(1), source 5: "interest at the rate of 12 percent per year on the amount of the underpayment".
- Day count: Schedule U lines 27–28, source 2: "Days on line 26 x .12" divided by 365.
- Short method factors, TY2025: underpayment × .0798, less underpayment × days paid before 4/15/26 × .0003287 (Schedule U lines 13–14, source 2).
- Form: Schedule U (Form 1, 1NPR, 2 or 3). The result goes on Form 1 line 44 or Form 1NPR line 74.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The rate is a fixed 12% a year, not the federal quarterly rate.
- The threshold is $500, not $1,000.
- No 110% rule for high-income individuals.
- The zero-prior-year exception requires full-year Wisconsin residence in the prior year. New residents cannot use it.
- Farmers and fishers keep the 90%/100% required payment. Wisconsin's documents give no 66⅔% figure.
- The January waiver applies only if nothing was owed for the September or earlier installments.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. Sources 1–3 mention no city, county or school-district income tax. No primary source was found that states this explicitly.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- January waiver date. The statute (§71.09(14)) and the 2026 1-ES say January 31. The 2025 Schedule U instructions say "by February 2, 2026". January 31, 2026 was a Saturday, so this is probably the weekend rule applied, but the instructions do not say so.
- Weekend rule placement. The 2026 1-ES prints the next-business-day note under the fiscal-year paragraph only. The 2025 Schedule U applies it to calendar-year dates (June 16, 2025).
- Farmers' March 1 date. The 1-ES (TY2026) says "on or before March 1, 2027". The 2025 Schedule U instructions say "on or before March 2, 2026" (March 1, 2026 was a Sunday).
- Active-duty military. §71.09(2) says the estimated-tax section "does not apply" to them. The Schedule U instructions list it as a waiver to apply for (code 07).

### Conflicts resolved

- **January waiver date.** Same rule. §71.09(14) says "on or before January 31". January 31, 2026 was a Saturday. §990.001(4)(c) moves a Saturday payment to the next day that is not a Sunday or holiday when the office is closed, which gives Monday, February 2, 2026. For tax year 2026, January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so §990.001(4)(b) gives Monday, February 1, 2027.
- **Weekend rule placement.** Resolved by statute. §990.001(4) applies to every payment due date, not only fiscal-year ones. The 2025 Schedule U's June 16, 2025 applies it.
- **Farmers' March 1 date.** Same rule. §71.09(4) uses "the first day of the 3rd month of the succeeding taxable year". March 1, 2026 was a Sunday, so the date moved to March 2, 2026. March 1, 2027 is a Monday, so the 2026 date stays March 1, 2027.
- **Active-duty military.** The statute governs: §71.09(2) says the section "does not apply" to them, so no estimated tax is owed. Code 07 is how the return claims it; the instructions' "waiver" wording does not add a condition the statute lacks.
- **Retired or disabled (new).** The statute governs. §71.09(11)(d) requires only retirement after age 62 or disability, in the current or prior year. The Schedule U instructions add "reasonable cause and not willful neglect", which the statute does not require.
