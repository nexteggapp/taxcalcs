# Montana

Montana calls the underpayment charge "interest on underpayment of estimated tax", not a penalty.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Anyone whose tax after withholding and nonrefundable credits is more than $500. The same rule applies to residents, nonresidents and part-year residents. No filing-status differences were found.

- [MT-PUB1], 2026: "the amount of annual estimated taxes owed by a taxpayer, after any withholding and nonrefundable credits, is more than $500"
- [MT-PUB1], 2026: "The same rules apply for all individuals, whether they are residents, nonresidents, or part-year residents."
- [MCA-2512](1)(b)(i) words the exception as "less than $500". See item 10.
- (corrected in verification) The statute governs, so the rule is: payments are required when that tax is $500 or more. [DOR-ESTI-PAGE]: "Taxpayers who expect to owe $500 or more in income tax (after subtracting withholding and credits) must make estimated payments". See item 10.

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90%. Prior year: 100%.
- There is no 110% tier. Nothing in the sources raises the prior-year percentage at any income level, and no taxpayer loses the prior-year option because of high income.
- The prior-year option requires a filed return for a 12-month preceding year.
- (added in verification) Prior-year liability means total tax less nonrefundable credits, as shown on that return. Tax paid for the current year means withholding, estimated payments and refundable credits. [ARM-304](1): "the total tax imposed by Title 15, chapter 30, MCA, less any nonrefundable tax credits allowed under Montana law". [ARM-304](4): estimated payments made by a partnership or S corporation "are not taxes paid by the owner for the current tax year."

Quotes:

- [MCA-2512](1)(a)(i)–(ii), 2025 code: "(i) 90% of the tax for the current tax year" and "(ii) … 100% of the individual's tax liability for the preceding tax year, if the preceding tax year was a period of 12 months"
- [MT-PUB1], 2026: "You may only use this method if you filed a return for the preceding tax year." It adds: "You cannot use this method if your preceding return was a short year."
- [MT-ESTI], 2025, Part I line 6: "Enter the lesser of line 2 or line 5." Line 2 is 90% of 2025 tax. Line 5 is 2024 tax liability, with no multiplier.

## 3. Due dates and shares

- Due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. Each installment is 25% of the required annual payment.
- If a due date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.
- If the obligation arises later in the year, the first payment catches up: 50% at the second installment or 75% at the third.
- The January installment carries no interest if the return is filed by January 31 and the tax is paid in full.
- (added in verification) The weekend rule comes from [MCA-1-1-307], which moves an act due "upon a holiday or a Saturday" to "the next business day". [MCA-1-1-216](1)(a) makes "each Sunday" a legal holiday. [MCA-2512](2) also applies [MCA-2604](1)(b)(i), which defers a date falling on an IRS-recognized holiday "that is not observed in Montana". For tax year 2026 the four dates (April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027) all fall on weekdays. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the January-filing exception extends to Monday, February 1, 2027 under [MCA-1-1-307].
- (added in verification) Short tax years have their own schedule. [ARM-305](3): "Estimated tax payments are not required for a tax year shorter than four months." [ARM-305](4) sets the shares at 100% for one installment, 50% for two, and 33 1/3% for three.

Quotes:

- [MCA-2512](3)(a): "each installment must be 25% of the required annual payment determined pursuant to subsection (1)."
- [MT-PUB1], 2026: "If any of these dates fall on a weekend or a holiday, your payment is due on the next business day."
- [MCA-2512](3)(b): "the taxpayer shall pay 50% for that installment and 25% for the third and fourth installments"
- [MCA-2512](5)(d): no interest on the fourth installment if "the taxpayer files a return on or before the last day of the month following the close of the tax year".
- [MT-ESTI], 2025: "If you filed your tax return by January 31 and paid the amount due in full … there is no underpayment interest penalty"

## 4. Withholding

Withholding and refundable credits are treated as paid evenly, one quarter on each due date.

- [MT-ESTI], 2025, Part II line 2: "Add Part I, lines 3a and 3b. Then, divide by 4 and enter the result in each column"

NOT FOUND: any election to use the actual withholding dates. I looked in [MT-ESTI], [MT-PUB1] and [MCA-2512], and none of them mentions one. (corrected in verification) Also confirmed absent from ARM subchapter 42.17.3 ([ARM-304] through [ARM-314]) and [ARM-15316].

## 5. Exceptions with no interest

- **Tax below $500.** [MCA-2512](1)(b)(i): "less than $500 after reductions for credits and withholding".
- **No liability in the prior year.** [MCA-2512](1)(b)(ii): "the individual did not have any tax liability for the preceding tax year, which was a tax year of 12 months". It also requires that the individual was "a citizen or resident of the United States throughout that tax year."
- **First-year residents.** There is no first-year rule as such. [MT-PUB1], 2026, exempts anyone who was "not required to file a Montana income tax return in the previous tax year." [MT-ESTI], 2025, limits this to "a nonresident or part-year resident and were not required to file a 2024 Montana income tax return." See item 10.
  - (corrected in verification) The regulation sides with [MT-ESTI]. [ARM-314](1): a nonresident or part-year resident "who was not required to file a Montana individual income tax return for the previous tax year" need not pay estimates. A resident who had no prior-year filing duty is exempt only through the zero-liability test above.
- **Part-year residents.** These are covered by the same rules and by the [MT-ESTI] exception quoted above. Worksheet ESW-TMSI in [MT-PUB1] computes their tax. (added in verification) [ARM-314](2): "Part-year residents moving out of Montana, other than those in (1), are required to pay."
- **Composite-return participants.** (added in verification) A composite return is one a partnership or S corporation files on behalf of its owners. [ARM-314](5): "A participant in a composite return for the current tax year is not required to pay estimated tax for the current tax year."
- **Farmers and ranchers.** They are wholly exempt from estimated payments if at least two-thirds of gross income comes from farming or ranching. They have no reduced percentage and no special date. [MCA-2512](1)(a) applies to each individual "except farmers or ranchers as defined in subsection (6)". [MCA-2512](6) sets the test at "at least 66 2/3% of the taxpayer's gross income". [MT-PUB1], 2026, adds: "(Montana does not apply a 'lookback' provision to determine farming and ranching gross income.)" Fishermen are not mentioned.
- **Retired or disabled.** [MT-PUB1], 2026: "You retired and were at least 62 years of age at the end of the previous tax year." The exception lasts for the retirement year and one more year. Disability in the previous or current year also qualifies. ~~[MCA-2512](1)(b)(iv) gives the same rule.~~ (corrected in verification) The statute's test differs from [MT-PUB1]. It requires retirement after reaching 62, not age 62 at the end of the prior year. [MCA-2512](1)(b)(iv): "the individual retired in the tax year after having attained the age of 62". The exception also covers "the tax year following the tax year in which the individual retired or became disabled." The statute governs. See item 10.
- **Casualty or disaster.** [MCA-2512](1)(b)(iii): "the underpayment was caused by reason of casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances that the department determines to constitute good cause".
- **Equal installments.** [MCA-2512](5)(e): no interest if the 90%/100% requirement is met "and the taxpayer has paid approximately equal quarterly installments".
- **Hardship extension.** (added in verification) [MCA-2512](7) lets the department extend an installment by up to 6 months. [ARM-306] requires a written request received 30 days before the installment date. [ARM-306](4) bars any extension "beyond the original due date of the return". [ARM-306](6): "Inability to pay does not constitute 'hardship.'"

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed. Taxpayers use Worksheet ESA in [MT-PUB1] for planning and Form EST-I Part III for the interest computation. The applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%. Any reduction from annualising must be recaptured in the next installment. The taxpayer must also mark a box on Form 2.

- [MCA-2512](4)(c): "First 22.5% Second 45% Third 67.5% Fourth 90%"
- [MCA-2512](4)(d): "A reduction in a required installment resulting from the application of an annualized income installment must be recaptured"
- [MT-PUB1], 2026: "When you file your Form 2 mark the 'Estimated payments made using the annualization method' box on Schedule IV, line 2."
- (added in verification) The periods end after months 3, 5, 8 and 12. [ARM-309](1): "January through March, January through May, January through August, and January through December". [MT-ESTI] Part III line 1 uses annualization factors 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1.
- (added in verification) [ARM-305](2): "Taxpayers using the annualization method must file Worksheet ESA with their income tax return." E-filers keep it as a record instead.

## 7. How the interest is computed

- **Method.** Interest is charged on each installment's shortfall for the days it is late. It runs until the payment date or April 15 of the following year, whichever is earlier. Payments apply to the earliest unpaid installment first. The computation is on Form EST-I, and the result goes on Form 2, Schedule IV, line 2.
  - [MCA-2512](5)(a): interest runs to "the date payment was made or to the 15th day of the 4th month of the year following the tax year".
  - [MCA-2512](5)(c): "an estimated payment must be credited against unpaid required installments in the order in which those installments are required to be paid."
  - (added in verification) [ARM-15316](3): underpayment interest "accrues to the original due date of the return". After that date, ordinary interest under [MCA-216] applies to the unpaid tax.
- **Rate rule.** The rate is the IRC § 6621 individual underpayment rate for the third quarter of the preceding year. It is set for each calendar year. Interest on tax year X uses the rate for the calendar year in which the return is due, which is X+1.
  - [MCA-216](7)(a)(i): "equal to the underpayment rate for individual taxpayers established by the secretary of the United States department of the treasury pursuant to section 6621".
  - [MT-PUB1], 2026: "interest on underpayment of estimated taxes for tax year 2026 will be calculated using the interest rate effective for 2027".
  - (added in verification) The regulation states the same rule. [ARM-2505](5): "the rate in effect on the original due date of the tax return shall be used."
- **Current rate.** 7%, effective January 1, 2026. Per [MT-ESTI], 2025, this rate applies to the 2025 tax year: "As of January 1, 2026, the interest rate is 7 percent." The daily rate is 0.000192. ~~The 2027 rate, which will apply to tax year 2026, was NOT FOUND (not yet published).~~
  - (corrected in verification) [DOR-PI] gives the unrounded daily rate: "7%, computed daily at 0.019178%". That is 7% ÷ 365. The form's 0.000192 is the same rate rounded.
  - (corrected in verification) The 2027 rate, which applies to tax year 2026, is 7% by the statutory formula. The department has not published it yet. [MCA-216](7)(a)(i) adopts the federal individual underpayment rate for the third quarter of 2026. [IRS-RR-2026-10]: "an underpayment rate of 7 percent are established for the calendar quarter beginning July 1, 2026." Confirm against the department's 2027 announcement or the 2026 Form EST-I when they appear.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold is $500, not $1,000.
- There is no 110% prior-year rule for high-income taxpayers.
- Farmers and ranchers owe no estimated payments at all. There is no January 15 or March 1 rule, and no prior-year lookback for the two-thirds test.
- People who retired at 62 or older, or became disabled, in the year or the prior year are automatically exempt. This needs no showing of reasonable cause.
- Interest uses one rate for the whole year: the IRC § 6621 rate for the third quarter of the preceding year. For tax year X, it is the rate for calendar year X+1.
- (added in verification) Montana's holiday calendar decides the weekend shift, not the federal one ([MCA-1-1-216], [MCA-1-1-307]). [MCA-2604](1)(b)(i) also adds IRS-recognized holidays that Montana does not observe.

## 9. Local income taxes

~~NOT FOUND. No Montana local income tax with estimated payments is mentioned in [MT-PUB1], [MT-ESTI] or the statutes reviewed.~~

(corrected in verification) None. Montana local governments cannot levy an income tax unless a statute specifically delegates that power. [MCA-7-1-112](1) prohibits "the power to authorize a tax on income or the sale of goods or services" without that delegation. No statute delegating it was found.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- **Threshold wording.** [MT-PUB1] requires payments when tax "is more than $500". [MCA-2512](1)(b)(i) and [MT-ESTI] line 4 exempt amounts "less than $500". Exactly $500 is required under the statute but not under the publication. (corrected in verification) Resolved: the statute governs, and payments are required at exactly $500. See Verification.
- **Prior-year exceptions.** The statute's exception is no liability in a 12-month prior year plus US citizenship or residence. [MT-PUB1] instead says "not required to file a Montana income tax return in the previous tax year." [MT-ESTI] limits the not-required-to-file test to nonresidents and part-year residents. These three tests differ. (corrected in verification) [MT-PUB1] does not replace the statutory test; it lists it too ("Your previous tax period covered 12 months and your Montana tax liability was zero"), without the citizenship condition. Resolved by [ARM-314](1). See Verification.
- **12-month requirement.** [MT-ESTI] Part I, line 5 asks for the "2024 tax liability" without stating the 12-month or filed-return conditions, which the statute and [MT-PUB1] both impose. (corrected in verification) Resolved: the statute governs.
- **Missing Part IV.** The [MT-ESTI] instructions refer to "Part IV" for annualisation, but the form has only Parts I–III. Part III is the annualisation part.
- **No rate for tax year 2026 yet.** Because the rate for tax year 2026 is the 2027 rate, no published rate exists yet for 2026 estimates. (corrected in verification) The statutory formula gives 7%. See item 7.
- **Retirement age.** (added in verification) [MT-PUB1] requires age 62 at the end of the previous year. [MCA-2512](1)(b)(iv) requires retirement after reaching 62, in the current or prior year. The statute governs.

### Conflicts resolved

- **Threshold.** The statute governs. [MCA-2512] was amended in 2025 only by [HB863], which did not touch subsection (1). Payments are required at exactly $500. [MT-ESTI] line 4 and [DOR-ESTI-PAGE] agree with the statute. [MT-PUB1]'s "more than $500" is loose wording.
- **Prior-year exceptions.** Two tests apply. The first is the statute's: zero liability in a 12-month prior year, with US citizenship or residence ([MCA-2512](1)(b)(ii)). The second is the regulation's: a nonresident or part-year resident not required to file last year ([ARM-314](1)). [MT-ESTI] matches both. [MT-PUB1] drops the citizenship condition and the nonresident limit, so it overstates the exception.
- **12-month and filed-return conditions.** The statute governs. EST-I line 5 is a form shortcut, and the data format should keep both conditions.
- **Retirement age.** The statute governs: the taxpayer must retire after reaching 62, and the exception covers the retirement year and the next year.
- **Part IV.** This is a drafting error in [MT-ESTI]. Part III is the annualisation part.

### 2026 changes

- [HB863] (Chapter 738, Laws of 2025) added [MCA-2512](8). It let the department waive interest for tax year 2024 only, and that power "terminates January 1, 2026." It does not affect tax year 2026.
- [LEG-REVIEW-2025] lists no 2025 amendment to [MCA-216], [MCA-2604] or any other part of [MCA-2512]. The 2025 code is the current compilation.
- HB 337 (2025) changes 2026 brackets and the top rate. It changes the tax amount, not the estimate rules. [MT-PUB1]: "House Bill 337 lowered the top marginal tax rate and increased the income brackets for tax year 2026."
- The estimated-tax rules [ARM-304], [ARM-305] and [ARM-306] were amended effective 9/21/2024, and [ARM-15316] effective 1/25/2025. The current text is the one quoted here.

### Open question (could change a number)

[MCA-216](2)(b)(i) imposes a late-payment penalty of "1.5% a month on the unpaid tax" for taxes due under "Title 15, chapter 30, part 25". That part contains the estimated-tax statute. [DOR-PI] lists "Estimated Taxes" under that 1.5% penalty. [ARM-306](3) says the [MCA-2512] interest "is in addition to the uniform penalties and interest provided in 15-1-216". [MT-ESTI] computes interest only. No source says whether the 1.5% penalty is assessed on a missed individual installment.
