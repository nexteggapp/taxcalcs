## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Anyone who expects tax after Illinois withholding, pass-through withholding and listed credits to exceed $1,000. There is no difference by filing status. Joint filers figure the estimate on joint income. The statute exempts estates, trusts, partnerships, S corporations and farmers.

- [ES26], tax year 2026: "You must make estimated income tax payments if you reasonably expect your 2026 tax liability to exceed $1,000 after subtracting your Illinois withholding, pass-through withholding, and tax credits".
- [ILCS-803] § 803(a): "Every taxpayer other than an estate, trust, partnership, Subchapter S corporation or farmer is required to pay estimated tax". The threshold is "$1,000 for taxable years ending on or after December 31, 2019".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax.
- There is no 110% tier and no AGI threshold. High-income taxpayers keep the 100% prior-year option.
- The statute's 100% option requires a filed prior-year return that showed a liability and covered a 12-month year.
- The statute has a 150% figure, but only for installments due between February 1, 2011 and January 31, 2012. It does not apply now.

Quotes:

- [ILCS-804] § 804(c)(1)(B)(i): "90% of the tax shown on the return for the taxable year".
- [ILCS-804] § 804(c)(1)(B)(ii): "100% of the tax shown on the return of the taxpayer for the preceding taxable year if a return showing a liability for tax was filed by the taxpayer for the preceding taxable year and such preceding year was a taxable year of 12 months".
- [2210-F25] Step 2, line 7: "enter the lesser of Column A, Line 6, or Column B, Line 3." Line 6 is 90% of this year's tax; Column B is the prior year's tax less credits.
- [2210-I25]: "We will waive the late-payment penalty for underpayment of estimated tax if you timely paid the lesser of 100 percent of the prior year's tax liability or 90 percent of the current year's tax liability."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

There are four equal installments, each 25%, due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. For tax year 2026 the dates are April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027.

- [ES26]: "four equal installments that are due on April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027."
- [ILCS-803] § 803(d): "There shall be paid 4 equal installments of estimated tax".
- [ILCS-804] § 804(c)(1)(A): each is "25% of the required annual payment".

Weekend and holiday shifting applies. [2210-I25] line 9a: "If this date falls on a weekend or holiday, use the next business day." The rule [ADM-5000] adopts IRC § 7503 for IITA due dates. None of the four 2026 dates falls on a weekend: they are a Wednesday, Monday, Tuesday and Friday. (corrected in verification: weekday check and rule added)

January waiver for filing by January 31: NOT FOUND in [ES26], [2210-I25], [ILCS-803] or [ILCS-804]. Also absent from the full exception list in [ADM-8010] (g). (confirmed in verification)

Payment application: [ES26]: "We will apply each payment to the earliest due date until that liability is paid, unless you provide specific instructions to apply it to another period."

## 4. How withholding is treated

Wage withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each installment date by default. The taxpayer may instead use actual withholding dates, but must document them with pay stubs or an employer letter. By statute, choosing actual dates covers all withholding.

Pass-through withholding is deemed paid on the last day of the entity's tax year.

- [ILCS-804] § 804(g)(1): "an equal part of such amount shall be deemed paid on each installment date for such taxable year, unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld".
- [2210-I25] line 10a: "We consider you to have paid Illinois tax withheld evenly over the entire year unless you send us copies of pay stubs or a letter from your employer".
- [2210-I25] line 10b, pass-through withholding: "Enter the entire amount in the quarter in which the pass-through entity's tax year ended."
- A taxpayer who uses actual withholding must file IL-2210. [2210-I25]: "you must complete this form if you ... choose to enter the actual amount of tax withheld in each quarter".
- Withholding that is not from compensation has its own statutory date. [ILCS-804] § 804(g)(3): "all other amounts pursuant to Article 7 shall be deemed a payment of estimated tax on the date the payment is made". The form and [ADM-8010] (e)(2) spread all withholding evenly instead. See item 10. (corrected in verification)

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

[2210-I25] lists five cases with no penalty. The rule [ADM-8010] (g) lists four more, given after this list. (corrected in verification)

- Tax below an amount: this year's tax, less credits, Illinois withholding and pass-through withholding, is $1,000 or less. On [IL1040-25], line 25 is "Illinois Income Tax withheld" and line 27 is "Pass-through withholding". So the test is net of withholding, not only of credits. (corrected in verification) [2210-I25]: "this year's tax liability (Form IL-1040, Line 14 minus Lines 15, 16, 17, 25, 27, 28, 29, and 30) is $1,000 or less."
- No prior-year return was required. [2210-I25]: "you were not required to file Form IL-1040 for last year".
- No liability in the prior year. [2210-I25]: "last year's Form IL-1040 had a zero tax liability". The statute adds that the prior year must be 12 months. [ILCS-804] § 804(d): "if the taxpayer had no tax liability for the preceding taxable year and such year was a taxable year of 12 months."
- Farmers, defined as at least two-thirds of gross income from farming, are fully exempt from estimated tax. There is no percentage and no date to meet. [ES26]: "We consider you a farmer if at least two-thirds of your total federal gross income is from farming." Fishermen are not mentioned.
  - On a joint return the farmer test uses the couple's combined income. [ADM-8000] (e)(3): the exemption applies "only if ⅔ of the total gross income of the couple is from farming." (corrected in verification)
- Age 65 or older and permanently in a nursing home. This applies to the taxpayer or the spouse. [ES26]: "65 years or older and permanently living in a nursing home". The statute is [ILCS-806]: no penalty if "65 years of age or older and is a permanent resident of a nursing home." [ADM-8000] (e)(4) extends it to a joint return when "either spouse" qualifies. (corrected in verification: statute added)

Further exceptions in the rule [ADM-8010] (g), not on the form (corrected in verification):

- Deceased taxpayer. (g)(6): no penalty "with respect to any underpayment of estimated tax arising subsequent to the death of the taxpayer."
- Armed services in a combat zone with a federal IRC § 7508 extension. (g)(7), citing IITA § 602(b).
- Innocent spouse, to the extent relieved under IITA § 502(c)(4). (g)(8).
- Bankruptcy estate, abated as under IRC § 6658. (g)(1)(B)(iv).

Not available:

- First-year and part-year residents: no explicit exception. NOT FOUND in [ES26], [2210-I25], [ILCS-804] or [ADM-8010] (g). A new resident who was "not required to file Form IL-1040 for last year" is covered by that exception. (confirmed in verification)
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND as a specific exception in [ILCS-804], [ADM-8010] (g) or [2210-I25]. (confirmed in verification)
- Casualty or disaster: no automatic exception. Reasonable-cause abatement applies, and the rule names these cases (corrected in verification). [ADM-700.400] (e)(5) lists "destruction by fire, other casualty or civil disturbance" of the residence or records. [ADM-700.400] (e)(13) treats an IRS disaster extension as reasonable cause for the matching Illinois payment, if paid by the IRS extended date. [ILCS-804] § 804(e): the penalty "shall not be imposed to the extent that the Director ... determines, pursuant to Section 3-8 of the Uniform Penalty and Interest Act that the penalty should not be imposed." [PUB103] says abatement for "reasonable cause" requires "a detailed explanation of the cause of the delay".

## 6. Annualized income installment method

Allowed, using Form IL-2210 Step 6, lines 38–56. The taxpayer checks the box on Form IL-1040, line 34c, and attaches IL-2210. If used, it must be used for all four installments.

- The periods are the first 3, 5 and 8 months, then the full year.
- The annualization factors are 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1.
- The applicable percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90%.
- [2210-I25]: "If you choose to annualize your income in Step 6, you must use this method for all four installments."
- [ILCS-804] § 804(c)(2)(C) sets the same 22.5/45/67.5/90% percentages.

If the Illinois Income Tax Act changes mid-year, each annualization period is computed under the law in effect at the end of that period. [2210-I25]: "computing your income and liability for each period according to the IITA as in effect as of the end of that period."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

It is a flat percentage of each late amount, set by how many days late the payment was. It is not a time-based interest charge.

- 1 to 30 days late: 2% of the late amount.
- 31 or more days late, or never paid: 10%.

The rates come from the Uniform Penalty and Interest Act, § 3-3(b-25)(1), for returns due on or after January 1, 2024. The penalty is computed on IL-2210 Step 4, Penalty Worksheet 1, and reported on IL-1040 line 34. The Department will compute the penalty and bill the taxpayer if they prefer.

Quotes:

- [UPIA-3-3] § 3-3(b-25)(1): "2% of any amount that is paid no later than 30 days after the due date and 10% of any amount that is paid later than 30 days after the due date."
- [2210-F25]: "1 - 30 .02 ... 31 or more .10".
- [ILCS-804] § 804(a): the penalty is "determined at the rate prescribed by Section 3-3 of the Uniform Penalty and Interest Act upon the amount of the underpayment".
- [2210-I25]: "We encourage you to let us figure your penalties and send you a bill instead of completing and filing this form yourself."

The 2025 form states its rates under the older subsection. [2210-I25]: "The penalty rates used on this form are for returns due on or after January 1, 2005." Subsection (b-20)(1), for returns due from 2005 through 2023, sets the same 2% and 10% rates. So the rates are the same under either subsection. (corrected in verification)

Interest is separate. [UPIA-3-2] sets the interest rate at the federal IRC § 6621 underpayment rate, simple daily interest, adjusted on January 1 and July 1. The rate is 7% for "01/01/2025 - 12/31/2026" per [RATES]. (corrected in verification: rate filled) Whether interest also accrues on late estimated installments, in addition to the penalty, is NOT FOUND stated in [2210-I25], [ES26], [UPIA-3-2] or [ADM-700.220]. IL-2210 computes no interest. See item 10 and the verification section.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The penalty is a flat 2% or 10% of the late amount, not a daily interest rate.
  - A payment 31 days late costs the same 10% as one paid a year late.
  - A small shortfall paid 31 days late costs far more than under the federal rule.
- There is no 110% rule. The prior-year safe harbor is always 100%.
- Farmers are fully exempt, with no January 15 or March 1 condition.
- Taxpayers 65 or older permanently in a nursing home are exempt.
- There is no January 31 filing waiver for the fourth installment.
- Pass-through-entity withholding counts as paid only at the end of the entity's year.
- Using actual withholding dates requires pay stubs or an employer letter.

## 9. Local income taxes with their own estimated payments

None found. The IDOR sources above describe no city, county or school-district income tax. The constitution bars home-rule units from income taxes unless a law grants the power. [CONST] Art. VII § 6(e): home-rule units have "only the power that the General Assembly may provide by law" to tax income. This verification did not search the statutes for such a grant. (corrected in verification: constitutional basis added)

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements between sources

- **Interest on late installments.** [PUB103] says interest "begins to accrue the day after the date the payment is due through the date you pay the tax". [UPIA-3-2] says interest accrues "upon tax due". Neither says explicitly whether interest runs on late estimated installments before the return due date. IL-2210 computes only the penalty.
- **Prior-year return conditions.** The statute requires the prior-year return to have "a liability for tax" and a 12-month year. IL-2210 does not repeat these conditions. It covers the zero-liability case through its own exceptions.
- **Withholding election.** [2210-I25] says "Enter one-fourth of the total tax withheld (or the actual tax withheld from your wages for each quarter)". It does not repeat the statute's rule that the election covers "all amounts". A per-quarter mix is therefore not clearly barred on the form.
- **Late-payment penalty overlap.** [PUB103] says the amount already penalized as an estimated-tax underpayment is subtracted before the late-payment penalty on the return balance. So the same dollars are not penalized twice under both.
- **Revision code.** [ES26] prints "IL-1040-ES (R-09/26)". That is a later revision date than one would expect for a form needed from April 2026. It may be a re-issue.

### Conflicts and their resolution

- **Interest on late installments.** Unresolved. No primary source says whether interest runs on a late installment before the return due date. [ADM-700.220] (c) says "Interest on tax shall accrue from the date of underpayment." Its Example 4 is a 1993 corporation that skipped all estimates and paid in full with the return. The example charges interest only on the penalty, from the return due date. That suggests no interest on the missed installments. For returns due from 2001 on, [ADM-700.220] (b) says "interest shall not accrue on penalties." The conflict is between "tax" in § 3-2 and the example's silence. A ruling or IDOR confirmation would settle it.
- **Prior-year return conditions.** Resolved: the statute governs. [ADM-8010] (d)(1)(A)(ii) repeats both conditions. A taxpayer whose prior year was shorter than 12 months cannot use the 100% prior-year amount. A taxpayer with no prior-year liability owes no penalty under § 804(d).
- **Withholding election.** Resolved: the statute governs. [ADM-8010] (e)(2): if actual dates are used, "all amounts withheld shall be considered as payments of estimated tax on the dates those amounts were actually withheld." A per-quarter mix is not allowed.
- **Non-wage withholding (new).** Unresolved in practice. [ILCS-804] § 804(g)(3) dates non-wage Article 7 withholding to the payment date. [ADM-8010] (e)(2) and [2210-I25] line 10a spread all withholding evenly, including 1099 withholding. The statute governs in law. IDOR's form, which most filers follow, uses even spreading.
- **Late-payment penalty overlap.** VERIFIED. [PUB103] p. 5: the tax already penalized as underpaid estimates "is subtracted from the total tax" for the late-payment base. [UPIA-3-3] (c) bases late-payment penalties on tax reduced by "any part of the tax which is paid on time".
- **Revision code.** Partly resolved. [ES26] pages 1–3 print R-09/26, but its payment voucher prints "IL‑1040‑ES (R‑12/25)". This suggests the voucher dates from December 2025 and the instruction pages were revised in September 2026. What the revision changed could not be found. The dates, threshold and 4.95% worksheet rate on the current version agree with the statute.
- **Rule threshold out of date (new).** Resolved: the statute governs. [ADM-8000] (a)(1) and [ADM-8010] (g)(1)(A) still say $500. P.A. 101-355 raised the statute to $1,000 for years ending on or after December 31, 2019. The forms use $1,000.
- **Farmer income test wording (new).** Minor. [ILCS-803] (e) uses "2/3 of his total estimated gross income". [ES26] says "total federal gross income". [ADM-8000] (b)(2) ties gross income from farming to IRC § 6654(i)(2). None states a different number.
