## 1. Does Washington tax individual income for tax year 2026?

**Wages, interest and dividends: no.** Washington has no individual income tax for 2026.

- Source 6: "Washington does not currently have an individual income tax."

**Long-term capital gains: yes.** The capital gains excise tax applies to individuals for tax year 2026. The rate is 7%, plus an additional 2.9% on Washington capital gains above $1,000,000. That makes 9.9% on the portion above $1,000,000.

- Source 8, RCW 82.87.040(1)(a): "Only individuals are subject to payment of the tax, which equals seven percent multiplied by an individual's Washington capital gains."
- Source 8, RCW 82.87.040(1)(b): "equals 2.90 percent multiplied by the portion of an individual's Washington capital gains exceeding $1,000,000."
- Source 3: "Beginning with tax year 2025, Washington's long-term capital gains tax has a tiered rate."

**A new income tax starts in 2028, not 2026.** SB 6346 (Chapter 238, Laws of 2026) imposes a 9.9% tax on Washington taxable income from January 1, 2028. It was signed March 30, 2026. It does not apply to tax year 2026.

- Source 12, section 201(1): "Beginning January 1, 2028, a tax is imposed on the receipt of Washington taxable income. Only individuals are subject to payment of the tax"
- Source 6: "The first returns for the new tax will be due in April 2029."
- The act itself took effect June 11, 2026; the tax it imposes starts January 1, 2028 (Source 12, certification page; Source 13). (corrected in verification)
- **Status on 2026-10-20 (corrected in verification).** It is law. A referendum is ruled out, but a lawsuit and a repeal initiative are both pending:
  - The Supreme Court held it is not subject to referendum (Source 14).
  - A constitutional challenge is pending in Klickitat County Superior Court (Source 15).
  - Initiative IP26-645, which would repeal it, is on the November 3, 2026 ballot (Sources 16 and 17).
  - Section 1202 voids most of the act if a court of final jurisdiction invalidates section 201.

  See the Verification section.

## 2. Estimated payment rules

### Capital gains tax, tax year 2026

**There is no quarterly estimated tax requirement.** RCW 82.87 requires the whole tax to be paid by the return due date. No threshold, no current-year or prior-year percentage, no installment schedule and no annualised method applies. Washington uses the word "estimated payment" for a payment made by the original due date when the return is extended.

- **Due date.** The payment is due on the federal return due date. For tax year 2026 that is April 15, 2027.
  - Source 8, RCW 82.87.110(3): "on or before the date fixed for the filing of the return, regardless of any filing extension."
  - Source 2: "For tax year 2026, you may submit your payment beginning Oct. 15, 2026, for returns due on April 15th, 2027."
- **Prepayment window (new for 2026).** A taxpayer may pay up to six months before the due date. This is optional. It is made through My DOR, not tax software.
  - Source 8, RCW 82.87.115(1) (2026 c 191 s 1): "may submit payment of the tax beginning six months before the due date for filing and payment for that taxable year."
  - Source 2: "You will not be able to make a prepayment using tax preparation software."
  - Source 2: a prepayment cannot be deducted when computing federal net long-term capital gain on the Washington return (RCW 82.87.020(3)).
- **Extension.** A filing extension requires a federal extension and a request through My DOR by April 15. It does not extend the payment date.
  - Source 1: "A filing extension does not extend the due date for submitting a payment."
- **Late payment penalty.** RCW 82.32.090(1) applies. It is 9% if the payment is late, 19% after the last day of the following month, and 29% after the last day of the second following month. The minimum penalty is $5.
  - Source 9, RCW 82.32.090(1): "there is assessed a penalty of nine percent of the amount of the tax"
- **Underpayment when extended.** If the taxpayer pays a self-computed amount by the original due date, the late payment penalty does not apply to any shortfall. A 5% substantial underpayment penalty applies instead if the payment was under 80% of the actual tax and the shortfall is at least $1,000.
  - Source 1: "If you submit an estimated payment by the original filing deadline, you will not owe late payment penalties."
  - Source 9, RCW 82.32.090(10): "was less than 80 percent of the actual tax due, and at least $1,000."
  - Source 9, RCW 82.32.090(10): the penalty is "five percent of the amount of the actual tax due". This is 5% of the whole actual tax, not of the shortfall.
  - Interest still runs on any shortfall from the original due date. RCW 82.87.110(3) says interest and chapter 82.32 penalties "apply to the deficiency" (Source 8). (corrected in verification)
- **Late filing penalty.** It is 5% of the tax due per month or part of a month, capped at 25%. This is in addition to late payment penalties. It is waived for circumstances beyond the taxpayer's control, or for a first lapse in five years.
  - Source 8, RCW 82.87.110(6)(a): "five percent of the tax due for the taxable year covered by the return for each month or portion of a month"
- **Interest.** The rate is the average federal short-term rate plus two points, reset each January. Source 7 lists 6% for 2026 and 6% for 2027 under "Excise tax". The table is not labelled as covering capital gains specifically. Capital gains tax is administered under chapter 82.32 RCW (RCW 82.87.110(3)), so this rate appears to apply.
  - Source 10, RCW 82.32.050(2): "an average of the federal short-term rate as defined in 26 U.S.C. Sec. 1274(d) plus two percentage points."
- **Withholding.** No withholding provision was found in RCW 82.87 or on the Department of Revenue pages.
- **Payment method.** Payment must be electronic unless the Department waives this before payment (Source 1).
- **Penalty form and waiver.** No underpayment penalty form was found; penalties are assessed by the Department. RCW 82.87.110(7) requires the Department to waive the RCW 82.32.090(1) late payment penalty on request if the taxpayer was not contacted for enforcement for that period and paid all capital gains returns on time in the preceding five calendar years. General waivers are under RCW 82.32.105(1), which was not reviewed.

**How the tax is computed (Source 8, RCW 82.87.020, .060 and .040; Source 1):**

1. Start from federal net long-term capital gain. It is computed as if IRC sections 55–59, 164, 1256, 1400Z-1 and 1400Z-2 did not exist (RCW 82.87.020(3)).
2. Adjust it to "adjusted capital gain". Add back losses from exempt or non-Washington sales and certain carryovers, and make section 1256 adjustments (RCW 82.87.020(1)).
3. Allocate the gain to Washington (RCW 82.87.100).
4. Subtract the deductions:
   - the standard deduction, which is a single amount for a married couple or domestic partnership;
   - charitable donations above a threshold, up to a cap;
   - qualified family-owned small business gains;
   - constitutionally exempt amounts.

   Exempt assets include real estate, retirement accounts, condemnation, certain livestock, depreciable business assets, timber and commercial fishing privileges.
5. Apply 7% to the result, plus 2.9% on the part of the result above $1,000,000.
6. Subtract credits:
   - B&O tax paid on the same sale (from tax year 2025);
   - tax paid to another jurisdiction (nonrefundable).

**Standard deduction for 2026: NOT FOUND.** Source 4 lists 2022 $250,000, 2023 $262,000, 2024 $270,000 and 2025 $278,000. The 2025 charitable threshold is $278,000 with a $111,000 maximum deduction. RCW 82.87.150 requires the adjusted amounts to be published by October 31. The adjustment made in October takes effect for taxes due in the following calendar year. So the tax year 2026 amount should appear by October 31, 2026. It was not on Sources 1 or 4 on 2026-10-20.

- Source 8, RCW 82.87.150(1): "The department must publish the adjusted applicable amounts on its public website by October 31st."

### SB 6346 income tax (does not apply to 2026; recorded for later years)

- Estimated tax is required when the estimated tax is over $5,000 and the individual must make federal estimated payments. Reporting periods and due dates follow the IRC.
- The annualised estimated tax is computed under IRC section 6654.
- No estimated payments are due before July 1, 2029.
- Source 12, section 501(3): "Estimated tax payments are not required under this section if the annualized estimated tax is less than $5,000."
- Source 12, section 501(6): "Estimated payments are not required under this section before July 1, 2029."
- Source 12, section 501(5): the Department will adopt rules for estimated payments on wages subject to federal withholding. No state withholding mechanism is set out.
- The underpayment penalty is 5% of the underpaid tax if total estimates fall below the tax on the return. The safe harbours are 90% of the current-year tax or 100% of the most recently filed return's tax.
  - Source 9, RCW 82.32.090(11): "(a) 90 percent of the tax shown on the return" … "(b) 100 percent of the tax shown on the most recently filed tax return"
- A pass-through entity may elect to pay the 9.9% tax at entity level and make the estimated payments in place of its owners (section 502).
- Sections 501 and 502 are codified as RCW 82A.04.510 and 82A.04.520. The current RCW 82.32.090(11) cites them by those numbers (Source 9). (corrected in verification)
- Section 205 allows a nonrefundable credit for Washington capital gains tax for the same tax year, "beginning in tax year 2028 with taxes due in 2029".

## 3. Local income taxes

None. State law bars counties and cities from levying a net income tax.

- Source 11, RCW 36.65.030: "A county, city, or city-county shall not levy a tax on net income."
- A second bar also applies. RCW 1.90.100 (Initiative 2111) bars the state and local governments from taxing personal income, except the new chapter 82A tax. (corrected in verification)
  - Source 18: "Neither the state nor any county, city, or other local jurisdiction … may tax any individual person on any form of personal income."

## 4. Ambiguities

- **"Excise" or "income" tax.** RCW 82.87 calls the capital gains tax an excise tax on the sale or exchange of long-term capital assets. For tax-calculation purposes it behaves like an income tax on long-term gains.
- **"Estimated payment" wording.** Source 1 and RCW 82.32.090(10) use "estimated payment" for a payment made by the original due date with an extension. It is not a quarterly estimate. Do not model quarterly installments for 2026.
- **9.9% tier base.** RCW 82.87.040(1)(b) applies the 2.9% to "Washington capital gains" above $1,000,000. That term (RCW 82.87.020(16)) is measured after the RCW 82.87.060 deductions. Source 3 also says "taxable Washington capital gains". So the $1,000,000 threshold applies after the standard deduction.
- **SB 6346 litigation and ballot measure (corrected in verification).** Checked against primary records. This does not affect tax year 2026.
  - The Supreme Court denied mandamus for a referendum. It held the act exempt from referendum (Source 14).
  - The constitutional challenge, case 26-2-00073-20, is pending, with no ruling posted (Source 15).
  - Initiative IP26-645 is on the November 3, 2026 ballot (Sources 16 and 17).
- **The 2026 standard deduction** is not yet published (see above).

### Conflicts resolved

- **Late payment penalty after a timely estimated payment.** RCW 82.87.110(3) says chapter 82.32 penalties apply to any deficiency. Source 1 says a timely estimated payment avoids late payment penalties. The Department's reading is the one to follow, for two reasons.
  - RCW 82.32.090(12) counts the estimated payment as a "return".
  - RCW 82.32.090(10) sets a separate penalty for an estimated payment that is too low.

  Interest is not covered by Source 1's statement, so it still applies under RCW 82.87.110(3).
- **SB 6346 effective date.** The bill page gives June 11, 2026. That is the date the act took effect. Section 201 imposes the tax only from January 1, 2028. Both are correct, for different things.

### SB 6346 status (the 2028 income tax)

- **Enacted.** Engrossed Substitute Senate Bill 6346, Chapter 238, Laws of 2026.
  - It passed the House on March 9, 2026 (51–46) and the Senate on March 11, 2026 (27–21).
  - It was approved by the Governor on March 30, 2026, and filed with the Secretary of State on March 31, 2026.
  - The act took effect June 11, 2026, except named sections that take effect July 1, 2026 or January 1, 2029 (Source 12, certification page).
- **Referendum blocked.** The Supreme Court denied a writ of mandamus in *Heywood v. Hobbs*, No. 105220-1 (Source 14). Its order of May 4, 2026 says ESSB 6346 is "not subject to referendum under article II, section 1." The order says the tax's constitutionality "is not before us."
- **Challenged in court.** *Petter et al. v. State of Washington*, Klickitat County Superior Court, case 26-2-00073-20, Judge Randall C. Krog (Source 15). The county page lists no ruling. It was last updated May 1, 2026. No primary record of a ruling was found.
- **On the ballot.** Initiative IP26-645 is certified for the November 3, 2026 general election (Source 17). The Attorney General's statement (Source 16) says it would "repeal a 9.9% tax on annual individual income over $1,000,000". It would also prohibit state and local taxes on individual income. Source 16 says "The only tax explicitly listed and repealed by the measure is the tax on income over $1,000,000." Neither source says how the prohibition would affect the capital gains tax.
- **Contingency in the act.** Section 1202: if a court of final jurisdiction invalidates section 201, sections 1–1003 and 1201–1209 "are null and void".

### 2026 changes

- 2026 c 191 adds RCW 82.87.115, which allows prepayment for tax year 2026 onward. It was already recorded.
- 2026 c 238 (SB 6346) amends RCW 82.32.090, adding subsection (11). It also amends RCW 1.90.100 to allow the chapter 82A tax. Neither changes tax year 2026.
- Initiative IP26-645 is on the November 3, 2026 ballot (see above). If approved, it could change the 2028 tax and possibly the wider legal position. It does not change tax year 2026 capital gains rules on its face.
