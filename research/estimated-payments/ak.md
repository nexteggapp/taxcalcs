## 1. Does Alaska tax individual income for tax year 2026?

**No.** Alaska has no individual income tax on wages, interest, dividends, capital gains or anything else. The state net income tax statute expressly excludes individuals. The individual tax provisions were repealed in 1980.

- Source 1: "The State of Alaska currently does not have an individual income tax, therefore no employee withholding for state income tax is required."
- Source 2, AS 43.20.012(a): "The tax imposed by this chapter does not apply to (1) an individual"
- Source 2: former individual provisions are marked "[Repealed, 10 ch 1 SSSLA 1980.]" (for example AS 43.20.015 and 43.20.017).

## 2. Estimated payment rules

Not applicable. No individual income-type tax exists.

## 3. Local income taxes

**None levied (corrected in verification).** The state's official report on municipal taxation (Source 5) shows no municipal income tax. Its 58 pages mention "income" only once, about property assessment ratios. Its table of contents covers sales, bed, raw fish, alcohol, tobacco and marijuana, and property taxes.

- Source 5: Chapter 29.45 "authorizes cities, boroughs, and unified municipalities to levy a property tax" and "also authorizes the implementation of sales and use taxes."

**Whether a municipality could levy one: NOT FOUND.** No express prohibition was found. AS 29.45 (Source 3) grants no income tax authority. Source 5 notes that home rule municipalities have "broad governmental powers" under the constitution. So the legal limit is not settled by the sources read.

## 4. Ambiguities

- **Employee unemployment insurance contribution on wages.** Alaska withholds an unemployment insurance contribution from employees' wages. It is not an income tax, but a wage-based calculator may need it.
  - Source 4: "The 2026 Taxable Wage Base is $54,200.00"
  - Source 4: "The 2026 Employee Rate is 0.50%"
- **Vestigial individual return.** AS 43.20.012(b) says "An individual may file a return under this chapter in order to receive a tax credit under AS 43.20.013". The credits cover political contributions (up to $100) and 16% of the federal dependent care credit. Payment "may not be made without an appropriation for that purpose" (AS 43.20.013(c)). Whether any such credit is paid for 2026 was not researched.
