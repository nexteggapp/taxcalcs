## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay estimated tax if your Louisiana tax after credits and withholding will exceed $1,000 for a single filer or $2,000 for joint filers. The statute states a single $1,000 figure; see item 10.

- [ESI-26]: "can reasonably be expected to exceed $1,000 for a single filer or $2,000 for joint filers."
- [RS-116](A): "one thousand dollars for declarations of estimated tax for income tax years starting on and after January 1, 2001, after deducting all allowable credits".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's tax.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax. The prior-year return must have been filed, must show a liability, and must cover a 12-month year. It cannot be a part-year resident return.
- There is no 110% tier and no AGI threshold. High-income taxpayers keep the 100% prior-year option. NOT FOUND: any higher percentage in [ESI-26], [210RI-25] or [RS-118].
- Third option: the prior year's facts taxed at the current year's rates and filing status (Exception 3).
- (corrected in verification) For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, Act 307 of 2026 lowers the current-year percentage from 90% to 70%. Farmers and fishers stay at 66 2/3%. The prior-year option stays at 100%. Exception 3 now uses the current year's single rate and standard deduction. [ACT-307]: "Ninety Seventy percent of the tax shown on the return for the taxable year" (struck text "Ninety", new text "Seventy").
- (corrected in verification) The quotes below from [RS-118] are the 2025 text. The legislature's statute page has not yet been updated for Act 307.

Quotes:

- [RS-118](B)(2)(b): "(i) Ninety percent of the tax shown on the return for the taxable year".
- [RS-118](B)(2)(b): "(ii) One hundred percent of the tax shown on the return of the individual for the preceding taxable year."
- [RS-118](D)(1)(a): "a return showing a liability for tax was filed ... and such preceding year was a taxable year of twelve months".
- [210RI-25] line 3: "If no return was filed for 2024 or you filed as a part-year resident for 2024, use the amount from Form R-210R, Line 1."
- [210RI-25], Exception 3: "This exception applies if your 2025 payments equal or exceed the tax computed on prior year's income using 2025 rates and filing status."

## 3. Due dates and share due

There are four equal installments of 25% each. The 2026 dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Weekend and legal-holiday dates move to the next business day.

If the requirement is first met later in the year, fewer installments are due: 1/3 each, 1/2 each, or the full amount.

The January installment or declaration is excused if the return is filed and the tax paid in full by January 31. This does not excuse earlier underpayments.

- [RS-118](B)(2)(a): "the amount of any required installment shall be twenty-five percent of the required annual payment."
- [ESI-26]: "falls on a weekend or legal holiday, the payment is due on the next business day."
- [ESI-26]: "if the taxpayer files their individual income tax return by January 31 and pays the total amount due."

## 4. Withholding

The statute treats an equal part of withholding as paid on each installment date, unless you establish the actual withholding dates. The 2025 instructions instead allow spreading withholding by the number of months in each period, or using actual dates. See item 10.

- [RS-118](E)(2): "an equal part of such amount shall be deemed paid on each installment date".
- [RS-118](E)(2), continuing: "unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld".
- [210RI-25]: "you may equally distribute the amounts to each period based upon the number of months in the period."
- [210RI-25], next sentence: "You may use the actual date when those amounts were withheld".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if 2025 tax after credits, withholding and timely extension payments is $1,000 or less (single, married filing separately, head of household, qualifying surviving spouse) or $2,000 or less (married filing jointly). This is Exception 1.
  - [210RI-25]: "If your filing status is Married Filing Joint AND the amount on Line 6 above is less than or equal to $2,000".
- No liability in the prior year: NOT FOUND as an exception. The prior-year option requires a return "showing a liability" ([RS-118](D)(1)(a)). Without one, [210RI-25] line 3 falls back to the 90% current-year amount.
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate rule. With no prior-year Louisiana return, only the current-year options apply.
- Part-year residents: the prior-year exceptions 2 and 3 do not apply if the prior-year return was part-year. [210RI-25]: "This exception does NOT apply to part-year residents for 2024." Current-year part-year and nonresident filers use [210NRI-25].
- Farmers and fishermen: this applies if at least two-thirds of estimated gross income comes from farming or fishing, including oyster farming.
  - The current-year percentage is 66 2/3%.
  - There is one declaration and payment, due January 15.
  - No estimate is required if the return is filed and the tax paid by March 1.
  - [ESI-26]: "files their income tax return on or before March 1 of the succeeding year and pays the total tax due".
  - [210RI-25]: "The declaration payment must be at least 66.66 percent of the estimated tax to satisfy any underpayment."
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in [ESI-26], [210RI-25] or [RS-118].
- Casualty, disaster and good-faith waiver: the secretary may waive the penalty on an application made within one year of the return due date, showing good faith. Good faith is presumed for extraordinary circumstances beyond your control. It is also presumed if you declared and paid before the return due date.
  - [RS-118](I)(1): "The failure to make the estimated payments was attributable to extraordinary circumstances beyond the individual's control."
  - [ESI-26]: "To request an underpayment penalty waiver, use Form R-20128, Request for Waiver of Penalties."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

The method is allowed, on Form R-210R. There are two versions.

- Exception 4, "Annualized Income":
  - It annualizes federal AGI through March 31, June 30 and September 30, which are calendar quarters, unlike the federal periods.
  - The factors are 4, 2 and 1.33, and the percentages 22.5%, 45% and 67.5%.
  - Farmers use 16.67%, 33.33% and 50%.
- Exception 5, "Installment Period Income": it compares payments with 90% of the tax on actual, not annualized, income through 3/31, 5/31, 8/31 and 12/31.
- (corrected in verification) For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, Act 307 repeals the annualized method, [RS-118](D)(1)(c), which was Exception 4. Exception 5 remains, at 70% instead of 90%. [ACT-307] Section 2: "R.S. 47:118(D)(1)(c) is hereby repealed in its entirety."

Quotes:

- [210RI-25]: "This exception provides for annualizing the income for the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd quarters."
- [210RI-25]: "Except for the first installment period, the remaining installment periods do not coincide with calendar quarters."
- [RS-118](D)(2): "ninety percent of the tax computed ... on the basis of the actual taxable income for the months in the taxable year".

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an interest-like charge on each installment's shortfall, by days late. It runs from the due date until paid, or until the 15th day of the fourth month after year end (April 15), whichever is earlier. For farmers, days run from January 15.

- The rate is 12% a year (0.0329% a day). It is fixed in [RS-118](A). It is not reset annually.
- (corrected in verification) For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, the penalty period ends on the 15th day of the fifth month after year end (May 15), not April 15. [ACT-307]: "The 15th fifteenth day of the fourth fifth month following the close of the taxable year" (struck "15th" and "fourth").

Compute it on Form R-210R (residents) or R-210NR (nonresidents and part-year residents).

- [RS-118](A): "a penalty of twelve percent per annum upon the amount of the underpayment".
- [210RI-25] line 18: "The penalty rate is 12 percent per year. The daily penalty rate is 0.0329 percent."
- [RS-118](C)(1): the period ends on "The 15th day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year".

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The threshold depends on filing status: $1,000 single, $2,000 joint.
- There is no 110% rule.
- The prior-year option requires a prior-year return showing a liability. A zero-tax prior year does not help.
- An extra safe harbor exists: the prior year's facts at the current year's rates (Exception 3).
- Exception 4 annualizes over calendar quarters (March, June, September), not the federal periods.
- The 12% statutory rate is far above the federal rate and does not move.
- (corrected in verification) From tax year 2026: the current-year safe harbor is 70%, there is no annualized method, and the penalty runs to May 15.
- A waiver requires a written application within one year of the return's due date.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Threshold: [RS-116](A) states one threshold of $1,000 regardless of filing status. [ESI-26] and [210RI-25] give $1,000 single and $2,000 joint.
- Withholding allocation: [RS-118](E)(2) deems an equal part paid on each installment date. [210RI-25] says withholding may be spread "based upon the number of months in the period". The periods are unequal: about 3.5, 2, 3 and 4 months to the four due dates. The two methods give different results.
- Exception 4 periods: they end at calendar quarters (3/31, 6/30, 9/30). The installment due dates are 4/15, 6/15 and 9/15, so the second and third periods run past their due dates. [210RI-25] notes this mismatch.
- Search snippet: a web search snippet stated a May 15, 2026 first installment. The primary document [ESI-26] says April 15, 2026, and that is used here. (corrected in verification) May 15 is the new end of the penalty period under [ACT-307]. It is not an installment date. [ESI-26] and [RS-117.1] keep April 15.
- 2026 penalty form: [210RI-25] is the 2025 form. No 2026 R-210R is published yet.

### Conflicts resolved

- Threshold for joint filers. NOT resolved; it changes a number. [RS-116](A) sets one $1,000 threshold for every individual. Act 307 did not amend 47:116, and the only 2024 bill to amend it (HB 19, Third Extraordinary Session) was not enacted. LDR applies $2,000 for joint filers in [ESI-26], [210RI-25] Exception 1 and [EST-PAGE]. It cites 47:116(A) for that figure, but the statute does not contain it. On a strict reading the statute governs and $1,000 applies to joint filers. In practice LDR, which assesses the penalty, stops the computation at $2,000 for joint filers. No rule or ruling explaining the $2,000 figure was found.
- Withholding allocation. Resolved in favour of the statute. [RS-118](E)(2) treats an equal part as paid on each installment date, or the actual dates if you establish them. Act 307 left (E) unchanged. The [210RI-25] option to spread "based upon the number of months in the period" has no statutory basis, though it is the more generous reading in some years.
- Exception 4 periods. Resolved for 2025: [RS-118](D)(1)(c) annualizes "through the end of the first, second, or third quarter", so calendar quarters are correct. The question no longer matters from 2026, because the method is repealed.
- Search snippet "May 15". Resolved. See item 10.
- 2026 penalty form. Confirmed: no 2026 R-210R is published. It will need to reflect [ACT-307].

### 2026 changes

[ACT-307], effective May 22, 2026 per [SUM-26], applies to tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. For individuals it changes four things:

- The current-year safe harbor falls from 90% to 70%, in both the required annual payment and Exception 5. Farmers and fishers stay at 66 2/3%.
- The penalty period ends on the 15th day of the fifth month after year end (May 15, 2027 for tax year 2026), not the fourth.
- The annualized-income exception, [RS-118](D)(1)(c), is repealed.
- The prior-year-facts exception, [RS-118](D)(1)(b), uses the current year's single rate and standard deduction.

[SUM-26]: "It reduces from 90% to 70% the percentage of current-year tax used to determine certain individual estimated tax payment requirements."

[ACT-307] does not change the $1,000 threshold, the 100% prior-year option, the installment dates or the 12% rate. [ESI-26] (PDF created December 3, 2025) predates the Act and does not mention it.

The 2026 worksheet rate is 3% with a $12,875 or $25,750 standard deduction ([ESI-26] lines 3 and 5). This changes the tax, not the payment rules.
