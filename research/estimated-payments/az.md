## 1. Who must make estimated payments

The test is income, not tax owed. You must pay if your Arizona gross income was greater than $75,000 in the prior year and exceeds $75,000 in the current year. For married filing jointly the figure is $150,000. Single, married filing separately and head of household all use $75,000.

"Arizona gross income" is federal AGI for a full-year resident. For a part-year resident it is the part of federal AGI reported to Arizona. For a nonresident it is the part of federal AGI from Arizona sources.

Quotes:
- A.R.S. §43-581(A), source 5: "exceeds $75,000 or $150,000 if a joint return is filed and whose Arizona gross income was greater than $75,000 in the preceding taxable year".
- Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1, table: Single $75,000 / $75,000; Married filing joint $150,000 / $150,000; Married filing separate $75,000 / $75,000; Head of Household $75,000 / $75,000.
- Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "If you met the income threshold for 2025, you must make estimated payments during 2026 unless you are sure you will not meet"
- Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "your Arizona gross income is that part of your federal adjusted gross income that you must report to Arizona."

Separately, there is no penalty if tax after withholding and credits is under $1,000 (question 5).

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current-year percentage: 90%.
- Prior-year percentage: 100%.
- Higher prior-year percentage: none. Arizona has no 110% rule.
- The prior-year option requires that you were required to file, and did file, a prior-year Arizona return. The sources state no 12-month requirement for the Arizona prior-year option.
- A high-income taxpayer does not lose the prior-year option.

Quotes:
- A.R.S. §43-581(A), source 5: "at least ninety percent of the tax due for the current taxable year or one hundred percent of the tax due for the preceding"
- Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "only if you were required to file and did file a 2025 Arizona income tax return."
- Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4, line 8: "Only enter an amount on line 8 if you filed an Arizona personal income tax return for 2024."

## 3. Due dates and share due by each

- Due dates for 2026: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027. The statute ties them to the federal dates.
- Equal quarters: yes, 25% each, "for the most part".
- Weekend and holiday shifting: yes, to the next business day after a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. The 2025 Form 221 prints "Jun-16-2025" because June 15, 2025 was a Sunday.
- January payment waived: you need not make the fourth payment if you file the 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay in full. The 2025 date was January 31, 2026.

Quotes:
- A.R.S. §43-581(A), source 5: "paid in four installments on or before the due dates established by the internal revenue code".
- Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "For the most part, you must make your payments in four equal installments."
- Form 140ES voucher, TY2026, source 2: "If any of the due dates fall on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, you may make the required payment ... next business day".
- Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "If you file your 2026 Arizona return by February 1, 2027 and pay in full ... you do not have to make the fourth"
- Form 221, TY2025, source 3, line 10: "(a) Apr-15-2025 (b) Jun-16-2025 (c) Sep-15-2025 (d) Jan-15-2026".

## 4. How withholding is treated

Withholding is treated as paid 25% on each due date. You may show it was paid on other dates.

- Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "The department considers you to have paid ¼ (25%) of Arizona income tax withheld on each payment due date unless you can show otherwise."

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if Arizona tax after withholding and credits is less than $1,000. A.R.S. §43-581(E)(2), source 5: "The taxpayer's Arizona income tax liability due on the taxpayer's return is less than $1,000."
- Income below the threshold in either year: no penalty. Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "If your Arizona gross income was less than the prescribed thresholds for either 2024 or 2025, you are not subject to the penalty."
- Federal exceptions apply. If you qualify for an exception under IRC §6654, there is no Arizona penalty. A.R.S. §43-581(E)(1), source 5: "The estimated tax payments made pursuant to this section are allowable exceptions under section 6654 of the internal revenue code."
- No liability in the prior year: this is tested on federal tax, not Arizona tax. Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "You had no federal tax liability for 2024. You were also a U.S. citizen or resident of the U.S. for the entire year".
- Federal tax under $1,000: also an exception. Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "The total tax shown on your 2025 federal income tax return minus your federal withholding is less than $1,000."
- First-year residents: no specific exception was found. The two-year income test in A.R.S. §43-581(A) uses prior-year Arizona gross income. For a new resident that is likely low or zero, so the requirement would not apply. That is my inference from the statute, not stated in any source.
- Part-year residents: no exception. They are covered, using Arizona-reported income (question 1).
- Farmers and fishermen: anyone who reports as a farmer or fisherman for federal purposes makes one installment, due January 15, 2027. No payment is needed if the return is filed by March 1, 2027 and paid in full. The sources give no separate percentage, so 90% / 100% applies; no 66 2/3% rule was found.
  - Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "If you report as a farmer or fisherman for federal purposes, you only have to make one installment for a taxable year."
  - Form 140ES instructions, TY2026, source 1: "file your 2026 Arizona return on or before March 1, 2027 and pay in full".
- Nonresident aliens: may make three payments (June 15, September 15, January 15), with 50% due in the first. Source 1: "The first installment must equal 50% of your total required payments."
- Retired or disabled: waiver available. Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "In 2024 or 2025, you retired after age 62 or became disabled, and your underpayment was due to reasonable cause."
- Casualty or disaster: waiver available. Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "The underpayment was due to a casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances, and it would be inequitable to impose the penalty."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, using the Annualized Income Installment Worksheet on page 2 of Form 221. The periods are federal-style (Jan 1 to Mar 31, May 31, Aug 31, Dec 31). The factors are 4.0, 2.4, 1.5, 1.0, and the percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5%, 90%. If you use it for one installment you must use it for all.

- Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "If you use the Annualized Income Installment Worksheet for any payment due date, you must use it for all payment due dates."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty equals the interest that would accrue on each installment's underpayment for the days unpaid. It is capped at 10% of the underpayment. It replaces interest; it is not added to interest.

- A.R.S. §42-1125(Q), source 6: "a penalty is assessed equal to the amount of interest that would otherwise accrue ... not exceeding ten percent of the amount not paid."

The rate is the federal short-term rate plus 3 percentage points, compounded annually under §42-1123.
- A.R.S. §42-1123(A), source 7: "the rate of interest for both overpayments and underpayments for all taxpayers is the federal short-term rate ... plus three percentage points."

The rate changes by quarter, so Form 221 computes the penalty in rate periods. Unpaid penalty compounds on January 1.
- Form 221 instructions, TY2025, source 4: "Penalty compounds annually on January 1 of each year."

Current rates, from ADOR's interest-rate page (source 8):

| Period | Rate |
|---|---|
| January 1 – December 31, 2025 | 7% |
| January 1 – March 31, 2026 | 7% |
| April 1 – June 30, 2026 | 6% |
| July 1 – December 31, 2026 | 7% |

The 2025 Form 221 prints 7% for rate periods 1–3, and "*%" ("to be determined") for January 1 – April 15, 2026.

Form: Arizona Form 221. The penalty goes on Form 140 line 76, Form 140PY line 85, or Form 140NR line 83.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- Whether you must pay at all depends on income, not on tax owed. You need Arizona gross income above $75,000, or $150,000 joint, in both the prior and current year. Under that, no estimates are required however much tax is owed.
- There is no 110% rule for high-income taxpayers. 100% of prior-year tax always works.
- The penalty is capped at 10% of the underpayment.
- The penalty compounds annually on January 1.
- Federal relief carries over. Qualifying for a federal §6654 exception, including one based on federal (not Arizona) tax, removes the Arizona penalty.
- The rate is the federal short-term rate plus 3 points. That is the same formula as the federal underpayment rate, but it comes from Arizona's own statute.

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Stale dates in the 2025 Form 221 instructions. They describe 2025 payment columns with 2026 dates: "In column (b), enter payments you made after April 15, 2026 through June 15, 2026". They give nonresident-alien dates as "June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2026". The 2025 form itself uses 2025 dates.
- Withholding in the rate tables. The 2025 Form 221 instructions say "Do not list the withholding attributable to the first payment due on April 15, 2026". This apparently means April 15, 2025.
- File-and-pay date. The 2025 instructions say "January 31, 2026". The 2026 instructions say "February 1, 2027", which is January 31 shifted off a Sunday. Federal uses January 31.
- Farmer definition. Arizona uses "report as a farmer or fisherman for federal purposes". It gives no two-thirds-of-income test and no 66 2/3% percentage. Whether the federal 66 2/3% rule applies through §43-581(D) ("in a manner similar to the manner prescribed in the internal revenue code") is not stated.
- Prior-year return requirement. The 140ES instructions require that you were "required to file and did file" a prior-year return. The Form 221 instructions require only that you "filed". Neither mentions a 12-month year for the Arizona prior-year option.

### Conflicts resolved

- **File-and-pay date.** Resolved: these dates do not conflict. §43-581(D) tells ADOR to follow the Internal Revenue Code. §6654(h) uses January 31. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the 2026 instructions move it to Monday, February 1. January 31, 2026 was a Saturday, and the 2025 instructions did not move it. So the shift is applied inconsistently from year to year, but each date is January 31 or the next business day.
- **Prior-year return requirement.** Not resolvable from the sources. The statute says only "one hundred percent of the tax due for the preceding taxable year". It mentions neither filing nor a 12-month year. The 140ES test ("required to file and did file") is stricter than Form 221's ("filed"). Neither is backed by statute text. A rule under §43-581(D) might settle it. Arizona Administrative Code rules were not checked.
- **Farmer percentage.** Still unresolved. §43-581(A) states only 90% and 100%. §43-581(D) points to the IRC, where farmers use 66 2/3%. ADOR's forms state neither percentage for farmers. This would change a farmer's required payment.
- **Stale dates in the 2025 Form 221 instructions.** These are drafting errors. The form's own dates are correct, and nothing turns on the wording.
