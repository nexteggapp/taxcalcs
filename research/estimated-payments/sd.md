## 1. Does South Dakota tax individual income for tax year 2026?

**No.** No state income tax applies to wages, interest, dividends, capital gains or anything else.

- Source 1: "South Dakota is one of seven states that does not impose a state income tax."

## 2. Estimated payment rules

Not applicable. No individual income-type tax exists.

## 3. Local income taxes

**None levied (corrected in verification).** The Department's municipal tax guide covers only these local taxes:

- municipal sales, use and gross receipts taxes under SDCL 10-52 and 10-52A;
- telecommunication, tourism, motor vehicle and lodging taxes.

It lists no income tax (Source 5).

- Source 5: "South Dakota law (SDCL 10-52 and 10-52A) allows municipalities to impose a municipal sales or use tax, and gross receipts tax."

**Whether a municipality could levy one: NOT FOUND.** No express authority or prohibition was found.

## 4. Ambiguities

- **No constitutional ban.** The constitution does not prohibit an income tax. It only requires a voter initiative or a two-thirds legislative vote to raise the rate of one.
  - Source 2, Art. XI, Sec. 13: "The rate of taxation imposed by the state of South Dakota on personal or corporate income … shall not be increased unless by consent of the people"
- **"One of seven states".** This is the Department of Revenue's own count. It does not say which states it counts, and the count may not reflect New Hampshire's 2025 repeal or Washington's capital gains tax. It does not change the South Dakota answer.
- **Statutes checked (corrected in verification).** The Title 10 chapter list has no individual income tax chapter (Source 4). The only income tax chapter is 10-43, "Income Tax On Banks And Financial Corporations". A new tax would need a voter initiative or a two-thirds vote in each house (Source 3).
