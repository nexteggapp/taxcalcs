## 1. Who must make estimated payments

The threshold is tax expected to exceed withholding by $500 or more. Payments are also required only if withholding is expected to be less than the smaller of 70% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax.

- Source 1 (OW-8-ES, TY2026): "you can reasonably expect your tax liability to exceed your withholding by $500 or more, and you expect your withholding to be less than the smaller of".
- Married individuals test their combined liability. Source 7 (§ 2385.7(A)(2)): "the combined tax liability of the married individuals can reasonably be expected to be Five Hundred Dollars ($500.00) or more".
- Single individuals use the same $500 test. Source 7 (§ 2385.7(A)(1)). No other filing-status difference was found.
- Estates and qualifying farmers are excluded. See answer 5.

## 2. Required annual payment

The required annual payment is the lesser of 70% of the current year's tax or 100% of the prior year's tax.

- Source 8 (§ 2385.9(B)): "Seventy percent (70%) of the tax shown on the return for the taxable year; or".
- Source 8 (§ 2385.9(B)): "One hundred percent (100%) of the tax shown on the return for the preceding taxable year of twelve (12) months."
- The prior-year option requires a 12-month prior year. It requires that a prior-year return was required. Source 3 (OW-8-P, TY2025, line 7): "Previous year's return must be for 12 months. If you were not required to file an income tax return for the previous tax year, stop here".
- Tax liability means tax after all credits other than withholding, estimates and extension payments. Source 5 (511 packet, TY2025, line 40): "the Oklahoma income tax due less all credits except amounts paid on withholding, estimated tax, and extension payments."
- Higher prior-year percentage above an income threshold: none. § 2385.9(B) sets 100% with no income condition (source 13). (corrected in verification)
- Loss of the prior-year option for high-income taxpayers: none. § 2385.9(B) has no income condition (source 13). (corrected in verification)
- Married-filing-separately variant: none in § 2385.9(B). § 2385.7(A) distinguishes only single and married individuals, for the $500 requirement test (source 13). (corrected in verification)

## 3. Due dates and shares

There are four equal installments: April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. Each is 25% of the required annual payment.

- Source 8 (§ 2385.9(A)): "The required annual payment of estimated tax shall be paid in four equal installments as follows".
- Source 1 (OW-8-ES, TY2026) lists "1st Quarter - April 15", "2nd Quarter - June 15", "3rd Quarter - September 15", "4th Quarter - January 15".
- Fiscal-year filers substitute the corresponding months. Source 8 (§ 2385.9(A)(2)).
- Weekend and holiday shift: the next business day. The trigger is a day the OTC is closed, not the federal calendar. Source 1: "If the due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday when the Oklahoma Tax Commission offices are closed, your payment is due the next business day."
- January waiver: the statute waives the January 15 installment if the return is filed and the tax paid by January 31. Source 8 (§ 2385.9(A)(1)): "if taxpayer files return and pays tax due on or before January 31, the payment of the installment due January 15 is waived". The OTC forms do not mention this waiver (see answer 10).

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid one-fourth on each due date, unless the taxpayer shows otherwise.

- Source 3 (OW-8-P, TY2025, line 10 note): "You are considered to have paid one-fourth of your withholding on each payment due date unless you can show otherwise."
- The taxpayer can therefore use actual withholding dates instead. The form does not name an election or a box for this.
- Estimated payments count in the quarter actually paid. Source 3: "Estimated tax must be entered in the quarter in which it was paid".
- A prior-year overpayment applied to this year counts in the first quarter. Source 3: "Include in the first quarter any overpayment of tax from your previous tax year's return".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below $1,000: no interest if the tax shown on the return is less than $1,000. Source 1 (OW-8-ES, TY2026): "No underpayment of estimated tax interest shall be imposed if the tax shown on the return is less than $1,000." Source 3 (OW-8-P line 3) applies this to tax after credits: "If less than $1,000, stop here; you do not owe the interest."
- Under $500 after withholding: no interest. Source 3 (OW-8-P line 6): "If less than $500, stop here; you do not owe the interest."
- No prior-year liability: the statute exempts a taxpayer who was a resident all of a 12-month prior year with no liability. Source 9 (§ 2385.13(C) as reprinted): "the taxpayer was an Oklahoma resident throughout the preceding taxable year of twelve (12) months and did not have any liability for tax". The OW-8-P test is different: no prior-year filing requirement (source 3, line 7). See answer 10.
- First-year residents: no separate rule. The § 2385.13(C) exception requires full-year prior-year residence, so it does not cover them. No first-year rule appears in §§ 2385.7, 2385.9 or 2385.13 (source 13). (corrected in verification)
- Part-year residents and nonresidents: no exception. They compute the tax as if all income were Oklahoma income, then prorate by Oklahoma-source income. Source 1: "Lines 1 through 5 shall be calculated as if all income were earned in Oklahoma."
- Farmers: fully exempt from estimated payments. There is no reduced percentage and no special due date. The test is at least 66 2/3% of gross income from farming in the current or prior year. Source 7 (§ 2385.7(B)(2)): "gross income from farming for the taxable year is at least sixty-six and two-thirds percent (66 2/3%)". The prior-year qualification carries forward one year only. Source 7: "In no event shall the qualification for the previous taxable year be carried forward for more than one (1) year." The claimant marks a box on Form 511 line 22 and attaches the federal return (source 5).
- Fishermen: no exception. § 2385.7(B) exempts only estates and farmers (source 13). (corrected in verification)
- Estates: exempt. Source 7 (§ 2385.7(B)(1)): "Estates".
- Retired or disabled: no exception. § 2385.7(B) and § 2385.13(C) contain no retirement, disability or casualty waiver (source 13). (corrected in verification)
- Casualty or disaster: no standing rule found. The OTC grants relief by Commission order per disaster, following IRS postponements. Source 12 (2025-09-30) postponed 2025 estimated payments for six counties: "2025 estimated payments due April 15, June 16, and September 15". The order cited is Commission Order No. 25-08-26-05.

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed. The statute follows the Internal Revenue Code method, and only for a 12-month year.

- Source 8 (§ 2385.9(C)): "taxpayers may compute the tax by placing taxable income on an annualized basis". Source 8: "shall only be permitted for a taxable year of twelve (12) months."
- For the penalty, use Form OW-8-P-SUP-I (TY2025) with OW-8-P. Mark the "annualized installment method" box on Form 511 or 511-NR line 40.
- For planning payments, use Form OW-8-ES-SUP (TY2026).
- Periods are 1/1-3/31, 1/1-5/31, 1/1-8/31 and 1/1-12/31, with annualization factors 4, 2.4, 1.5 and 1 (sources 2 and 4).
- Applicable percentages are 17.5%, 35%, 52.5% and 70%, not the federal 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90% (sources 2 and 4, line 16).
- If used for one installment, it must be used for all. Source 4 (OW-8-P-SUP-I, TY2025): "If you use this form for any payment due date, you must use it for all payment due dates."

## 7. Penalty computation

The charge is called "interest", not a penalty. It is simple interest at 20% a year on each installment's shortfall for the days it is late.

- Rate: 20% a year, fixed in the statute. It is not tied to the federal rate or to Oklahoma's 1.25% monthly delinquency rate. Source 9 (§ 2385.13(A) as reprinted): "there shall be added to the amount of the underpayment interest thereon at an annual rate of twenty percent (20%)". Source 1 (OW-8-ES, TY2026) states the same 20% rate.
- Underpayment: the required installment minus the amount paid by its due date. Source 9 (§ 2385.13(B)): "the excess of the required installment over the amount paid on or before the due date of the installment."
- Period: from the installment due date to the earlier of payment or the 15th day of the fourth month after year end. Source 9 (§ 2385.13(B)): "shall run from the due date of the required installment to the earlier of the fifteenth day of the fourth month".
- Formula: balance times days times 20%, divided by 365. Source 3 (OW-8-P, TY2025, Part 3, Column E): "Column C x Number of days in Column D x 20% divided by 365".
- Payments apply to the earliest balance first. Source 3: "the earliest date of payment being applied first."
- A balance paid with the return counts as paid on the earlier of the filing date or the original due date. Source 3: "should be listed using the date you filed your return or the original due date, whichever is earlier."
- Form: OW-8-P, with OW-8-P-SUP-I for the annualized method. The interest goes on Form 511 or 511-NR line 40. If the taxpayer does not compute it, the OTC sends a bill (source 5).
- A pending change: HB 2730 would replace the 20% rate with Wall Street Prime plus three percentage points. It passed the House 87-11 on 02/24/2026 with an effective date of November 1, 2026 (source 14). The Senate referred it to Revenue and Taxation on 04/01/2026, and the status page (source 10) shows no later action. It is not law. The 20% rate still appears on the TY2026 OW-8-ES. (corrected in verification)

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise a taxpayer

- The current-year safe harbor is 70%, not 90% (source 8).
- The prior-year safe harbor is 100% for everyone. No 110% rule was found (sources 1, 3, 8).
- The charge is a fixed 20% a year, far above the federal rate (source 9).
- The requirement threshold is $500 of tax over withholding, not $1,000 (source 7).
- A separate rule removes all interest if the tax shown on the return is under $1,000 (source 1).
- Farmers are fully exempt at a 66 2/3% gross-income test, using the current or prior year. Oklahoma has no reduced farmer percentage or special farmer due date (source 7).
- The January waiver deadline is January 31, the same as federal, but it appears only in the statute (source 8).
- The annualized percentages are 17.5/35/52.5/70% (source 4).
- The due-date shift follows days the OTC is closed (source 1).
- Amended returns: one filed by the original due date, including extensions, replaces the original tax for this computation. One filed later does not. Source 5 (line 40): "the tax shown on the amended return will not be used to compute the amount of underpayment."

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Prior-year exception test. The statute (source 9, § 2385.13(C)) requires full-year Oklahoma residence and no prior-year liability. OW-8-P line 7 (source 3) instead excuses anyone "not required to file an income tax return for the previous tax year". A nonresident with no prior filing requirement passes the form's test but not the statute's.
- January 31 waiver. § 2385.9(A)(1) (source 8) waives the January 15 installment if the return is filed and paid by January 31. OW-8-ES, OW-8-P and the 511 packets do not mention it, and OW-8-P Part 3 has no step for it.
- Withholding timing. OW-8-P line 10 deems withholding paid one-fourth per quarter "unless you can show otherwise". Part 3 says withholding is "deemed paid on the quarterly due date(s)" and asks for amounts "withheld, on or before the quarterly due date". The form does not say how to elect actual dates.
- Farmer percentage wording. The statute says 66 2/3%. The 511 and 511-NR packets say "66.67% (or two-thirds)". OW-8-ES says "at least two-thirds". The OTC "Pay Taxes" page (source 11) says "66.66%". (corrected in verification)
- § 2385.13 text. The text cited comes from HB 2730's reprint of the 2021 statute. Verification confirmed it word for word in the Legislature's Title 68 compilation (source 13), last amended by Laws 2021, c. 436. OAC 710:50-13 could not be read on an official site. (corrected in verification)
- Penalty test basis. OW-8-ES and § 2385.13(C) use "tax shown on the return" for the $1,000 test. OW-8-P line 3 uses tax after refundable and nonrefundable credits.

### Resolved conflicts

- Prior-year exception test. The statute governs. § 2385.13(C) was last amended by Laws 2021, c. 436, and no later amendment was found (source 13). OW-8-P line 7 is broader for one case only. A taxpayer who filed a 12-month prior-year return showing $0 Oklahoma tax has a $0 required payment under § 2385.9(B)(2), whatever their residence. The cases differ only for a taxpayer with no prior-year return who was not a full-year resident. The form excuses them. § 2385.13(C) does not, and § 2385.9(B)(2) does not say what "tax shown on the return" means when there is no return. This remains unresolved. OAC 710:50-13 may address it but could not be read on an official site.
- January 31 waiver. The statute governs. § 2385.9(A)(1) has been unamended since Laws 2000, c. 314 (source 13). The forms omit the waiver but do not contradict it.
- Withholding timing. Unresolved. § 2385.14 says only that withheld tax is a payment on account. OW-8-P allows actual dates "unless you can show otherwise" but gives no way to report them.
- Farmer percentage. The statute's 66 2/3% governs (source 13). The 511 packets round to 66.67%, and the Pay Taxes page truncates to 66.66%. The difference matters only at exactly two-thirds.
- Penalty test basis. The statute says "tax shown on the return" without defining it. The OTC reads it as tax after all credits, including refundable credits (source 3, line 2; source 5, line 40). No statute or official rule text resolving this was found.

- No change to the estimated tax rules for tax year 2026 was found. §§ 2385.7, 2385.9 and 2385.13 are unamended since 2000, 2000 and 2021 (source 13).
