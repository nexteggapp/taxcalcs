## 1. Who must make estimated payments

A declaration is required when the Missouri estimated tax, after credits and withholding, is expected to be at least $100. The same rule applies to residents and nonresidents. A nonresident's tax is prorated by the share of Missouri-source AGI.

- MO-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "You are required to file a declaration of estimated tax if your Missouri estimated tax is expected to be $100.00 or more (Section 143.521.1, RSMo)."
- RSMo 143.521.3, source 4: "less the amount which the taxpayer estimates to be the sum of any credits allowable, including tax withheld."

Filing status: spouses eligible to file jointly may make a combined declaration.

- RSMo 143.521.4, source 4: "a husband and wife may make a combined declaration of estimated tax as if they were one taxpayer".

The penalty form uses a higher threshold of $500 (see answers 5 and 10).

## 2. Required annual payment

The required annual payment is the lesser of:

- 90% of the current year's tax (66 2/3% for farmers), or
- 100% of the prior year's tax.

There is no 110% rule and no income threshold. A high-income taxpayer keeps the prior-year option.

- MO-2210 line 6, TY2025, source 2: "Required Annual Payment - Enter the smaller of Line 2 or Line 5".
- RSMo 143.761.4(1), source 6: "The tax shown on the return of the individual or corporation for the preceding taxable year".

Filed return, liability and 12-month year: the prior-year option requires a prior-year return that showed a tax liability and covered 12 months.

- RSMo 143.761.4(1), source 6: "if a return showing a liability for tax was filed for the preceding year and such preceding year was a taxable year of twelve months".
- MO-2210 Exception 1, TY2025, source 2: "The 2024 return must cover a period of 12 months and show a tax liability."

Missouri also keeps a fourth safe harbor that federal law no longer has: tax on the prior year's income computed at the current year's rates and exemptions.

- MO-2210 Exception 2, TY2025, source 2: "Tax on Prior Year's Income using 2025 Rates and Exemptions".

This exception applies only to a taxpayer who had to file a return for the prior year. It does not require that return to show a liability. (corrected in verification)

- 12 CSR 10-2.067(8)(D), source 9: "in case of a taxpayer required to file a return for the preceding taxable year."

## 3. Due dates and shares

There are four equal installments of 25%, due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15.

- MO-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "in equal installments on or before April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15."

If the requirement is first met later in the year, fewer installments are due:

| Requirement first met | First due | Installments |
|---|---|---|
| By April 1 | April 15 | 4 |
| April 2 to June 1 | June 15 | 3 |
| June 2 to September 1 | September 15 | 2 |
| After September 1 | January 15 | 1 |

Source: MO-2210 Part III instruction B, TY2025, source 2.

Weekend and holiday shift: yes.

- MO-1040ES, TY2026, source 1: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the voucher will be considered timely if filed on the next business day."

January rule: a taxpayer who files the return and pays in full by January 31 is treated as having paid by January 15.

- MO-2210 instruction H, TY2025, source 2: "If you filed your return and paid the balance of tax due on or before January 31, 2026, consider the balance paid as of January 15, 2026."
- RSMo 143.521.6, source 4: "If on or before January thirty-first ... an individual files a return for the taxable year ... and pays in full".

## 4. Withholding

By default, withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each due date. The taxpayer may use the actual withholding dates instead.

- RSMo 143.761.5(2), source 6: "an equal part of such amount shall be deemed paid on each installment date for such taxable year, unless the taxpayer establishes the dates on which all amounts were actually withheld".
- MO-2210 instruction F, TY2025, source 2: "You may consider an equal part of the income tax withheld during the year as paid on each required installment date".

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if the tax after credits, minus withholding, is less than $500. MO-2210 line 4, TY2025, source 2: "Subtract Line 3 from Line 1. If less than $500, stop here; you do not owe the penalty."
- No liability in the prior year: no zero-liability exception. The prior-year option requires a return that "showed a tax liability" (answer 2).
- First-year residents and part-year residents: NOT FOUND. Nonresidents follow the same requirement as residents, on prorated tax (MO-1040ES item 5).
- Farmers (Missouri gross income from farming at least two-thirds of total): exempt if the return is filed and the tax paid by March 1. Otherwise they owe one installment at 66 2/3%, due January 15. MO-2210 instruction J, TY2025, source 2: "you filed a Missouri Individual Income Tax Return and paid tax on or before March 1, 2026, you are exempt from charges". Fishing income is expressly excluded from farming income. 12 CSR 10-2.067(2)(B), source 9: income from "farming any aquatic forms of animal and vegetable life (other than oyster farming) does not constitute gross income from farming". (corrected in verification)
- Combat zone: exempt. MO-2210 instruction I, TY2025, source 2: "You may be exempt from a penalty for underpayment of estimated tax if you served in the U.S. Armed Forces in an area designated by the President as a combat zone".
- Retired, disabled, casualty or disaster: none. The regulation says the addition to tax is imposed even with reasonable cause. (corrected in verification) 12 CSR 10-2.067(3), source 9: "imposed without regard to any reasonable cause or lack of willful neglect for the underpayment."

## 6. Annualised income method

Allowed, but as an exception to the penalty rather than a reduced required installment. MO-2210 Part III lines 20-24 offer two income-based exceptions:

- Exception 3: 90% of the tax on annualised income.
- Exception 4: 90% of the tax on actual year-to-date income.

The percentages are 22.5%, 45% and 67.5% for the first three installments. Neither exception can be used for the fourth installment. A different exception may be chosen for each installment.

- MO-2210, TY2025, source 2: "Exception 3 may not be used for the fourth installment period."
- MO-2210, TY2025, source 2: "You may apply a different exception to each underpayment."
- RSMo 143.761.4(2) and (3), source 6.

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is an "addition to tax": interest on each installment's shortfall, for the days from the due date to the payment date or April 15. Days in 2025 are charged at the 2025 rate and days in 2026 at the 2026 rate.

- RSMo 143.761.1, source 6: "there shall be added to the tax for the taxable year an amount determined at the rate determined by section 32.065 upon the amount of the underpayment".
- MO-2210 line 28a, TY2025, source 2: "Divide this amount by 365 days and multiply the product by eight percent."

Rate rule: the director sets the rate by October 22 each year, equal to the September adjusted prime rate rounded to the nearest full percent. The rate changes only if it differs by at least a full point from the rate then in effect. It takes effect January 1.

- RSMo 32.065.2, source 7: "Any such adjusted rate of interest shall be equal to the adjusted prime rate charged by banks, rounded to the nearest full percent, and shall become effective on January first".

Current rates: 8% for 2025 and 7% for 2026, from the TY2025 MO-2210 (lines 28a and 28b, source 2) and 12 CSR 10-41.010 (source 10). The DOR rate table lists 7% for 2027, so a TY2026 underpayment is charged 7% in both 2026 and 2027. (corrected in verification)

- DOR Statutory Interest Rates page, source 11: the deficiency rate row reads "2027 7%".

A short method is available if all payments were made equally and the taxpayer does not annualise. For TY2025 it multiplies the underpayment by 0.05043, minus 0.0001918 per day for amounts paid before April 15, 2026.

Form: Form MO-2210. The penalty goes on Form MO-1040 line 55.

## 8. Differences from federal that would surprise

- There is no 110% prior-year rule.
- A zero-liability prior year does not satisfy the prior-year option.
- The prior-year-income-at-current-rates safe harbor (Exception 2) still exists.
- The annualised method is an exception that is unavailable for the fourth installment.
- The declaration threshold is $100, but the penalty form stops at $500.
- The farmer exemption covers farmers only. The regulation expressly excludes fishing income (12 CSR 10-2.067(2)(B)). (corrected in verification)

## 9. Local income taxes

Kansas City and St. Louis levy 1% earnings taxes. The St. Louis rate is verified (source 8). The Kansas City rate has no primary source here: kcmo.gov returned no page content. (corrected in verification) Each is administered by its city, not by the Missouri DOR. Whether either city mandates quarterly estimates for individuals was not researched.

- St. Louis page, source 8: "The one percent earnings tax is collected from : Residents of the City of St. Louis , regardless of the location of their employer."

## 10. Ambiguities and conflicts

- **Threshold.** The statute and MO-1040ES require a declaration at $100. The MO-2210 says no penalty if the tax after withholding is "less than $500". No statute or regulation for the $500 figure was found.
- **Zero prior-year tax.** RSMo 143.761.4(1) and MO-2210 Exception 1 require that the prior-year return "show a tax liability". But MO-2210 Part I line 6 takes "the smaller of Line 2 or Line 5", and line 5 is skipped only if no 2024 return was filed. A filer with a 2024 return showing $0 tax would get a required annual payment of $0 under Part I, which contradicts the statute.
- **Day count.** MO-2210 line 27a counts days to "either date of payment, the due date of the next installment, or December 31, 2025, whichever is earlier". Instruction E instead gives 260, 199 and 107 days to December 31 for the first three installments, and RSMo 143.761.3 runs the period to payment or April 15. The phrase "due date of the next installment" on line 27a conflicts with both.
- **Scope of the January 31 rule.** The MO-1040ES says the declaration "need" not be filed on January 15 if the return is filed and paid by January 31 when "no declaration was required to be filed during the tax year". The MO-2210 instruction H applies the January 31 rule generally. RSMo 143.521.6 covers both cases.

### Conflicts

- **$100 declaration vs $500 penalty cutoff.** Not resolved. RSMo 143.521.1 lets the director raise the declaration amount, but MO-1040ES still says $100. 12 CSR 10-2.067(3) imposes the addition whenever a declaration was required. No statute, rule or order for the $500 cutoff was found. When tax minus withholding is $100 to $499, the form says no penalty, but the statute and rule would charge one.
- **Zero prior-year tax.** Not resolved. RSMo 143.761.4(1) excludes a prior-year return with no liability. MO-2210 Part I line 5 accepts any 2024 tax figure, including $0, which gives a required payment of $0. The statute governs on its face. Exception 4 (143.761.4(4)) may still give $0 when the prior year's income produces no tax at current rates. Unresolved: what DOR actually assesses when the form gives $0 and the statute does not.
- **Day count.** Resolved. RSMo 143.761.3 runs each shortfall from its due date to payment or April 15. The form carries shortfalls forward (lines 19b and 19d), so line 25 is cumulative. Stopping each column at the next due date (line 26 instruction) then reproduces the statute's total. Instruction E's 260 / 199 / 107 days fit only non-cumulative amounts. Combining them would double count. The short-method factor also matches the statute.
- **Scope of the January 31 rule.** Resolved. RSMo 143.521.6 covers both cases: with no earlier declaration required, the return is the declaration; with a declaration on file, the return is the January 15 amendment. MO-1040ES states only the first case. MO-2210 H is consistent with the statute.
- **Annualising for the fourth installment (found in verification).** No conflict in effect. The statute does not exclude the fourth installment. But for a January 15 installment the annualisation period is all twelve months, so it equals 90% of the year's tax. The form's exclusion changes no result.

### 2026 changes

- The DOR lists the 2027 deficiency rate at 7%, the same as 2026.
- 12 CSR 10-41.010 was amended for 2026. From 2026 the director posts each year's rate on the DOR website by October 22 instead of adding it to the rule.
- SS SCS HCS HJRs 173 & 174 (2026) were truly agreed to and finally passed. If voters approve, the General Assembly must pass laws cutting the top income tax rate as revenue grows, until the tax is gone. The summary of the final version gives no fixed cut formula. The House committee version had one (0.01 points per $20 million). It does not change estimate rules and does not affect tax year 2026.
- The TY2026 MO-1040ES rate chart keeps the top rate at 4.7% over $9,436.
- The revisor shows no amendment of RSMo 143.521 (1998), 143.531 (1990), 143.761 (1989) or 32.065 (1995).
