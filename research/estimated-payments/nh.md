## 1. Does New Hampshire tax individual income for tax year 2026?

**Wages: no.**

- Source 1: "The State of New Hampshire does not have an income tax on an individual's reported W-2 wages."

**Interest and dividends: no. The tax is repealed for 2025 onward, so it does not apply in 2026.** The last year taxed was 2024, at 3%.

- Source 1: "The I&D Tax was repealed for taxable periods beginning after December 31, 2024."
- Source 2: "[Repealed by 2021, 91:99, II, eff. Jan. 1, 2025.]"
- Source 1, rates: 5% for periods ending before December 31, 2023; 4% for periods ending on or after December 31, 2023; 3% for periods ending on or after December 31, 2024.

**Capital gains: no individual tax found.**

**Business profits of sole proprietors: yes, through the Business Profits Tax (BPT).** The BPT is levied on "business organizations". The statute defines these to include proprietorships, so an individual running a business can owe it. The rate is 7.5% for taxable periods ending on or after December 31, 2023. Gross business income must be over $109,000 for taxable periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025.

- Source 4, RSA 77-A:1, I: "any enterprise, whether corporation, partnership, limited liability company, proprietorship, association, business trust"
- Source 4, RSA 77-A:1, III(d): for a proprietorship, the base is "net profit or loss from a business, profession, rental, or farming activities"
- Source 3: "every business organization with gross business income from all business activities of more than $109,000 must file a BPT return."
- Source 4, RSA 77-A:2, III: the rate is "7.5 percent upon the taxable business profits of every business organization."
- The Business Enterprise Tax (BET) is a separate tax at 0.55% of compensation, interest and dividends paid by the enterprise. It applies above $298,000 of gross receipts or enterprise value tax base (Source 3). It is not an income tax.

## 2. Estimated payment rules

**Interest and Dividends Tax.** None for 2026, because the tax is repealed. TIR 2025-001 says no 2025 estimates should be filed. The 2024 return and all four 2024 estimates were still due. (corrected in verification)

- Source 5: "Estimated I&D Tax payments for tax year 2025 should not be filed."

**Business Profits Tax (applies only to a sole proprietor or other business organization above the threshold).** The details below come from RSA 77-A. The DRA's estimate penalty computation and forms were not researched.

- **Who must pay.** A declaration and estimates are required unless the estimated tax is under $200.
  - Source 4, RSA 77-A:6, II: "if the estimated tax is less than $200, a declaration need not be filed"
- **Due dates and shares.** 25% is due on the 15th day of the 4th, 6th, 9th and 12th months of the taxable year. For a calendar year that is April 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.
  - Source 4, RSA 77-A:7, I(a): "25 percent is due and payable on the fifteenth day of the fourth month of the subsequent taxable year"
- **Credit against estimates.** The BET credit may be applied against estimated BPT payments (RSA 77-A:7, I(a)).
- **Return due date.** For a proprietorship (an "all other" organization) the return is due on the 15th day of the 4th month after year end (RSA 77-A:6, I).
- **Current- and prior-year percentages, annualised method, underpayment penalty rate and form (filled in verification).** These are in RSA 21-J:32 (Source 6) and RSA 21-J:28 (Source 7). (corrected in verification)
  - **Required installment.** Each installment is 25% of 90% of the current-period tax. RSA 21-J:32, II(a): "if the estimated tax were equal to 90 percent of the tax for the taxable period".
  - **Penalty.** The penalty is interest at the annual underpayment rate on the underpaid amount. It runs from the installment date to the earlier of payment or the return due date (RSA 21-J:32, I and III).
  - **Rate.** The rate is the IRC section 6621(a)(2) underpayment rate in effect on the preceding September 1, plus 2 points (RSA 21-J:28, II).
  - **Exceptions.** No penalty is due for an installment if cumulative payments meet the lesser of four amounts (RSA 21-J:32, IV):
    - (a) the prior period's tax, if a 12-month prior return showed tax;
    - (b) the prior period's facts and law at current rates;
    - (c) 90% of annualised tax, using 3, 3 or 5, 6 or 8, and 9 or 11 months;
    - (d) nothing, in the first period a return is required.
  - **Form.** DP-2210/2220, "Exceptions and Penalty for the Underpayment of Estimated Tax" (Source 8). The 2025 instructions say "Enter 90% of Line 1" and cite RSA 21-J:32, IV.
  - **2026 rate: NOT FOUND.** The DRA rate table in the DP-2210/2220 instructions could not be extracted from the PDF. A DRA news page for 2026 rates returned "Page Not Found".
- **Withholding:** no state wage withholding exists. None was found in Sources 1–4.

## 4. Ambiguities

- **"Taxable periods beginning after December 31, 2024".** Source 1 uses this wording, while RSA 77 says "eff. Jan. 1, 2025". Both give the same result for calendar-year individuals: 2024 was the last taxed year.
- **BPT on sole proprietors.** The BPT is a business tax, but it reaches an individual's Schedule C, rental or farm profit once gross business income is over $109,000. A reasonable-compensation deduction for the proprietor's own services is allowed, so many small proprietorships owe little or nothing (RSA 77-A:4, III(a)). A $75,000 safe-harbour deduction is available as an election (RSA 77-A:4, III(c)).
- **BPT threshold for 2026.** Source 3 states $109,000 for periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025, and says the threshold "is adjusted biennially". RSA 77-A:6, I still prints $92,000, with a biennial CPI adjustment from 2023. A biennial cycle starting in 2025 implies $109,000 for 2026, but no source states the 2026 figure explicitly.
- **TIR 2025-001** was read in verification (Source 5). It confirms the repeal for periods beginning after December 31, 2024. (corrected in verification)

### Conflicts resolved

- **I&D repeal date.** RSA 77 says "eff. Jan. 1, 2025". Source 1 says periods "beginning after December 31, 2024". TIR 2025-001 explains the difference. The 2021 act repealed the tax for periods after December 31, 2026. A 2023 act (Chapter 79, Laws of 2023) moved it to periods after December 31, 2024. For calendar-year filers both wordings give the same answer: 2024 was the last taxed year.
- **BPT threshold for 2026.** RSA 77-A:6, I requires the threshold to be adjusted every two years "for tax years beginning January 1, 2023". That puts the adjustments in 2023, 2025 and 2027. Source 3's $109,000 applies "for taxable periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025". So $109,000 applies to 2026. This is an inference from the statute's two-year cycle; no source prints a 2026 figure.
