# New Jersey

New Jersey calls the underpayment charge "interest", not a penalty.

## 1. Who must make estimated payments

Residents and nonresidents who expect to owe more than $400 after withholding and other credits. People whose gross income is at or below the filing threshold do not need to pay estimates. The filing threshold is $20,000 for joint, head-of-household and qualifying-widow(er) filers, and $10,000 for single and married-filing-separately filers.

- [NJ-ES], 2026: "you are required to make estimated tax payments if you estimate that you will owe more than $400."
- [NJ-ES], 2026: "If your gross income for the entire year is at or below the filing threshold, … [you] do not need to make estimated payments."
- [NJSA-8-4](a): a declaration is required if tax "can reasonably be expected to be more than $400 in excess of any credits allowable".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 80%, or 66⅔% for farmers.
- Prior year: 100% of the prior year's tax, if the prior-year return covered a full 12 months.
  - (corrected in verification) The 12-month condition comes from the forms. [NJSA-9-6](c)(2), which sets the underpayment, says only "100% of the tax shown on the tax return of the taxpayer for the preceding taxable year". The 12-month condition in the statute attaches to Exception 1 ([NJSA-9-6](d)(1)(A)). See item 10.
- There is effectively no 110% tier. [NJ-150K] says the statute creates a 110% safe harbor above $150,000 taxable gross income ($75,000 married/CU filing separately). It also says the Division computes interest only against the lesser of 80% current or 100% prior. No taxpayer loses the prior-year option.
  - (corrected in verification) The statute confirms why. [NJSA-9-6](d)(3) substitutes 110% only in Exceptions 1 and 2 ("subparagraphs (A) and (B) of paragraph (1)"). It does not touch the underpayment base in [NJSA-9-6](c), which stays at the lesser of 80% current or 100% prior. Paying 100% of prior-year tax on time already leaves no underpayment under (c), so the 110% never binds.

Quotes:

- [NJ-2210], 2025: "at least 80% of your 2025 tax liability … or 100% of your 2024 tax liability"
- [GIT-8], January 2026: "the lesser of 80% of this year's total tax (66 2⁄3% for farmers) or 100% of last year's total tax."
- [NJ-150K]: "only on the difference between what was actually paid and either 100% of last year's tax or 80% of the current year's tax".

## 3. Due dates and shares

- Due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15, in four equal installments.
- If a due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, it moves to the next business day.
- If the obligation arises later in the year, the remaining payments are equal: ⅓ of the estimate each from June, ½ each from September, or all of it in January.
- The January payment is waived if the return is filed by February 15, 2027 and the balance is paid in full.

Quotes:

- [NJ-ES], 2026: "additional payments must be made on or before June 15, 2026, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027."
- [GIT-8], January 2026: "If a due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, that payment is due the following business day." Its 2025 table lists June 16.
- [NJ-ES], 2026: "as long as you file your 2026 Income Tax return by February 15, 2027, and pay the entire balance due."
- [NJSA-8-4](h), added in verification, is the statutory basis: the return filed "on or before February 15 of the succeeding taxable year" with full payment counts as the January declaration.
- [NJSA-8-5](b), added in verification, sets the catch-up: a declaration filed after April 15 and by June 15 is paid "in three equal installments", and one filed by September 15 in two.
- (corrected in verification) February 15, 2027 is a Monday and the third Monday of February. For tax year 2025, [GIT-8] moved the same deadline to February 17, 2026 because February 15, 2026 was a Sunday and the next day a holiday. No source yet states the 2027 date. See item 10.

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is treated as paid evenly, one quarter on each due date, unless the taxpayer shows otherwise. New Jersey's "withholding" for this purpose also includes refundable credits and excess UI/DI/FLI contributions. Examples are the property tax credit, the NJ EITC, the child tax credit and the PTE/BAIT credit.

- [NJ-2210], 2025, line 6: "considered to have been paid evenly (one-fourth of total amount) on each payment due date, unless you can show otherwise."

## 5. Exceptions with no interest

- **Tax below $400.** [NJ-2210], 2025, line 3: "If less than $400, do not complete the rest of this form".
- **No liability in the prior year.** [GIT-8], January 2026: "we will not assess interest if you meet one of the exceptions, or you had no tax obligation on your previous year's tax return." [NJ-2210], 2025: "Your prior year tax liability is zero if a return was not required to be filed."
- **First-year residents and part-year residents.** No separate exception exists. (corrected in verification) Nonresidents use [NJ-2210NR], 2025, which has the same $400 test, 80% / 100% base and four exceptions. Its Exceptions 2–4 multiply the tax by the nonresident "Income Percentage (line 41, 2025 NJ-1040NR)", the share of income from New Jersey sources. No source says which form a part-year resident uses.
- **Death.** (corrected in verification) [NJSA-9-6](c): "No underpayment shall be deemed to exist with respect to a declaration or installment otherwise due on or after the taxpayer's death."
- **Farmers.** A farmer is someone with at least two-thirds of estimated income from farming, including oyster farming. The percentage is 66⅔%, paid in full by January 15. [NJ-ES], 2026: "you can submit your estimated tax payments at any time on or before January 15, 2027." It adds: "the estimated tax must be paid in full." NOT FOUND: a March 1 file-and-pay alternative. Fishermen are not mentioned.
- **Retired or disabled; casualty or disaster.** None exists. (corrected in verification) [NJSA-9-6] has no such exception. [NJSA-49-11](a) lets the Director waive interest only "in excess of the rate of three percentage points above the prime rate". The base prime-plus-3% charge cannot be waived for reasonable cause. [NJSA-49-11](b) waives interest caused by erroneous written advice from the Division.
- **Per-quarter exceptions on Form NJ-2210, Part II.** There are four. Meeting any one of them removes interest for that quarter:
  - Exception 1: the prior year's tax at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% by the four due dates.
  - Exception 2: tax on the prior year's gross income at current-year exemptions and rates, at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%.
  - Exception 3: annualised income (see item 6).
  - Exception 4: 90% of tax on actual income for the first 3, 5 and 8 months.
  - [GIT-8], January 2026: "If you meet any of the four exceptions for any quarter, it eliminates interest charges on the underpayment for that quarter."

## 6. Annualised income installment method

Allowed, but in New Jersey's own form, as Exceptions 3 and 4 on Form NJ-2210, Part II, with worksheets on page 2.

- **Exception 3** annualises gross income through March 31, May 31 and August 31, using factors 4, 2.4 and 1.5. The tax must be paid at 20%, 40% and 60%, which equals 80% times 25%, 50% and 75%. [NJSA-9-6](d)(1)(C) sets it at "80% of the tax for the taxable year" (two-thirds for farmers) on annualised income.
- **Exception 4** uses actual taxable income for those three periods at 90%.
- Neither exception has a January 15 column.
- Estates and trusts use different period ends and factors.

Quotes:

- [NJ-2210], 2025: "Exception 3 – Tax on annualized 2025 income" with columns "20% of Tax / 40% of Tax / 60% of Tax".
- [NJ-2210], 2025: "Exception 4 – Tax on 2025 income over 3, 5, and 8-month periods" with columns "90% of Tax".

## 7. How the interest is computed

- **Method.** Interest is charged on each quarter's underpayment from its due date to the payment date, but no later than the return's original due date. It is computed on Form NJ-2210 and entered on Form NJ-1040, line 52. There are two ways to compute it:
  - **Option 1** is a table of multipliers: .018, .027, .036 and .025 for the four periods in 2025. It is only for calendar-year filers whose payments were timely.
  - **Option 2** uses the rate schedule. Interest is "imposed each month or fraction thereof". It is monthly, not daily.
  - [NJ-2210], 2025: "3% above the average predominant prime rate and is imposed each month or fraction thereof the underpayment exists."
- **Rate rule.** The rate is the prime rate plus 3%, compounded annually. Prime is the average predominant prime rate in effect on December 1 of the prior year. The rate is reset in a later quarter only if prime moves by more than one point.
  - [TB-21R]: "Prime Rate for this purpose is the average predominant Prime Rate … that was in effect on December 1 of the year prior".
  - [NJSA-9-6](c) applies "the rate as is required under the State Tax Uniform Procedure Law", and only to "the 15th day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year". [NJSA-49-3] sets that rate at "three percentage points above the prime rate assessed for each month or fraction thereof, compounded annually".
  - [NJ-ES], 2026: "Interest will be assessed at the prime rate plus 3%, compounded annually."
- **Current rate.** 10.00% for January 1 – December 31, 2026, per [TB-21R]: "Prime Rate (7.00%) + 3% = 10.00%, compounded annually." The 2025 rate was 10.75%. [NJ-2210] shows 10.75% for 4/16/25–1/15/26 and 10.00% for 1/16/26–4/15/26.

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The current-year percentage is 80%, not 90%.
- The threshold is $400 and the test is "more than".
- In practice there is no 110% rule.
- Interest accrues monthly, by month or fraction of a month, at prime plus 3%, compounded annually.
- Refundable credits count as withholding.
- Exception 2 lets the taxpayer use tax on the prior year's income at the current year's rates.
- The January payment waiver runs to February 15, not January 31.
- Farmers must pay 100% of the 66⅔% amount by January 15. No March 1 alternative was found.

## 9. Local income taxes

NOT FOUND. No local income tax with individual estimated payments is mentioned in [NJ-ES], [NJ-2210] or [GIT-8].

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- **January waiver date and condition.** [NJ-ES] for 2026 says the return must be filed by February 15, 2027 and the balance paid. [GIT-8] says only to "pay your balance from the previous calendar year in full by February 17". That date was for tax year 2025, and that text does not mention filing.
- **Prior-year overpayment.** [NJ-ES] says it is "automatically credited to the first quarterly installment". [NJ-2210], 2025, line 6 says it "should be included in the amount of your payment in column B", which is the June column.
- **The $400 boundary.** [NJ-ES] and [GIT-8] say "more than $400". [NJ-2210] line 3 says to stop if less than $400, and the line instructions say "If line 3 is $400 or more, complete lines 4 through 13." Exactly $400 is treated inconsistently.
- **Exception 1 and zero prior-year tax.** [GIT-8] says "You must have filed a tax return and had a tax obligation with New Jersey for the prior year". [NJ-2210] treats prior-year tax as zero if no return was required. [GIT-8] elsewhere says a zero prior-year obligation means no interest.
- **The 110% safe harbor.** The statutory 110% safe harbor described in [NJ-150K] is not applied in practice, per that same notice.

### Resolved conflicts

- **January waiver date.** [NJSA-8-4](h) governs: file and pay in full "on or before February 15 of the succeeding taxable year". [GIT-8]'s February 17 is the tax-year-2025 date after a weekend and holiday shift. For tax year 2026 the nominal date is February 15, 2027. [GIT-8] also omits filing; the statute requires both filing and payment. Still open: whether the Division moves February 15, 2027 (a Monday, the third Monday of February) to February 16. No 2026 GIT-8 or NJ-2210 exists yet to say.
- **Prior-year overpayment credit.** UNRESOLVED. [NJ-ES] credits it "to the first quarterly installment". [NJ-2210] line 6 puts it in "column B", the June column. The statutes read do not address it. This changes the interest on the April installment.
- **The $400 boundary.** [NJSA-8-4](a) governs: a declaration is needed only if tax is "more than $400" over credits. [NJSA-8-4](f) lets a taxpayer with "$400 or less" file by January 15. Exactly $400 requires no estimates. [NJ-2210] line 3's "$400 or more" instruction is a form inconsistency.
- **Exception 1 and zero prior-year tax.** [NJSA-9-6](d)(1)(A) requires "a return showing a liability for tax was filed" for a 12-month year. [GIT-8]'s description of Exception 1 matches the statute. [NJ-2210]'s "zero if a return was not required" sentence has no statutory basis in Exception 1. A taxpayer with zero prior-year tax still owes no interest through [NJSA-9-6](c)(2): the lesser of 80% current or 100% of zero is zero. [GIT-8]'s "no tax obligation on your previous year's tax return" rule rests on that subsection.
- **12-month condition on the 100% base.** [NJSA-9-6](c)(2) states no 12-month condition. [NJ-2210] general instructions add one. The statute governs in principle. The difference matters only after a short prior year.
- **The 110% safe harbor.** Resolved by [NJSA-9-6]: the 110% substitutes only in Exceptions 1 and 2. The (c) base is unchanged, so 110% never produces interest.
