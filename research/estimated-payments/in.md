## 1. Who must make estimated payments

You must pay estimated tax if you have income without enough Indiana withholding and expect to owe $1,000 or more of combined state and county tax. The threshold counts county income tax, which Indiana collects on the same return. No filing-status difference was found.

- [IB3]: "Has a total unpaid state and local income tax liability that is $1,000 or more for the taxable year."
- [IC-4.1](b): "no such declaration shall be required if the estimated tax can reasonably be expected to be less than one thousand dollars ($1,000)."
- [ES40-26] words it as "more than": "You expect to owe more than $1,000 when you file your tax return, then you should pay estimated tax." See item 10.
- (corrected in verification) The statute governs: $1,000 or more is covered. See the Verification section.
- (corrected in verification) From January 1, 2026, [IC-4.1](b) also excludes income on which a pass-through entity remits tax for you under IC 6-3-2.1 (the PTET election). [IC-4.1](b): "or for which tax is not remitted on behalf of the individual under IC 6-3-2.1".

## 2. Required annual payment

- Current year: 90% of this year's state plus county tax after credits.
- Prior year: 100% of last year's tax.
- Higher prior-year percentage: 110% if prior-year federal AGI was more than $150,000, or more than $75,000 if married filing separately. Farmers and fishermen keep 100%.
- High-income taxpayers do not lose the prior-year option. They use 110% of it.
- Prior-year return: for tax year 2025, if no 2024 return was filed, line 7 is "N/A" and only the 90% option is available. A part-year resident in the prior year must annualize the prior-year tax.
- New for tax year 2026: [IC-4.1](a)(3), effective 1-1-2026, treats prior-year tax as $0 for someone who filed no prior-year return and can show no prior-year liability.
- No 12-month requirement is stated in the Indiana documents. The statute applies IRC Section 6654, which has one (see item 10).

Quotes:

- [IB3]: "(1) 90 percent of the tax shown on the current year's return; (2) 100 percent of the tax shown on the previous year's return."
- [2210-25] CAUTION box: "If your 2024 federal adjusted gross income is more than $150,000 ($75,000 if your 2024 filing status is married filing separately)".
- [2210-25] line 7: "If you did not file a 2024 tax return, enter "N/A" for line 7 and see instructions for line 8."
- [IC-4.1](a)(3): "Section 6654 ... shall be applied as if the tax liability for the preceding taxable year ... was zero dollars ($0)."

## 3. Due dates and share due

There are four equal installments, each 25% of the required annual payment. The 2026 dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027.

Weekend and holiday dates shift to the next business day. The 2025 schedule shows the shift: the second 2025 installment is dated June 16, 2025.

The January installment is waived if you file and pay in full by January 31. For tax year 2025 that date became February 2, 2026, because January 31, 2026 was a Saturday.

- [ES40-26]: "The remaining three payments are due June 15 and Sept. 15, 2026, and Jan. 15, 2027."
- [2210-25] line 14: "Divide the amount on line 8 by four and enter the result in each column."
- [IB3]: "If the due date falls on a national or state holiday, Saturday, or Sunday, payment is timely".
- [IB3]: "pays the entire tax due by January 31, the taxpayer will not receive a penalty for the installment payment due January 15."
- [2210-25] Section B: "pay the tax due by Feb. 2, 2026, you will not be required to make a fourth installment".

## 4. Withholding

Withholding is presumed paid evenly, one quarter per installment. You may instead use the actual withholding dates. You may be asked for pay statements as proof.

Nonresident pass-through withholding follows the timing of the income, but only if you prove the income was uneven and file IT-2210A. Estate and trust distributions follow the quarter of the distribution.

- [IB3]: "the taxes will be presumed to have been remitted evenly across all four quarters of the taxable year."
- [IB3]: "Withholding payments will be treated as occurring on the date of actual withholding (i.e., the date on which you are paid)".
- [2210-25] line 15: "divide the amount on line 5 by four and enter the result in each column."

## 5. Exceptions with no penalty

- Tax below an amount: no penalty if tax after credits, less withholding and pass-through entity tax (PTET) credit, is under $1,000. [2210-25] line 6: "Subtract line 5 from line 3 - If less than $1,000, STOP HERE! You do not owe a penalty".
- No prior-year liability: for tax year 2025 there is no such exception on the form. A missing 2024 return means line 7 is "N/A". For 2026 and later, [IC-4.1](a)(3) treats a no-liability prior year as $0, which makes the required payment $0.
- First-year residents: NOT FOUND as a separate exception. A taxpayer with no prior-year Indiana return falls under the "N/A" rule above.
- Part-year residents: the required installment is divided by the number of installment periods in which you lived in Indiana. A prior-year part-year return must be annualized. [2210-25] line 14: "you must divide line 8 by the number of installment periods during which you were a resident of Indiana."
- Farmers and fishermen: this applies if two-thirds of gross income in the current or prior year came from farming or fishing. The current-year percentage is 66 2/3% (.667). There is one due date, January 15. No penalty applies if the return is filed and paid by March 1. For tax year 2025 that date was March 2, 2026.
  - [2210-25] Section A: "you have only one payment due date for 2025 estimated tax - Jan. 15, 2026."
  - [2210-25] Section A: "Option 2. File your Form IT-40 by March 2, 2026, and pay all the tax due."
- Retired or disabled: NOT FOUND in [2210-25], [IB3] or [ES40-26].
- Casualty or disaster waivers: NOT FOUND in [2210-25], [IB3] or [ES40-26]. See item 10 on reasonable cause.

## 6. Annualised income installment method

The method is allowed. Use Schedule IT-2210A. Its periods are the federal ones: through 3-31, 5-31, 8-31 and 12-31. Its factors are 4.0, 2.4, 1.5 and 1.0, and its percentages are 22.5%, 45%, 67.5% and 90% [2210A-25].

- [IB3]: "Schedule IT-2210A should be used to compute the annualized income installment amount."

## 7. How the penalty is computed

The penalty is a flat 10% of each period's underpayment. It is not an interest charge, and it does not depend on how many days late the payment was.

- A late payment counts toward the next period.
- An overpayment in a later period first fills earlier shortfalls, but this does not reduce the penalties already incurred.
- The 10% rate is fixed in the statute. It is not reset each year.

Compute it on Schedule IT-2210, either by the short method (Section D) or the regular method (Section E).

- [2210-25]: "The penalty is 10% of the underpayment for each underpaid installment period."
- [IB3]: "If a payment is made after the installment due date, the payment is considered to be made in the following installment period."
- [IB3]: "this carryback will not reduce any penalties."
- [IC-4.1](b): "there shall be added to the tax a penalty in an amount prescribed by IC 6-8.1-10-2.1 (b)."
- [IC-2.1](b): "the penalty described in subsection (a) is ten percent (10%) of".
- Short method, [2210-25] line 13: "Multiply line 12 by 10% (.10)."

## 8. Differences from federal that may surprise

- The penalty is a flat 10% per underpaid quarter, not interest by the day. One day late costs as much as months late.
- County income tax is part of the state estimate. The ES-40 voucher asks you to split each payment between state and county tax. A wrong split carries no penalty. [IB3]: "a failure to allocate a payment properly will not result in a penalty."
- A part-year resident divides the required payment over only the periods of residence.
- The file-by-January-31 waiver of the fourth installment exists, as it does federally. The farmer filing deadline is March 1, as it is federally.

## 9. Local income taxes

These exist. Indiana counties levy local income tax. The Indiana Department of Revenue collects it on the state return and on the same Form ES-40, with a county code. There is no separate local estimated payment system.

- [ES40-26] line E: "Amount of county income tax due (line C multiplied by the appropriate county tax rate(s))".

## 10. Ambiguities and disagreements

- Threshold wording: [ES40-26] says "more than $1,000". [IB3], [2210-25] and [IC-4.1] treat exactly $1,000 as covered ("$1,000 or more"; a declaration is not required only if tax is "less than" $1,000). (corrected in verification) [IB3] is not consistent either: its Penalties section says "exceed $1,000". Resolved in the Verification section.
- 12-month rule: the Indiana forms do not say whether the prior-year return must cover 12 months. [IC-4.1](a) requires payments "in the installments as provided by Section 6654 of the Internal Revenue Code", and IRC 6654(d)(1)(B)(ii) has a 12-month condition. Whether Indiana adopts that condition is not stated.
- Federal waivers: it is unclear whether the federal waivers in IRC 6654(e)(3), for casualty, disaster, retirement and disability, carry over through [IC-4.1](a). No Indiana document mentions them.
- Reasonable cause: [IC-2.1](d) lists failures to file, pay tax shown, remit trust tax, or pay a deficiency. It does not name estimated-tax underpayment, so whether reasonable cause can waive this penalty is unclear.
- Fourth-installment waiver date: [IB3] gives January 31. [2210-25] gives Feb. 2, 2026, the weekend-shifted date. The farmer date is March 1 in [IB3] and March 2, 2026 in [2210-25].
- No-return rule: the 2026 rule in [IC-4.1](a)(3) is newer than [IB3] (March 2023) and [2210-25]. No 2026 IT-2210 exists yet to show how DOR will apply it.

### Conflicts resolved

- Threshold at exactly $1,000. The statute governs, and it was not amended on this point in 2025 or 2026. [IC-4.1](b) excuses a declaration only "if the estimated tax can reasonably be expected to be less than one thousand dollars ($1,000)". So $1,000 exactly is covered. [2210-25] line 6 agrees: it stops only below $1,000. The "more than $1,000" wording in [ES40-26] and "exceed $1,000" in the [IB3] Penalties section are informal and do not govern.
- Fourth-installment and farmer dates. These do not conflict. [IB3] states the rule (January 31, March 1). [2210-25] applies the weekend shift for 2025 (Feb. 2, 2026; March 2, 2026). [IB3]: "If the due date falls on a national or state holiday, Saturday, or Sunday, payment is timely". For 2026 the dates are January 31, 2027 (a Sunday, so February 1, 2027) and March 1, 2027 (a Monday). The weekday arithmetic is ours; no 2026 IT-2210 exists yet.
- 12-month rule. Still unresolved. [IC-4.1](a) applies IRC 6654 "at the time or times and in the installments as provided by Section 6654". No Indiana document says whether the 12-month condition of IRC 6654(d)(1)(B)(ii) carries over.
- Federal waivers for casualty, disaster, retirement and disability. Still unresolved. No Indiana primary source mentions them.
- Reasonable cause. Confirmed as stated. [IC-2.1](d) waives the penalty for failures "failure to file a return, pay the full amount of tax shown ... or pay the deficiency". It does not name an estimated-tax underpayment.
- The 2026 no-return rule. Confirmed. P.L.205-2025, SEC.10 added [IC-4.1](a)(3), effective 1-1-2026. No 2026 IT-2210 is published to show how DOR applies it.

- Retired or disabled, and casualty or disaster: confirmed NOT FOUND. ### 2026 changes

- P.L.205-2025, SEC.10, effective 1-1-2026: the [IC-4.1](a)(3) zero prior-year rule; the [IC-4.1](b) exclusion for income on which tax is remitted under IC 6-3-2.1; and recaptured credits are included in "estimated tax" ([IC-4.1](a)(1)).
- SEA 243 (2026), Sec. 17, effective July 1, 2026, rewords [IC-4.1]. [SYN-26]: "Clarifies that the estimated payment tax penalty for individual taxpayers is calculated by the rate prescribed in IC 6-8.1-10-2.1(b)". The 10% rate does not change. The enrolled act text was not read.
- The state rate used on the 2026 worksheet is 2.95%. [ES40-26] line D: "Amount of state income tax due (line C multiplied by .0295)". The 2025 [2210A-25] uses 3%. This changes the tax, not the estimated-payment rules.
- No 2026 change to the $1,000 threshold, the 90%, 100% or 110% percentages, the due dates, or the 10% penalty was found in [SYN-26].
